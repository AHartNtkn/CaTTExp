# Schedules in free Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-categories, round 3

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Rounds 1 and 2 and their verified answers are archived in `answered/gray-closed-scheduling-2026-11-18.md` and `answered/gray-closed-scheduling-r2-2026-11-18.md`. The smallest open case is Question 1.*

## Background

**Schedules.** Fix a finite poset P on labels {1, …, n}, with numerical order as the left/right order of incomparable labels. Let S(P) be the category with:
- objects: the linear extensions of P;
- generating arrows: swaps ji → ij of adjacent P-incomparable labels with i < j;
- equations: commuting squares for swaps at disjoint positions, and the braid hexagon for three pairwise incomparable labels.

Then S(P)(w, v) is a singleton if Inv_P(v) ⊆ Inv_P(w) and empty otherwise. Here Inv_P(w) is the set of pairs i < j, incomparable, with j before i in w. (Proof: move the first differing label left, then resolve the first swaps by a square or a hexagon and induct.)

**Gray monoids.** In the free strict Gray monoid (a monoid in 2-Cat for Gray's lax tensor) on a set of objects and generating 1-cells, fix the image in the free strict monoidal category, i.e. the string diagram. Then the hom-category between the 1-cells with that diagram is S(P). Here P is the diagram's dependency order, with left/right given by planarity. The 2-cells are whiskered interchangers (f ⊗ B′)∘(A ⊗ g) ⇒ (A′ ⊗ g)∘(f ⊗ B), "right factor first ⇒ left factor first".

**The ω-version.** The hom ω-category of the Steiner complex ν(I^⊗n), from bottom to top, is not thin: two hexagon 2-cells are joined by the 3-cell ***. Gray's 2-tensor is its intelligent 2-truncation (Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches*, A.25 and A.31). For a dependency poset P, the subcomplex K_P ⊆ I^⊗n spanned by the faces (order ideal J, S ⊆ min(P∖J)) has paths equal to the linear extensions of P. Its truncated hom is S(P).

## Setting: the biclosed version

Formulas are built from atoms with ⊗ and the residuals B\A and A/B, with no unit. 𝒢 is the free **Gray-monoidal biclosed strict 2-category** on a set of generating 1-cells fᵢ : Pᵢ → Qᵢ between formulas. Its structure:
- a strict 2-category with a Gray monoidal structure (lax interchangers, as above);
- closed structure: isomorphisms of hom-categories Φ : 𝒢(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ 𝒢(B, A\C), and the mirror one for /.
  - Φ is **strictly natural in the context variable B**.
  - Φ is natural in the **bound variable A only up to the interchanger**.
  - Strict naturality in A would force (f ⊗ B′)(A ⊗ g) = (A′ ⊗ g)(f ⊗ B), collapsing distinct schedules into one 1-cell. That is not wanted.

**Two warnings, both established.**
1. *Nesting is not invariant under naive normalization.* For a generator h : U → U with U = X\Y,

   ```
   h = Λ(ev) ∘ h = Λ(ev ∘ (1 ⊗ h))
   ```

   So h can be "ambient" or "inside a closure". β-reduction ev ∘ (1 ⊗ Λk) = k also changes nesting. Any invariant notion of "event inside a closure" must start from a βη-canonical form.
2. *Unit-free planar coherence.* In the free unit-free biclosed monoidal 1-category, a balanced sequent (each atom twice, opposite polarities) has at most one morphism, and exactly one when it is provable in the Lambek calculus with product. So the purely structural parts of 1-cells are unique.

## What is known from round 1 (proved)

1. **A word invariant.** Sending each generator to its label gives an invariant W of 1-cell equality with values in words (composition order), with W(Λu) = W(u), W(ev) = ε and W(D ⊗ u) = W(u). It is realised by the one-object model whose 1-cells are words, with a unique 2-cell between words with the same multiset. So 1-cells with different generator order are distinct.
2. **Closure trees are not invariants of components.** With generators a : X → A and f : A ⊗ B → C, c = Λ_A(f), e = ev: the interchanger gives θ : s = e∘(a⊗U)∘(X⊗c) ⇒ t = e∘(A⊗c)∘(a⊗B), and t = f∘(a⊗B) by β. Every representative of s has a generator under currying (a Boolean biclosed poset rules out the needed structural prefix), while t is tensor-only. So any βη-normal form fixing tensor-only terms gives s and t different closure trees, though they are connected by a 2-cell. "Cross-level cells are generated by ordinary interchangers" does not imply "the canonical tree is invariant".
3. **Question 1, partial.** For φ : P → C/B and b : X → B, the forward comparison α = e_B ∗ χ_{φ,b} : t₁ ⇒ t₂ exists, W distinguishes t₁, t₂, and Λ_X(t₂) = (C/b)∘φ by strict naturality in the context variable. Whether 𝒢(t₁, t₂) = {α} and 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ is open: the tensor-only schedule theorem does not control 2-cells built by currying, uncurrying and η-expansion.
4. **Mapping-space biclosedness is too weak for Question 4.** Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari (arXiv 2006.14495, Rem. 2.20): Map(X ⊗ Y, Z) ≇ Map(X, Fun^{opgr}(Y, Z)) in general; only cores agree.

## What is known from round 2 (proved)

5. **An integer model.** Let 𝓜 be the strict 2-category with:
   - objects the integers;
   - for q ≥ p, the hom 𝓜(p, q) equal to the poset (ℤ, ≤) of weights; otherwise empty;
   - composition adding weights;
   - w(p ⊗ u) = w(u) + p·δ(u) and w(u ⊗ q) = w(u), where δ(u) = target − source;
   - p\r = r − p and r/q = r − q, with left currying subtracting p·δ and right currying leaving the weight unchanged.

   𝓜 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category. An interchanger raises the weight by δ(f)·δ(g). A matrix-valued version (objects in ℤ^r) records ordered generator pairs.
6. **Question 1, reverse direction.** With distinct atoms, 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ and 𝒢(t, s) = ∅. For t₁ and t₂ the weights are 0 and 1, from |B| = 1, |C| = 2 and |P| = |X| = 0. For s and t they are −1 and 0. Every forward 2-cell between them uses **exactly one** interchanger whose two arguments each contain one generator. Every other interchanger in it has a generator-free argument.
7. **Undirected components.** π₀ 𝒢(A, C) ≅ 𝓑_Σ(A, C), the free ordinary unit-free biclosed monoidal category. Two 1-cells are connected by a zigzag of 2-cells iff their images there agree.
8. **The image and W do not classify 1-cells.** Take generators f : A → B and g : B → C with padding L, R, and let m = L ⊗ (g ∘ f) ⊗ R.
   - Currying g with the right padding gives u ⇒ m.
   - Currying g with the left padding gives m ⇒ v.
   - u, m and v have equal images in 𝓑_Σ, and W(u) = W(v).
   - Their weights (with |L| = |R| = |A| = 0, |B| = 1, |C| = 2) are −1, 0 and 1, so the three 1-cells are distinct.
   - The side of currying is data of the 1-cell, and schedules with only generator events cannot describe this component.

## Two further observations (proved)

9. **Additive models cannot see interchangers with a structural argument.** Call a model *additive* if 1-cells carry a weight in an ordered abelian group, δ(u) = weight(target) − weight(source) is additive under composition and tensor, and an interchanger χ_{f,g} adds a bilinear term β(δ(f), δ(g)). The model 𝓜 of item 5 and its matrix version are additive. Every structural 1-cell (ev, curried evaluations, associators, unitors) has δ = 0, so every interchanger with a structural argument has weight 0 in every additive model. Such models therefore cannot decide whether two 2-cells differing in those interchangers are equal.
10. **The axioms do not make evaluation central.** For ev : A ⊗ (A\C) → C and a generator g : Y → Y′, compare (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y), related by χ_{ev,g}. Currying A on the left (using strict naturality in the context variable and the target) turns this into the same question for m_Y := Φ(ev ⊗ Y) and g, so currying only transports it. Likewise for χ_{ev,ev′} at independent positions. Unless centrality is imposed (as in Garner–Hirschowitz–Pardon's premonoidal construction), structural maps are non-central events in 𝒢. In the quotient 𝒢_c of 𝒢 in which every interchanger with a structural argument is an identity, Question 1 follows at once from item 6.

## Questions

1. **(Forward uniqueness, smallest case.)** Prove 𝒢(t₁, t₂) = {α} and 𝒢(s, t) = {θ}, or show that they fail. By item 6 it suffices to show that interchangers with a generator-free argument are coherent: any two 2-cells between the same 1-cells that differ only in such interchangers are equal by the Gray and closure equations. By item 9 this needs a rewriting or coherence argument, or a non-additive model; by item 10 it is not automatic, since structural interchangers need not be identities.
2. **(1-cells within a component.)** For a fixed arrow of 𝓑_Σ, describe the 1-cells of 𝒢 over it. Is a 1-cell determined by its image together with the matrix-valued weight of item 5, or with a "curry side" label on each generator? Is each hom-category a product of schedule categories S(P) whose events include curry-side choices, as for u ⇒ m ⇒ v of item 8? By item 10, should the invariant schedule structural events (evaluations, curried evaluations) as well as generator events?
3. **(Thinness and rewriting.)** Is every hom-category of 𝒢 thin? Give a terminating and confluent presentation of its 2-cells, or a pair of distinct parallel 2-cells.
4. **(ω-version.)** With biclosedness on full hom ω-categories, are the hom ω-categories of the free Gray-monoidal biclosed (∞,ω)-category over a fixed arrow of 𝓑_Σ products of ν(K_P)-homs for suitable P?
5. **(Is evaluation central?)** In the free Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category 𝒢, is χ_{ev,g} an identity (equivalently, is ev central), or are (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y) distinct 1-cells? In the (∞,ω) setting, is evaluation for Campion's Gray tensor (biclosed on mapping spaces) central, i.e. is the corresponding interchanger invertible or trivial? The answer decides whether the right theory is 𝒢 or its quotient 𝒢_c of item 10, and whether 𝒢_c is still sound for the (∞,ω) Gray tensor.

## What counts as a solution

A proof of Question 1; a decision for Question 5; a complete invariant of 1-cells for Question 2; a rewriting system or a non-thin example for Question 3; or a reference treating 2-cells of free Gray-monoidal (bi)closed 2-categories.

# Researcher Response

**The proposed 1-cell invariants in Question 2 fail even without generating 1-cells.** There are infinitely many distinct structural 1-cells
\[
H^n:T\longrightarrow T,\qquad n\ge 0,
\]
with the same ordinary biclosed image, empty generator word, and zero value in every integer or matrix model of item 5. Moreover, there are 2-cells in both directions between any two of them.

Consequently, the category over a fixed ordinary biclosed arrow need not be **isomorphic** to a product of schedule categories \(S(P)\). This does not rule out an equivalence to a poset: distinct 1-cells could be uniquely isomorphic. I do not establish forward uniqueness in Question 1 or non-thinness in Question 3.

Here is the construction and its verification.

## 1. Two structural retractions

Choose atoms \(A,B,C,D\), and put
\[
\begin{aligned}
U&=A\backslash(A\otimes B),&
X&=A\otimes B,&
X'&=A\otimes U,\\
V&=(C\otimes D)/D,&
Y&=C\otimes D,&
Y'&=V\otimes D.
\end{aligned}
\]

The adjunction units are
\[
\eta^L_{A,B}
=\Lambda_A(1_{A\otimes B}):B\longrightarrow U,
\]
and
\[
\eta^R_{D,C}
=\Lambda_D^R(1_{C\otimes D}):C\longrightarrow V.
\]

Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
u&=1_A\otimes\eta^L_{A,B}:X\longrightarrow X',
&
e&=\operatorname{ev}^L_{A,A\otimes B}:X'\longrightarrow X,\\
v&=\eta^R_{D,C}\otimes1_D:Y\longrightarrow Y',
&
d&=\operatorname{ev}^R_{C\otimes D,D}:Y'\longrightarrow Y.
\end{aligned}
\]
The β equations give strict equalities of 1-cells
\[
eu=1_X,\qquad dv=1_Y. \tag{1}
\]

Write composition rightmost first, and set \(T=X\otimes Y\). Consider
\[
\boxed{
H=
(e\otimes1_Y)
(1_{X'}\otimes d)
(u\otimes1_{Y'})
(1_X\otimes v)
:T\longrightarrow T.
} \tag{2}
\]

Its chronological order is \(v,u,d,e\). Every arrow in (2) is structural, and every formula is unit-free.

## 2. There are structural 2-cells \(1_T\Rightarrow H\Rightarrow1_T\)

For clarity, denote these comparisons by
\[
\iota:1_T\Rightarrow H,
\qquad
\pi:H\Rightarrow1_T.
\]

They are ordinary whiskered interchangers:
\[
\iota
=
(e\otimes1_Y)
*
\chi_{u,d}
*
(1_X\otimes v), \tag{3}
\]
and
\[
\pi
=
\bigl[(e\otimes1_Y)(1_{X'}\otimes d)\bigr]
*
\chi_{u,v}. \tag{4}
\]

For (3), the source before applying (1) is
\[
(e\otimes1_Y)
(u\otimes1_Y)
(1_X\otimes d)
(1_X\otimes v)
=1_T,
\]
while the target is exactly \(H\).

For (4), the target before applying (1) is
\[
(e\otimes1_Y)
(1_{X'}\otimes d)
(1_{X'}\otimes v)
(u\otimes1_Y)
=1_T.
\]

There is also a definite equation between these comparisons:
\[
\boxed{\pi\circ\iota=1_{1_T}.} \tag{5}
\]

Indeed, the interchanger composition axiom says
\[
\chi_{u,dv}
=
\bigl[(1_{X'}\otimes d)*\chi_{u,v}\bigr]
\circ
\bigl[\chi_{u,d}*(1_X\otimes v)\bigr].
\]
Because \(dv=1_Y\), its left side is an identity 2-cell. Postwhiskering by \(e\otimes1_Y\) gives (5).

Thus \(1_T\) is a retract of \(H\) in the hom-category \(\mathcal G(T,T)\). At this stage, nothing proves that \(H\) and \(1_T\) are distinct. That requires a model.

## 3. A biclosed Gray model separating \(H\) from the identity

Let
\[
F=\langle \ell,r\rangle
\]
be the free group on two generators. Define a strict 2-category \(\mathcal D\) as follows.

### The underlying 2-category and tensor

The objects are integers. For every pair \(m,n\), the hom-category \(\mathcal D(m,n)\) has:

- one object for each element of \(F\), representing a 1-cell \(m\to n\);
- exactly one morphism between each ordered pair of its objects.

Composition of 1-cells is group multiplication:
\[
g\circ f=gf.
\]
The identity 1-cell has label \(1\in F\). All composition operations on 2-cells are uniquely determined.

Tensor on objects is addition. Tensoring a 1-cell by an object translates its source and target and leaves its group label unchanged:
\[
a\otimes(k:m\to n)
=
(k:a+m\to a+n),
\]
and similarly on the right.

Choose the unique 2-cell with the prescribed endpoints as each interchanger. The tensor sections and associativity are strict, and every Gray coherence equation holds because parallel 2-cells are unique. The tensor unit is \(0\).

### Left closure

Define
\[
a\backslash c=c-a.
\]
For fixed \(a\), the right adjoint to \(a\otimes-\) acts on 1-cell labels by
\[
R_a^L(k)=\ell^a k\ell^{-a}. \tag{6}
\]

The currying isomorphism
\[
\mathcal D(a+b,c)\longrightarrow\mathcal D(b,c-a)
\]
is
\[
\Lambda_a^L(k)=\ell^a k, \tag{7}
\]
with inverse
\[
\operatorname{uncurry}_a^L(k)=\ell^{-a}k. \tag{8}
\]

These are isomorphisms of categories: their maps on 1-cell labels are inverse bijections, and all 2-cells are unique.

The required naturality equations hold strictly. With \(h=\ell^a\), context naturality is
\[
\Lambda_a^L(kq)
=hkq
=\Lambda_a^L(k)q.
\]
Codomain naturality is
\[
R_a^L(t)\Lambda_a^L(k)
=(hth^{-1})(hk)
=htk
=\Lambda_a^L(tk).
\]

The unit and counit have labels
\[
\eta^L=h,\qquad \varepsilon^L=h^{-1}.
\]
Their naturality equations are
\[
R_a^L(k)h=hk,
\qquad
h^{-1}R_a^L(k)=kh^{-1},
\]
and their triangle identities reduce to
\[
h^{-1}h=1.
\]
Equivalently, β and η follow immediately from (7)–(8).

### Right closure

Define
\[
c/a=c-a,
\]
and use
\[
R_a^R(k)=r^a k r^{-a},
\qquad
\Lambda_a^R(k)=r^a k,
\qquad
\operatorname{uncurry}_a^R(k)=r^{-a}k.
\]
The verification is identical.

All corresponding coherence equations on 2-cells hold by uniqueness. In particular, the bound-variable comparisons supplied by interchangers cause no additional constraint in this model.

This construction also normalizes closure at the tensor unit:
\[
\ell^0=r^0=1.
\]
It therefore does not rely on choosing a nonidentity unit or counit for tensoring by the unit.

### Evaluating the witness

Interpret each of \(A,B,C,D\) as the integer \(1\). Then
\[
u\longmapsto\ell,\qquad
e\longmapsto\ell^{-1},\qquad
v\longmapsto r,\qquad
d\longmapsto r^{-1}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
H\longmapsto \ell^{-1}r^{-1}\ell r.
\]
This is a nonempty reduced free-group word, so
\[
\boxed{H\ne1_T\quad\text{in }\mathcal G.} \tag{9}
\]

More generally,
\[
H^n\longmapsto
(\ell^{-1}r^{-1}\ell r)^n.
\]
There is no cancellation between consecutive copies of the displayed word. Its \(n\)-th power has reduced length \(4n\). Hence
\[
\boxed{H^m\ne H^n\quad\text{whenever }m\ne n.} \tag{10}
\]

This model uses exactly the fixed-object adjunction formulation in the question. In particular, it does not impose strict naturality in the bound variable or strict equality of the two mixed residual composites.

## 4. Why the proposed invariants all agree

Let
\[
Q:\mathcal G\longrightarrow\mathcal B_\Sigma
\]
be the ordinary biclosed quotient from item 7.

### Ordinary biclosed image

In \(\mathcal B_\Sigma\), tensor is bifunctorial. Therefore
\[
Q(H)
=(eu)\otimes(dv)
=1_T.
\]
Thus
\[
Q(H^n)=1_T
\qquad(n\ge0). \tag{11}
\]

The ordinary planar coherence statement in the question is consistent with this calculation: it identifies the ordinary images of these structural arrows.

### Generator word and per-generator labels

No generating 1-cell appears in \(H^n\). Therefore
\[
W(H^n)=\varepsilon.
\]
Any assignment of curry-side labels **only to generating 1-cells** is likewise empty for every \(H^n\).

### Integer and matrix weights

In every normalized integer or matrix model of item 5, the units and evaluations used above have displacement zero and weight zero.

For example,
\[
|U|=|A\otimes B|-|A|=|B|,
\]
so \(|X'|=|X|\). The unit is the curry of an identity 1-cell; its weight is zero because the displacement entering the currying correction is zero. The evaluation is the uncurry of an identity, with the same conclusion. The right-closure calculation is identical.

Tensoring these arrows by objects leaves their zero weights unchanged, and composition adds weights. Hence
\[
w(H^n)=0
\]
for every \(n\), every atom valuation, and every matrix dimension.

Combining this with (10) proves:

> The ordinary biclosed image, the generator word, all the matrix-valued weights from item 5, and all curry-side labels attached to generating 1-cells are jointly insufficient to classify 1-cells.

This failure already occurs for the empty generating signature.

## 5. The exact schedule-product obstruction

Whiskering and composing \(\iota,\pi\) gives
\[
\iota_n:1_T\Rightarrow H^n,
\qquad
\pi_n:H^n\Rightarrow1_T.
\]
Therefore, for every \(m,n\), there is a 2-cell
\[
H^m\xRightarrow{\pi_m}1_T
\xRightarrow{\iota_n}H^n.
\]

The category over the fixed ordinary arrow \(1_T\) thus has infinitely many distinct 1-cells, with 2-cells in both directions between every pair in this family.

Every \(S(P)\) is a poset category, and any product of poset categories is again a poset category. In such a category, arrows in both directions force equality of their endpoints. Equations (9)–(10) therefore imply
\[
\boxed{
Q^{-1}(1_T)\text{ is not isomorphic to any product of schedule categories }S(P).
} \tag{12}
\]

This argument does not depend on the product being finite.

The distinction between **isomorphism** and **equivalence** is essential. A thin category can have distinct, uniquely isomorphic objects. Consequently, (12) does not yet refute an equivalence with a product of schedule posets after passing to a skeleton.

## 6. The remaining 2-cell question is explicit

Equation (5) makes
\[
E=\iota\circ\pi:H\Rightarrow H
\]
an idempotent:
\[
E^2
=\iota(\pi\iota)\pi
=\iota\pi
=E.
\]

The unresolved equality is
\[
\boxed{\iota\circ\pi\stackrel{?}{=}1_H.} \tag{13}
\]

If (13) fails, it gives two distinct parallel 2-cells and answers Question 3 negatively. If the hom-category is thin, (13) must hold, and \(\iota,\pi\) are inverse isomorphisms.

The separating model above cannot decide (13): it has exactly one 2-cell between every pair of parallel 1-cells. Its purpose is to distinguish **1-cells**, and it does that.

There is also a qualification to the proposed reduction in Question 1. The one-crossing count gives a factorization of each forward comparison of the form
\[
z_+\circ m\circ z_-,
\]
where \(z_\pm\) use interchangers with a generator-free argument, and \(m\) is a typed instance of the unique interchanger involving both generators. Thinness of the structural comparisons determines the transports for a **fixed** \(m\). A complete proof must additionally identify the comparisons obtained from different admissible typed instances of \(m\), using the Gray and closure equations. The crossing count alone does not supply that identification.

Thus the established result here is the structural counterexample to the proposed invariants and to an exact schedule-product classification. Forward uniqueness, a complete invariant, the equality (13), and the equivalence formulation of the higher-dimensional question remain unresolved.
