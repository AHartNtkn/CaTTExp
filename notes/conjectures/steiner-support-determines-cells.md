# Cells of free strict ω-categories on Steiner complexes are determined by their support

*Self-contained problem statement. No project context needed.*

> **Status: SOLVED for Gray tensors of globular pasting diagrams, and for any acyclic regular directed complex (2026-11-18).** The result follows from Hadzihasanovic, *Combinatorics of higher-categorical diagrams* (arXiv 2404.07273), Prop. 8.3.17 and Thm 11.2.18. Open for arbitrary Steiner complexes. See "Literature status" at the end.

## Background and definitions

Throughout, we use Steiner's theory of augmented directed complexes:
- R. Steiner, *Omega-categories and chain complexes*, Homology Homotopy Appl. 6 (2004);
- D. Ara, G. Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches pour les ∞-catégories strictes*, Mém. SMF 165 (2020), arXiv:1607.00668.

**Augmented directed complex (ADC).** A chain complex of free abelian groups K = (Kₙ, ∂)ₙ≥₀ with augmentation ε : K₀ → ℤ. Each Kₙ comes with a distinguished basis Bₙ. Write Kₙ* for the submonoid of non-negative combinations of Bₙ. For a chain c, supp(c) ⊆ B is the set of basis elements with non-zero coefficient. For c ∈ Kₙ, write ∂c = (∂c)₊ − (∂c)₋ with (∂c)₊ and (∂c)₋ in Kₙ₋₁* of disjoint supports.

**Atoms.** For b ∈ Bₙ, define ⟨b⟩ₙ^± = b and, downward, ⟨b⟩ₖ⁻ = (∂⟨b⟩ₖ₊₁⁻)₋ and ⟨b⟩ₖ⁺ = (∂⟨b⟩ₖ₊₁⁺)₊.
- The basis is **unital** if ε(⟨b⟩₀⁻) = ε(⟨b⟩₀⁺) = 1 for all b.
- It is **strongly loop-free** if the relation generated by "a < b whenever a ∈ supp((∂b)₋) or b ∈ supp((∂a)₊)" is a strict partial order on B.

A **Steiner complex** is an ADC with a unital, strongly loop-free basis.

**The strict ω-category ν(K).** An n-cell is a table x = (x₀⁻, x₀⁺, …, xₙ⁻, xₙ⁺) with
- xₖ^± ∈ Kₖ*,
- xₙ⁻ = xₙ⁺,
- ∂xₖ^± = xₖ₋₁⁺ − xₖ₋₁⁻ for k ≥ 1,
- ε(x₀^±) = 1.

Composition along k adds chains in dimensions above k and glues boundaries. Identities pad with equal entries.

Known facts:
- Steiner: if K is a Steiner complex then ν(K) is the strict ω-category freely generated (as a polygraph/computad) by the atoms ⟨b⟩, b ∈ B.
- Steiner and Ara–Maltsiniotis: Steiner complexes are closed under the Gray tensor product, (K ⊗ L)ₙ = ⊕_{p+q=n} K_p ⊗ L_q with ∂(x ⊗ y) = ∂x ⊗ y + (−1)^p x ⊗ ∂y and basis {b ⊗ c}. Moreover ν(K ⊗ L) ≅ ν(K) ⊗ ν(L), the Gray tensor of strict ω-categories.

**Support of a cell.** For a cell x of ν(K), let Supp(x) ⊆ B be the smallest set that
- contains supp(xₖ^±) for all k and both signs, and
- is downward closed: if b ∈ Supp(x), then supp(⟨b⟩ₖ^±) ⊆ Supp(x) for all k < dim b.

## The problems

Let K be a Steiner complex. The main case of interest is K = λ(Θ₁) ⊗ … ⊗ λ(Θₘ), a Gray tensor product of the Steiner complexes of finite globular pasting diagrams (Batanin trees). Here λ(Θ) is the complex whose basis is the set of cells of the pasting diagram, with ∂ given by target minus source.

**Problem 1 (0/1 chains).** For every cell x of ν(K) and every k and sign, xₖ^± has all coefficients in {0, 1}.

**Problem 2 (support determines the cell).** If x and y are cells of ν(K) of the same dimension with Supp(x) = Supp(y), then x = y.

**Problem 3 (support is functorial).** Let K, L be Steiner complexes and F : ν(K) → ν(L) a strict ω-functor. For every cell x of ν(K),

```
Supp(F x)  =  ⋃_{b ∈ Supp(x)}  Supp(F⟨b⟩).
```

**Problem 4 (sources of the total cell).** Suppose K = λ(Θ₁) ⊗ … ⊗ λ(Θₘ) has dimension d. Let T := ⟨Θ₁⟩ ⊗ … ⊗ ⟨Θₘ⟩ be the "total" d-cell, where ⟨Θ⟩ is the composite of the pasting diagram Θ in ν(λΘ).
- Show that Supp(T) = B.
- Show that for any (d−1)-cell x with Supp(x) = Supp(∂⁻T), we have x = ∂⁻T. This would follow from Problem 2, and similarly for ∂⁺.

Counterexamples are as useful as proofs. If Problem 2 fails in general, the most useful outcome is the largest natural class of K for which it holds, and whether Gray tensors of globular pasting diagrams belong to it.

## What is known and where the obvious argument stalls

**Problem 1 (sketch).** Steiner's theory shows that every cell is a composite of atoms. A coefficient 2 would require a basis element to occur in two composands. Strong loop-freeness should forbid this. The argument: if b occurs in both x and y and x ∗ₖ y is defined, then b < … < b, using the ordering of composands along the k-boundary. This is believed to be standard, but we want a reference or a written proof.

**Problem 2 (sketch).** The cell can be rebuilt from its dimension and its support, top-down:
- The top chain xₙ is the sum of the n-dimensional elements of Supp(x), using Problem 1.
- Given xₖ₊₁^±, write ∂xₖ₊₁⁻ = P − N. Then xₖ⁻ = N + R and xₖ⁺ = P + R. Here R ("the part of the k-boundary untouched by higher cells") should equal the set of k-dimensional elements of Supp(x) that are *not* faces of any element of Supp(x) of dimension > k.

This needs a sublemma: no element of R is a face of a higher-dimensional element of Supp(x). Equivalently, an element of the k-boundary of x that is consumed (or produced) by some higher generator of x cannot reappear in both boundaries. We expect this to follow from loop-freeness, via a layering of x into generators: such a reappearance forces either coefficient 2 at some intermediate stage or a cycle in the ordering. This sublemma is where we stalled.

**Example.**
- In K = λ(D¹) ⊗ λ(D¹) (the lax square), take vertices a, b, c, d, edges p : a→b, q : b→d, r : a→c, s : c→d, and the 2-generator σ with ∂σ = (r + s) − (p + q).
- The two 1-cells from a to d have supports {a, b, d, p, q} and {a, c, d, r, s}, and the unique 2-cell with support B is σ.
- A 2-cell from r + s to p + q would need top chain −σ. This is impossible because H₂(K) = 0 and chains are non-negative.

**Why the problems are asked.** They give a decidable criterion for when two composites in a free Gray-enriched strict ω-category coincide, by comparing finite sets of generators. That criterion is the basis of a type-theoretic rule for such composites.

## What would count as a solution

Any of the following:
- proofs of Problems 1–4, or references to them in the literature;
- a counterexample to Problem 2, together with the largest natural class of Steiner complexes for which it holds;
- a proof of Problem 2 just for Gray tensors of globular pasting diagrams.

## Literature status (searched 2026-11-18)

**Status: solved for the main case K = λΘ₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘₘ, and more generally for any K coming from an acyclic regular directed complex. The fully general Steiner-complex case is still open. Problems 1–4 follow from Hadzihasanovic, *Combinatorics of higher-categorical diagrams* ([arXiv 2404.07273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07273)).**

The argument, assembled from that book:

1. **Thetas and their Gray tensors are acyclic regular directed complexes.**
   - Globes and thetas are acyclic molecules (Lemma 9.1.16).
   - Acyclic oriented graded posets are closed under Gray products (Prop. 8.3.28) and joins (Prop. 8.3.32).
   - So P := Θ₁ ⊗ … ⊗ Θₘ is an acyclic molecule.
2. **Cells of ν(K) are molecules over P.**
   - For P dimension-wise acyclic, ν(ℤP) ≅ Mol/P (Thm 11.2.18), and ℤP is the Steiner complex λΘ₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘₘ (Prop. 11.2.35 and 11.2.36).
   - Here Mol/P is the strict ω-category whose cells are isomorphism classes [f : U → P] of morphisms from molecules (Def. 5.2.8), with pasting as composition.
3. **For acyclic P, every morphism from a molecule is an inclusion** (Prop. 8.3.17).
   - So each cell is a closed subset U ⊆ P that is a molecule, with its orientation induced from P, together with a dimension k ≥ dim U (k > dim U means a degenerate cell).
   - Its chain table is xⱼ^± = Σ (∂ⱼ^± U)ⱼ, i.e. the j-dimensional elements of the j-boundaries, all with coefficient 1.
4. **Problem 1.** Every coefficient is 0 or 1, since chains are sums over subsets.
5. **Problem 2.** U is the downward closure of the supports of the chains: every element of U lies below a maximal element, and the maximal elements occur in the chains. So Supp(x) = U, and the pair (dim, Supp) determines the cell.
6. **Problem 4.**
   - The total cell T is [P ⊆ P], so Supp(T) = P = B.
   - The cells ∂^±T are the boundary submolecules ∂^±P. Any (d−1)-cell with the same support is the same subset, hence equal by Problem 2.
7. **Problem 3** (when L is also acyclic). Composition in Mol/P is union of subsets, and boundaries are closed subsets. Write x as a pasting of atoms ⟨b⟩, b ∈ Supp(x). Then:
   - Supp(Fx) is the union of the Supp(F⟨b⟩) over the top-dimensional atoms used;
   - each lower b in Supp(x) lies in the boundary of a higher atom b′, so Supp(F⟨b⟩) ⊆ Supp(∂F⟨b′⟩) ⊆ Supp(F⟨b′⟩).

   This is a short derivation, not a quoted theorem.

**General Steiner complexes.** Hadzihasanovic, Example 11.2.19, shows that for regular directed complexes that are *not* dimension-wise acyclic, ν(ℤP) is a "more degenerate quotient" of Mol/P. Comment 11.2.37 records that compatibility with Gray products is not expected beyond the acyclic case. Problem 2 for arbitrary strongly loop-free Steiner complexes was not found in the literature. The case the project needs is covered.

Related: Campbell, *Parity complexes redux* ([arXiv 2605.09427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09427)), shows that parity complexes are equivalent to strong Steiner complexes. In Street's parity complexes, cells are given as pairs of subsets.
