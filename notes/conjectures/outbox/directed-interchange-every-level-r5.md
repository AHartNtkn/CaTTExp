# Free higher categories with directed interchange in every dimension (fifth round)

*A self-contained problem. No background beyond standard higher category theory is assumed. Rounds 1–4, with their answers, are in `answered/`. Questions are in §4, smallest first.*

## 1. Setting

**Globular sets and composition.** Work with reflexive globular sets with source and target maps s, t, identities, and for k < n a partial composition ∗ₖ of n-cells along k-cells. Composition is written in **diagrammatic order** (u ∗ₖ v means "first u, then v"), and it is strictly associative and unital. The boundary formulas hold: for k < m and (m+1)-cells u, v,

```
s(u ∗ₖ v) = s(u) ∗ₖ s(v),     t(u ∗ₖ v) = t(u) ∗ₖ t(v).        (B)
```

For k < l < n and n-cells in interchange position put `L(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₗ b) ∗ₖ (c ∗ₗ d)` and `R(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₖ c) ∗ₗ (b ∗ₖ d)`. A strict ω-category requires L = R.

**The Gray case (directed interchange along 0-cells only).** In a category enriched in strict ω-categories with the lax Gray tensor product (Ara–Maltsiniotis, arXiv 1607.00668), for 2-cells α : f ⇒ f′ : x → y and β : g ⇒ g′ : y → z there is a 3-cell

```
χ_{α,β} : (f ◁ β) ∗₁ (α ▷ g′)  ⇛  (α ▷ g) ∗₁ (f′ ◁ β)
```

and nothing in the reverse direction. Composition along k-cells with k ≥ 1 is strict. For a finite globular pasting diagram Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ, the free such structure has homs ν(λΘᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘⱼ). Here λ is Steiner's directed complex, ⊗ is Steiner's tensor with `∂(x ⊗ y) = ∂x ⊗ y + (−1)^{|x|} x ⊗ ∂y`, and ν is Steiner's functor to strict ω-categories (Steiner, HHA 2004). Each hom is generated by tuples of cells, and a cell is determined by the set of generators it uses.

**The aim** is the same for directed interchange along k-cells for **every** k, including inside homs.

## 2. What is already known (all proved)

**(K1) Raw higher interchangers are impossible.** If every pair of n-cells in interchange position had an (n+1)-cell L → R, then applying this to identities and using (B) forces L = R one dimension down. That contradicts directedness.

**(K2) A single comparison for each simultaneous variation is impossible.** The obvious repair of (K1) adds, for 3-cells A : a ⇛ a′, …, D : d ⇛ d′ in interchange position, a 4-cell between the parallel 3-cells

```
L(A,B,C,D) ∗₂ χ_{a′}     and     χ_a ∗₂ R(A,B,C,D).        (N)
```

This cannot be required for every instance. Take Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², with two 3-cells A : α ⇛ α′ and B : β ⇛ β′ side by side along a 0-cell, and the instance (A, 1, 1, B). Its two sides map to the hom 2-cells

```
U = (v₀ ∘₀ u₁) ∘₁ c₁₁,     V = c₀₀ ∘₁ (u₀ ∘₀ v₁)
```

of ν(λD² ⊗ λD²), where `u_j = A ⊗ g_j`, `v_i = f_i ⊗ B` and `c_ij = α_i ⊗ β_j`. There is no 4-cell between U and V in either direction: the degree-3 chain would need a positive coefficient at u₀ (or at v₀), but every basis element of degree 3 has a non-positive coefficient there.

**(K3) The two variables have opposite naturality directions.** The degree-3 basis elements are `r_j = A ⊗ β_j` and `t_i = α_i ⊗ B`. Their differentials are

```
∂r_j = c_1j + u₁ − c_0j − u₀,     ∂t_i = c_i0 + v₁ − c_i1 − v₀.
```

So when only A varies, the Gray structure has a naturality 4-cell from the "χ first" side to the "variation first" side (V ⇛ U). When only B varies, it goes the other way (U ⇛ V). The simultaneous variation is the 5-cell A ⊗ B. It goes between two routes of 4-cells, `S → V → T` ⇛ `S → U → T`, where S and T are the composites through c₀₁ and c₁₀. In short, the higher structure is **tensor-shaped**: one Leibniz-signed naturality cell per variable, and higher cells between routes.

**(K4) Supports must use an expanded basis.** If every composite's support were the union of the supports of its parts, L and R would always have equal support. Then "support determines the cell" would force strict interchange.

**(K5) One finite basis forces finitely many cells.** If one finite set B admits injective support maps in every dimension, the structure has finitely many non-identity cells in total.

**(K6) No single directed complex presents the answer, and neither does a hom-wise family of them.** In ν(K), the two composites defining χ have the same chain table, so they are equal. A description must therefore be an iterated tower: objects, then homs presented again by objects and homs, and so on.

**(K7) The first uniform case through hom-dimension 3.** For Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², directing interchange inside the hom 𝒦(x, z) adds exactly two generators of hom-dimension 3 beyond the 25 tensor generators. They are the interchangers χ₁ of (A⊗g, f′⊗B) and χ₂ of (f⊗B, A⊗g′), the only pairs of hom-2-cells composable along a hom-0-cell. Their two sides have different supports. Neither occurs in the boundary of A ⊗ B.

**(K8) The (K7) 3-skeleton cannot be extended by 4-generators alone (round 2, proved).** Write h_i^j = α_i ⊗ g_j, k_j^i = f_i ⊗ β_j, P_ij = k_j^0 h_i^1, Q_ij = h_i^0 k_j^1, so c_ij : P_ij ⇒ Q_ij. The tensor 3-generators are r_j : c_0j ; a_j^+ ⇛ a_j^- ; c_1j and t_i : b_i^- ; c_i1 ⇛ c_i0 ; b_i^+ (a_j^±, b_i^± the whiskered edge cells u_j, v_i), and the interchangers are χ₁ : b_0^+ ; a_1^+ ⇛ a_0^+ ; b_1^+ and χ₂ : a_0^- ; b_1^- ⇛ b_0^- ; a_1^-. The six composite hom-2-cells P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ are S = b_0^- ; c_01 ; a_1^+, V₀ = c_00 ; b_0^+ ; a_1^+, V₁ = c_00 ; a_0^+ ; b_1^+, T = a_0^- ; c_10 ; b_1^+, U₀ = a_0^- ; b_1^- ; c_11 and U₁ = b_0^- ; a_1^- ; c_11. Whiskering gives t̄₀ : S ⇛ V₀, χ̄₁ : V₀ ⇛ V₁, r̄₀ : V₁ ⇛ T, r̄₁ : S ⇛ U₁, χ̄₂ = χ₂ ∗₁ 1_{c₁₁} : U₀ ⇛ U₁ and t̄₁ : U₀ ⇛ T. Since ∂(A⊗B) = t₁ − t₀ + r₁ − r₀, requirement 4 forces a 4-cell between the route t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀ from S to T and a route from S to T using r̄₁ and t̄₁. In the free 3-skeleton with exactly the 25 tensor generators plus χ₁, χ₂ and no 2-equations, the first route exists, but the second would have to go S ⇛ U₁ and then U₁ ⇛ U₀ against χ̄₂: in the directed graph of these composites the only S → T path is t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀. So no list of 4-generators recovers A ⊗ B. The naturality cells of χ₁, χ₂ are trivial here (the edge homs have no 3-generators).

The repair must change dimension-3 data: an extra 3-generator such as h : S ⇛ U₀ whose image under the Gray quotient is r̄₁, or a 2-equation such as U₀ = U₁. Inverting χ₂ would also work but breaks requirement 3.

**(K9) The orientation of χ₁, χ₂ in (K7) is forced by substitution (proved).** One might try to avoid (K8) by orienting every inner interchanger by the factor order of the enclosing tensor (always "Θ₂-cell first ⇛ Θ₁-cell first"), which keeps χ₁ and reverses χ₂. This fails requirement 5. The free object on Σ(D² ∨ D²) has, inside its hom, the interchanger of its two 2-cells A′ (left) and B′ (right), oriented as in the Gray case: "B′ first ⇛ A′ first". The assignment A′ ↦ f₀ ⊗ B and B′ ↦ A ⊗ g₁ (with the hom-0-cells and hom-1-cells sent to f₀⊗g₀, f₀⊗g₁, f₁⊗g₁ and to the matching edges) respects all boundaries, so substitution sends that interchanger to a 3-cell from "A ⊗ g₁ first" to "f₀ ⊗ B first", which is exactly χ₂ in its (K7) orientation. The pair χ₁ is forced the same way via A′ ↦ A ⊗ g₀, B′ ↦ f₁ ⊗ B. So χ₂ cannot be reversed: adding the reversed cell as well breaks requirement 3, which is the "inverting χ₂" option above. Inner interchangers are oriented by position (right cell first ⇛ left cell first), as at the outer level, and some repair of dimension-3 data is needed (see (K10)).

**(K10) The smallest case is repaired by a factorization (round 3, proved).** The six hom-2 composites P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ of (K8) are the maximal paths of an oriented 3-cube with coordinates A (edges a_j^±), B (edges b_i^±) and C (edges c_ij): S = BCA, V₀ = CBA, V₁ = CAB, T = ACB, U₀ = ABC, U₁ = BAC. The six whiskered hom-3 cells t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀, r̄₁, χ̄₂, t̄₁ are its six square faces (adjacent transpositions); every proper interval carries at most one of them. Add one hom-3 generator and one hom-3 equation:

```
h : S ⇛ U₀,        r̄₁ = h ∗₂ χ̄₂.        (F)
```

Then the maximal hom-3 interval is the free category on S → V₀ → V₁ → T, S → U₀ → U₁, U₀ → T, with 11 non-identity cells with pairwise distinct label sets, two routes S → T (t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ and h ; t̄₁), and no reverse of χ̄₁ or χ̄₂. Killing the inner interchangers gives exactly the free Gray diamond with q(h) = q(r̄₁), and A ⊗ B lifts to one 4-generator W : t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ ⇛ h ; t̄₁ with no non-identity contexts. Supports use one label per whiskered context, so r̄₁ is no longer a basis element (its support is that of h χ̄₂). The equation (F) cannot be dropped or replaced by a 4-cell h χ̄₂ ⇛ r̄₁ without a new class of cells killed by the quotient. h is not an interchanger: S and U₀ each contain a c_ij, and every hom-2 path containing a c_ij spans the whole external hom-0 interval.

h is **not forced**. The mirror repair k : U₁ ⇛ T with t̄₁ = χ̄₂ ∗₂ k works equally well, and so does the symmetric repair with both h and k and both equations (12 cells, second route h ; χ̄₂ ; k, same exact Gray quotient). The alternative 2-equation U₀ = U₁ makes χ̄₂ an endomorphism and needs further equations (at least χ̄₂² = χ̄₂ under set supports), so it is not preferred.

## 3. Requirements

A **tensor-shaped lax-interchange ω-category** has, for every k < l, elementary interchangers χ : L ⇛ R for (l+1)-cells with no reverse cell in free objects; one naturality cell per varying argument, oriented by the Leibniz sign; higher cells between routes of lower naturality cells; and coherence equations to be determined. The free objects F(Γ) must satisfy:
1. an explicit combinatorial description by generators with explicit boundaries;
2. two cells of the same dimension using the same generators, in the expanded basis, are equal;
3. every elementary interchanger exists and its reverse does not;
4. making all interchangers with k ≥ 1 identities gives the free Gray-enriched category, and making all interchangers identities gives the free strict ω-category;
5. closure under substitution of cells for generators.

**Q1 (three summands).** For Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD² ∨ ΣD², hom ν(λD² ⊗ λD² ⊗ λD²): the hom-2 paths are words in more coordinates, interchangers of different pairs meet, and square 2-faces (disjoint transpositions) appear. - (ii) which variation cells and equations accompany existing factorizations.

Does the source/sink pattern of (K11) hold in general: does every position-flipping face create exactly one extra source/sink pair one dimension up? Does the side alternate with dimension?

**Q3 (substitution).** Extend the forcing substitution of (K11) to a proof that the rule of Q2 is closed under substitution of cells for generators (requirement 5), including degenerate and composite-valued substitutions. Alternatively, find a substitution that forces two incompatible choices.

## 1. Q2: the ambient source/sink count is false

### The exact counting rule

Let \(e:u\to v\) be an arrow of a finite directed graph. Reverse \(e\), keeping all other arrows fixed. Write \(d^-,d^+\) for the degrees before reversal. Then

\[
\Delta\#\operatorname{Src}
=
\mathbf 1_{d^-(v)=1}-\mathbf 1_{d^-(u)=0},
\]

\[
\Delta\#\operatorname{Snk}
=
\mathbf 1_{d^+(u)=1}-\mathbf 1_{d^+(v)=0}.
\tag{1}
\]

Only the two endpoints change degree, which proves the formulas.

Thus a reversal creates exactly one additional source and one additional sink precisely when

\[
d^-(v)=1,\qquad d^+(u)=1,\qquad
d^-(u)>0,\qquad d^+(v)>0.
\tag{2}
\]

An internal arrow on a directed branch of an isolated polygon satisfies these conditions. An arrow in the full comparison graph need not.

### A 24-path interval of the triple tensor

Consider the hom-1 boundary

\[
(\beta _0\alpha _0)\gamma _0
\longrightarrow
(\alpha _1\beta _1)\gamma _1.
\]

There are four hom-2 operations:

- \(A,B\): the updates in the first two factors;
- \(C\): the crossing \(\beta\alpha\to\alpha\beta\);
- \(D\): the third-factor update \(\gamma_0\to\gamma_1\).

The third-factor arrow remains last. The sequential hom-2 paths are the \(24\) permutations of \(A,B,C,D\).

The hom-3 adjacent exchanges are

\[
BC\to CB,\qquad CA\to AC,\qquad
DX\to XD\quad(X=A,B,C),
\]

together with the position-dependent rule

\[
AB\to BA
\quad\text{if \(C\) follows the adjacent pair},
\]

\[
BA\to AB
\quad\text{if \(C\) precedes the pair}.
\tag{3}
\]

Compare this with the graph using \(BA\to AB\) at every position. Exactly three contextual arrows reverse:

| Before reversal | After reversal | Change in sources | Change in sinks |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| \(BACD\to ABCD\) | \(ABCD\to BACD\) | \(0\) | \(+1\) |
| \(BADC\to ABDC\) | \(ABDC\to BADC\) | \(0\) | \(0\) |
| \(DBAC\to DABC\) | \(DABC\to DBAC\) | \(+1\) | \(0\) |

The middle reversal creates **neither** a source nor a sink. Its endpoint degrees change from

\[
(1,2),\ (2,1)
\quad\text{to}\quad
(2,1),\ (1,2).
\]

For the complete graphs,

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
&\operatorname{Src}&\operatorname{Snk}\\ \hline
\text{constant \(BA\to AB\)}
&\{DBCA\}&\{ACBD\}\\
\text{position-dependent rule}
&\{DBCA,DABC\}&\{ACBD,BACD\}.
\end{array}
\tag{4}
\]

There is also a direct proof of (4). A source must begin with \(D\), since otherwise moving the preceding letter past \(D\) supplies an incoming arrow. A sink must end with \(D\). After deleting \(D\), source and sink conditions reduce to those of the binary hexagon.

Counting geometric faces instead of contextual arrows does not restore the proposed count. The first two reversals are two suffix contexts of the \(AB\)-square at \(C=0,D=0\); together they contribute one ambient sink and no source. The third reversal is the square at \(C=0,D=1\); it contributes one ambient source and no sink.

**This refutes a count of one additional ambient source/sink pair per flipped face.** It preserves the isolated-hexagon calculation. A rule formulated separately for isolated faces must still account for their other incidences when those faces are assembled.

### Alternation is not forced by the local factorization problem

A source-only construction works for any simple oriented polygon, provided equations may factor composite paths.

Let its directed arms be

\[
p_i:s_i\to t_i,\qquad q_i:s_{i+1}\to t_i,
\]

with source indices modulo \(r\). Its signed boundary is

\[
z=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}(p_i-q_i).
\]

For \(r\ge2\), add \(h_i:s_0\to s_i\), \(1\le i<r\), and impose

\[
p_0=h_1;q_0,
\qquad
h_i;p_i=h_{i+1};q_i
\quad(1\le i<r-1).
\tag{5}
\]

In the abelianization of the path presentation, these equations telescope to

\[
z=h_{r-1}+p_{r-1}-q_{r-1}.
\]

The remaining boundary paths

\[
h_{r-1};p_{r-1},
\qquad q_{r-1}
\]

are parallel from \(s_0\) to \(t_{r-1}\). Dimension does not enter this construction.

Thus the local boundary problem does not force source/sink alternation. Whether global coherence and substitution force an alternating choice remains unresolved.

## 2. Q1: explicit data for the triple tensor

### Word conventions

Write the three hom-2 updates as

\[
a=x_2,\qquad b=y_2,\qquad c=z_2.
\]

Start with hom-1 order \(zyx\), and write

\[
p:yx\to xy,\qquad
q:zx\to xz,\qquad
r:zy\to yz.
\]

The two maximal crossing words are

\[
rqp,\qquad pqr,
\]

and the tensor hom-3 generator has direction

\[
\tau:rqp\longrightarrow pqr.
\tag{6}
\]

A word denotes the sequential hom-2 composite of its letters. Each letter is whiskered at the positions determined by the preceding letters. Endpoint superscripts are suppressed: preceding updates determine them.

For a prescribed set of \(m\) updates, take words using those updates once and having crossing subsequence \(rqp\) or \(pqr\). There are

\[

=
8,\ 40,\ 240
\quad\text{for }m=1,2,3.
\tag{7}
\]

The original hom-3 arrows are:

## 4. The overlap requiring \(\mu\)

The additional factorization is visible in the degree-\((2,1,2)\) tensor five-cell.

Use the forty words in \(a,c,p,q,r\). First apply the binary repair \(h_q\), together with \(\lambda_x\) and \(h_z\), in all applicable contexts. Do not yet add \(\mu\).

The resulting graph has exactly two sources:

\[
S=crqpa,\qquad U=acrqp.
\tag{15}
\]

These sources are relevant to the actual tensor five-cell

\[
F=x_2\otimes y_1\otimes z_2.
\]

Suppressing tensor symbols, its differential is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial F={}&
x_1^+y_1z_2-x_1^-y_1z_2
+x_2y_0^+z_2-x_2y_0^-z_2\\
&-x_2y_1z_1^++x_2y_1z_1^-.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]

The chosen opposite \(W_z\)-faces have hom-2 sources:

\[
W_z^-\text{ followed by }a:\quad S=crqpa,
\]

\[
a\text{ followed by }W_z^+:\quad U=acrqp.
\]

To use these two embedded lifts in one sequential hom-5 boundary with hom-2 source \(G\), existing hom-3 contexts would have to give paths

\[
G\to S,\qquad G\to U.
\tag{17}
\]

Since \(S,U\) are distinct sources, no such \(G\) exists.

This obstructs assembling **these chosen face lifts** using the current hom-3 arrows. It does not exclude adding alternative hom-4 lifts with different hom-3 boundaries.

### The repair

Set

The significant point is that (19) factors a **previously added joining generator**, not an original tensor face. A construction that repairs the individual tensor hom-4 generators once and subsequently adds only higher-dimensional variation data does not assemble these prescribed faces.

In a directed acyclic graph, a path cannot repeat a vertex. Its edge set consequently determines the path: the set has a unique initial vertex and a unique successive edge at every visited vertex. ### What is established about the Gray quotient

Let \(P\) denote the original sequential presentation and \(P'\) its extension by the six factorization families. After imposing inner interchangers as identities, each new joining arrow becomes its factored old arrow. The \(\mu\)-equations eliminate through the \(\lambda_x\)-equations. Thus

\[
P'/(\text{inner identities})
\cong
P/(\text{inner identities}).
\tag{20}

The added data introduce no additional quotient relations among the original arrows.

Equation (20) is a **relative quotient statement**. Establishing that the completed presentation gives the full Gray tensor requires the missing hom-4 coherence data.

## 5. Q3: a composite-valued substitution explains the new factor

The following calculation assumes the explicit normalization:

> Crossing against a composite hom-1 path is the composite of its pairwise crossings.

That normalization is not a consequence of (B), associativity, and units alone.

Under it, substitute into the binary repair

\[
A\longmapsto a,\qquad B\longmapsto c;r.
\tag{21}
\]

Both images have the required hom dimension two. The binary joining arrow must then map to a hom-3 arrow

\[
crqpa\longrightarrow acrqp.
\tag{22}
\]

Before adding \(\mu\), (15) shows that no such path exists in the chosen presentation.

With \(\mu\), define

\[
\sigma(h)
=
(crqpa\to crqap);(\mu p).
\tag{23}
\]

To check the factorization equation, write

\[
\eta:crqpa\to crqap,\quad
i:acrqp\to carqp,\quad
j:carqp\to craqp,
\]

and

\[
\lambda'=c\lambda_x\,p:crqap\to carqp.
\]

The substituted binary arrows satisfy

\[
\sigma(e)=i;j,
\qquad
\sigma(d)=\eta;\lambda';j.
\]

Equation (18) gives \(\lambda'=(\mu p);i\). Therefore

\[
\sigma(d)
=
\eta;(\mu p);i;j
=
\sigma(h);\sigma(e).
\tag{24}
\]

So this composite substitution requires additional hom-3 reachability relative to the earlier skeleton, and \(\mu\) supplies it. It does not establish closure under every substitution or force this particular choice of factor in every possible completion.

There is a general distinction here. Declaring \(h,H,K,\mu,\ldots\) as typed operations with schematic equations gives substitution closure for the **term quotient** by structural induction. It does not prove that the proposed finite contextual bases describe that quotient, that supports remain injective, or that reverse interchangers remain absent.

## Remaining questions

The calculations establish the ambient source/sink counterexample, the six displayed factorization families, the tensor hom-4 boundaries, and the finite hom-3 support result.

They do not provide the complete hom-4 presentation for the triple tensor, support injectivity in hom dimensions four and five, the lift of \(x_2\otimes y_2\otimes z_2\), or a substitution-closed theory satisfying requirements 1–5. They also give no inconsistency proof for requirements 1–3 and 5.
