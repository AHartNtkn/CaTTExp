# Limits, colimits, representables and Yoneda in CaTT style

*Design notes, first pass, 2026-11-18. Companion to `catt-survey.md` (project files). Scope: representables, Yoneda-style principles, and (weighted, lax, ω-)limits and colimits as CaTT-style rules. Internal hom / Gray tensor and universes are handled in other threads; the interfaces are marked **[→ Gray]** and **[→ universes]**.*

*Markers. **[paper]**: checked against the cited paper this session. **[recall]**: from background knowledge, not re-checked. **[conj]**: my conjecture, unproved. **[proposal]**: a design choice being put forward.*

---

## 0. Summary

1. **Adopt Mikhail's CaTT_lim as the limit core** ([2412.13310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13310)). It already has the right shape: cone and transfor *contexts* generated by judgments, plus one filler schema (`ucone`, `uni`) with free-variable side conditions. Section 2 gives its rules in full, checked against the PDF.
2. **Add markings to diagrams** so some cone cells must be invertible. This gives pseudo and strict (homotopy) limits, pullbacks, equalizers and comma/hom objects. By Abellán–Haugseng–Martini, weighted limits reduce to partially lax ones, so **weighted limits need no separate rule**, only a marking and substitution along a "display" map (§4).
3. **Colimits are the dual rule family**, obtained by reversing the 1-cells as Mikhail remarks. Since the ambient is not self-dual, this is a second, mirrored rule family, not a meta-operation (§4.3).
4. **Representables need no new rule.** With a terminal object ⊤ (the limit of the empty diagram) and lax colimits:
   - *elements* of an object C are 1-cells `⊤ → C`, and their composition is CaTT in the hom `Hom(⊤, C)` via suspension (§5.1);
   - the *free ω-category on a context Γ* is definable as `⟦Γ⟧ := laxcolim_Γ (const ⊤)` (§5.2) **[conj]**;
   - the Yoneda principle "maps `⟦Γ⟧ → C` are Γ-shaped families of elements of C" is then just the colimit's universal property.
5. **Main finding, which needs a decision.** If the ambient ωCat is a model of CaTT, its own higher cells **cannot be lax transformations**. Every CaTT coherence is invertible, including the interchange cell that relates `α ∘ f` in two orders, so whiskering forces *pseudo*-naturality. Lax transformations then come from universal constructions (lax cotensors `[⟦Γ⟧, C]_lax` and Gray tensors), not from the ambient's 2-cells. The alternative, a lax ambient, means changing the core coherence rule to a Gray-style one with non-invertible interchange (§3).
6. **What this reaches.** All finite (lax, pseudo, weighted) limits and colimits; cotensors by shapes; hom objects; an ℕ-like object `Hom_{⟦x, s:x→x⟧}(x, x)` with iteration (§6). **What it does not reach:** colimits indexed by an *object* rather than a context (sequential colimits, general Kan extensions) and induction (dependent elimination). Both need internal hom [→ Gray] or fibrations [→ universes].

## 1. Conventions

- **The ambient.** We are working "CaTT over ωCat": the base type ⋆ is read as "ω-categories", 1-cells as functors, and higher cells as transfors. `Ob` in Mikhail's paper is our ⋆.
- **Diagrams are substitutions.** A diagram of shape Γ in the ambient is a substitution `Δ ⊢ γ : Γ`, as in CaTT_lim. A *generic* diagram is the identity on Γ, so `lim_Γ` is the limit of the generic diagram and `lim_Γ[γ]` is the limit of γ. This is a big ergonomic win of the context-as-diagram design and should be kept.
- **Orientation.** "Lax" follows Mikhail. For `f : x → y` in Γ, the cone cell is `p_f : p_y → p_x ∗ f` (diagrammatic composition). "Oplax" is the opposite in dimension 1, and other variants come from opposites in other dimensions (Benjamin–Markakis, [2402.01611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01611)).

## 2. Literature: what each source gives

### 2.1 Mikhail, CaTT_lim [paper]

Source: T. J. Mikhail, *A type-theoretic definition of lax (∞,∞)-limits*, [arXiv 2412.13310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13310), Definition 4.15. Rules are transcribed from the PDF with notation lightly normalised.

**Linearity (Def. 3.1).** For `Γ ⊢ t : A` and x ∈ FV(t) with dim x = dim t:

```
(VAR) x ∝ x
(OP)  x ∝ op_{Γ,s→t}[γ]   if there is a unique x_i ∈ FV(Γ) with x ∝ γ_i
(COH) x ∝ coh_{Γ,s→t}[γ]  likewise
```

**Cones as contexts.**

```
(EK)  c : Ob  cone(∅; c)

      Γ, c:Ob, Π  cone(Γ; c)
      Γ, x:X, c:Ob, Π ⊢ s →_A t
      τCond(s:A, x:X, c:Ob, Π)      σCond(t:A, x:X, Π)
(KE)  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
      Γ, x:X, c:Ob, Π, p_x : s →_A t   cone((Γ, x:X); c)
```

- `σCond(t:A, x:X, Π)` says that t:A is "categorical", that x ∝ t, and that
  `FV(t:A) = FV(x:X) ∪ ⋃ { FV(p:T_p) | p ∈ FV(Π), y ∈ FV(σ(x):∂X), y ∝ τ(p) }`.
- `τCond` is the same with `FV(c:Ob)` in place of `FV(x:X)`, and without linearity.
- In words: the target of `p_x` is "x composed after the projections of x's source", and its source is "the projection of x's target". Both are fixed by free-variable conditions, never by naming a composite.

**Universal cone.**

```
(Θ, x:X, Θ') cone(Γ; c)     Γ ⊢uni_Γ κ : Θ     Δ ⊢ γ : Γ
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
           Δ ⊢ ucone_{Γ,x}[γ] : X[κ ∘ γ]
```

Here `lim_Γ := ucone_{Γ,c}`, and `⊢uni` is an auxiliary judgment that builds the universal cone variable by variable.

**Conical transfors** (`ctrf`, the Gray-shaped contexts `Γ ⊗ Dⁿ` adapted to cones) and **postcomposition with a cone** (`star`) are further context judgments. They also have free-variable side conditions; `Star` asks that `FV(u:X)` be the free variables of the appropriate iterated source or target of α, plus the relevant projections.

**Universal property.**

```
K cone(Γ; c)    M ctrf(Γ; c)    dim(α) = n_M
Λ, α:T_α, Δ ⊢_{K,Θ} x : X       Λ, α:T_α, Δ ⊢star_{K;α} w : M
Ω ⊢uni_Γ θ : (K, Θ)             Φ ⊢ ω : Ω
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
J1:  Φ ⊢ uni_{Γ,x}[ω] : X[θ∘ω]         if x = α
J2:  Ω ⊨ uni_{Γ,x}[id] : X[θ]          if x ∈ FV(Δ)
```

**Invertibility judgment.** `Γ ⊨ u : s → t` implies `Γ ⊢ u`, and comes with `inv(u)`, `eta(u) : 1_s → u·inv(u)` and `eps(u) : inv(u)·u → 1_t`, all stable under substitution.

**What the universal property says.** The functor `ucone_* : Hom(c, lim_Γ) → Cones(Γ; c)` is essentially surjective at every dimension (§4.4 of the paper). J1 produces the "witness" cell (the mediating map or higher cell), and J2 produces the invertible cells that say it factors correctly.

**Status [paper].**
- Finite computads only.
- Cones for arbitrary Γ depend on Conjecture 3.6; only the globular case is fully proved (Thm. 3.3).
- Gray shapes are proved to exist only for n = 1 (Thm. 4.4).
- There is no semantics.
- Colimits are only mentioned ("reversing the orientation of all 1-dimensional cells").
- Weighted limits, Yoneda and exponentials are not mentioned.

**Aesthetic verdict.** This is in-style. The cost is the size of the auxiliary machinery (`cone`, `ctrf`, `pctrf`, `star`, `pstar`, `⊢uni`, `⊢_{K,Θ}`), each with its own rules. A user never writes these judgments, but a kernel would have to implement all of them.

### 2.2 Loubaton, categorical theory of (∞,ω)-categories [paper, abstract/ToC level]

Source: [arXiv 2406.05425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05425).

- Gray cylinders and Gray cones are defined as pushouts, via Steiner theory (§1.2).
- Lax transformations are maps `A ⊗ [1] → B`.
- **Lax Grothendieck construction** (Thm 4.1.4.2): lax functors to ω correspond to left cartesian fibrations.
- **Yoneda** (Thm 4.2.1.20): `C → Ĉ := Hom(Cᵗ, ω)` is fully faithful.

Relevance: this is the semantic target, and also a warning. Loubaton's Yoneda lemma is stated with **ω, the (∞,ω)-category of (∞,ω)-categories, as a universe**. Without universes (Anthony's position) we can only have *representability* statements (maps out of a shape correspond to data), not "presheaves are a category." §5 is designed around that restriction.

### 2.3 Abellán–Haugseng–Martini [paper, abstract level]

Source: *Free fibrations, lax colimits and Kan extensions for (∞,2)-categories*, [arXiv 2602.07604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07604).

- **Thm F:** the I-lax colimit of `F : A → Cat` is the total space of the classifying fibration with the cartesian 2-cells and the cocartesian 1-cells over I *inverted*. Dually, the I-lax limit is a category of sections.
- **Weighted (co)limits are partially lax (co)limits** over the fibration classified by the weight, and conversely.
- Kan extensions are I_b-lax limits over comma categories.

Relevance: this justifies **"marking" as the only extra ingredient for weighted limits**, at least in dimension 2. For ω this is **[conj]**.

### 2.4 Gepner–Heine, oriented colimits [paper, abstract/intro level]

Source: *Colimits in oriented category theory*, [arXiv 2608.02592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02592), Aug 2026.

- They argue that lax colimits are "incompatible with … the Gray tensor product", and propose *oriented* (co)limits, which agree with lax ones up to dimension 1.
- The oriented colimit of a constant functor at A is `A ⊗ I` (Gray), while the lax colimit is `A × I`.
- The higher Grothendieck construction computes oplax colimits, and E-lax colimits are localised Grothendieck constructions.

Relevance: **CaTT_lim's transfors are Gray-shaped, so its "lax" limits are probably oriented ones in their sense [conj].** This matters for cotensors (§6.3): oriented limits of a constant diagram give Gray-hom functor categories, i.e. *lax* transformations.

### 2.5 Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari [paper, abstract level]

Source: *Fibrations and lax limits of (∞,2)-categories*, [arXiv 2012.04537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04537).

- They build a uniform theory of 2-(co)limits covering lax, weighted and pseudo limits through four variances of fibration.
- They give a cofinality notion, and prove 2-limits are unique when they exist.

Relevance: cofinality is the tool for proving that our derived constructions (§6) agree with the intended ones.

### 2.6 Coherators and representables [recall]

Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis (as made precise for CaTT by BFM, [2106.04475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04475)):
- A weak ω-category is a presheaf on the coherator Θ_CaTT that sends globular sums to limits.
- Read in the other direction: **disks are co-ω-category objects**, and `Hom(D^•, C)` is an ω-category for any C.

This is the semantic content of §5. The CaTT judgment `Γ ⊢ t : A` *is* a co-operation on disks, `⟦Dⁿ⟧ → ⟦Γ⟧`.

### 2.7 Directed Yoneda in simplicial HoTT [recall]

Riehl–Shulman and GWB ([2407.09146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09146), [2602.02218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02218)):
- *Dependent* Yoneda is directed path induction for covariant families.
- It needs type families, so it belongs to the fibrations story [→ universes].
- Its non-dependent shadow, "a natural transformation out of a representable is determined by the image of the identity", is what §5 targets.

## 3. The ambient's own cells must be pseudo (needs a decision)

**Claim [paper-level, BM 2406.12127 plus a direct argument].** In any model of CaTT, every coherence `coh_{Γ, t→u}[σ]` is invertible.

- **Direct argument:** `coh_{Γ, u→t}` satisfies the same side condition, so it exists, and the composites with it are again coherences that are parallel to identities.
- **Consequence for transformations.** Take an ambient 2-cell `α : F ⇒ G : C → D` and an element `f : x ⇒ y` of C (§5.1).
  - The two whiskerings `(α ∘ y)·(F ∘ f)` and `(G ∘ f)·(α ∘ x)` are related by the interchange coherence.
  - So the "naturality square" of α at f is **invertible**.
  - Hence 2-cells of a CaTT ambient behave like **pseudo** (strong) transformations, not lax ones.
- **Matching semantics [recall].** 2-categories with *lax* transformations form a Gray-category with a non-invertible interchange (Gray 1974), not a tricategory with lax 2-cells. Loubaton's ω is likewise enriched over the *cartesian* product, and lax transformations appear only as maps out of `A ⊗ [1]`.

Survey §13 recorded "lax by default." There are two coherent readings:

| Option | Ambient 2-cells | Where lax transformations live | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P (recommended)** | pseudo transfors; CaTT unchanged | Objects of lax functor categories `[⟦Γ⟧, C]_lax` (oriented cotensors, §6.3) and maps out of Gray tensors [→ Gray]. A lax transformation `F ⇒ G` is a 1-cell `C → [⟦D¹⟧, D]_lax` over F, G. | Lax naturality is a *construction*, not a primitive cell. Whiskering always gives pseudo naturality. |
| **L** | lax transfors | Directly as ambient 2-cells | The core coh rule must change. Interchange-type coherences must be *directed*, so the side condition has to tell "invertible" coherences (Var-balanced) from "lax" ones (e.g. the Gray interchange). This is a Gray-CaTT; no published variant exists that I know of. Every CaTT result (models, computads, BM invertibility) would need redoing. |

Option P keeps every existing CaTT result and still makes lax structure first class through limits. Option L is the more radical and arguably more "directed" design. I recommend **P**, and the rest of this note assumes it. Most of §4 and §6 survives under L too.

## 4. Limits and colimits

### 4.1 Base: CaTT_lim as is [proposal]

Take Definition 4.15 of Mikhail wholesale for finite diagrams.

Kernel-level simplifications worth trying, since they are aesthetic, not semantic:
- **Merge the star/pstar/uni bookkeeping into a single "lifting" judgment.** The rule's content is: for every ctrf shape M and every partial filler θ of the "M-shaped cell of cones," produce the next variable. This is a generic *horn-filling* statement: the filler schema is "each conical-transfor horn has a filler, which is invertible except at its top cell." Stating it as a horn judgment `M horn(Γ; c) ⊢ x` would bring it close to how `coh` reads.
- **Linearity (`x ∝ t`)** is the sole new kind of side condition. It is the same concern as the post's "no cancellation," so it fits. Keep it.

### 4.2 Marked diagrams: pseudo, strict and partial laxness [proposal]

**Idea.** A diagram context may *mark* some of its positive-dimensional variables. The cone rule makes the projection cell of a marked variable invertible, by placing it in Mikhail's `⊨` judgment, or in an ICaTT invertibility type ([2602.16602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16602)).

```
Γ⁺ ::= Γ with a subset μ ⊆ Var_{≥1}(Γ) of marked variables

(KE-marked)   as (KE), with conclusion
              Γ, x:X, c:Ob, Π, p_x : s →_A t   cone((Γ,x:X)^{μ}; c)
              and, if x ∈ μ, the extra judgment
              Γ, x:X, c:Ob, Π, p_x ⊨ p_x : s →_A t
```

Side conditions:
- **(M1) Closure.** If x ∈ μ then every variable whose type mentions x at top dimension and that is "parallel" to x is also marked. This is needed so the higher projection cells over a marked cell are invertible. The exact form is **[conj]**; it should mirror Benjamin–Markakis's "empty top-dimensional support" criterion.
- **(M2) The universal property quantifies only over cones with the same marking.** That is, `ctrf` shapes inherit the marking, and J2 already produces `⊨` cells.

What it gives:

| Marking | Limit | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| none | lax (oriented?) limit | lax limits, comma-like constructions |
| all | pseudo = homotopy limit of the diagram | products (no difference for discrete Γ), pullbacks, equalizers, iso-inserters |
| some | partially lax | comma objects `f ↓ g` (mark one leg), hom objects (§6.2), inserters |

**Weighted limits.**
- In CaTT style a weight is a *display substitution* `π : Ψ → Γ` together with a marking of Ψ: the "Grothendieck construction of the weight."
- `lim^{(Ψ,π)} γ := lim_{Ψ^μ}[γ ∘ π]`.
- AHM show this recovers all weighted limits in dimension 2; for ω this is **[conj]**.
- The side condition on π ("π is a (co)cartesian display") is the open part. A first version can **omit it**: any π gives *some* partially lax limit, and only the comparison theorem with "weighted limits" needs π to be a fibration.

### 4.3 Colimits [proposal]

Mikhail says colimits are obtained by reversing all 1-cells. Concretely, there is a second rule family `cocone`, `uccone`/`colim_Γ`, `couni`:
- It is identical except that **`p_x` and the apex swap roles in dimension 1**.
- Example: for `f : x → y`, the cocone cell is `q_f : q_x → f ∗ q_y`, with legs `q_x : x → c`.

Opposites are a meta-operation on *terms in one ambient*. They are not a symmetry of the ambient ωCat, since `ωCat^op` is not ωCat. So colimits really are **new rules**, though generated by the same schema. Recommendation: present a single schema parameterised by an orientation `ε ∈ {lim, colim}` × a laxness marking, so the kernel has one implementation.

### 4.4 Open orientation question

Which of the 2ⁿ op-variants are needed?
- Mikhail notes the other lax/oplax variants come from opposites. With marking, the useful set is probably only {lax, oplax} × {lim, colim}. The higher-dimensional orientation can be fixed once by convention.
- This is because (§6.3) cotensors already give access to the remaining variants via `[⟦Γ⟧^{op_S}, C]`. **[conj]**

## 5. Representables and Yoneda

### 5.1 Elements are maps from the terminal object [proposal; no new rule]

Let `⊤ := lim_∅` (Mikhail Ex. 4.10). Define, purely as notation:

```
x : C          means   x : ⊤ →_⋆ C
f : x →_C y    means   f : x →_{⊤→C} y          (an ambient 2-cell)
...and so on in all dimensions.
```

- **Composition of elements.** The hom `Hom(⊤, C)` is a hom-ω-category of the ambient. Its pasting schemes are *suspensions* `ΣΘ[⊤, C]` of pasting schemes Θ, and its coherences are suspended coherences.
  - So "CaTT inside C" is CaTT in the ambient, restricted to suspended shapes.
  - Benjamin–Markakis's `Σ ⊣ Hom` and "homs of free are free" ([2402.01611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01611)) say this restriction loses nothing.
- **Functor action** is whiskering: `F x := F ∘ x`, `F f := F ∘ f`. Then `F(g·f) ≅ F g · F f`, `F(id) ≅ id`, and all higher functoriality are ambient coherences. This is exactly the post's §"Functors" observation, now with C, D as *variables*.
- **Components** of `α : F ⇒ G` are `α_x := α ∘ x`. Naturality cells are interchange coherences, hence pseudo (§3).

**Semantic check [recall/conj].** In ωCat with cartesian homs, `Hom(1, C) ≃ C`, since `1 × Dⁿ = Dⁿ`. So "elements of C" are elements of C.

**What does not follow without more rules.**
- **Extensionality / density:** "if `F x ≅ G x` naturally in elements x, then `F ≅ G`." That needs `⊤` and the disks to be jointly dense, which is the internal-hom/Gray thread's problem [→ Gray].
- I suggest an explicit rule only if needed. It would read: *a family of components indexed by the generic element, plus naturality, is a transfor.* That is the cylinder rule `Hom(⟦Γ⟧,D)(u,v) ≃ Hom(⟦(Γ ⊗ D¹)^μ⟧, D)` restricted to representable domains. Here `(Γ ⊗ D¹)^μ` is Mikhail's `gray` context with its cylinder cells *marked*: under Option P the ambient's 2-cells are pseudo, so the marked Gray cylinder (≈ Γ × D¹) is the right shape, and the unmarked one classifies lax transformations instead. It is well-formed because the `gray` judgment generates `Γ ⊗ D¹` as a finite context.

### 5.2 Shapes as objects: three options

The goal is an object `⟦Γ⟧` for every finite context Γ, behaving like the free ω-category on Γ.

**Option R-A: primitive shape objects.**

```
Γ ⊢                         Δ ⊢ σ : Γ
─────────────              ──────────────────────────
⊢ ⟦Γ⟧ : ⋆                  ⊢ ⟦σ⟧ : ⟦Γ⟧ → ⟦Δ⟧

+ universal property: Γ-families of elements of C ↦ maps ⟦Γ⟧ → C,
  essentially surjective in all dimensions.
```

- **Pros:** simple to state, and the contexts of CaTT become first class.
- **Cons:** the universal property has to be stated with exactly the machinery of `ucone`/`uni` (cones, transfors, invertible witnesses). It duplicates CaTT_lim.
- Functoriality `⟦σ∘τ⟧ ≅ ⟦τ⟧∘⟦σ⟧` is either a definitional equality or more generated cells. A definitional version needs a strict sub-ambient and is semantically dubious, because ambient composition is weak.

**Option R-B (recommended): shapes are lax colimits of the point [conj].**

```
⟦Γ⟧ := colim^{lax}_Γ [ !_Γ ∘ ⟨⊤⟩ ]
```

- `(c:⋆) ⊢ !_Γ : Γ` is the **constant substitution**. It sends each variable `x : A` to the unique-up-to-coherence inhabitant of `A[!]` over the one-point context.
  - Such an inhabitant exists because every type over `(c:⋆)` is inhabited by a coherence: `Var = {c}` on both sides, so the `coh` side condition holds.
  - So `!_Γ` sends objects to c, 1-cells to `id_c`, and 2-cells to unitors or identities, as needed. Γ need not be a pasting scheme.
- **Universal property, unfolded.** A lax cocone over const ⊤ with apex C has:
  - legs `q_x : ⊤ → C`, i.e. elements of C;
  - for `f : x → y`, a cell `q_f : q_x → id_⊤ ∗ q_y ≅ q_y`, i.e. an element-level arrow;
  - and so on in higher dimensions.

  **So a cocone is a Γ-shaped family of elements of C, up to unit coherences.** The colimit's universal property is then precisely

  > **(Yoneda / corepresentability)**  `Hom(⟦Γ⟧, C) ≃ Γ-families of elements of C`.

  Under strict units (CaTT_su), the unit whiskerings vanish and the cocone context over const ⊤ is literally Γ, renamed, plus the apex.
- **Semantics.** The lax colimit of the constant point diagram over I is I itself: either the Grothendieck construction of the terminal functor, or Gepner–Heine's `1 × I = 1 ⊗ I = I`. So `⟦Γ⟧ ≃ Free(Γ)`, possibly up to an opposite in some dimensions depending on orientation **[conj]**. The convention in §1 should be chosen so that no op appears. Checking this in the strict model (Steiner complexes) is a good formalisation target (§8).
- **Derived structure:**
  - `⟦∅⟧` is initial;
  - `⟦x:⋆⟧ ≃ ⊤`;
  - `⟦Dⁿ⟧` are the walking n-cells;
  - a term `Γ ⊢ t : A` of dimension n gives a 1-cell `⟦t⟧ : ⟦Dⁿ⟧ → ⟦Γ⟧`, by interpreting t as an element of `⟦Γ⟧` through §5.1;
  - a substitution `Δ ⊢ σ : Γ` gives `⟦σ⟧ : ⟦Γ⟧ → ⟦Δ⟧` from the universal property, with `⟦σ∘τ⟧ ≅ ⟦τ⟧∘⟦σ⟧` generated by `couni`.

  So **original CaTT becomes the internal language of the full sub-ω-category on the shapes `⟦Γ⟧`**, which is the Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis picture (§2.6) made internal.
- **Pros:** no new rule, and contexts become objects for free. "Basic objects are free ω-categories," the direction Anthony liked, comes out *as a theorem*.
- **Cons:** it depends on lax colimits being in the kernel. Every use of `⟦Γ⟧` goes through the heavy `couni` machinery unless the elaborator special-cases it.

**Option R-C: a two-sorted "elements" judgment as surface syntax.**
- Users write `C : ⋆ | x y : C, f : x →_C y ⊢ …`, a CaTT context *fibred over* an ambient object.
- The elaborator translates this to §5.1 notation and §5.2 (R-B) universal maps.
- It has no kernel cost, and it is what users should see. It is the ergonomics layer over R-B, not a competing option.

| | New kernel rules | Free objects | Yoneda | User syntax |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-A | shape former + its own universal property | primitive | primitive | direct |
| **R-B** | none beyond lax colimits | derived **[conj]** | is the colimit property | needs R-C sugar |
| R-C | none (elaboration) | via R-B | via R-B | best |

**Recommendation:** R-B in the kernel and R-C at the surface.

## 6. Derived constructions and test cases

All of these are finite (marked) limits and colimits of finite diagrams.

### 6.1 Basic (co)limits
- Terminal `⊤ = lim_∅`; initial `⊥ = colim_∅`.
- Products: lim over a discrete Γ; coproducts dually.
- Pullbacks and pushouts: the cospan or span with all cells marked.
- Equalizers and coequalizers, and walking-iso quotients (needs ICaTT's walking equivalence as a context).

### 6.2 Hom objects
For `a, b : C` (elements), define `Hom_C(a, b)` as the partially lax limit of the diagram

```
x:⋆, y:⋆, z:⋆, f : x → z, g : y → z     with g marked,
substituted by  x ↦ ⊤, y ↦ ⊤, z ↦ C, f ↦ a, g ↦ b.
```

A cone with apex c is `p_x, p_y : c → ⊤` (trivial), `p_z : c → C`, an invertible `p_g : p_z ≅ b∘!`, and `p_f : p_z → a∘!`. That is a "c-parameterised arrow between b and a" (or a to b, depending on orientation), so this is the comma `a ↓ b` = `Hom_C(a, b)`.

- **Check [conj]:** elements of `Hom_C(a, b)` are the arrows `a → b` of C, and its elements' arrows are C's 2-cells. This is the internal counterpart of Benjamin–Markakis's Hom.

### 6.3 Cotensors by shapes, and lax functor categories
- `[⟦Γ⟧, C] := lim_Γ [ !_Γ ∘ ⟨C⟩ ]`, the lax limit of the constant diagram at C.
- If CaTT_lim's limits are oriented (§2.4), this is the **Gray-hom functor category: functors `⟦Γ⟧ → C` and lax transformations** **[conj]**. With all cells marked it is the pseudo functor category.
- **Lax transformations under Option P.** A lax transformation `F ⇒ G : C → D` is a 1-cell `C → [⟦D¹⟧, D]_lax` whose composites with the two evaluations are F and G.
  - "Whose composites are F and G" is an element of a pullback, so a lax transformation is an element of a finite marked limit.
  - Consequence: **lax transformations between functors with an arbitrary domain need only cotensors by `⟦D¹⟧`, not a general internal hom.** The general internal hom `[C, D]` for an *arbitrary* object C is not a limit [→ Gray].
  - When C is itself built as a colimit of shapes, `[colim_i ⟦Γ_i⟧, D] ≃ lim_i [⟦Γ_i⟧, D]` gives it anyway **[conj]**.

### 6.4 An ℕ-like object (test case for "ordinary math")
- Let `L := (x:⋆, s : x → x)`. This is a finite, non-pasting-scheme context.
  - `⟦L⟧` is the free ω-category on a loop, i.e. "Bℕ".
  - Its universal property: maps `⟦L⟧ → C` are pairs (element c, endo-arrow e on c). **This is iteration.**
- `N := Hom_{⟦L⟧}(x, x)` (§6.2). Its elements are the arrows `sⁿ`, so N is the "object of natural numbers."
  - It is **discrete, up to equivalence, if the free weak ω-category on a 1-dimensional computad is equivalent to the free 1-category [conj; believed folklore]**. A good formal target.
- **Operations:**
  - zero = `id_x`;
  - successor = postcomposition with s, i.e. the functoriality of Hom in its second argument;
  - for (C, c, e), the map `N → Hom_C(c, c)` sending n to eⁿ is the action on homs of the map `⟦L⟧ → C`.
- **What is missing:**
  - Primitive recursion and induction need a *dependent* eliminator, which needs families [→ universes / fibrations].
  - "N is discrete" is a property we can state (all 2-cells invertible, and all parallel arrows equal up to a higher cell) but cannot *prove* inside the theory without an h-level discipline.
- This fits the limited-normalisation constraint: nothing computes, and recursion is a universal property only.
- **Also:** the poset `(ℕ, ≤)` is the slice `x ↓ ⟦L⟧`, a finite comma object (§6.2). So "sequence-shaped" indexing objects exist; what is missing is colimits indexed by them (§7).

### 6.5 Other "acts like" structures
Monoid-like, category-like and module-like objects can be presented by *finite contexts with marked cells* (e.g. a free monoid object `⟦x, m: x→x, …⟧`).

But a structure with *equations*, such as a vector space, needs either:
- (a) colimits that impose equations as invertible cells (coequaliser- or localisation-style), which fits here; or
- (b) a notion of "algebra for a presented theory." The latter skirts Anthony's "no algebraic structure in the meta-theory" rule unless the theory is itself just a context.

Option (a) is the in-style route: a presentation is a context plus a marking, and the presented object is its marked colimit. **This should be explored next, starting with the integers, where cancellation shows up.**

## 7. Limits of this design (what needs other threads)

| Missing | Why it is not a finite (co)limit | Candidate |
|---|---|---|
| Colimits indexed by an *object* I (sequential colimits, Kan extensions along non-shapes) | The diagram is a map `I → C`, not a context. Its universal property mentions `[I, C]`. | Left adjoint to `C → [I, C]` [→ Gray]. | Infinite diagrams | Contexts are finite computads | Object-indexed (co)limits, first row |
