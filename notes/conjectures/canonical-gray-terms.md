# Conjecture CT: canonical terms for cells of Gray tensors of pasting diagrams

*Self-contained problem statement. No knowledge of the surrounding project is assumed. Status (2026-11-18): **strict CT is false** for D¹ ⊗ D¹ ⊗ D¹ when T is built from layerings (§8); CT holds for D² ⊗ D¹; **CT′ holds for all generators of dimension ≤ 4** and is open beyond, where it reduces to relative coherence RC(n) for loop-free contexts.*

## 1. Setting

**CaTT terms.** Fix the dependent type theory CaTT of Finster–Mimram (arXiv 1706.02866): types are `★` or `u →_A v`; terms are variables or `coh_{P, A}[σ]`, where P is a globular pasting diagram (as a context), A a type over P satisfying the usual full-support side condition, and σ a substitution into the ambient context. There are no definitional equalities: two terms are equal only if they are syntactically identical. A context X is **well formed** when each variable's type is well formed, i.e. for `x : u →_A v` both u and v are terms of type A **syntactically**.

**Gray tensors.** For globular pasting diagrams Θ, Δ, let λΘ, λΔ be their Steiner complexes and ν(λΘ ⊗ λΔ) the strict ω-category generated by the tensor of directed complexes (Steiner, HHA 2004). Its generators are pairs y ⊗ x of cells of Θ and Δ, of dimension dim y + dim x, with boundary given by the Leibniz formula. Every cell of ν(λΘ ⊗ λΔ) is a *molecule* (Hadzihasanovic, arXiv 2404.07273), and is determined by its set of generators (Steiner; Hadzihasanovic Prop. 8.3.17).

**The family context.** Fam(Θ ⊗ Δ) is meant to be the CaTT context with one variable v_g for each generator g of ν(λΘ ⊗ λΔ), where the type of v_g is `T(∂⁻g) → T(∂⁺g)` and T(c) is a CaTT term over the earlier variables denoting the cell c (a composite of generators of lower dimension). Maps out of Fam(Θ ⊗ Δ) are then "Θ ⊗ Δ-shaped diagrams", e.g. lax transformations between Θ-shaped diagrams when Δ = D¹.

## 2. Statement

**Conjecture CT.** For all finite globular pasting diagrams Θ, Δ there is an assignment T from cells c of ν(λΘ ⊗ λΔ) of dimension ≥ 1 to CaTT terms over Fam(Θ ⊗ Δ) such that:

- (a) T(g) = v_g for every generator g;
- (b) T(c) is built from the variables by coh terms over globular pasting diagrams, and its strict interpretation (sending v_g to g) is c;
- (c) for every cell c, `T(c) : T(∂⁻c) → T(∂⁺c)` holds **syntactically**, where ∂^± are the source and target in ν. In particular Fam(Θ ⊗ Δ) is well formed.

**Weak form CT′.** Replace (c) by: (c′) T(c) has some type `u → v` where u and v differ from T(∂⁻c), T(∂⁺c) by invertible coherence cells (coh terms of the "full" kind over pasting diagrams); and the context can be made well formed by inserting these coherences into the types of the variables.

**More generally,** the same for iterated tensors λΘ₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘₙ.

## 3. Why it is not immediate

- Cells of a Gray tensor are molecules, not globular pasting diagrams. A hexagon in D¹ ⊗ D¹ ⊗ D¹ is a composite of three squares, each whiskered by edges. Any CaTT term for it is a composite of whiskered generators along a chosen *layering*, and different layerings give syntactically different terms.
- (c) asks that, for the chosen T, the type CaTT computes for T(c) is literally the pair of chosen terms for its boundary. The type of a coh term `coh_{P, u→v}[σ]` is `u[σ] → v[σ]`, so this needs u[σ] ≡ T(∂⁻c). Since u is a term over P and T(∂⁻c) is chosen independently, matching requires the choices to be made compatibly in all dimensions at once.
- Low dimensions are easy: for Θ, Δ of dimension ≤ 1 one can choose binary composites consistently by hand. The first non-trivial case is where a generator's boundary contains a hexagon, e.g. D¹ ⊗ D¹ ⊗ D¹ or D² ⊗ D¹.

## 4. Known related results

- Steiner (HHA 2004): loop-free unital directed complexes, ν, and the fact that cells are determined by their chain tables; tensors of loop-free complexes.
- Hadzihasanovic, *Combinatorics of higher-categorical diagrams* (arXiv 2404.07273): molecules, **layerings** (every molecule decomposes as a composite of whiskered atoms), and the support results. Layerings give terms satisfying (a)–(b); the open point is (c).
- Campion (arXiv 2311.00205), Chanavat (arXiv 2603.22242): the (∞,ω) Gray tensor of pasting diagrams is the free one on the Steiner tensor. This settles the semantic side; CT is purely syntactic.
- Finster–Mimram, Benjamin–Markakis: in CaTT, any two parallel terms over a pasting diagram that use all its variables are connected by an invertible coherence. This gives CT′ whenever the two mismatched terms are composites over the *same* pasting diagram, but not in general.

## 5. What has been tried

- Choosing T(c) as the composite along a fixed layering (e.g. the lexicographically first one) gives (a) and (b). Whether the induced types match syntactically depends on whether the source of the chosen layering of c is the chosen layering of ∂⁻c; for hexagonal boundaries it is not, with the naive choice.
- A single "composite" coh per cell, whose type is chosen freely, satisfies the support condition but needs the chosen boundary terms to be of the form u[σ] for u over the coh's pasting diagram, which fails when the boundary uses lower-dimensional composite cells not among σ's arguments.

## 6. Why it matters

It is exactly what is needed to write Gray-shaped contexts (lax naturality squares, cubes, lax transformations between diagrams) in a type theory with no definitional equalities. A proof of CT, or of CT′ with an explicit algorithm, gives such contexts for every pair of pasting diagrams; a counterexample would show that some definitional equality (or a new rule for typing generators by molecules) is unavoidable.

## 7. What would count as a solution

An explicit construction of T satisfying (a)–(c), or (a), (b), (c′); or a pair Θ, Δ for which no assignment satisfies (a)–(c), together with an explanation of which coherences are forced.

## 8. Partial results (2026-11-18)

- **D² ⊗ D¹: CT holds.** For t : u ⇒ v in D² and the interval i, ∂⁻(t ⊗ i) = (u ⊗ i) followed by (t ⊗ 0) whiskered on the right by y ⊗ i, and ∂⁺(t ⊗ i) = (t ⊗ 1) whiskered on the left by x ⊗ i, followed by (v ⊗ i). With binary composites for 2-edge paths and whiskering coherences typed f ∗ h ⇒ g ∗ h, all layer boundaries agree syntactically.
- **D¹ ⊗ D¹ ⊗ D¹: CT fails for layered T.** The source hexagon of the top cell has a unique layering whose first two layers are a square whiskered on the right and a square whiskered on the left, meeting at a 3-edge path. The first layer's target contains a subterm denoting a 2-edge path b c that no subterm of the second layer's source can denote (every term uses exactly the variables of its strict image). So the two sides bracket the 3-edge path differently.
- **CT′ for generators of dimension ≤ 3:** conjugate each layer by associators to a fixed bracketing of paths. **Dimension 4:** follows from relative coherence in dimension 2 (any two parallel 2-terms with the same strict image are connected), proved in the project's `relative-coherence.md`. **Dimension ≥ 5:** reduces to relative coherence in dimension n − 2 for loop-free contexts, open.

So the remaining question is CT′ in dimension ≥ 5, or equivalently that relative-coherence statement.
