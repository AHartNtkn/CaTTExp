# Interpreting CaTT in (∞,ω)-categories: coherent choice, and which (∞,ω)

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. The questions are Q1–Q4 under "Questions"; the most useful partial answer is Q2 for a fixed small fragment (Q2a).*

## Background

**CaTT.** CaTT (Finster–Mimram, LICS 2017) is a dependent type theory with one sort former, `⋆` and `arr A t u` (cells of type A from t to u), and one term former, `coh`. For a *pasting context* Θ (a context presenting a globular pasting diagram) and two parallel terms u, v over Θ satisfying a support (free-variable) condition, `coh Θ (u → v) σ` is a cell, for every substitution σ into Θ. Substitution is strict and syntactic. The models of CaTT are exactly Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis weak ω-categories: presheaves on the CaTT coherator sending globular sums to globular products (Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, arXiv 2106.04475, §3).

**A directed variant.** A variant L of CaTT keeps the same syntax but changes the side condition of `coh` so that, across objects, interchange is a directed cell (lax, not invertible). Its intended strict models are strict ω-categories with lax transformations (Ara–Maltsiniotis's ∞-Cat_lax, arXiv 1607.00668, Ex. C.18), and its intended homotopical models are oriented (∞,ω)-categories in the sense of Gepner–Heine (arXiv 2510.10504), whose homs carry Campion's Gray tensor (arXiv 2311.00205). The strict soundness of L is proved, using that cells of free strict ω-categories on Steiner complexes are determined by their supports.

**What is known about the homotopy theory.**
- Strict ω-categories have the Lafont–Métayer–Worytkiewicz model structure, cofibrantly generated by the boundary inclusions ∂Oₙ → Oₙ (arXiv 0712.0617, §4).
- Leinster's operad is the universal cofibrant replacement of the strict ω-category operad for that weak factorisation system (Garner, arXiv 0804.2663). Markakis extends this to generalised signatures: free algebras on computads are the cofibrant objects (arXiv 2303.11978).
- For Batanin–Leinster weak ω-categories, Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara prove 2-out-of-3 for ω-weak equivalences and call a (left semi-)model structure future work (arXiv 2406.13240, introduction). We know of no model structure on weak ω-categories.
- Cat_ω (Loubaton, arXiv 2406.05425, the inductive notion lim Cat_n) is presentable and cartesian closed (Prop. 2.2.1.54). Campion's Gray tensor is built on flagged (∞,∞)-categories and transferred to Cat_n for n ≤ ω and to the version with coinductive equivalences.
- CaTT's invertible cells are the coinductively invertible ones (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2406.12127).

**The gap.** To use CaTT (or L) as an internal language for an (∞,ω)-category C, one needs a CaTT model, i.e. a weak ω-category with strictly functorial substitution, that *presents* C. In (∞,1)-category theory the analogous step for Martin-Löf type theory is a coherence/strictification theorem (local universes, model categories presenting the ∞-category). We know of no analogue for CaTT and (∞,ω)-categories.

## Questions

**Q1 (homotopy theory).** Is there a homotopy theory on GM weak ω-categories (equivalently, on models of CaTT), e.g. a (semi-)model structure or a relative category whose weak equivalences are the ω-weak equivalences, whose underlying ∞-category is Cat_ω for some notion of (∞,ω)-category? Which notion: inductive, coinductive, or flagged?

**Q2 (presentation).** Does every (∞,ω)-category arise, up to equivalence, as the image of a CaTT model? Equivalently, is the functor from CaTT models to (∞,ω)-categories (once Q1 defines it) essentially surjective?
- **Q2a (smallest case).** For C a strict ω-category regarded as an (∞,ω)-category, the answer is yes trivially. Is it yes for C = an (∞,2)-category presented by a 2-complicial set, or a Bergner–Rezk Θ₂-space? A construction of a CaTT model whose underlying (∞,2)-category is C would settle the dimension-2 case.

**Q3 (directed version).** The same as Q1–Q2 with L in place of CaTT and oriented (∞,ω)-categories (Gepner–Heine) in place of (∞,ω)-categories. Does the Gray-enriched structure make the coherent-choice problem easier or harder? (L's coherence cells are invertible only within one hom; cells across objects are directed.)

**Q4 (which notion).** CaTT's invertibility is coinductive, but the limit and Yoneda results one wants to use with CaTT (Loubaton) are for the inductive notion. Is there an argument that the "free" statements CaTT needs (e.g. Gray tensors of globular pasting diagrams are free (∞,n)-categories, Campion Cor. 3.5) hold in both, so that the choice does not matter for the theory's soundness?

## What would help most

A precise statement of what is known for Q1 in the literature, with references; and for Q2a, either a construction or an obstruction. A negative answer to Q2 (some (∞,ω)-category not presented by any strict-substitution model) would be equally valuable.

# Researcher Response

**There is a relative-category answer to Q1, and there is a construction that resolves strict coherent choice for an extracted globular model. What I cannot establish is the reconstruction theorem needed for general Q2a.** There is also a precise obstruction to combining the coinductive weak equivalences with the ordinary inductive nerve of strict ω-categories.

I distinguish below between results in the literature and deductions proved here.

## 1. Q1: the relative category exists; its geometric identification remains unestablished

### 1.1 CaTT and Batanin–Leinster are already connected

For the particular cat-coherator used by CaTT, there is an established equivalence
\[
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathrm{CaTT})
\simeq
\operatorname{Wk}\omega\operatorname{Cat}_{\mathrm{BL}}.
\]
Benjamin–Markakis–Sarti, *CaTT contexts are finite computads*, §7, derives this directly from the computadic description and the corresponding nerve theorems. Consequently, Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara’s results apply to CaTT models. This statement concerns the CaTT coherator; it is not automatically a comparison between models of every possible cat-coherator. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.00398?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Let \(W\) be the transported class of FHM ω-weak equivalences: strict model morphisms that are essentially surjective in every dimension, using coinductively invertible cells. FHM prove:

- **Theorem 3.3.3:** \(W\) satisfies 2-out-of-3.
- **Corollary 3.3.4:** \(W\) satisfies 2-out-of-6.
- On strict ω-categories, \(W\) agrees with the Lafont–Métayer–Worytkiewicz weak equivalences.

These are the theorem numbers in the PDF; the arXiv HTML has numbering errors. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13240?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Thus, as a formal consequence, there is a relative category
\[
\bigl(\operatorname{Mod}(\mathrm{CaTT}),W\bigr)
\]
and an ∞-categorical localization
\[
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{CaTT}}
:=
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathrm{CaTT})[W^{-1}].
\]

A model structure is unnecessary for this localization to be defined. The unresolved comparison is an equivalence between \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{CaTT}}\) and a separately defined geometric theory.

### 1.2 There is more progress than the 2024 weak-equivalence paper alone indicates

FHM’s November 2025 paper, *ω-equifibrations between strict and weak ω-categories*, supplies candidate fibrations. Writing \(I\) for the free sphere-to-globe inclusions, it proves
\[
I\text{-inj}
=
W\cap\operatorname{EqFib}
\]
(Proposition 4.1.5), and constructs a set \(J\) such that
\[
\operatorname{EqFib}=J\text{-inj}
\]
(Theorem 4.3.1). Its introduction still describes the weak analogue of the folk model structure as a goal. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09849?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The 2026 CaTT literature has not turned these results into the requested model-structure theorem:

- Benjamin–Champin–Markakis, *A type theory for invertibility in weak ω-categories*, explicitly describes the model structure as open. Its “fibrant marked ω-categories” are defined by algebraic conditions; the paper does not establish a model structure having exactly those fibrant objects. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16602?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- Their June 2026 *Computads with invertible generators for weak ω-categories* still describes the model structure as conjectural. Its cofibrancy result concerns the weak factorisation system generated by sphere-to-globe inclusions. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.30254?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**I did not find a model or semi-model structure with \(W\) on CaTT models, or a theorem identifying \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{CaTT}}\) with the inductive, coinductive, or flagged geometric theory.**

The existence of the operadic or computadic weak factorisation systems does not provide that identification: a weak factorisation system alone supplies neither the required weak-equivalence axioms nor a geometric comparison.

## 2. Q2a: a construction of coherent choice, with the remaining step isolated

There is a useful construction that goes beyond choosing individual composites. It constructs an actual set-valued CaTT model, with strict substitution, from an algebraically fibrant complete Segal \(\Theta\)-space.

The following is a **deduction from the cited machinery**, not a presentation theorem asserted in those papers.

### 2.1 Statement

Let \(\Theta=\Theta_\omega\) be Joyal’s category, and let \(\mathcal M\) be the injective complete Segal \(\Theta\)-space model category.

After making fixed choices independent of the input, there is a functor
\[
S:
\operatorname{AlgFib}(\mathcal M)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathrm{CaTT})
\]
such that
\[
S(Y)_n\cong (UY)(D_n)_0.
\]

Here:

- \(U\) forgets the chosen algebraic fillers;
- \(D_n\) is the \(n\)-globe;
- the subscript \(0\) means vertices of the simplicial set \((UY)(D_n)\).

Moreover, \(S\) sends geometric weak equivalences to \(W\). Thus it induces a functor
\[
\mathbf{Cat}^{\mathrm{ind}}_\omega
\longrightarrow
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{CaTT}}.
\]

This is a comparison **from geometric categories to algebraic models**. It does not yet supply an inverse comparison or prove that the extracted algebraic model reconstructs its input.

### 2.2 Geometric ingredients

For a globular pasting shape \(P\in\Theta\), write
\[
\operatorname{Sp}(P)\hookrightarrow yP
\]
for its full globular spine: the colimit, in simplicial presheaves, of the representable globes in its defining globular sum.

We use the following properties of \(\mathcal M\):

1. Cofibrations are monomorphisms.
2. Each spine inclusion is a trivial cofibration.
3. Each \(yP\) is discrete and fibrant.

The second property is the Segal condition. For the third, \(P\) is a finite-dimensional gaunt strict category, so its discrete nerve satisfies both Segal and completeness conditions. The \(\Theta_\omega\)-space formulation and its spine localizations are described by Campion, §1.1; the strict/Rezk-complete intersection is described in Example 1.30. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00205v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Choose a set \(J\) of generating trivial cofibrations. Nikolaus’s algebraic fibrancy construction gives
\[
F:\mathcal M
\rightleftarrows
\mathcal A:=\operatorname{AlgFib}_{J}(\mathcal M):U.
\]
The unit \(K\to UFK\) is a trivial cofibration; fibrations and weak equivalences in \(\mathcal A\) are detected by \(U\); and \(F\dashv U\) is a Quillen equivalence. Every member of \(\mathcal A\) is fibrant. These are the relevant conclusions of Nikolaus, §2 and Theorem 2.20. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.1342?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 2.3 The key point: the universal pasting resolutions map by trivial fibrations

Because \(yP\) is discrete, the simplicial mapping set
\[
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal M}(K,yP)
\]
is discrete for every simplicial presheaf \(K\).

For a trivial cofibration \(j:A\hookrightarrow B\), fibrancy of \(yP\) implies that
\[
\operatorname{Map}(B,yP)
\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Map}(A,yP)
\]
is a trivial Kan fibration. Between discrete simplicial sets, this is a bijection. Therefore \(yP\) has **unique** fillers against every member of \(J\), and hence a canonical algebraic fibrancy structure.

The spine inclusion consequently extends to an algebraic morphism
\[
q_P:F\operatorname{Sp}(P)\longrightarrow yP.
\]

This map is a weak equivalence: its composite with the unit is the spine inclusion,
\[
\operatorname{Sp}(P)
\longrightarrow
UF\operatorname{Sp}(P)
\xrightarrow{Uq_P}
yP,
\]
and both the unit and the composite are weak equivalences.

It is also a fibration. In a \(J\)-lifting problem against \(Uq_P\), take the distinguished filler in its domain. Its image must be the prescribed filler in \(yP\), by uniqueness of fillers there.

Thus
\[
\boxed{q_P:F\operatorname{Sp}(P)\longrightarrow yP
\text{ is a trivial fibration}.}
\]

This argument is what permits compatible choices for every CaTT operation.

### 2.4 Realize the coherator by induction

Write \(\mathbb T\) for the CaTT coherator, and
\[
p:\mathbb T\longrightarrow\Theta
\]
for strict interpretation: an operation is sent to its interpretation in the corresponding free strict pasting category.

On the initial category of globular pasting shapes, set
\[
R(P)=F\operatorname{Sp}(P).
\]
This preserves the prescribed globular sums **in \(\mathcal A\)**, because \(F\) preserves colimits.

The coherator is constructed by successively freely adjoining lifts of admissible pairs. This is the explicit universal construction in Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, §3.3. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.04475?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Suppose an operation
\[
h:D_n\longrightarrow P
\]
is being adjoined and its boundary has already been interpreted. We obtain a commutative square
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
F(\partial yD_n)&\longrightarrow&R(P)\\
\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow q_P\\
F(yD_n)&\xrightarrow{\;y(p h)\circ q_{D_n}\;}&yP .
\end{array}
\]

Here \(\partial yD_n\) is the union of the two hemisphere subrepresentables, recursively glued along their common boundary. Its inclusion in \(yD_n\) is monic. Since \(F\) is left Quillen, the left map is a cofibration. The right map is the trivial fibration proved above.

Choose a lift. The universal property of freely adjoining \(h\) extends \(R\) to the next stage. Repeating this constructs
\[
R:\mathbb T\longrightarrow\mathcal A
\]
preserving globular sums, together with the natural comparison \(q:R\Rightarrow yp\).

The choices are made once while constructing \(R\).

### 2.5 Extract the CaTT model

For \(Y\in\mathcal A\), define
\[
S(Y)(P)=\mathcal A(R(P),Y).
\]

Since \(R\) preserves globular sums, this presheaf sends them to globular products. It is therefore a CaTT model.

For globes,
\[
\begin{aligned}
S(Y)(D_n)
&=\mathcal A(F(yD_n),Y)\\
&\cong\mathcal M(yD_n,UY)\\
&\cong(UY)(D_n)_0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Substitution is precomposition. For composable arrows \(a,b\) of the coherator,
\[
S(Y)(b\circ a)=S(Y)(a)\circ S(Y)(b)
\]
as an equality of functions. This provides the requested strict syntactic substitution.

### 2.6 The construction is homotopical in this direction

A trivial fibration \(Y\to Z\) in \(\mathcal A\) induces a map \(S(Y)\to S(Z)\) with exact globular boundary lifting: a prescribed boundary and a cell downstairs form a lifting problem against
\[
F(\partial yD_n)\hookrightarrow F(yD_n).
\]
Hence \(S(Y)\to S(Z)\) is a globular trivial fibration, and in particular belongs to \(W\).

This extends to all weak equivalences by the following direct form of Ken Brown’s argument. Given a weak equivalence \(f:Y\to Z\), factor its graph as
\[
Y\xrightarrow{i}V\xrightarrow{p}Y\times Z,
\]
with \(i\) a trivial cofibration and \(p\) a fibration. Since all three targets are fibrant, both projections
\[
p_Y:V\to Y,\qquad p_Z:V\to Z
\]
are fibrations. From \(p_Yi=1_Y\) and \(p_Zi=f\), both are weak equivalences. Thus \(S(p_Y)\) and \(S(p_Z)\) belong to \(W\); 2-out-of-3 then gives \(S(i),S(f)\in W\).

Consequently the singular construction induces the displayed functor
\[
\mathbf{Cat}^{\mathrm{ind}}_\omega\longrightarrow\mathcal H_{\mathrm{CaTT}}.
\]

### 2.7 Why this still does not settle Q2a

For an \((\infty,2)\)-category \(C\), first regard \(C\) as an \((\infty,\omega)\)-category and choose an algebraically fibrant complete Segal \(\Theta_\omega\)-space presenting it. The construction above produces a CaTT model.

The missing claim is that a specified realization of that model is equivalent to \(C\).

There is a formal left adjoint to \(S\):
\[
\mathcal L_R(A)
=
\int^{P\in\mathbb T} A(P)\cdot R(P),
\]
where the integral is an ordinary coend in \(\mathcal A\), and \(\cdot\) denotes a copower. Thus there is a counit
\[
\mathcal L_R S(Y)\longrightarrow Y.
\]

**The preceding proof does not show that this counit is a weak equivalence.** Nor does it show that \(\mathcal L_R\) is homotopical for \(W\). Objectwise weak equivalences \(R(P)\simeq yP\) do not supply the required reconstruction theorem.

Therefore the precise partial conclusion is:

> Every geometric input admits a functorially extracted CaTT model after choosing algebraic fibrancy and fixing the interpretation of the coherator. Strict coherent choice is achievable. Recovery of the geometric input from that model remains an additional comparison theorem.

I do not have a proof or a counterexample for that recovery statement for arbitrary \((\infty,2)\)-categories.

### 2.8 An unconditional smaller presentation fragment

There is a positive answer when every hom \((\infty,1)\)-category of \(C\) is equivalent to an ordinary category. Campbell calls this the **2-truncated** case and proves that it is precisely the case presented by bicategories; see Theorem 7.28 and the comparisons with Rezk’s \((2,2)\)-\(\Theta\)-spaces in Theorems 8.7 and 8.12. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.01999?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Choose a presenting bicategory, strictify it to a strict 2-category, and then regard that as a strict ω-category and hence as a CaTT model. Bicategorical strictification supplies the required equivalence. [sas.rochester.edu](https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/leinster-book.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This covers \((2,2)\)-categories. It does not cover the unrestricted invertible higher cells permitted in your general \((\infty,2)\)-case.

## 3. Q1/Q4: an obstruction to the ordinary inductive interpretation

The choice of geometric interpretation already matters for strict ω-categories.

### 3.1 A precise incompatibility

Let
\[
\iota:\omega\operatorname{Cat}_{\mathrm{str}}
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}(\mathrm{CaTT})
\]
be the usual interpretation of strict ω-categories as CaTT models. Let \(N_{\mathrm{ind}}\) be their strict \(\Theta\)-nerve followed by inductive Rezk completion.

**Proposition — deduction.** There is no functor
\[
H:\mathcal H_{\mathrm{CaTT}}
\longrightarrow\mathbf{Cat}^{\mathrm{ind}}_\omega
\]
whose restriction along \(\iota\) agrees with \(N_{\mathrm{ind}}\) on all strict ω-categories.

**Proof.** Take the coherent walking ω-equivalence \(E=\omega^c\mathcal E\) of Hadzihasanovic–Loubaton–Ozornova–Rovelli. Their Remark 1.29 says that \(E\) is a strict polygraph with two objects \(p,q\); their Theorem 1.33 proves that
\[
E\longrightarrow 1
\]
is a folk weak equivalence. Thus \(\iota E\to\iota1\) belongs to \(W\). The coherent \(E\) is essential here: the earlier incoherent walking equivalence is not folk contractible. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.14509?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

On the other hand, \(E\) is gaunt. Indeed, for each \(n\), count the \(n\)-generators in a formal \(n\)-cell of a strict polygraph. The strict axioms preserve the count, composition adds counts, and an identity has count zero. If \(a\) had a strict inverse, then
\[
\ell_n(a)+\ell_n(a^{-1})=0,
\]
so \(a\) would have no \(n\)-generators and would be an identity.

Therefore its discrete \(\Theta\)-nerve is already Rezk complete. It has
\[
N_{\mathrm{ind}}(E)(D_0)=\{p,q\},
\]
whereas \(N_{\mathrm{ind}}(1)(D_0)\) is a singleton. They are not equivalent. Equivalently, one can use the fully faithful inclusion of gaunt ω-categories into the inductive theory, explicitly noted by Campion. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00205v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

A functor out of \(\mathcal H_{\mathrm{CaTT}}\) must send \(E\to1\) to an equivalence. Agreement with \(N_{\mathrm{ind}}\) would make it a nonequivalence. This is a contradiction. \(\square\)

The same problem occurs for compatibility with the raw flagged strict nerve.

### 3.2 The marking hypothesis is substantive

The preceding proposition depends on the chosen nerve. Henry–Loubaton, Proposition 4.58, prove that the Street nerve **marking all coinductively invertible cells** preserves folk weak equivalences. That nerve sends \(E\to1\) to an equivalence. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.11424?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Thus the phrase “a strict ω-category regarded as an \((\infty,\omega)\)-category” requires a choice in unbounded dimension. The raw strict nerve and the coinductively marked nerve can give different answers.

This is an obstruction to a specified compatibility requirement. It is not a counterexample to bare essential surjectivity for an unspecified comparison functor.

For an inductive algebraic theory, retaining a specified class of marked equivalences is a possible alternative to declaring every coinductively invertible cell an equivalence. FHM discuss precisely such a marked approach in Remark 2.4.11, while leaving its development to further work. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13240v3?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

There is a further terminological distinction: the coinductive completion should not be identified with the inverse limit formed using the **left** localization functors \(\mathbf{Cat}_{n+1}\to\mathbf{Cat}_n\). Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde’s November 2026 revision proves that coinductive completion and that localization-limit are distinct stages. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11005v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 4. Q4: the bounded free-shape statements do transfer

For the specific free shapes covered by Campion, there is a positive answer.

Let
\[
Q_\epsilon:
\mathbf{Cat}^{\mathrm f}_\omega\longrightarrow
\mathbf{Cat}^{\epsilon}_\omega,
\qquad
\epsilon\in\{\mathrm{ind},\mathrm{coind}\},
\]
be the two completions.

Campion’s Corollary 3.5 identifies strict and flagged Gray products on a monoidally suitable site. Theorem 3.14 and Corollary 3.15 make the relevant completions strong monoidal localizations. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00205v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Consequently, for \(P,Q\) in such a site,
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_\epsilon N_{\mathrm f}P
\otimes_\epsilon
Q_\epsilon N_{\mathrm f}Q
&\simeq
Q_\epsilon\bigl(N_{\mathrm f}P\otimes N_{\mathrm f}Q\bigr)\\
&\simeq
Q_\epsilon N_{\mathrm f}(P\otimes_{\mathrm{str}}Q).
\end{aligned}
\]

Likewise, applying \(Q_\epsilon\) to a pasting pushout supplied by Campion gives a pushout in the completed theory, since \(Q_\epsilon\) preserves colimits.

The relevant site shapes are finite-dimensional and gaunt. Inductive and coinductive equivalences agree at finite categorical level, so both completions fix these shapes. Hence **finite globular pasting shapes and their Gray products have the same resulting free shapes in both theories**, subject to Campion’s site hypotheses. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00205v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

There is also a bounded extension of this observation. Loubaton has an adjoint triple
\[
\tau_n^{\,i}\dashv i_n\dashv\tau_n,
\qquad
i_n:\mathbf{Cat}_n\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Cat}^{\mathrm{ind}}_\omega.
\]
Thus \(i_n\) is fully faithful and preserves colimits. A homotopical cell construction using globes only in dimensions at most \(n\) can therefore be performed in \(\mathbf{Cat}_n\), then included in the inductive theory. Coinductive completion fixes the result. This applies even to infinitely many attachments with a uniform dimension bound. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Three restrictions remain essential:

1. **Campion’s hypotheses concern specified torsion-free strict computads.** They do not identify every finite CaTT context with a strict computad having the same homotopical realization.
2. **An unbounded sequence is different from a uniformly bounded construction.** A localization preserves colimits into its target; its fully faithful right adjoint need not preserve those colimits.
3. **Agreement of source shapes does not identify mapping spaces into arbitrary targets.** The source \(D_0\) already distinguishes the inductive realization of \(E\) from its coinductive completion.

For a coinductively complete target \(B\), the valid comparison is the adjunction formula
\[
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathrm{coind}}(QA,B)
\simeq
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathrm{ind}}(A,jB).
\]
This compares a target with its inclusion as a local object. It does not compare an arbitrary inductive \(C\) with \(QC\).

Therefore **the finite free-shape lemmas can be used in both theories**. That fact alone does not establish soundness for arbitrary models or transport all of Loubaton’s limit and Yoneda constructions.

## 5. Q3: Gray enrichment introduces a separate comparison requirement

The strict target needs to be described at the enrichment level. Ara–Maltsiniotis’s Example C.18 constructs \(\infty\text{-}\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathrm{lax}}\) as a **left Gray ∞-category**. Each individual
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{lax}}(A,B)
\]
is a strict ω-category; composition between different homs is Gray-enriched. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.00668?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Gepner–Heine define oriented categories by the corresponding Gray enrichment. Their current §2.5 also distinguishes nonunivalent enrichment from univalent completion, so their base should not be identified with Loubaton’s completed \(\mathbf{Cat}_\omega\) merely from the notation. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10504?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Taking your strict soundness theorem for \(L\) as a premise, it supplies a strict interpretation. A homotopical presentation theorem still needs compatibility with the composition maps
\[
\mathcal C(y,z)\otimes\mathcal C(x,y)
\longrightarrow
\mathcal C(x,z),
\]
including their units and associativity data. Independently replacing each hom by an equivalent algebraic model does not provide those compatible maps.

The construction in §2 gives a concrete criterion for handling coherent choice in \(L\):

- realize its arities by discrete fibrant shapes;
- prove that their spine inclusions are weak equivalences;
- use the cellular presentation of its operation theory to lift against the resulting trivial fibrations.

Gray enrichment alone does not establish those hypotheses. Its role is to specify the additional composition structure that a comparison must preserve.

Since the actual admissibility predicate for \(L\) is not given here, I cannot establish an analogue of FHM’s relative category for \(L\), or an equivalence with oriented categories. The unresolved assertion remains a presentation theorem: a suitable realization must preserve the intended weak equivalences and recover each geometric input from its extracted algebraic model.
