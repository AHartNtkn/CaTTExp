# An explicit all-degree presentation of free higher categories with directed interchange

*A self-contained problem. The setting and the results it builds on are stated in §1–§2 of `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r10-2026-11-20.md` (summaries (K43)–(K73), each with a reference to its full proof). That file is the background for this one. Its notation is used here unchanged.*

## Background, in brief

Work with strict ω-categories in which interchange along k-cells, for each pair k < l, is replaced by a directed cell (an *elementary interchanger* χ). A finite pasting diagram Γ generates free objects F_E(Γ) for each *admissible* theory E on the fixed signature of the completed theory **T₁₂** ((K47)). The signature is:
- strict associative and unital composition;
- an elementary interchanger for every pair k < l;
- naturality cells for each varying argument, with orientation fixed by Leibniz signs;
- higher cells between routes;
- the auxiliaries of (K47): signed lifts of binary globe tensors at every cut, H_L, H_R, K, J and the shifted join.

Among the admissible theories:
- **V** is the least one ((K58)). It consists of the forced base B, with its defining pairs, and the guarded quotient equations of round 5.
- **V + S**, for S ⊆ {R, inner law}, adds the switched laws.

What is known about these free objects:
- They are finite in each degree at fixed boundaries, and equality and reachability in them are decidable ((K50), (K58), (K65), r10 Response 3).
- (K52) gives convergent rewriting systems for T₁₂ degree by degree, but its forbidden-factor lists are produced recursively and no closed description is known.
- (K65) and r10 Response 3 give normal forms for V + S by rank selection over the finite raw layers.
- Both constructions are *recursive procedures*. They are given relative to a raw syntax that is described in prose across several rounds: the signature, the contextual letters of each degree and their weights, the defining pairs of B, and the guards.

## Why it matters

A proof assistant (Agda, without universes, with `--safe`) is meant to check these free objects, not trust them. The generic part is already formalized:
- normal forms as the first word per component of a one-step graph, for any finite presentation;
- the finiteness of word universes from a positive conserved weight;
- the decision of directed reachability with supports.

What cannot be formalized as things stand is the input that this machinery needs in each degree: the raw syntax itself. Degree four exists. No uniform description of degree d does. A presentation that is explicit, finite in its schemas and uniform in d is what would let the whole tower be defined once by induction on degree, instead of degree by degree.

## Questions

1. **(Signature.)** Give the signature of T₁₂ as finitely many operation schemas, indexed by degree d, cut k, and the shape of the arguments. Give typing rules (source and target of each operation, as functions of the arguments' faces) such that the raw d-terms on a finite pasting diagram form an inductive family defined uniformly in d by induction on d. Say which schemas are new at each d and which are instances of a uniform rule.

2. **(Equations.)** State as finite schemas over the signature of question 1, uniform in d:
   - the defining pairs of B;
   - the guarded equations of V, with each guard as an explicit decidable condition on the arguments;
   - the switched laws R and inner law.

   Say which equations are one-step replacements inside a contextual word and which act only on lower labels (r10 Response 3 says that above degree 4 the new equations act only inside lower labels).

3. **(Contextual letters and weights.)** Give a closed description of the contextual d-letters between fixed normalized lower faces. Give an explicit positive weight on letters, conserved by every equation of question 2, and an explicit bound on the operands and whiskers of a letter in terms of the faces. The aim is a computable finite list of letters at fixed boundaries, given by a formula rather than by a search whose termination is argued separately.

4. **(Closed form above degree four.)** (K50) and (K52) record that no closed-form normal-form theorem is known above degree 4.
   - Give one for T₁₂, for V, or for V + S. One form it could take is a combinatorial model whose d-cells are explicit data, for example decorated cut sequences in a Steiner complex as in r10 Response 1, with equality decided by comparing that data.
   - Or show in a precise sense that none exists. For example: the forbidden-factor languages of (K52) are not regular uniformly in d, or the fibre sizes of (K65) are not given by any primitive-recursive formula in the boundary.

5. **(Degree five on grids.)** As a test case, give the answers to questions 1–3 explicitly for degree 5 on the suspended grids Γ_{m,n} = Σ[m] ∨ Σ[n]. Confirm or correct the claim that the 5-letters there are exactly the positional squares, whose composite-operand squares reduce to pastes of atomic squares (r10 Response 1, r9 Response 2).

## What counts as a solution

Definitions precise enough to transcribe into a proof assistant without choices left to the reader, with proofs of:
- the typing rules;
- conservation of the weight;
- finiteness of the letter lists;
- that the presentation defines the same free objects as the prose presentations of (K47) and (K58).

For question 4, either the normal-form theorem with proof, or the precise impossibility result. Partial answers are welcome, for example questions 1–3 for V alone, or for d ≤ 6.

## 3. A box for the operands, independent of the top word

Let \(g\ge2\) be two plus the number of vertices and edges of the explicit tree, counting all levels. In ranks one and two use path length and number of traversed slots. In rank three expand a block crossing into atomic swaps and count swaps and generating advances, denoting the result by \(m(A)\). The bounds are \(g,g,M=g+\binom g2\): each slot is traversed once in a 2-word, an advance progresses along its own slot, and a named pair of slots can change order just once in a 3-history. Both subdivisions preserve the atom count. Use the following integer charges. They are intrinsic to the fixed diagram; use its constants also on subintervals. Put \(C=g+M+1\), and let \(J_0(A)\) sum, over the atomic events, the number of slots strictly after the active slots minus the number strictly before. On \(r,t,\iota,h\) respectively assign the preliminary weights
\[
 |b|(Cm(A)+J_0(A)),\quad |a|(Cm(B)-J_0(B)),\quad
 m(A)m(B),\quad |tB|(Cm(A)+J_0(A))-m(A)m(B).
\]
Here \(|tB|\) is the length of the 2-target of \(B\). Use one on a globular 4-generator. Sum these weights, unchanged by exterior actions, to obtain \(w\). For \(m_4=w+f(q_3-)\) use the top hom-degree-three chain in the Gray image and the following cochain. For every multidegree \(\alpha\) of total four with entries \(\le2\), choose \(i\) its first two if present, otherwise its second one. On multidegree \(\alpha-e_i\) add \(2h_i\) if the sum before \(i\) in \(\alpha\) is even and \(2(g-h_i)\) otherwise. Here \(h_i\) is the ordinal of the vertex, or of the vertex specifying the generating arrow in its suspended branch, starting at zero. Sum over the eligible multidegrees. For \(d\ge5\) count proper occurrences with charge one, with the following overrides:

| Head | Charge |
|---|---|
| shifted \(h\), \(H_R\), double-variation \(H\) | 2 |
| \(J\) | 4 |
| cut-zero unary on moving \(z\) and stationary edge \(b\) | \(m_{d-1}(z)|b|\) |
| cut-zero \(H_L(x,B)\) | \(m_4(x)|s_2B|\) |

A fixed action carries the charge of the active occurrence; an identity contributes zero. These formulas include both orientations, and \(s_2 B\) in the last line has global degree two.

Here are the conservation checks used in the bound. The inequalities \(|J_0(A)|\le g m(A)\), \(m(B)\le M\) and \(|tB|\ge1\) give strict positivity for every proper low head. A block crossing has intrinsic moment zero by summing over its rectangle; exterior left/right slot padding changes the moment by minus/plus its length times the event count. Thus (7) preserves moment and (14)–(17) have exactly the displayed inner-square corrections. Additivity gives the two splits on either operand of \(\iota\), the unary composition laws, and the join factorization by subtraction. The triple equation has weight \(C|a||b||d|\) on both sides. The cochain term is invariant under every equality since it evaluates in Gray. Its coboundary is two on the specified multidegrees, zero on the others: the \(i\)-face height increases by one, and contributions from any other changing factor cancel. In (K47)'s degree-five boundary calculation this is exactly \(w(sZ)-w(tZ)\) on \(q_4Z\). Exterior vertex padding introduces no eligible new \(i\), since the selected entry either remains positive or is interior to the support. Inside a hom, a strictly lower fixed action leaves its top chain unchanged. Thus \(m_4\) is additive, preserved by fixed actions, and \(m_4(sZ)=m_4(tZ)\). In higher ranks the two unary subdivisions partition a product, and transports preserve both its factors. All other proper upper heads are rigid under top equations. For conservation *between* faces, the square permutes its operands, a unary moves one pure insertion past equal-charge preceding heads, and \(W\) balances \(1+1+1=2+1\) when shifted above the bottom calculation. The \(E_{32}\) routes have one outer unary charge on one side and the matching \(H_L\) charge on the other at cut zero; at positive cuts both are one. The \(E_{23}\) routes have the matching unary charges and \(1+1+1=2+1\) in addition. On the double seed all five paths have the same charge: at cut zero it is the sum of the two unary charges plus five, and at positive cuts the two variable contributions are each one. The top balances \(1+1+1=1+2\). In a raise the bottom insertion carries the same moving grade; at every subsequent level the two old hemispheres of the *same* side in the two layers have equal charges (constant heads, or the equal grades of parallel operand layers joined by the variation). The two preceding bridges have equal charges. The resulting two attachments therefore have equal grade, for either sign. This is the (K47) induction, including its separate endpoint check for the grade-four faces of \(H_L,H_R,K,J\); the charge of a unit specialization is always read on its specialized word. It proves positivity, additivity, and \(m_j(sZ)=m_j(tZ)\) for \(j\ge4\). In particular all the calculations descend through the guard and switch tables. **Operand bound.** For \(4\le j\le\dim X\) let \(b_j(X)\) be the grade of the \(j\)-row, using \(X\) itself at its top. Either side gives the same number below the top, since there is a connecting \((j+1)\)-row. In a structural paste the \(b_j\) of any constituent of rank at least \(j\) is at most the \(b_j\) of the paste. Indeed at cut \(j-1\) the row concatenates; below it Godement expansion adds the active grades under fixed actions; above it the grade of every intermediate row equals the exterior grade by conservation. This argument includes a fixed pad at its own rank and at all ranks below it. It also applies to a constituent of a chosen hemisphere: a \(j\)-row at the hemisphere's own rank has its actual grade.

For each proper head of output \(d\), **both immediate faces bound every stored operand in every one of its ranks \(j\ge4\)**, in the preceding sense. The following detail is important because the head itself can have charge one on operands of arbitrarily large grade. In a principal disk with an operand of hom rank \(t\), mark its pure action in the two \(t\)-rows. More precisely the induction assertion is the grade inequality, for each \(j\le c+t\), \(j\ge4\), in each hemisphere of rank at least \(c+t\) below the principal. It suffices to exhibit the occurrence through structural pastings, using the preceding structural inequality for any intervening flanks.

* The square displays both operands in both edge rows. A unary raise has its moving input in both insertion rows, and the rows below the insertion select the old edge rows on the corresponding layer. For the join and \(W\), both \(S,U\), and both \(S,T\), contain \(A\) and \(B\) as scheduled updates. For \(H_L,K,E_{32}\) both moving rows contain \(x\); their two-event-operand rank is read in the three-event histories containing \(B\). The right-hand seeds give the same test with \(y,A\). For \(J,G,H,E_{33}\) the rank-three histories in hom degrees contain both \(x\) and \(y\): for \(J\) read \(yaxbc,xhyf\); for the other three read \(yabcx,xhfy\). In higher hemispheres these rows are reached by projection. For the flanks of all these occurrences use the structural inequality, also for each lower \(j\).
* In a raise the new moving operand is a structural insertion in \(b_p^\pm\). Each face of \(b_{p+1}\) contains a bottom bridge; each higher face contains a preceding bridge. This propagates the bound on the moving operand and all its lower grades. For the fixed operand of rank \(q\), a face at level \(a\ge q\) contains \(r_a^\epsilon(x^i)\) in fixed actions, so the old assertion gives the bound. At an interior bridge take the actual restricted fixed rank if necessary. For the new principal's lower hemispheres at levels \(q\le a\le p\) use the old row at the selected layer (at \(p\), an endpoint of the insertion). For levels above \(p\) the context formula includes the old row at that level, with its actual flanks. These arguments use both choices of \(\epsilon\); hence they also apply when the order of raising switches. Lower \(j\) travel with the same structural constituent, even if a temporal pad changes the particular \(j\)-face spelling. No comparison of a large input to the *charge of the bridge* is required.

Now fix normalized faces \(s,t\) at rank \(d-1\). Set
\[
 B=\max\bigl(\{2,g,M\}\cup
   \{b_j(s),b_j(t):4\le j<d\}\bigr).
\]
Every operand and every flank of a proper contextual letter \(s\to t\) has all its row grades through its own rank bounded by \(B\) (use the diagram bounds through three). Indeed apply the structural inequality first to the contextual face and then the head inequality to the intrinsic face. The same bounds hold at intermediate vertices of a top path starting at \(s\), by conservation in rank \(d-1\) or the global low bounds. They hold recursively **inside the fields**: take any chronological summand of a bounded row, use positivity at the top and conservation below, then apply the same argument to its pads and operands. This is induction on rank, independent of top-path length.

Here is a literal finite product for the list. Form the following boxes through rank \(d-1\). At zero use the diagram vertices. At rank \(i\) take a starting \((i-1)\)-record and a string of length \(0,\ldots,B\) of records. A record ranges over the rank-\(i\) tag list of §1, the Cartesian product of the boxes at its declared input ranks, and one optional pair of pads at each rank \(1,\ldots,i-1\) (each side independently empty or from that rank's box). Discard improper heads, mismatches and out-of-bound grades, and identify lower values using the normal data in §4. Empty strings retain the starting vertex. Take one copy per normalized value; a witness spelling, if needed, is the first in length-lexicographic order with diagram order, row-table order, increasing indices and left fields before right fields. At rank \(d\) use just **one** record and test its two computed endpoints against \(s,t\). This filter enumerates exactly the structurally collected contextual letters, prior to top relations. The length cutoff bounds even the unexpanded block spelling in rank three, since every proper block expands to at least one event. Pads that are identities can always be encoded by absence. All bounds in this product are obtained from the diagram and the two faces. The same product, retaining all endpoints in the box, gives the alphabet for paths.

For an explicit size estimate, let \(t_*\) be one plus the total number of tags through \(d\), including input globes, as given by the finite index ranges of §1. Put \(D=4(d+2)(B+2)\), \(K=2(g+t_*+B+d+2)\). An entire box record has at most \(D^{d+1}\) nodes in its ordered tree: at each rank there are at most \(B(2+2d)\) descending fields plus the start field, and the depth in ranks is at most \(d\). Equivalently count a tag, presence bits and the length as slots in a fixed padded array of branching \(D\). At most \(K^{D^{d+2}}\) such arrays suffice, including the tag and zero padding. This coarse closed bound also permits direct enumeration as fixed-length arrays with the rank and typing filter. A loop can still have infinitely many powers between its two *fixed* faces, already \(H^n:w\to w\) on \(\Gamma_{2,2}\). The bound here is on letters and on vertices of a weight layer. To bound the number of letters in a **word**, its own top weight is needed as well.

## 4. Closed normal data by finite transformations

Here is a combinatorial model for all four settings. It avoids computing either a top word component or a list of its forbidden factors. The elementary observation is that a typed positive presentation of bounded weight can be separated by transformations of an explicitly bounded finite ordinal.

Fix \(s,t\) and top weight \(w\). Use the lower box in §3, and let \(v\) be its number of possible \((d-1)\)-vertices after filtering. Let \(A\) be its alphabet of contextual letters with **both** endpoints in this box, including the two actual endpoints in every name. Use only charges at most \(w\), and write \(a=|A|\). List the local pairs \((u,v')\) in this alphabet by instantiating the top rows of §2 under all fixed pads from the box, keeping weight at most \(w\). No chronological prefix or suffix is needed in this list. For \(d>4\) the only positive pairs needed are the outer unary moving and stationary splits and transports. All input values to those instances occur on the displayed sides: in a split the two pieces are operands on the expanded side and the parent is on the direct side, up to fixed vertex transports. A transported vertex is a low-rank field. The exterior pads have the §3 bound by the structural inequality, also if collected into flanks of the several children. Unit specializations give identical words or the corresponding lower cases. For \(d\le4\) the noncontext variables in the finite low equation table all come from the globally bounded first three ranks. Thus this pair list uses only lower data. Above rank three equality of field values is supplied by this same construction one rank down. The rank-zero labels are the tree vertices.

Set \(N=1+v\sum_{i=0}^{w}a^i\), with \(a^0=1\), and \([N]=\{0,\ldots,N-1\}\). Take **all** arrays \(T\in[N]^{A\times[N]}\) in lexicographic order. Each column of states for letter \(e\) describes the function \(z\mapsto T(e,z)\); \(T_p\) denotes the product in reading order along a word \(p\), with the identity function on the empty word. Define the bit
\[
 D(T)=\prod_{(u,v')}\ \prod_{z=0}^{N-1}[\,T_u(z)=T_{v'}(z)\,].
\]
The normal data of \(p:s\to t\) are the lower normal data of \(s,t\), \(w\), and the array
\[
             \boxed{\quad \big(D(T)\,(1+T_p(z))\big)_{T,\ 0\le z<N} .\quad}       \tag{1}
\]
Fix the ordering of \(A\) by the records in §3. Equivalently encode the last array in base \(N+1\), with first entry least significant. The box, ordinal, product of bits and transformation product specify the coordinates prior to any same-degree equality computation. The allowed data are the images in (1) of the typed products of length \(0,\ldots,w\) and charge \(w\) with the indicated endpoints. This gives a bounded comprehension for the carrier as well. For implementations using an array index, \(T(e,z)\) is just digit \(eN+z\) of a base-\(N\) counter through \(N^{aN}-1\); for \(N=1\) there is one all-zero array. This form trades space for a direct formula.

**Separation proof.** A contextual consequence of any local pair has equal products for every array satisfying the pair list, by associativity of function composition. Rank induction handles a change of spelling in a lower field. Conversely form, *for this proof only*, the set of classes of typed paths in this finite alphabet of charge at most \(w\), under the listed pairs in chronological contexts. Keep both endpoints, also for empty paths. Add a sink. Its cardinality is at most \(N\): a path has one starting vertex and at most \(w\) letters. Each letter acts on the right by adjoining it if composable and within the weight limit, and goes to the sink otherwise. The sink is fixed. This action is well-defined because the pairs preserve endpoints and weight. It satisfies each listed pair on **every** state. Indeed a bad initial match fails for both; with a good match either both complete, with equivalent results, or both overflow, since every partial weight is at most the final weight. Proper pairs have two nonempty sides; weight-zero pairs on the normalized syntax have two empty sides. Embed the state set into \([N]\) and let every extra state be fixed. The resulting array has \(D(T)=1\). At the empty path of \(s\) its products return the actual classes of the two proposed words. Hence distinct classes give different coordinates in (1). This proof of the size bound does not enter the definition of (1).

Finally, these classes are exactly the desired free cells. Evaluation of the §1 records gives typed terms; collecting a term gives its chronological record word. Their composites are identities modulo structural laws by the collection induction. Under these maps the unsimplified generating pairs are precisely the placed instances in §2, in either direction. In a derivation starting in the chosen layer, endpoints and weight stay fixed, intermediate vertices and all lower fields stay in §3's box, and lower replacements give equal normal fields. Conversely each listed pair is an instance in the free object. The conservativity argument for the guarded rows applies on each \(\Gamma\); it does not require an ambient algebra with decidable equality. This proves the claimed identification with (K47),(K58), including substitutions with composite operands. Composition, action and substitution on the data can be defined by taking the first bounded representative, performing the indicated operation, and applying (1); equality of the data is simply entrywise equality. This is a closed form relative to the lower labels, uniformly at each rank. In contrast to the rank formula in (K65), its coordinates have no parameter giving the incidence graph on top words or a normal form in a second theory.

## 5. All two-chain grids in global degree five

A 2-word is a shuffle of consecutive \(a\)- and \(b\)-advances, retaining endpoints. A 3-event replaces neighboring \(b_j a_i\) by \(a_i b_j\); a block is the rectangular crossing with its two (7) subdivisions. At four, use \(\iota(F,G)\) on two disjoint adjacent spatial intervals of a 2-word with proper 3-histories \(F,G\), and its fixed flanks. Its weight is \(|F||G|\), in atomic events. Indeed all outer hom factors are chains, so there is no proper outer unary variation or join and the hom-degree-three Gray chain vanishes. The four-equations are the applicable balancing, transports, and restricted or switched splits of §2. This gives normalized lower labels on *any* \(m,n\) by (1).

The complete proper rank-five head table is as follows. Square brackets retain the fixed history prefix and suffix at the indicated action, as in (26),(27). Letters on disjoint intervals below include their fixed whiskerings.

| Cut, head, charge | Typed operands and faces |
|---|---|
| \(2,\delta(X,Y),1\) | \(X:u\to u',\ Y:v\to v'\) are proper 4-paths on consecutive temporal intervals. Faces \((uY)(Xv')\) and \((Xv)(u'Y)\). |
| \(1,N_L(X,G),1\) | \(X:F\to F'\) varies the left 3-history, \(G:v\to v'\) is the right history. With \(F,F':u\to u'\), the faces are \(\iota(F,G)[\,\epsilon\mid Xv\mid u'G\,]\) and \([\,uG\mid Xv'\mid\epsilon\,]\iota(F',G)\). |
| \(1,N_R(F,Y),1\) | \(Y:G\to G'\) varies the right history. Faces \([\,\epsilon\mid uY\mid Fv'\,]\iota(F,G')\) and \(\iota(F,G)[\,Fv\mid u'Y\mid\epsilon\,]\). |
| \(1,h_1(X,Y),2\) | \(X:F\to F',\ Y:G\to G'\) are proper 4-paths in adjacent spatial intervals. Faces \(YF\,;\,\iota(F,G')\,;\,XG'\) and \(GX\,;\,YF'\,;\,\iota(F',G')\), with insertions on the current input layers as in (K32). |

In the last line \(YF\), for instance, inserts \(Y\) while the left update \(F\) is still pending; the two outer intervals are those of \(F,G\). Give each head the rank-1 through rank-4 flanks of §1 and test the two resulting 4-faces. There are no input globes of this degree. Two consecutive positive cut-zero factors are individual chains and have no proper input of hom rank two. At cut one the output hom rank is three, giving exactly the two unaries and \(h\); at cut two the output hom rank is two, giving the square. Larger cuts have no proper operation of this output rank. This exhausts the table including unit specializations that evaluate to existing letters. There are no positive top relations: the only candidate top subdivisions and transports require an outer unary. Thus at five the normal form on **every** such grid is simply the typed list of these records over normalized lower labels.

For explicit list bounds use all legal subintervals of the two named chains, 2-shuffles of length at most \(m+n\), and 3-histories of at most \(mn\) atomic swaps. At four, given the proposed 5-faces \(s,t\), take 4-words of length at most \(\max(m_4(s),m_4(t))\) over the placed \(\iota\)'s just described; the same bound covers the positive 4-operands and the two 4-pads. Normalize these lower words before testing the table. No match or factorization in degree five is involved. If the face grades differ, the list is empty by conservation.

In particular **two rows** suffice to exclude all three cut-one heads: a positive 4-path needs at least two different \(a\)'s and two different \(b\)'s in its spatial interval, since one of its letters exchanges disjoint positive histories. A disjoint positive 3-partner needs another of each. This also proves the transposed statement for two columns. In a \(3\times3\) shuffle take the three consecutive intervals \(b_1a_1\mid b_2a_2\mid b_3a_3\), with \(F_i\) the atomic crossing on interval \(i\). Then \(N_L(\iota(F_1,F_2),F_3)\) and \(N_R(F_1,\iota(F_2,F_3))\) are typed and proper. In the analogous four-interval shuffle \(h_1(\iota(F_1,F_2),\iota(F_3,F_4))\) is typed and proper. These witnesses prove the respective thresholds in both axes. Their distinct tags survive because the top list is free.

Even on two rows, **composite-operand squares are single letters**. On \(\Gamma_{2,4}\) let \(H_e,H_l\) be the consecutive doubled-block 4-loops of (K34), with matching history endpoints and the fixed insertions on the early and late intervals. The single \(\delta(H_eH_e,H_l)\) moves the late action past both early actions. Moving it past them separately gives a parallel two-letter path. Both have the late-then-two-early source and the two-early-then-late target by the square face formula. The first has grade one and the second grade two. This separates them in \(T_{12}\) and in every switch setting. The subdivision of a rank-four \(\iota\) and the subdivision of a rank-five \(\delta\) are different assertions. There is a related positive statement about **reachability**. For \(X=X_1X_2\), start with the late operand followed by \(X_1,X_2\), exchange it first with \(X_1\) and then with \(X_2\); for \(Y=Y_1Y_2\), start with \(Y_1,Y_2\) followed by the early operand and exchange \(Y_2\) first. Fixed insertions use the current initial or terminal face of the other operand. The square formula identifies the initial and final 4-paths with the faces of the direct square, in each case by functoriality of fixed actions. Iteration gives an atomic-operand witness with the same endpoints. This explains precisely which computation may use only atomic squares. Also distinguish the diagram here from the single outer suspension \(\Sigma(\Sigma[m]\vee\Sigma[n])\) used in the Agda grid example; the distribution of active cuts depends on these parentheses.
