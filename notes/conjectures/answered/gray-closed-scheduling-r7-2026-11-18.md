# Schedules in free Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-categories, round 7

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked. The smallest open case is Question 1.*

## Background

**Schedules.** Fix a finite poset P on labels {1, …, n}, with numerical order as the left/right order of incomparable labels. Let S(P) be the category with:
- objects: the linear extensions of P;
- generating arrows: swaps ji → ij of adjacent P-incomparable labels with i < j;
- equations: commuting squares for swaps at disjoint positions, and the braid hexagon for three pairwise incomparable labels.

Then S(P)(w, v) is a singleton if Inv_P(v) ⊆ Inv_P(w) and empty otherwise. Here Inv_P(w) is the set of pairs i < j, incomparable, with j before i in w. (Proof: move the first differing label left, then resolve the first swaps by a square or a hexagon and induct.)

**Gray monoids.** In the free strict Gray monoid (a monoid in 2-Cat for Gray's lax tensor) on a set of objects and generating 1-cells, fix the image in the free strict monoidal category, i.e. the string diagram. Then the hom-category between the 1-cells with that diagram is S(P). Here P is the diagram's dependency order, with left/right given by planarity. The 2-cells are whiskered interchangers (f ⊗ B′)∘(A ⊗ g) ⇒ (A′ ⊗ g)∘(f ⊗ B), "right factor first ⇒ left factor first".

**The ω-version.** The hom ω-category of the Steiner complex ν(I^⊗n), from bottom to top, is not thin: two hexagon 2-cells are joined by the 3-cell ***. Gray's 2-tensor is its intelligent 2-truncation (Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches*, A.25 and A.31). For a dependency poset P, the subcomplex K_P ⊆ I^⊗n spanned by the faces (order ideal J, S ⊆ min(P∖J)) has paths equal to the linear extensions of P. Its truncated hom is S(P).

## Setting: the biclosed version

Formulas are built from atoms with ⊗ and the residuals B\A and A/B, with no unit. 𝒢 is the free **Gray-monoidal biclosed strict 2-category** on a set of generating 1-cells fᵢ : Pᵢ → Qᵢ between formulas. Its structure:
- a strict 2-category with a Gray monoidal structure (lax interchangers, as above);
- closed structure: isomorphisms of hom-categories Φ : 𝒢(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ 𝒢(B, A\C), and the mirror one for /.
  - Φ is **strictly natural in the context variable B**.
  - Φ is natural in the **bound variable A only up to the interchanger**.
  - Strict naturality in A would force (f ⊗ B′)(A ⊗ g) = (A′ ⊗ g)(f ⊗ B), collapsing distinct schedules into one 1-cell. That is not wanted.

**Two warnings, both established.**
1. *Nesting is not invariant under naive normalization.* For a generator h : U → U with U = X\Y,

   ```
   h = Λ(ev) ∘ h = Λ(ev ∘ (1 ⊗ h))
   ```

   So h can be "ambient" or "inside a closure". β-reduction ev ∘ (1 ⊗ Λk) = k also changes nesting. Any invariant notion of "event inside a closure" must start from a βη-canonical form.
2. *Unit-free planar coherence.* In the free unit-free biclosed monoidal 1-category, a balanced sequent (each atom twice, opposite polarities) has at most one morphism, and exactly one when it is provable in the Lambek calculus with product. So the purely structural parts of 1-cells are unique.

## What is known (proved)

1. **A word invariant.** Sending each generator to its label gives an invariant W of 1-cell equality with values in words (composition order), with W(Λu) = W(u), W(ev) = ε and W(D ⊗ u) = W(u). It is realised by the one-object model whose 1-cells are words, with a unique 2-cell between words with the same multiset. So 1-cells with different generator order are distinct.
2. **Closure trees are not invariants of components.** With generators a : X → A and f : A ⊗ B → C, c = Λ_A(f), e = ev: the interchanger gives θ : s = e∘(a⊗U)∘(X⊗c) ⇒ t = e∘(A⊗c)∘(a⊗B), and t = f∘(a⊗B) by β. Every representative of s has a generator under currying (a Boolean biclosed poset rules out the needed structural prefix), while t is tensor-only. So any βη-normal form fixing tensor-only terms gives s and t different closure trees, though they are connected by a 2-cell. "Cross-level cells are generated by ordinary interchangers" does not imply "the canonical tree is invariant".
3. **A forward comparison.** For φ : P → C/B and b : X → B, the forward comparison α = e_B ∗ χ_{φ,b} : t₁ ⇒ t₂ exists, W distinguishes t₁, t₂, and Λ_X(t₂) = (C/b)∘φ by strict naturality in the context variable. Whether 𝒢(t₁, t₂) = {α} and 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ is open: the tensor-only schedule theorem does not control 2-cells built by currying, uncurrying and η-expansion.
4. **Mapping-space biclosedness is too weak for classifying 1-cells.** Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari (arXiv 2006.14495, Rem. 2.20): Map(X ⊗ Y, Z) ≇ Map(X, Fun^{opgr}(Y, Z)) in general; only cores agree.

5. **An integer model.** Let 𝓜 be the strict 2-category with:
   - objects the integers;
   - for q ≥ p, the hom 𝓜(p, q) equal to the poset (ℤ, ≤) of weights; otherwise empty;
   - composition adding weights;
   - w(p ⊗ u) = w(u) + p·δ(u) and w(u ⊗ q) = w(u), where δ(u) = target − source;
   - p\r = r − p and r/q = r − q, with left currying subtracting p·δ and right currying leaving the weight unchanged.

   𝓜 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category. An interchanger raises the weight by δ(f)·δ(g). A matrix-valued version (objects in ℤ^r) records ordered generator pairs.
6. **No reverse comparisons.** With distinct atoms, 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ and 𝒢(t, s) = ∅. For t₁ and t₂ the weights are 0 and 1, from |B| = 1, |C| = 2 and |P| = |X| = 0. For s and t they are −1 and 0. Every forward 2-cell between them uses **exactly one** interchanger whose two arguments each contain one generator. Every other interchanger in it has a generator-free argument.
7. **Undirected components.** π₀ 𝒢(A, C) ≅ 𝓑_Σ(A, C), the free ordinary unit-free biclosed monoidal category. Two 1-cells are connected by a zigzag of 2-cells iff their images there agree.
8. **The image and W do not classify 1-cells.** Take generators f : A → B and g : B → C with padding L, R, and let m = L ⊗ (g ∘ f) ⊗ R.
   - Currying g with the right padding gives u ⇒ m.
   - Currying g with the left padding gives m ⇒ v.
   - u, m and v have equal images in 𝓑_Σ, and W(u) = W(v).
   - Their weights (with |L| = |R| = |A| = 0, |B| = 1, |C| = 2) are −1, 0 and 1, so the three 1-cells are distinct.
   - The side of currying is data of the 1-cell, and schedules with only generator events cannot describe this component.

## Further observations (proved)

9. **Subtractive models cannot see interchangers with a structural argument.** Call a model *additive* if 1-cells carry a weight in an ordered abelian group, δ(u) = weight(target) − weight(source) is additive under composition and tensor, and an interchanger χ_{f,g} adds a bilinear term β(δ(f), δ(g)). If moreover the residuals are group subtraction (A\C = C − A, as in the model 𝓜 of item 5 and its matrix version), every structural 1-cell (ev, curried evaluations, units) has δ = 0, so every interchanger with a structural argument has weight 0. Such models cannot decide whether two 2-cells differing in those interchangers are equal. This blindness is specific to subtractive residuals; see item 13.
10. **The axioms do not make evaluation central.** For ev : A ⊗ (A\C) → C and a generator g : Y → Y′, compare (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y), related by χ_{ev,g}. Currying A on the left (using strict naturality in the context variable and the target) turns this into the same question for m_Y := Φ(ev ⊗ Y) and g, so currying only transports it. Likewise for χ_{ev,ev′} at independent positions. Unless centrality is imposed (as in Garner–Hirschowitz–Pardon's premonoidal construction), structural maps are non-central events in 𝒢. In the quotient 𝒢_c of 𝒢 in which every interchanger with a structural argument is an identity, Question 5 follows at once from item 6.

11. **Structural loops.** Take atoms A, B, C, D and U = A\(A⊗B), X = A⊗B, X′ = A⊗U, V = (C⊗D)/D, Y = C⊗D, Y′ = V⊗D. Let u = 1_A ⊗ Λ_A(1) : X → X′, e = ev : X′ → X, v = Λ^R_D(1) ⊗ 1_D : Y → Y′ and d = ev^R : Y′ → Y, so that e u = 1 and d v = 1 by β. Put T = X ⊗ Y and (composition rightmost first)

    ```
    H = (e ⊗ 1_Y)(1_{X′} ⊗ d)(u ⊗ 1_{Y′})(1_X ⊗ v) : T → T.
    ```

    - Whiskered interchangers give ι = (e⊗1) ∗ χ_{u,d} ∗ (1⊗v) : 1_T ⇒ H and π = [(e⊗1)(1⊗d)] ∗ χ_{u,v} : H ⇒ 1_T. The Gray axiom for χ_{u,dv} gives **π ∘ ι = 1**, so E = ι ∘ π is an idempotent on H.
    - **H^n are pairwise distinct.** In the model whose hom-categories are chaotic on the free group ⟨ℓ, r⟩ (objects integers, composition multiplication, left currying k ↦ ℓ^a k, right currying k ↦ r^a k), H ↦ ℓ⁻¹ r⁻¹ ℓ r.
    - All H^n have image 1_T in 𝓑_Σ, empty generator word and zero weight in every model of item 5. Since 2-cells H^m ⇒ 1_T ⇒ H^n exist, the category over 1_T is not isomorphic to any product of schedule categories.
    - This particular model does not satisfy strict equality of the two mixed curryings of (A\C)/B = A\(C/B). Item 14 removes that dependence.
    - **Forward comparisons.** Item 6's count factors each forward comparison as z₊ ∘ m ∘ z₋, with z_± using only interchangers with a generator-free argument and m a typed instance of the one generator–generator interchanger. Different typed instances of m must still be identified.
12. **Evaluation is not central in 𝒢.** In the same model with a third free generator γ for a generating 1-cell g, (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) ↦ ℓ⁻¹γ and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y) ↦ γℓ⁻¹, which are distinct. So χ_{ev,g} joins distinct 1-cells, and this uses only left closure. A complete invariant of 1-cells must schedule structural events (units, evaluations), not only generator events.

13. **A polarized model sees structural interchangers.** Let 𝓟 have objects (P, N) ∈ ℤ², A ⊗ B = A + B and A\C = C/A = C + swap(A), where swap(P, N) = (N, P). A 1-cell X → Y exists iff (P − N)(X) = (P − N)(Y), and it is an integer weight w. Put d(u) = P(source) − P(target).
    - Composition adds weights, w(A ⊗ u) = w(u) + P(A)·d(u) and w(u ⊗ B) = w(u).
    - Left currying is w ↦ w − P(A)·d(u), and right currying keeps the weight.
    - The homs are codiscrete.
    - 𝓟 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category in the sense above.
    - **ev is not central.** Here d(ev_A) = P(A) + N(A) ≠ 0. With A ↦ (1, 0), C ↦ (0, 0), X ↦ (1, 1), X′ ↦ (0, 0) and g : X → X′, the 1-cells (X′ ⊗ ev) ∘ (g ⊗ A ⊗ A\C) and (g ⊗ C) ∘ (X ⊗ ev) have weights 1 and 2. The same separates the two orders of two independent evaluations, with no generator involved.
    - Together with item 12, the central quotient 𝒢_c of item 10 is a proper quotient of 𝒢.

14. **Strict mixed currying does not help, and left closure alone suffices.**
    - *The model 𝒟.* Keep the chaotic free-group Gray monoid of item 11. Choose any k_c ∈ ⟨ℓ, r⟩ for c ∈ ℤ, put h_{a,c} = k_{c−a} k_c⁻¹, and let both curryings with bound object a and codomain c be f ↦ h_{a,c} f.
    - *Properties.* The multipliers telescope, so the two mixed curryings are equal, R_b R_a = R_{a+b}, and h_{0,c} = 1.
    - *Separation.* With |A| = |B| = 1, |C| = |D| = 2, k₁ = ℓ, k₄ = r⁻¹ and every other k_c = 1, H ↦ ℓ⁻¹r⁻¹ℓr. So H ≠ 1_T and the H^n are pairwise distinct even in the theory 𝒢_mix that imposes strict mixed currying.
    - *Left closure only.* Replace v and d by v_L = 1_C ⊗ Λ_C(1_{C⊗D}) and d_L = ev, through W = C\(C⊗D). The resulting H_L also maps to ℓ⁻¹r⁻¹ℓr. So the free left-closed theory already has non-identity structural loops 1_T ⇒ H_L ⇒ 1_T with π ∘ ι = 1.
15. **Countermodels for ι ∘ π = 1_H need non-invertible retractions.** In a Gray monoid, if f is strictly invertible, then χ_{f,g} is invertible, with inverse (f ⊗ B′) ∗ χ_{f⁻¹,g} ∗ (f ⊗ B). Hence in any model where u, e, v and d are strict isomorphisms, ι ∘ π = 1. That covers every refinement of the free-group, 𝒟 and 𝓟 models. A countermodel must have u ∘ e ≠ 1.
16. **Two different "centrality" questions.**
    - *Outer comparison.* For functors e, g between strict ω-categories, (e ⊗ 1)(1 ⊗ g) and (1 ⊗ g)(e ⊗ 1) are both e ⊗ g. So the interpretation of 𝒢's 1-cells as functors and its 2-cells as transformations sends every interchanger, generator or structural, to an identity, and cannot test directedness.
    - *Internal interchanger.* Evaluation applied to an interchanger of internal cells can be non-invertible. For S = J ⊗ J (J = [1]) with square γ : h₁v₀ ⇒ v₁h₀, let q : J → S/J curry 1_S and let α : v₀ → v₁ be the image of the arrow of J. Then e(χ_{α,u}) = γ.
17. **𝒢's two hom-category isomorphisms do not hold in the intended model.**
    - In S/J there is an arrow α : v₀ → v₁.
    - In J\S there is none: such an arrow would need the reverse of γ.
    - A single 2-category of strict ω-categories, with one orientation of transformation as 2-cells and ℋ(I, Z) ≅ Z, would identify both S/J and J\S with ℋ(J, S), identically on objects. That is impossible.

    So the Gray tensor's left and right closures are matched by 2-cells of opposite orientation (lax for one side, oplax for the other; Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari, Ex. 2.5). Campion's tensor gives (A\C)/B ≃ A\(C/B) canonically, but not literally.
18. **The correct structure is two-sided Gray enrichment.** For strict 2-categories with the Gray tensor, put ℍ(A, C)(P, Q) = 2Cat(P ⊗ A ⊗ Q, C), with probes P, Q on either side. Its one-sided restrictions are two different hom 2-categories: ℍ_ℓ (left probe, D_k ⊗ A → C) and ℍ_r (right probe, A ⊗ D_k → C). Then:
    - left currying is an isomorphism ℍ_r(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ ℍ_r(B, A\C), and right currying is an isomorphism ℍ_ℓ(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ ℍ_ℓ(A, C/B);
    - with probes on both sides, the bound argument absorbs the probe on its side (writing 𝒟 for data maps): ℍ(A ⊗ B, C)(P, Q) ≅ 𝒟(B ⊗ Q, (P ⊗ A)\C) = ℍ(B, (P ⊗ A)\C)(I, Q), and ℍ(A ⊗ B, C)(P, Q) ≅ 𝒟(P ⊗ A, C/(B ⊗ Q)) = ℍ(A, C/(B ⊗ Q))(P, I);
    - the squares 2Cat(J ⊗ A ⊗ J, C) give a strict double hom with mixed lax/oplax modifications.
19. **The free unit-free model 𝒢′_Σ.** Models have a probe sort and a data sort, adjunctions only between data types, and gluing along designated probe colimits (needed only for one-hole contexts). The free model 𝒢′_Σ on a signature Σ of generating 1-cells exists as a term model with an explicit universal property. The Gray model is a model. An erasure model (probes act by identities) shows that the free unit-free biclosed monoidal category 𝓑_Σ embeds faithfully in 𝒢′_Σ.
20. **What collapses and what survives in 𝒢′.**
    - Data arrows (functors) tensor bifunctorially, so the structural loops of items 11 and 14 are trivial: H = H_L = 1_T, and the maps ι, π of item 11 are identities.
    - Non-invertible Gray interchange survives one dimension up. For two transformations, the two schedules are related by the modification F(χ ▷ 1_A), which has no inverse in 𝒢′ (in the Gray model, with the atom A sent to the terminal 2-category, it is the 2-cell of the walking Gray square).
    - So in 𝒢′ the schedule structure of the Background lives on generators of probe degree J (transformations X ⊗ J → Y or J ⊗ X → Y, and more generally cells X ⊗ D_k → Y), with interchangers as modifications. Items 21–25 show that this structure is not a product of schedule categories.

21. **Transformation-level homs are not posets (proved).** Take one data atom A and two generating left-parameter transformations α, β : 1_A ⇒ 1_A (maps J ▷ A → A with both endpoints 1_A). Write α·β for "β, then α". With κ : D₂ → J ⊗ J the Gray square (right factor first ⇒ left factor first), F_{α,β} = α ∘ (1_J ▷ β) gives a forward crossing σ_{α,β} = F_{α,β} ∘ (κ ▷ 1_A) : α·β ⇒ β·α. Assigning the generators to the two probe positions the other way round gives σ_{β,α} : β·α ⇒ α·β. Let q = σ_{β,α} ∘ σ_{α,β}.
    - *Separating model.* Let 𝒳 be the strict 2-category with one object, 1-cells ℕ under addition, 2-cells only n ⇒ n, labelled by k ∈ ℕ, with all compositions adding labels. T : J ⊗ 𝒳 → 𝒳, identity at the ends, sending the J-arrow to 1 and its interchanger with n to the cell labelled n, is a transformation Id ⇒ Id. Interpreting α and β as T sends each crossing to label 1 and q^r to label 2r.
    - *Consequence.* (ℕ, +) embeds in End(α·β), and q is not invertible. A category equivalent to a poset has trivial endomorphism monoids, so the hom is not equivalent to any product of schedule categories S(P). The same happens with one generator τ : 1_A ⇒ 1_A and its self-interchanger on τ·τ.
    - *Where the schedule picture breaks.* A fixed planar diagram fixes the left/right positions of events. Here α and β are endo-transformations of the same arrow, so either can occupy either probe position, and both crossings are well typed.
22. **Fixed-bound naturality of evaluation and unit is strict (proved).** For e_{A,C} : A ⊗ (A\C) → C and a right-parameter family γ : C ⊗ P → C′, put R_A^P(γ) = Λ_A(γ ∘ (e_{A,C} ⊗ P)). Then e_{A,C′} ∘ (A ⊗ R_A^P(γ)) = γ ∘ (e_{A,C} ⊗ P), and similarly for the unit η^A and for right closure. The identity transformation of an evaluation, unit or counit data arrow has trivial interchange with everything, since its parameter map factors through ! : J → I.
23. **Varying the bound argument changes sides (proved).** For H = A\C and a right-parameter transformation a : X ⊗ J → A with endpoints f₀, f₁, the mate ā = Λ_X(e ∘ (a ⊗ H)) : J ⊗ H → X\C is a left-parameter transformation between f₀^*, f₁^*. For a left-parameter b : J ⊗ Y → H with endpoints g₀, g₁, the Gray square transported through currying is M_{a,b} : (ā g₁)·(f₀^* b) ⇒ (f₁^* b)·(ā g₀). The right-closure mirror goes (f₁^* b)·(ā g₀) ⇒ (ā g₁)·(f₀^* b). Both run right factor first ⇒ left factor first.
24. **A noninvertible structural crossing (proved).** With A = X ⊗ J, C = A ⊗ J ⊗ Y, a = 1_A viewed as a right-parameter transformation X ⊗ J → A (its endpoints X → X ⊗ J differ), and b = η^A_{J⊗Y}, the triangle identity gives M_{a,b} = Λ_X(X ⊗ κ ⊗ Y). With X and Y sent to terminal 2-categories this is the walking Gray square, which has no reverse. So structural terms with a genuine J-parameter are directed events, and their side is set by the parameter position, not by the symbol "evaluation" or "unit".
25. **The ω-version is obstructed the same way (proved).** 𝒳 is gaunt. Through the strong monoidal gaunt reflections of Campion (arXiv 2311.00205, Thm 3.14, Cor. 3.15), T gives T_∞ : J ⊗ i𝒳 → i𝒳 in (∞,ω)-categories, and the powers q^r stay distinct in the homotopy category of the transformation hom. In ν(K_P) the inversion weight ρ(J, i) = #{j ∈ J : j > i, j ∥ i} satisfies ρ(∂F) = −1 on every oriented 2-face, so cubical schedule homs have no nonidentity endo-2-cells.

## Questions

Questions 1–3 are about 𝒢′ of items 18–25. Questions 4–5 are about the original 𝒢 of the Setting, whose closed structure is strict isomorphisms of hom-categories.

1. **(The smallest transformation hom.)** In the free 𝒢′_Σ with one data atom A and generating transformations α, β : 1_A ⇒ 1_A, describe the category whose objects are the composites α·β and β·α and whose morphisms are modifications. Is it the free category on the two crossings σ_{α,β} and σ_{β,α}, so that End(α·β) is the free monoid on q (≅ ℕ)? Or do the Gray axioms impose relations on these crossings? Same question for one generator τ : 1_A ⇒ 1_A and the composites τ^n.
2. **(Position-rigid composites.)** Call a set of generating transformations *position-rigid* over a fixed arrow of 𝓑_Σ when every pair of them can be crossed in at most one direction in composites over that arrow (for instance when their endpoint data arrows are pairwise distinct and no generator is an endo-transformation). In that case, is the category of composites, with modifications, equivalent to a product of schedule categories S(P), with P the dependency order? If not, give the smallest counterexample. Is the ω-analogue with ν(K_P) true under the same hypothesis?
3. **(Which structural terms are events?)** Items 22–24 show that structural data arrows contribute no event, while structural terms with a genuine J-parameter can be directed events. Give a criterion on βη-normal terms of 𝒢′ that decides which subterms are transformation events and on which side, or show that no criterion depending only on the term's type exists.
4. **(Is ι ∘ π the identity in 𝒢?)** For H of item 11, or H_L of item 14, is ι ∘ π = 1_H in the original 𝒢? By item 15, a countermodel must have u ∘ e ≠ 1. If ι ∘ π = 1_H, are the isomorphisms H^n ≅ 1_T unique?
5. **(Forward uniqueness in 𝒢, smallest case.)** Prove 𝒢(t₁, t₂) = {α} and 𝒢(s, t) = {θ}, or show that they fail. This includes identifying the forward comparisons built from different typed instances of the crossing interchanger (item 11, last point).

# Researcher Response

**The crossings in Question 1 satisfy exactly the positive braid relations.** More precisely, the category generated by those crossings is a retract of the full modification category. This proves that currying and probe gluing impose no additional equations between crossing words. It does **not** prove that every modification is a crossing word.

There is also a negative answer to the type-only alternative in Question 3. I give both results below, followed by a conditional obstruction to Question 2 whose dependence on the chosen probes matters.

## 1. The crossing-generated category is the positive braid category

Let \(S\) be a set of generating left-parameter transformations
\[
a:1_A\Rightarrow1_A,\qquad a\in S.
\]
Let \(\mathcal C_S\) be the full modification category whose objects are the finite composites of these generators. Write \(xy\) for \(x\cdot y\), with \(y\) performed first.

Define \(\mathsf{Br}^{+}_S\) algebraically:

- Its objects are words in \(S\).
- A generating morphism exchanges two adjacent letters.
- Its equations are
  \[
  s_i s_j=s_j s_i\quad (|i-j|\ge2),
  \qquad
  s_i s_{i+1}s_i=s_{i+1}s_i s_{i+1}.
  \tag{1}
  \]
- Composition is concatenation of crossing expressions, subject to these equations.

The letters label the strands, so each crossing changes the boundary word accordingly. No inverse crossings are adjoined.

### Proposition

There are functors
\[
\mathsf{Br}^{+}_S
\xrightarrow{\ F\ }
\mathcal C_S
\xrightarrow{\ R\ }
\mathsf{Br}^{+}_S
\qquad\text{such that}\qquad
RF=1.
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(F\) sends an adjacent crossing to the corresponding whiskered modification constructed from \(\kappa\).

Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\text{Two crossing expressions are equal in }\mathcal G'
\iff
\text{they are equal using (1).}
}
\tag{3}
\]

The proof has two parts: deriving the relations and constructing a model that separates every pair not identified by them.

### 1.1. The Gray equations give the braid relations

Set
\[
\mathcal C=\mathbb H_\ell(A,A)(1_A,1_A).
\]
Composition of transformations makes \(\mathcal C\) a strict monoidal category. Its morphisms are modifications.

The Gray square gives
\[
c_{x,y}:xy\longrightarrow yx.
\]
The unit and composition equations for the interchanger give
\[
c_{x,1}=1_x=c_{1,x},
\tag{4}
\]
\[
c_{x,yz}
=(1_y\otimes c_{x,z})\circ(c_{x,y}\otimes1_z),
\tag{5}
\]
and
\[
c_{xy,z}
=(c_{x,z}\otimes1_y)\circ(1_x\otimes c_{y,z}).
\tag{6}
\]
Here \(\otimes\) denotes the monoidal product in \(\mathcal C\), induced by composition of transformations.

These crossings are natural with respect to **all** modifications. For example, given \(m:x\Rightarrow x'\), apply the Gray naturality equation in \(D_2\otimes J\) to
\[
D_2\triangleright(J\triangleright A)
\xrightarrow{\,1_{D_2}\triangleright y\,}
D_2\triangleright A
\xrightarrow{\,m\,}
A.
\]
It yields
\[
(1_y\otimes m)\circ c_{x,y}
=
c_{x',y}\circ(m\otimes1_y).
\tag{7}
\]
The other variable is treated using \(J\otimes D_2\).

These are the axioms of a strict monoidal category with a possibly noninvertible braiding. Berger explicitly uses that convention and identifies it with doubly degenerate lax Gray \(3\)-categories in §2.5. Thus this correspondence applies to directed interchangers, without assuming invertibility. [math.univ-cotedazur.fr](https://math.univ-cotedazur.fr/~cberger/bmonoid.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For completeness, naturality of \(c_{x,-}\) with respect to \(c_{y,z}\) gives
\[
(c_{y,z}\otimes1_x)\circ c_{x,yz}
=
c_{x,zy}\circ(1_x\otimes c_{y,z}).
\]
Expanding the two block crossings using (5) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(c_{y,z}\otimes1_x)
\circ(1_y\otimes c_{x,z})
\circ(c_{x,y}\otimes1_z)
\\
&\qquad=
(1_z\otimes c_{x,y})
\circ(c_{x,z}\otimes1_y)
\circ(1_x\otimes c_{y,z}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
This is Yang–Baxter. Crossings at disjoint positions commute by the bifunctoriality of composition on modifications.

Therefore (1) holds, and \(F\) is defined.

### 1.2. A model proving that there are no further relations

Give \(\mathsf{Br}^{+}_S\) its strict monoidal structure by concatenating words and juxtaposing crossing expressions. Far commutation makes juxtaposition bifunctorial.

Let \(\mathcal X_S\) be the strict \(2\)-category with one \(0\)-cell \(*\) and
\[
\mathcal X_S(*,*)=\mathsf{Br}^{+}_S.
\]
Thus its \(1\)-cells are words and its \(2\)-cells are positive colored braids. Choose \(1\)-cell composition so that
\[
w\circ v=wv.
\]

For each \(a\in S\), define
\[
T_a:J\otimes\mathcal X_S\longrightarrow\mathcal X_S
\]
by the following assignments:

1. Both endpoint restrictions are \(1_{\mathcal X_S}\).
2. The generating \(J\)-arrow at \(*\) maps to the one-letter word \(a\).
3. The mixed interchanger with a word \(w\) maps to the positive block crossing
   \[
   b_{a,w}:aw\longrightarrow wa.
   \]

The direction agrees with the question. The source of \(\chi_{u,w}\) performs \(w\) and then \(u\), so its image is \(aw\). Its target performs \(u\) and then \(w\), so its image is \(wa\).

These assignments define a strict \(2\)-functor. The interchanger’s composition equation becomes the factorization
\[
b_{a,vw}
=
(1_v\otimes b_{a,w})\circ(b_{a,v}\otimes1_w).
\tag{9}
\]
For naturality, it suffices to check an adjacent crossing in \(w\). If \(|w|=m\), write
\[
\delta_m=s_m\cdots s_1
\]
for the block crossing, with composition rightmost first. The required equation is
\[
\delta_m s_{i+1}=s_i\delta_m.
\tag{10}
\]
Move the final \(s_{i+1}\) past \(s_1,\ldots,s_{i-1}\), apply
\[
s_{i+1}s_i s_{i+1}=s_i s_{i+1}s_i,
\]
and move the resulting \(s_i\) past \(s_{i+2},\ldots,s_m\). This proves (10) using precisely (1).

Now interpret the data atom \(A\) as \(\mathcal X_S\) and every generator \(a\) as \(T_a\) in the standard two-sort \(2\mathrm{Cat}\) model from item 19. This model interprets both closures: the lax Gray tensor is ordinarily biclosed, with lax and oplax internal homs on its two sides. Tensoring preserves the designated ambient probe colimits because it has right adjoints. The relevant adjunctions are stated explicitly by Bourke–Gurski, §3. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.1320?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Interpret a modification in this model and evaluate its component at \(*\). This defines
\[
R:\mathcal C_S\longrightarrow\mathsf{Br}^{+}_S.
\]
It returns the same word on objects. On a basic crossing,
\[
R(c_{a,b})=b_{a,b}:ab\longrightarrow ba,
\]
which is the single positive crossing. Hence \(RF=1\).

This proves (2) and (3). In particular, any equation deduced using the biclosed operations or probe gluing must hold in this model, so it cannot identify different positive braid classes.

### 1.3. Consequences for \(\alpha\beta,\beta\alpha\)

Put
\[
U=\alpha\beta,\qquad V=\beta\alpha,
\]
and write
\[
p=\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}:U\to V,\qquad
r=\sigma_{\beta,\alpha}:V\to U.
\]

The subcategory generated by \(p,r\) is exactly the free category on the directed two-cycle:

| Hom-set | Crossing-generated morphisms |
|---|---|
| \(U\to U\) | \((rp)^k\), \(k\ge0\) |
| \(U\to V\) | \(p(rp)^k\), \(k\ge0\) |
| \(V\to V\) | \((pr)^k\), \(k\ge0\) |
| \(V\to U\) | \(r(pr)^k\), \(k\ge0\) |

It is a retract of the **full** two-object modification category.

For the full endomorphism monoid, the conclusion is therefore
\[
\mathbb N
\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C_S}(U)
\twoheadrightarrow
\mathbb N,
\tag{11}
\]
with composite the identity and the section sending \(k\) to \(q^k=(rp)^k\).

Equation (11) does not establish
\(\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C_S}(U)\cong\mathbb N\).
An additional modification could have the same image under \(R\) as a crossing word. Excluding that requires fullness of \(F\).

### 1.4. Consequences for \(\tau^n\)

For one generator \(\tau\), the result is
\[
B_n^+
\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal C_{\{\tau\}}}(\tau^n)
\twoheadrightarrow
B_n^+,
\tag{12}
\]
where
\[
B_n^+
=
\left\langle
s_1,\ldots,s_{n-1}
\ \middle|\
\begin{array}{ll}
s_i s_j=s_j s_i&(|i-j|\ge2),\\
s_i s_{i+1}s_i=s_{i+1}s_i s_{i+1}
\end{array}
\right\rangle .
\]
Thus the adjacent crossings generate exactly \(B_n^+\).

This detects more than crossing count. For example, in \(\operatorname{End}(\tau^3)\),
\[
s_1^2s_2^2\ne s_2^2s_1^2,
\tag{13}
\]
although both have four crossings and induce the identity permutation. To verify the inequality algebraically, assign
\[
s_1\longmapsto
U=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
s_2\longmapsto
V=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
They satisfy
\[
UVU=VUV=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
whereas
\[
U^2V^2=\begin{pmatrix}-3&2\\-2&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
V^2U^2=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\-2&-3\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Faithfulness of \(F\) transfers this distinction to \(\mathcal G'\).

The same model also proves a statement about **all** modifications:
\[
\mathcal C_{\{\tau\}}(\tau^m,\tau^n)=\varnothing
\qquad(m\ne n).
\tag{14}
\]
More generally, no modification between generator words can change their multiset of letters, because the corresponding hom-set in \(\mathsf{Br}^{+}_S\) is empty.

## 2. Question 3: type alone cannot determine parameter dependence

For a specified left-parameter presentation
\[
t:J\triangleright X\longrightarrow Y,
\]
the relevant distinction is whether \(t\) factors as
\[
J\triangleright X
\xrightarrow{\,!\triangleright1_X\,}
X
\xrightarrow{\,h\,}
Y.
\tag{15}
\]
Such a factorization makes \(t\) the identity transformation of \(h\). A type-only event test must at least distinguish this case from nondegenerate parameter dependence.

### 2.1. An entirely structural counterexample using only \(J\)

Let \(i_0,i_1:I\to J\) be the endpoint maps and \(!:J\to I\). For a data atom \(X\), consider
\[
d=(i_0!)\triangleright1_X,
\qquad
e=1_{J\triangleright X}.
\]
They have the same ordinary data-arrow type
\[
d,e:J\triangleright X\longrightarrow J\triangleright X,
\tag{16}
\]
and both are read as left-parameter transformations in
\(\mathbb H_\ell(X,J\triangleright X)\).

The first is degenerate:
\[
d=(i_0\triangleright1_X)\circ(!\triangleright1_X).
\tag{17}
\]
The second is not. Interpret \(X\) as the terminal strict \(2\)-category in the standard model. Then \(e\) becomes
\[
1_J:J\to J.
\]
Every map factoring through \(J\to I\) sends the generating arrow of \(J\) to an identity, whereas \(1_J\) preserves it. Thus no factorization (15) exists for \(e\).

Neither term uses residual introduction or elimination, so both have no biclosed \(\beta\)- or \(\eta\)-redex.

Their endpoint maps are:

| Term | Initial endpoint | Final endpoint |
|---|---|---|
| \(d\) | \(i_0\triangleright1_X\) | \(i_0\triangleright1_X\) |
| \(e\) | \(i_0\triangleright1_X\) | \(i_1\triangleright1_X\) |

Therefore **ordinary source–target type, even with the left-parameter designation fixed, does not determine event status for structural terms**.

The fact that \(e\) is an identity data arrow does not make it an identity transformation under this parameter presentation. Those are assertions in different dimensions.

### 2.2. Recording both endpoints still does not suffice for general terms

For the full syntax, there is an even simpler same-boundary pair:
\[
1_{1_A},\ \alpha:1_A\Rightarrow1_A.
\]
The identity transformation is degenerate. The generator \(\alpha\) is not: in the model of item 21, its component is \(1\in\mathbb N\), whereas an identity transformation has component \(0\).

Thus a classifier using only the complete transformation boundary also fails for general \(\beta\eta\)-normal terms.

These two statements have different scopes: the first uses only structural terms but has different endpoint pairs; the second has identical endpoints and uses a signature generator.

The side is determined by the **designated parameter presentation**:
\[
J\triangleright X\to Y
\quad\text{or}\quad
X\triangleleft J\to Y.
\]
Whether that parameter dependence is degenerate is additional information. These counterexamples do not provide a decision procedure that examines the entire term.

## 3. A conditional obstruction to position-rigidity

There is a zero-generating-transformation counterexample to Question 2 **if the probe stock includes the following quotient probe**. Item 19, as stated here, does not specify enough designated colimits to guarantee it.

Let
\[
M=\{1,e\},\qquad e^2=e,\quad e\ne1,
\]
and let \(K=B^2M\) be the strict \(2\)-category with:

- one \(0\)-cell;
- only its identity \(1\)-cell;
- \(M\) as its \(2\)-endomorphism monoid.

Horizontal and vertical composition are multiplication in \(M\); commutativity gives strict interchange.

Suppose \(K\) and its probe maps are available. For a data atom \(X\), set
\[
Y=K\triangleright X,\qquad
f=i\triangleright1_X:X\to Y,
\]
where \(i:I\to K\) selects its \(0\)-cell.

The probe map \(k:D_2\to K\) selecting \(e\) gives a modification
\[
z=k\triangleright1_X:1_f\Rightarrow1_f.
\]
Its boundaries are \(1_f\), and
\[
z^2=z.
\]
Moreover \(z\ne1_{1_f}\): interpreting \(X\) as the terminal strict \(2\)-category sends these modifications to the distinct \(2\)-cells \(e\) and \(1\) of \(K\).

Take the generating-transformation set to be empty. It is position-rigid vacuously, but its empty composite \(1_f\) has a nonidentity endomorphism. Under probe erasure, \(f\) maps to \(1_X\).

A category equivalent to a poset has singleton endomorphism monoids, since an equivalence is fully faithful. Hence this category cannot be equivalent to a product of schedule posets.

The probe \(K\) is available if the probe category is full and closed under all finite nonempty connected colimits. One construction is:

1. Coequalize the two boundary maps \(J\rightrightarrows D_2\), making the generating \(2\)-cell an endomorphism of one \(1\)-cell.
2. Push out along \(J\to I\), collapsing that \(1\)-cell to an identity. This gives \(B^2\mathbb N\).
3. Coequalize the maps \(D_2\rightrightarrows B^2\mathbb N\) selecting its generator and its square.

However, this is **not an unconditional counterexample under an unspecified collection of designated pasting colimits**. It also uses the boundary type \(K\triangleright X\). It does not settle the variant restricted to fixed pure formula boundaries and modifications generated exclusively by crossings of the stipulated transformation generators.
