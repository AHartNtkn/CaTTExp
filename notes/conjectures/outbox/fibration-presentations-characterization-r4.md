# Grothendieck constructions over free bases: when do the plain Gray-generator attaching maps exist?

*A self-contained problem. It assumes familiarity with (∞,ω)-categories, the Gray tensor product, polygraphs (computads) and Grothendieck constructions, and nothing else. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked.*

**What we are asking for.** The most general theorem about the **unaugmented** relative Gray-generator presentation of §2. Correctness is now automatic once the presentation is well defined (§3 item 16), so the whole question is an existence problem for positive attaching expressions. We want an exact criterion for their existence, valid in every dimension and checkable by a terminating procedure, or a proof that no terminating procedure exists. Existence of presentations, dimension bounds and a uniform presentation with auxiliary generators are settled (§3 items 12–15) and are not asked again. One more counterexample or special class is not what we want.

## 1. Setting

### 1.1 Marked (∞,ω)-categories and left cartesian fibrations

Work in Loubaton's model (*Categorical theory of (∞,ω)-categories*, arXiv 2406.05425).
- A **marked** (∞,ω)-category has a chosen class of positive-dimensional cells containing the equivalences and closed under composition. A^♯ marks every cell. D_n^t is the n-globe with its top cell marked. ⊗ is the marked Gray tensor.
- A map p : E → B is a **left cartesian fibration** (Def. 3.2.2.1) when it has the unique right lifting property against `K ⊗ {0} → K ⊗ [1]^♯` for `K ∈ {D_n^♭ : n ≥ 0} ∪ {D_n^t : n ≥ 1}`. Straightening (Thm 4.1.4.2) identifies left cartesian fibrations over B^♯ with functors B → ω (the (∞,ω)-category of (∞,ω)-categories). The fibration classifying T is written ∫T.

### 1.2 Polygraphs and localisation

- For a finite polygraph Ψ, `Free(Ψ)` is the free (∞,ω)-category on Ψ and `F(Ψ)` the free strict ω-category. Ψ is **homotopical** when the comparison Free(Ψ) → F(Ψ) is an equivalence. `F_m(Π)` is the free strict m-category on an m-polygraph Π.
- A marking is a set of generators. `J[S⁻¹]` is the localisation of J at a set S of cells.
- A **presentation** of a fibration p : E → B over a polygraph base Γ is a finite marked polygraph Ψ with a map to Γ and a set S of marked generators such that `Free(Ψ)[S⁻¹] → Free(Γ)` is equivalent to p.

### 1.3 Why it matters

A W-weighted limit of a diagram F is the marked lax limit of F ∘ p over the total space of the fibration p classifying W (Loubaton, Cor. 4.2.2.6, Cor. 4.2.3.18). If p has a finite presentation with a finite correctness certificate, a proof checker can accept the weighted limit as a marked lax limit over a finite index. So a theorem for all dimensions gives checkable weighted limits for every finite weight with free values over every homotopical finite base.

## 2. The expected answer

For a base n-polygraph Γ and a strict weight T : F(Γ) → strict ω-categories with each value T(b) = F_m(Π_b), Π_b finite and homotopical, the expected presentation is the **relative Gray-generator presentation**:
- the generators of every fibre Π_b over the base vertices b;
- for each base k-generator q (k ≥ 1) with source vertex b and each j-generator g of Π_b, one marked generator `q ⊗ g` of dimension k + j, with boundary given by the Leibniz formula `∂(q ⊗ g) = ∂q ⊗ g ± q ⊗ ∂g`, read through T;
- localisation at the generators q ⊗ g with j > 0.

So the presentation has dimension ≤ n + m.

## 3. What is known

1. **Localisation lemma.** If S is a set of marked cells of J and q : J → J[S⁻¹], restriction is an equivalence of (∞,ω)-categories of marked cones, natural in the apex.
2. **Tensor-localisation and mixed localisation.** For any set S of positive-dimensional cells, `X[S⁻¹] ⊗ I ≃ (X ⊗ I)[{s ⊗ 0, s ⊗ 1, s ⊗ i}⁻¹]`. For finite homotopical X and Y there is a finite cellular model 𝓡_Y(X), natural in X, with one generator x ⊗ y per pair of generators, and `𝓡_Y(X)[(x ⊗ y)⁻¹ : dim x, dim y > 0] ≃ X × Y`.
3. **Bases of dimension ≤ 2.** For every finite homotopical 2-polygraph base and fibres F_m(Π_b) with Π_b finite homotopical, the expected presentation is correct. Proof: for a base path p = e₁; …; e_r the map δ_r : I → [r] (0 ↦ 0, 1 ↦ r) and A ⊗ [r] ≃ (A ⊗ I) ∐_A ⋯ ∐_A (A ⊗ I) give a map from the cylinder of T(p) into the **unlocalised** graph presentation; attach a double Gray cylinder block for each base 2-generator along these maps; conclude with Loubaton's theorem that pullback preserves colimits (Thm 3.2.3.15, Cor. 3.2.3.16).
4. **Bases of dimension 3.** For a finite 3-polygraph base Γ such that Γ and its 2-skeleton are homotopical (more precisely: the 2-skeleton is homotopical and attaching the 3-generators is a homotopy pushout), the expected presentation is correct for every fibre dimension m, with generators of dimension ≤ m + 3. The 3-generators of the base are handled by left and right whiskering subdivisions of the outer interval, which reduce each 3-generator to a cylinder over its 2-dimensional boundary data, where item 3 applies.
5. **Suspension bases.** For Q = F_d(Λ) with Λ finite homotopical and base ΣQ, the expected presentation is correct for every fibre dimension, via K = 1^co ⋆ Q and the suspension-base classification (Loubaton, Cor. 4.1.2.19).
6. **The obstacle in base dimension ≥ 4.** Gluing the local totals over the base generators gives the right generators. But the attaching maps are first obtained in the **localised** category Free(Ψ)[S⁻¹], while adjoining generators to Ψ needs attaching maps in Free(Ψ) itself, and such lifts need not exist (in Free(s)[s⁻¹] the inverse of s exists; in Free(s) no arrow goes back). For bases of dimension ≤ 3 the lifts were constructed by hand, by subdividing cylinders along composites in the boundary of each generator. For base dimension ≥ 4 what is missing is a **compatible** choice of such lifts for all higher-dimensional boundary expressions at once.
7. **A finite certificate exists for the proved cases:** homotopicality certificates for the fibres and base attachments, the weight data, the attaching expressions, the generator list indexed by base–fibre pairs, the marking and localisation lists, and face-compatibility checks on the intersections of the cylinders.
8. **Homotopicality of the base is needed.** For the base (x; σ : 1_x ⇒ 1_x), F(Γ) = B²ℕ groupoidifies to K(ℤ,2), while every Free(Ψ)[S⁻¹] with Ψ finite groupoidifies to a finite CW complex. So even the terminal weight has no finite presentation.
9. **The expected presentation fails from base dimension 4.** Base Γ: one vertex b, a : b → b, μ : aa ⇒ a, an associator α : (μ₁;μ₀) ⇛ (μ₀;μ₀) (words in right-to-left order; a subscript is the position of the rewrite), and a pentagon 4-generator P. Fibre Π: x, g : x → x, δ : g ⇒ gg, κ : (δ₀;δ₁) ⇛ (δ₀;δ₀). Weight: T(b) = F₃(Π), T(a) = Z the constant functor at x, T(μ)_x = g, T(α)_x = δ, T(P)_x = κ.
   - Both polygraphs and all their skeleta are homotopical: Π is the oriental polygraph of the semisimplicial set with one simplex in each dimension ≤ 3, and Γ is the even dual of the one for dimension ≤ 4 (Chanavat, arXiv 2603.22242, Thm 3.35).
   - With the fibre-first Leibniz convention ∂(g ⊗ q) = ∂g ⊗ q + (−1)^{|g|} g ⊗ ∂q, the mixed generators are t = a ⊗ x, c : t ⇒ tg, u : gtt ⇒ t, and the 3-generators D = a ⊗ δ, M = μ ⊗ g : (u₀;c₀) ⇛ (c₂;u₀), V = α ⊗ x : (c₁;u₂;u₀) ⇛ (δ₀;u₁;u₀), together with κ.
   - The 2-cells gt⁴ ⇒ t are 8 (18 rewriting paths up to interchange). Every contextual instance of a 3-generator gives exactly 8 edges. Over the short side R → E → L of the pentagon, the only V-step out of the lift of R lands at a vertex with no outgoing edge. So the target face of P ⊗ x does not exist in the unlocalised category: it would need M⁻¹, which exists only after localisation. No weak attaching map can repair this, since it would project to the missing strict 3-cell.
   - Reversing M repairs this pentagon but changes M's Gray boundary.
10. **Finite presentations exist, with auxiliary generators.** For every finite polygraph Γ with homotopical skeleta, and every (∞,ω)-functor T : F(Γ) → ω whose values have finite weak polygraphic presentations (localisations allowed), ∫T has a presentation (Free(Ψ), ⟨Q ∪ S⟩)[S⁻¹] with Ψ finite and homotopical and Q, S finite sets of positive-dimensional generators, keeping the cartesian marking. The proof gives no dimension bound and no control of the generators. It goes through finite complicial models: closure under finite homotopy pushouts and products, and a conversion L_d of a finite stratified simplicial set into a finite semisimplicial set whose oriental polygraph is homotopical. It then inducts over the base cells with Loubaton's pullback–colimit theorem and the suspension classification for globes. For weights whose values are ≃ F(Π_b), the weight can moreover be made strict, since the homs of ω between gaunt strict categories are gaunt.
11. **Homotopicality of the base passes to skeleta.** The inclusion F(Γ_{≤r}) ↪ F(Γ) is discrete Conduché (the generator support of a strict cell is well defined and additive), so pulling back along it preserves colimits (Loubaton Thm 2.2.2.10), and the comparison for Γ_{≤r} is a pullback of the comparison for Γ. Hence Γ is homotopical iff all its skeleta are.
12. **Every pair has a presentation of dimension ≤ n + m, and n + m is optimal.** For Γ of dimension n and weights with values Free(Π_b)[W_b⁻¹], dim Π_b ≤ m, there is a presentation of dimension ≤ n + m. Proof: a bounded relative realization lemma for finite complicial models (thin markings reflected from the fibrant target after each horn stage), a dimension calculus (homotopy pushouts by double mapping cylinders give max{b, c, a + 1}, products add dimensions, globe attachments keep dimension), totals over globes of dimension m + k, and a conversion L_D with (L_D K)_r = K_r × Inj([r], [D]) whose oriental polygraph is homotopical (Chanavat, Thm 3.35). Optimality: Γ = ∂D_{n+1}, Π = ∂D_{m+1}, constant weight; the total groupoidifies to Sⁿ × S^m. In particular item 9's example has a presentation of dimension ≤ 7.
13. **A uniform presentation Ω.** For every finite homotopical Γ and every coherent weight with values Free(Π_b)[W_b⁻¹] there is a finite marked polygraph Ω with dim Ω ≤ n + m and a finite localisation set S_Ω with Free(Ω)[S_Ω⁻¹] ≃ ∫T over B^♯, such that each Π_b is a literal subpolygraph, and each base k-generator q and fibre j-generator g give a designated cartesian-marked generator z_{q,g} of dimension k + j (localised when j > 0) whose 0-endpoints are the original fibre vertices. **The positive-dimensional proper faces of z_{q,g} are auxiliary copies**, related to the required composites by explicitly localised comparison cylinders. Ω need not be homotopical. Key lemma: adjoining fresh globes along two prescribed vertices, with no positive-dimensional identifications, preserves homotopicality.
14. **Coherent weights and non-free values** are covered by item 13, with finite roofs for the transport data.
15. **A positive semi-decision procedure.** Equivalence of two specified finite complicial models (over a fibrant base, preserving and reflecting the cartesian marking) has a finite certificate: finite anodyne extensions L ↪ A, K ↪ U, A ↪ V, maps f : K → A, g : A → U, h : U → V and finite cylinders witnessing gf ≃ i, hg ≃ j. So once attaching expressions for all q ⊗ g in the unlocalised Free(Ψ) are supplied, correctness is certifiable, and enumerating expressions and certificates semi-decides "the unaugmented presentation is well defined and correct". What is missing is a **bound on the positive mixed-generator expressions** to search. Additivity of base-generator support bounds the top base lifts, but not the lower mixed steps. No undecidability reduction is known either.

16. **Well defined ⇒ correct.** Suppose every q ⊗ g has an *actual globular Gray attaching map* in the unlocalised stages. This means positive expressions in Free(Ψ) realising the Gray pasting ∂D_{j+k} → (D_j ⊗ ∂D_k) ∐_{∂D_j ⊗ ∂D_k} (∂D_j ⊗ D_k) read through T, including the compositions with previously adjoined q ⊗ h. Equality of boundaries alone, or equality only in the final total, does not count. Then Free(Ψ)[S⁻¹] ≃ ∫T as marked (∞,ω)-categories over F(Γ)^♯, with S = {q ⊗ g : dim q, dim g > 0}. Proof outline:
    - (a) *Gaunt totals.* A left or right cartesian fibration over a gaunt base with gaunt vertex fibres has gaunt total. This goes by induction on globe dimension through the hom-fibrations, which are cartesian of the opposite variance (Loubaton Prop. 3.2.2.6, Thm 3.2.2.24).
    - (b) *Rigid marked factors.* Marked factors are determined by their projection and their distinguished face (unique lifting against D_{d−1} → D_d^t). So any positive representative of an old marked factor has its canonical image in the completed lower total E_{k−1}.
    - (c) *Triangular boundary.* The Leibniz boundary of q ⊗ g uses old-stage expressions together with earlier q ⊗ h (dim h < dim g), and its dependence on the latter is fixed by the fibre attaching map ⊗ id_{D_k}.
    - (d) *Comparison after base change.* Each new relative cellular stage is compared with the local presentation over ∂D_k ↪ D_k (a suspension base) after base change to E_{k−1}. No compatible unlocalised map from a boundary block is needed. Then localise, and finish with homotopicality of the base skeleta and pullback–colimit compatibility.
    So different choices of positive expressions give equivalent presentations. The finite certificate consists of the attaching expressions plus a check that each realises its Gray pasting through T; no separate equivalence certificate is needed.

## 4. Questions

**Question 1 (main: decide existence of the attaching expressions).** Given a finite homotopical polygraph Γ and a strict weight T with finite homotopical fibres Π_b, is it decidable whether every mixed generator q ⊗ g admits an actual globular Gray attaching map (item 16) by positive expressions in the unlocalised stages? By item 16 this is exactly the question whether the unaugmented presentation is well defined and correct. Either give a terminating procedure with proof, ideally as an exact criterion on (Γ, T) valid in every dimension, or prove undecidability by a reduction. Possible forms of a positive answer:
- a bound on the length of the mixed-generator rewriting paths that must be searched, derived from the dimensions and supports of base and fibre generators;
- a criterion stated directly on the strict Gray tensor of each base generator with the weight, e.g. that every face of the Leibniz boundary is reachable by positive (unlocalised) moves in the strict free category on the stage below.
The reduction for undecidability could go from reachability in positive rewriting systems, encoded in the base associativity data as in item 9. Item 9 shows that the expressions can fail to exist because a needed step is the inverse of a localised generator.

**Question 2 (conditions on the base alone).** Is there a condition on Γ alone, checkable on finite data and valid in every dimension, under which the attaching expressions exist for **every** strict weight with free homotopical values? Item 9 shows that this is not automatic from base dimension 4, even for oriental bases. If such a condition exists, prove necessity and sufficiency. If the only bases that work for every weight are those covered by items 3–5, prove that.

**Question 3 (bases that are not homotopical, read weakly).** Drop the homotopicality hypothesis on the base by taking the weak base Free(Γ) instead of F(Γ). Let Γ be any finite n-polygraph, and T : Free(Γ) → ω a coherent weight whose values are Free(Π_b)[W_b⁻¹] with Π_b finite and dim Π_b ≤ m. Prove that ∫T → Free(Γ)^♯ has a finite marked presentation of dimension ≤ n + m, or give a counterexample. Item 8's obstruction does not apply: over Free(σ) for the bubble σ : 1_x ⇒ 1_x, the total of the terminal weight is Free(σ) itself. A proposed route:
- (i) **Resolve the base.** Free(Γ) is an iterated homotopy pushout of globes by definition, so the bounded-model calculus behind item 12 gives a finite complicial model of dimension ≤ n. The conversion L_n then gives a finite homotopical polygraph Γ′ (the oriental of a semisimplicial set, Chanavat Thm 3.35) of dimension ≤ n, together with a finite set W′ of generators such that Free(Γ′)[W′⁻¹] ≃ Free(Γ).
- (ii) **Present over the resolution.** Apply item 13 to T ∘ ℓ, where ℓ : F(Γ′) → Free(Γ).
- (iii) **Localise the total.** Localise the result at the cartesian lifts z_{w,x} (w ∈ W′, x a source-fibre vertex).

Step (iii) needs the following lemma, which is the precise question:

> **Lemma (totals over a localisation).** For an (∞,ω)-category B, a set S of cells, ℓ : B → B[S⁻¹] and T : B[S⁻¹] → ω, the map ∫(T ∘ ℓ) → ∫T exhibits ∫T as the localisation of ∫(T ∘ ℓ) at the cartesian lifts of the cells of S.

One reduction: B[S⁻¹] is a pushout of B along marked trivialisations ∐ D_{k+1}^t → ∐ D_k^♭, and pullback along left cartesian fibrations preserves colimits (Loubaton Thm 3.2.3.15). So it suffices to prove the lemma for a single trivialisation D_{k+1}^t → D_k^♭, where it should follow from item 2. Please prove the lemma (or give a reference), and confirm or repair steps (i)–(iii).

## 5. What counts as a solution

A terminating recognition procedure with proof, or an undecidability proof, for Question 1; an exact base criterion, or a proof that none exists beyond items 3–5, for Question 2; a proof or counterexample for Question 3; or a published reference.

## Researcher Response 1

### Question 3. Localising the base at a cell localises the total at its lifts at fibre objects

Write \(E_U=\int(T|_U)\) for the pullback of the classified fibration to a base \(U\to B[S^{-1}]\). For a cell \(s:D_k\to B\) and an object \(x\) of its source fibre, let \(s_x\) denote the cartesian generator at \(x\) of the globe total, mapped into \(E_B\). Then
\[
 E_B[\{s_x:s\in S,\ x\in\operatorname{Ob}T(\ell(s_0s))\}^{-1}]
       \simeq E_{B[S^{-1}]}
\]
over \(B[S^{-1}]\), with the induced cartesian marking. One may take representatives of the equivalence classes of source-fibre objects. In particular this gives a finite marked presentation of dimension at most \(n+m\) in Question 3. The final two sections give a terminating search for marked strict 2-cell factors and a decomposition result for Question 2.

**Products and localisation.** If \(K\to L=K[V^{-1}]\) is a localisation, \(A\times K\to A\times L\) localises at the copies \((1_x,v)\) for objects \(x\) of \(A\) and \(v\in V\). Here \(1_x\) denotes the iterated identity in the dimension of \(v\). To see this using marked categories, \((K,\langle V\rangle)\to L^\flat\) is a pushout of the coproduct of marked trivialisations \(D_d^t\to D_{d-1}^\flat\), one per marked generator of dimension \(d\). These trivialisations are final, and final maps are closed under colimits and cobase change. Product with \(A^\flat\) preserves final maps, and localisation at the marking sends a final map to an equivalence. The product target has minimal marking, so localising the product source gives \(A\times L\). The marking on the source product consists of pairs \((e,w)\) with \(e\) an equivalence and \(w\) marked. Horizontal composition through a corner of the product expresses such a pair, up to the unit coherences, as \((e,1)\) followed by \((1,w)\), taking the identities at the appropriate vertices. The first factor is an equivalence. At a fixed vertex of \(A\), inversion of \(V\) inverts the marking it generates in \(K\). Thus the displayed objectwise copies suffice. Equivalent choices of \(x\) give equivalent inclusions of \(K\) in the product. [Loubaton, Propositions 3.2.1.7 and 3.2.1.11, Lemma 3.2.5.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425)

**The globe calculation.** Localising the top cell of \(D_k\) gives the collapse \(r:D_k\to D_{k-1}\). Indeed, a map out of \(D_k\) selects a \(k\)-cell, and the space of equivalences in each iterated hom is equivalent, by evaluation at one endpoint, to the space of its objects. The inverse assigns an identity. For \(k=1\) the pullback of a weight from \(D_0\) is constant with value \(A\). Its comparison of totals is \(A\times D_1\to A\), which localises at the arrows over the top cell at the objects of \(A\) by the product calculation.

For \(k\geq2\), write \(r=\Sigma u\) with \(u:C=D_{k-1}\to C'=D_{k-2}\), and denote the top cell of \(C\) by \(\sigma\). The two pole fibres are \(A,H\). The weight on \(\Sigma C'\) has evaluation \(h':A\times C'\to H\), so the one on \(\Sigma C\) has evaluation \(h=h'(1\times u)\). Put \(J(C)=1^{\mathrm{co}}\star C\). Naturality of the suspension classification gives the following comparison of totals on underlying categories:
\[
 (A\times J(C))\amalg_{A\times C} H
 \longrightarrow
 (A\times J(C'))\amalg_{A\times C'} H.
\]
The copy of \(C\) here is the uncollapsed endpoint of the Gray cylinder defining \(J(C)\). [Loubaton, Construction 2.3.1.21, Theorem 3.2.4.9 and Corollary 4.1.2.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425)

Item 2 applied to \(u\otimes I\) gives the three cells \(\sigma\otimes0,\sigma\otimes1,\sigma\otimes i\) to invert. Collapsing the zero endpoint gives \(J(C)\to J(C')\) as localisation at the surviving endpoint cell \(\sigma\) and the cone cell \(\kappa_\sigma\). This follows by mapping the endpoint pushout to any target, since the collapsed endpoint already inverts its copy of \(\sigma\). Apply the product calculation to both \(u\) and \(J(u)\). In the left-hand total every \((1_x,\sigma)\) already maps to an equivalence through \(h\), since \(h\) factors through \(A\times C'\). Thus, by the mapping-space universal property of the two pushouts, precisely the inversion conditions on \((1_x,\kappa_\sigma)\) suffice to extend a map out of the left-hand total to the right. These cells are the cartesian globe lifts at the source-fibre objects, by the suspension classification. This proves the assertion for one collapse. It also proves it with the cartesian markings retained. The comparison preserves those markings, and the pullback along a face section of \(r\) gives a marking-preserving section of the comparison. Consequently a map extending a marked map on the source preserves every mark on the target.

**Passage to the base.** Form the homotopy pushout of \(B\leftarrow\coprod_{s\in S}D_{|s|}\to\coprod_{s\in S}D_{|s|-1}\). The globe calculation for mapping spaces identifies its base with \(B[S^{-1}]\). Pullback of the classified fibration over that pushout gives a pushout of the marked globe totals with \(E_B\). A marked map out of \(E_B\) extends across it exactly when it inverts all the displayed globe lifts, and its space of extensions is then contractible. This proves the lemma. [Loubaton, Theorem 3.2.3.15 and Corollary 3.2.3.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425#page=156)

**Finite dimension and the proposed resolution.** Build the finite complicial model of the weak base by its defining globe attachments. At rank \(r\), the two inputs of the attachment have dimensions at most \(r-1\) and \(r\), and its attaching sphere has dimension at most \(r-1\). Item 12's relative realisation and double mapping cylinder therefore retain the bound \(r\). Apply its \(L_n\) conversion and mark the generators corresponding to thin simplices. Conversion gives a localisation \(\ell:F(\Gamma')\to\mathrm{Free}(\Gamma)\) with \(\dim\Gamma'\le n\), finite inversion set \(W'\), and \(\Gamma'\) homotopical by the semisimplicial pasting theorem. Every object of the weak base is equivalent to a generating vertex: the full subcategory on those vertices contains every generating globe, so the universal property of the free construction factors its identity through that subcategory. Thus each value of \(T\ell\), including at vertices introduced by the resolution, admits a fibre presentation of dimension at most \(m\). [Chanavat, Theorem 3.35](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22242)

Apply item 13 over \(F(\Gamma')\). In its resulting presentation, adjoin to the inversion set the finitely many designated \(z_{w,x}\), for \(w\in W'\) and \(x\) a presenting vertex of its source fibre. Such vertices represent all fibre objects up to equivalence, since forming the free category and then localising introduces no new object classes. The lemma identifies the result, with its marking, with \(\int T\). Enlarging the inversion set keeps the dimension at most \(n+m\).

One can check the bound directly on the original weak skeleta. At rank zero the total is a finite coproduct of the fibre models of dimension at most \(m\). The total over a \(k\)-globe has a model of dimension at most \(m+k\) by the globe calculation of item 12, and its boundary total has dimension at most \(m+k-1\) by induction on the boundary's skeleta. Pullback preserves the defining pushout of the weak base, so the double mapping cylinder for the total has dimension at most \(\max\{m+k-1,m+k,(m+k-1)+1\}=m+k\). This check also covers the weak bubble base.

### Question 1. A finite search for marked strict 2-cell factors

Fix a stage of §2, two specified 1-arrows in its strict free category and a specified base 2-cell \(\beta\). There is a terminating search for **all marked strict 2-cells between those arrows projecting to \(\beta\)**. In particular, erasing or length-increasing transport does not obstruct termination at this dimension.

A marked expression can be written as a sequence of whiskered generating 2-cells. This follows by expanding compositions into single-generator steps using interchange. The only possible steps are an object lift of a base 2-generator or an interchange square for a base edge and a fibre edge. Indeed, the cartesian marking at this stage is generated by these cells, with identities, compositions and whiskerings. Read the sequence backwards from the specified target 1-arrow. Every step of the first kind spends one unit of a budget initially equal to the number of generating 2-cells in \(\beta\), counted with multiplicity. Projection sends it to one base occurrence and sends every interchange square to an identity. This budget holds for every writing by additivity.

For steps of the second kind, write intermediate 1-arrows in traversal order. In the fibre-first convention the backward square replaces a fibre letter \(f\) followed by the lift of \(a\) with a lift of \(a\) followed by the word \(T(a)f\). Let \(K=2+\max_{a,f}|T(a)f|\), taking the maximum to be zero for an empty list. Give a fibre letter with \(d\) base edges still ahead of it the weight \(K^d\), and let \(P\) be the sum over fibre letters. A backward square consumes \(K^d\) and contributes at most \((K-2)K^{d-1}\), with \(d\ge1\). All other summands are unchanged. This includes the case of an empty transport word. A backward object lift can change \(P\) but decreases the first budget. Thus the lexicographic pair (remaining budget, \(P\)) decreases at every step.

Every word has finitely many cuts and the list of rules is finite, so this well-founded, finitely branching search has a finite tree. Retain the paths with the specified source and projection. Equality with \(\beta\) can itself be checked by adjacent exchanges of independent steps. Its exchange class is finite since the initial word, step count and finite set of base rules bound all intermediate word lengths. This proves both termination and completeness for these factors.

Transport multiplicities really enter the problem. Take a triangle with edges \(a:0\to1\), \(b:1\to2\), \(c:0\to2\) and a 2-generator \(ba\Rightarrow c\). Give its three vertices free one-loop fibres generated by \(g,h,v\). Set \(T(a)g=h^N\), \(T(b)h=v\), \(T(c)g=v^N\) and let the 2-generator act by the identity transformation, for \(N>0\). In the face of its prism on \(g\) over the path \(ba\), a backward square at \(b\) increases the number of \(v\)'s by exactly one. No other step can change that count. The projection is an identity on \(ba\), hence no object lift of the triangle can occur. The endpoints have zero and \(N\) occurrences of \(v\), respectively, so every writing uses exactly \(N\) such squares. The polygraphs and the sets of letters used by the transports are fixed throughout the family.

The rank used here depends on the linear order of edges in a 1-arrow. In the next search dimension the intermediate states are 2-pastings, and a marked 3-step can change the pasting along which another step would have to be ranked. The argument gives neither a bound in that case nor a reduction proving undecidability of actual Gray-face recognition.

### Question 2. Bases assembled along vertices

Let \(U(\Gamma)\) mean that every admissible weight has all the attachments of item 16. Suppose a finite homotopical base is a union of two subpolygraphs \(A,C\) meeting in a single vertex \(b\). Then
\[
                 U(\Gamma)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad U(A)\ \text{and}\ U(C).
\]
Hence the three classes in items 3–5 do not exhaust the universally good connected bases.

First fix a weight on the union. Given attaching expressions on both pieces, adjoin them in the stage order of item 16. At the intersection the stages share precisely the given fibre polygraph at \(b\). Every base face of a generator in one piece belongs to that piece, and its proper-fibre-face substitution uses earlier pairs over that same generator. Each witness therefore remains an actual Gray pasting in the union. This proves the gluing implication.

For restriction, use hereditary support in the strict free base. This support retains all generators of a writing, their iterated boundaries and their vertices. The support of a composite is the union of the supports, and an identity has the support of its underlying cell. The strict axioms preserve these rules, including the unit laws because the support of a face is contained in that of the cell. Thus if a positive pasting projects into \(A\), each of its constituent labels projects into \(A\). In particular a mixed label over \(q\) projects to an iterated identity on \(q\), whose hereditary support still contains \(q\). A fibre label projects to its vertex. Apply this observation at each stage to the positive expressions and their prescribed factors for generators of \(A\). They use only labels over \(A\), and their fibre-boundary substitutions and Gray face identifications restrict with them.

There is also a categorical check on the stage used in this restriction. The inclusion \(F(A)\hookrightarrow F(\Gamma)\) is discrete Conduché by the same support calculation for units and factorisations. Its pullback preserves cellular pushouts. For a generating disk over \(A\) it retains the whole attachment. For any other generating disk the pullback of its boundary inclusion is an equivalence, since the only cell of a globe outside its boundary, up to identities, is its top cell, and that cell projects outside \(A\). Starting with the vertex fibres, one obtains exactly the unlocalised stages with the retained labels. This verifies that the restricted expressions live in the stages required by item 16. Applying the same argument to the weak base attachments also gives homotopicality of the subpolygraphs from homotopicality of \(\Gamma\). [Subpolygraph pullback lemma, §1](../answered/fibration-presentations-all-dimensions-2026-11-19.md) and [Loubaton, Theorem 2.2.2.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425)

Finally, any admissible weight on \(A\) extends across \(C\) by the constant weight with value its fibre at \(b\). The assignments agree at \(b\) and define a strict weight by freeness. Restriction of witnesses for this extension proves \(U(\Gamma)\Rightarrow U(A)\), and interchanging the pieces proves the other necessity. For a concrete connected example, adjoin one incoming edge at the source vertex of \(D_4\). The resulting globular sum \([1]\vee D_4\) is homotopical, and both pieces have \(U\) by items 3 and 5. It has dimension four and three vertices, while a suspension has two poles as its only vertices.

Iterating this equivalence reduces the universal test to the pieces that admit no further vertex separation. Termination or undecidability for general Gray-face recognition and a criterion for universal validity on those remaining pieces are still open here.

## Researcher Response 2

### A terminating search for the marked strict 3-cell factors on positive base 2-pastings

Fix the prescribed strict 3-stage, a base 3-cell \(\gamma\), and a total 2-cell \(U\) over its 2-source. Suppose each base 2-generator in the hereditary support of \(\gamma\) has a nonempty source path and a nonempty target path. Then **all marked strict 3-cells with source \(U\) and projection \(\gamma\) can be enumerated by a terminating procedure**. The 2-target may be left unspecified. The fibre 2-generators may have arbitrary boundaries, and transport may erase them or send them to arbitrarily large fixed composites. This applies, in particular, to the strict lifting obstruction in item 9.

Work in the subpolygraph on the hereditary support, using restriction as in Response 1. Write a 2-cell as a sequence of whiskered generating 2-cells, with 1-arrows in traversal order. Its base projection is a sequence of string rewrites. We call an occurrence of a base edge in a current string a *wire*. A base rewrite consumes its input wires and produces fresh output wires, even if the input and output happen to have the same spelling. Two integer ranks on a total 2-cell will be used between applications of object lifts of base 3-generators.

**Widths and levels.** Initially each wire has width one and level zero. At each base 2-generator application, divide the combined width of its inputs equally among its outputs. Give each output level one greater than the maximum level of its inputs. Wires outside the rewrite retain both labels. Pure fibre 2-cells and crossings of a fibre edge with a base edge induce identity rewrites in the base. At these steps keep the labels on the continuing wires. The path hypotheses ensure that every width is positive and every level is defined.

Let \(N\) be the number of base rewrites in this 2-cell, and let \(b\) be the maximum of one and the base output arities in the support. Scale all widths by \((b!)^N\). Every scaled width is a positive integer, since each of the at most \(N\) successive divisions is by a divisor of \(b!\). For a pure fibre 2-generator occurrence, let \(d\) be the sum of the scaled widths of the wires still ahead of it in traversal order. Its whole rewriting window projects to a single cut of the base string.

Choose integers \(K,H\ge2\). Define
\[
 A=\sum_{\text{pure fibre 2-occurrences}} K^d,
 \qquad
 C=\sum_{\text{fibre-edge crossings}} H^{\text{level of crossed wire}} .
\]
Both sums are invariants of the strict 2-cell. To check this, exchange two disjoint steps in a writing. Two disjoint base rewrites assign the same labels to their outputs in either order. A base rewrite disjoint from a crossed wire preserves that wire's level. If it takes place ahead of a pure fibre occurrence, it preserves the **sum** of the widths ahead of the occurrence. A rewrite behind it leaves that sum unchanged. Two steps projecting to identities preserve all wire labels. These checks include disjoint windows sharing an endpoint. They suffice since exchange generates the equivalence of sequential writings.

**Decrease at a 3-generator.** In the fibre-first convention the two mixed cases have different effects on these ranks. They can be read on the two generating prisms before substituting paths for their boundaries.

- For a base edge \(a\) and a fibre 2-generator \(\rho\), the source of the prism has the pure occurrence \(\rho\) on the source side of the \(a\)-wire. Its target has the occurrences in \(T(a)\rho\) on the target side. The remaining 2-steps are crossings for the two 1-boundaries of \(\rho\). This is the cylinder naturality 3-cell. Choose \(K\) greater than every top-generator count \(|T(a)\rho|_2\). The contribution \(K^d\) is replaced by at most \(|T(a)\rho|_2K^{d-w}\), where \(w\ge1\) is the scaled width of that wire. Thus \(A\) decreases.
- For a base 2-generator \(\mu:p\Rightarrow t\) and a fibre edge \(f\), the prism compares the object lift of \(\mu\) and the crossings of \(f\) along \(t\), on the source face, with the crossings of \(f\) along \(p\) and the object lift of \(\mu\), on the target face. The vertical component of \(T(\mu)\) is a 1-arrow. Strict naturality supplies the equality of the resulting 1-arrows, so there is no pure fibre 2-generator on either face. Along a nonempty path the first edge is crossed by \(f\) itself, even if its subsequent transport is an identity. Thus at least one crossing on an output wire of \(\mu\) is consumed. All crossings introduced are on input wires of that same occurrence of \(\mu\). Choose \(H\) greater than the maximum number of crossings on any of these finitely many target faces. If the maximum input level is \(h\), the source contributes at least \(H^{h+1}\) and the target contributes at most \((H-1)H^h\). Thus \(C\) decreases while \(A\) stays fixed.

The face descriptions include the positions relative to the base rewrite. They follow by reading the two proper-base faces of the Gray prism as the successive crossings along the indicated paths, and the two proper-fibre faces as its vertex lifts. They specialize to \(D\) and \(M\) in item 9. In either case whiskering the prism preserves the comparison. Steps outside it keep the same base labels and cuts, and the projected 2-pasting has the same \(N\) on both sides. [Explicit prism in the pentagon calculation, §1.3](../answered/fibration-presentations-all-dimensions-2026-11-19.md)

**Search.** Start at \(U\) with budget \(|\gamma|_3\), the number of top base-generator occurrences counted with multiplicity. Only an object lift of a base 3-generator spends one unit. It may change the projected 2-pasting and both ranks. Every marked 3-step therefore decreases the lexicographic triple
\[
                 (\text{remaining budget},A,C).
\]
There are finitely many possible next steps and these can be listed effectively. A fixed 2-cell has finitely many sequential writings up to the explicit choices of step positions. Exchanges preserve the number of steps, and that number and the initial word bound all intermediate word lengths. In these writings a contextual generator application is a replacement of its consecutive source steps with fixed left and right word contexts. Indeed, a single generating 3-cell whiskered by a 2-cell horizontally can first be whiskered by a boundary word, with the remaining disjoint 2-steps moved into the vertical context by interchange. Vertical whiskering directly supplies the prefix and suffix. Empty step lists can be matched at each of the finitely many cuts. This lists all whiskered generators, and serial composition of these gives all marked 3-cells.

The search tree is consequently finite. Keep paths with exactly the specified projection, and filter by a target 2-cell if one is prescribed. Equality of given writings in the free strict base is decidable by the free-category word problem. [Ara et al., *Polygraphs*, Appendix D.3](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~yves.guiraud/articles/polybook.pdf)

For the eight 2-cells in the pentagon calculation, taking \(K=2,H=8\) gives the same rank on each of the eighteen writings as on its interchange class. With unit input widths the \(D\)-edge changes \((A,C)\) from \((2,1)\) to \((0,2)\), and the \(M\)-edge changes it from \((1,8)\) to \((1,1)\). All base output arities in this example are one, so the scaling factor is one.

This proves a bound for the strict 3-cell search from one specified face. At a base 2-generator with empty input, positive conserved widths cannot be assigned by this construction. With empty output, the second prism may consume no output crossing. In dimension four a fixed projected 3-cell can relate distinct wire subdivisions of the same boundary. A terminating recognition procedure for the full weak attaching problem in Question 1 and a general criterion for Question 2 remain to be established.

## Researcher Response 3

### Reduction of both questions to connected fibres

Write \(A(Γ,T)\) for existence in Question 1. There is an effective transformation
\[
                  A(Γ,T)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A(Γ_X,V)
\]
in which every fibre of \(V\) is nonempty and connected. It preserves the individual unlocalised attaching expressions, including the proper-fibre faces. In particular a decision procedure for connected fibres in all dimensions would decide Question 1. There is also a restriction on any answer to Question 2. If \(Γ\) works for all weights, so does **the polygraph \(Γ_X\) of every finite set action on it**, even for weights on \(Γ_X\) that distinguish different lifts of the same generator of \(Γ\).

**The change of base.** A finite set action \(X\) assigns a finite set to each vertex of \(Γ\) and a function to each generating edge. The two boundary paths of each generating 2-cell must induce the same function. These are finite checks. Equivalently \(X\) is a functor from the ordinary category of base paths modulo the 2-cell relations to finite sets. Let \(B_X\) have objects \((b,i)\) for \(i\in X(b)\). A positive-dimensional cell of \(B_X\) is a cell of \(F(Γ)\) together with an index at its 0-source. Following a 1-path transports the index by \(X\). Parallel 1-faces of a higher cell transport it to the same index. This defines every face, identity and composition in \(B_X\).

Form \(Γ_X\) with one generator \((q,i)\) over each generator \(q\), for each index at its source vertex. For a vertex \(q\) itself use \(X(q)\). Lift the given boundary writings by transporting the index. Then \(F(Γ_X)=B_X\) up to the indicated naming of cells. Every strict writing lifts from its initial index, and every unit, associativity or interchange equation lifts with that same index. Conversely, forgetting indices takes each equation to the corresponding equation downstairs. Both the generation and the equality assertions follow.

**The homotopicality promise.** The projection \(B_X\to F(Γ)\) is discrete Conduché. In a lifting problem for a unit or a composition the specified lift of the result gives its initial index. The indices throughout the unit or the composable pair are now unique, by the cell description above. Thus pullback preserves colimits. Pull back the defining weak cellular construction of \(\mathrm{Free}(Γ)\) over \(F(Γ)\). For a generating \(k\)-globe, \(k>0\), let \(I,J\) be the sets at its two poles and \(f:I\to J\) its transport. Its pullback consists of one globe for each \(i\in I\), with its terminal vertex attached to \(f(i)\) in the discrete set \(J\). The same description holds for its boundary. In particular all of \(J\) is retained, including vertices outside the image of \(f\). [Loubaton, Definition 2.2.2.2 and Theorem 2.2.2.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425)

These descriptions also compute the weak gluings at the terminal vertices. On each globular sum, a nonconstant diagram in the pullback globe uses exactly one source index. Indeed the two poles of the parameter globe are distinct, and a composable string can cross between them only once. Constant diagrams give precisely the stated vertex identifications. The same argument applies to the boundary, with a discrete pair for \(k=1\). Hence the pushout description at the vertices holds levelwise in the Θ-nerve. It is a homotopy pushout there since the inclusion of the terminal vertices in the disjoint union of the copies is injective at each level. Passing from the pulled-back boundary to the pulled-back globe consequently attaches one \(k\)-cell per \(i\), with the lifted boundary already described. Rank zero gives the vertices \((b,i)\). Induction identifies the pulled-back construction with \(\mathrm{Free}(Γ_X)\). Pulling back the comparison equivalence for \(Γ\) now gives \(\mathrm{Free}(Γ_X)\simeq B_X\).

**Correspondence before localisation.** For any strict weight \(V\) on \(B_X\) form a strict weight \(T\) on \(F(Γ)\) by setting
\[
                        T(b)=\coprod_{i\in X(b)} V(b,i).
\]
On an edge its restriction to summand \(i\) is the functor for the edge lifted from \(i\), valued in the transported summand. Higher transformations are specified on each summand by the corresponding lifted cell. This is strict since the lift of a composite is the composite of its lifts. The fibre polygraph is the disjoint union of the summand polygraphs, so finiteness and homotopicality are preserved.

Identify a pure generator of a summand with its copy downstairs. Identify \((q,i)\otimes g\) with \(q\otimes g\) for \(g\) in that summand. These identifications give a bijection of positive attaching choices stage by stage. At rank zero the total vertices already carry the summand indices. Assume the preceding choices have been matched. Their free weak stages then have identical generators, attaching maps and markings on the total side. The two projections differ by forgetting the base index. Every projected globe or globular pasting with specified initial total vertex lifts uniquely to \(B_X\) from that vertex's index. This follows by transporting along its 1-arrows, using the 2-face equalities of the action. Equivalently, on each globular sum a map into \(B_X\) is a map into \(F(Γ)\) with its initial index. The targets here are gaunt, so the same calculation applies to the projections of weak expressions and their coherences.

For each old marked factor, its required base projection upstairs is therefore forced by the projection downstairs and its initial total vertex. Every prescribed weight component in that factor is literally its summand component. On the proper-fibre part of the Gray boundary, the fibre attaching map is included from the summand, and its compositions with earlier pair generators are carried to the same compositions by the induction hypothesis. Thus a realisation of the prescribed pasting in either free weak stage gives the identical realisation in the other. Restrictions to common faces agree term by term, including the two specified vertices of a 1-globe. This proves the bijection and \(A(Γ,T)\iff A(Γ_X,V)\).

**Applying the reduction.** Given an arbitrary \(T\), compute \(X(b)\) as the connected components of the undirected generating 1-graph of its fibre. The functors on edges induce functions on these components. A component of a base 2-cell at any fibre object is an arrow connecting the two transported objects, so the functions satisfy the 2-cell relations. Restricting the cells of \(T\) to the indicated components gives \(V\) on \(B_X\). Each component is homotopical because both free constructions split as disjoint unions and the comparison respects the summands. The coproduct reconstruction above recovers \(T\), including its specified fibre polygraphs. Empty fibres contribute empty index sets. This proves the claimed effective reduction for every input.

In particular, if \(U_{\mathrm{conn}}\) denotes validity for all weights with nonempty connected fibres, the following equivalence holds for every finite homotopical base.
\[
 U(Γ)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 U_{\mathrm{conn}}(Γ_X)\ \text{for every finite set action }X.
\]
Necessity even gives \(U(Γ_X)\), by the coproduct construction with arbitrary \(V\). Sufficiency applies the component construction to the weight being tested. One may restrict this test to actions with connected category of elements. Indeed each undirected component of \(Γ_X\) gives a subaction since transport follows an edge within that component, and attachments in distinct components are independent. The empty base has no attachments. The right side still ranges over finite actions of unbounded size and over connected weights. A terminating base test in Question 2 and a terminating attaching test for the connected-fibre inputs of Question 1 remain open here.
