# The homotopy theory of CaTT models: the unit condition from left properness, and the directed coherator

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Everything under the "Known" headings is proved and is not re-asked.*

**What we are asking for.** The most general statement: a decision of the unit condition (U) for **all** computad-cofibrant CaTT models (equivalently (11) for all finite computads), and the directed comparison for the variant L in every dimension, built on the realization of L's coherator in Known (VIII). Known (VIII) below reduces (U) to one comparison, (N), on top of the left semi-model structure (FEA). Special computads or one more degree are not what we want.

## Background

**CaTT.** CaTT (Finster–Mimram, LICS 2017) is a dependent type theory with one sort former, `⋆` and `arr A t u` (cells of type A from t to u), and one term former, `coh`. For a *pasting context* Θ (a context presenting a globular pasting diagram) and two parallel terms u, v over Θ satisfying a support (free-variable) condition, `coh Θ (u → v) σ` is a cell, for every substitution σ into Θ. Substitution is strict and syntactic. The models of CaTT are exactly Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis weak ω-categories: presheaves on the CaTT coherator sending globular sums to globular products (Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, arXiv 2106.04475, §3).

**A directed variant.** A variant L of CaTT keeps the same syntax but changes the side condition of `coh` so that, across objects, interchange is a directed cell (lax, not invertible). Its intended strict models are strict ω-categories with lax transformations (Ara–Maltsiniotis's ∞-Cat_lax, arXiv 1607.00668, Ex. C.18), and its intended homotopical models are oriented (∞,ω)-categories in the sense of Gepner–Heine (arXiv 2510.10504), whose homs carry Campion's Gray tensor (arXiv 2311.00205). The strict soundness of L is proved, using that cells of free strict ω-categories on Steiner complexes are determined by their supports.

**What is known about the homotopy theory.**
- Strict ω-categories have the Lafont–Métayer–Worytkiewicz model structure, cofibrantly generated by the boundary inclusions ∂Oₙ → Oₙ (arXiv 0712.0617, §4).
- Leinster's operad is the universal cofibrant replacement of the strict ω-category operad for that weak factorisation system (Garner, arXiv 0804.2663). Markakis extends this to generalised signatures: free algebras on computads are the cofibrant objects (arXiv 2303.11978).
- For Batanin–Leinster weak ω-categories, Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara prove 2-out-of-3 for ω-weak equivalences and call a (left semi-)model structure future work (arXiv 2406.13240, introduction). We know of no model structure on weak ω-categories.
- Cat_ω (Loubaton, arXiv 2406.05425, the inductive notion lim Cat_n) is presentable and cartesian closed (Prop. 2.2.1.54). Campion's Gray tensor is built on flagged (∞,∞)-categories and transferred to Cat_n for n ≤ ω and to the version with coinductive equivalences.
- CaTT's invertible cells are the coinductively invertible ones (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2406.12127).

**Known (I): the relative category and a strict coherent choice.**
- **A relative category.** Models of CaTT are equivalent to Batanin–Leinster weak ω-categories (Benjamin–Markakis–Sarti, arXiv 2405.00398, §7). With W the Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara ω-weak equivalences (2-out-of-6, arXiv 2406.13240 Thm 3.3.3, Cor. 3.3.4), 𝓗_CaTT := Mod(CaTT)[W⁻¹] exists. FHM (arXiv 2511.09849) characterise trivial fibrations as W ∩ ω-equifibrations (Prop. 4.1.5) and equifibrations as J-injectives (Thm 4.3.1). No (semi-)model structure is known.
- **Strict coherent choice, one direction.** Let 𝓜 be the injective complete Segal Θ_ω-space model category and 𝒜 its algebraically fibrant objects (Nikolaus). For each globular pasting shape P, yP is discrete and fibrant, so F Sp(P) → yP is a trivial fibration in 𝒜. Lifting the CaTT coherator's cell attachments against these gives R : 𝕋 → 𝒜 preserving globular sums, and S(Y) := 𝒜(R(−), Y) is a CaTT model with strict substitution and S(Y)_n = (UY)(D_n)₀. S sends weak equivalences to W, so it induces Cat_ω → 𝓗_CaTT. It has a formal left adjoint 𝓛_R(A) = ∫^P A(P) · R(P).
- **(2,2)-categories are presented** (Campbell, arXiv 1907.01999: 2-truncated (∞,2)-categories are bicategories; strictify).
- **Obstruction to the raw inductive nerve.** The coherent walking ω-equivalence E (Hadzihasanovic–Loubaton–Ozornova–Rovelli, arXiv 2404.14509, Thm 1.33) is a gaunt strict ω-category with E → 1 a folk weak equivalence, so no functor 𝓗_CaTT → Cat^ind_ω agrees with the raw inductive nerve on strict ω-categories. The Street nerve marking coinductively invertible cells does send E → 1 to an equivalence (Henry–Loubaton, arXiv 2301.11424, Prop. 4.58).
- **Free shapes agree.** Finite globular pasting shapes and their Gray products are the same free objects in the inductive and coinductive theories (Campion, arXiv 2311.00205, Cor. 3.5, Thm 3.14, Cor. 3.15).

**Known (II): coinductive completion and reconstruction tests.**
- **S factors through coinductive completion.** Write Q : Cat^ind_ω ⇄ Cat^coind_ω : j for coinductive completion. By Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde (arXiv 2603.11005: Lemma 5.9, Prop. 5.29 and 5.30, Cor. 5.31), completion is the localisation at ∞-surjective maps, and its units are ∞-surjective. S₀ sends every ∞-surjective map to W, because for an 𝒜-fibration, π₀-surjectivity of the Kan fibration Map(yDₙ, UY) → Map(Bₙ, UY) ×_{Map(Bₙ,UZ)} Map(yDₙ, UZ) gives strict globular boundary lifts, which are FHM trivial fibrations. Hence S ≃ S̄ ∘ Q with S̄ = S j.
- **The underived left adjoint is not relative.** The coend 𝓛_R ⊣ S₀ is an ordinary adjunction. For gaunt strict C with discrete nerve NC (canonically algebraically fibrant), π₀ Map(𝓛_R A, NC) ≅ Mod(CaTT)(A, C). Applied to the coherent walking equivalence w : E → 1, this would make Mod(1, E) → Mod(E, E) a bijection if 𝓛_R(w) were an equivalence, which is false. So 𝓛_R does not send W to inductive equivalences.
- **S₀ preserves categorical level.** If Y presents an (∞,n)-category, every cell of S₀(Y) above dimension n is coinductively invertible. The proof extends the cell strictly over N(Σ^{k−1} I), then uses that FHM weak equivalences reflect invertibility.
- **A necessary test for reconstruction.** If the ordinary counit ε_Y : 𝓛_R S₀(Y) → Y is an equivalence, then 𝒜(Y, NC) → Mod(CaTT)(S₀Y, C) is bijective for every gaunt strict C. In particular, for contractible Y every strict CaTT map S₀(Y) → E must be constant.
- **Marked targets.** Marking the coinductive equivalences of C does not change C. Localising at them gives QC (ORW Prop. 5.29).
- **Directed version, conditional.** Suppose the coherator of L has a Gray-enriched cellular presentation, and suppose the hom maps K(Q_a, Q_b) → K(Q_a, Y_b) are trivial fibrations. Then lifting into the enriched category of commuting squares gives an enriched R_L that strictly preserves composition. Still to be supplied are arity colimits in the semantic category, ordered tensor domains D^r ⊗ D^s (for example via a coloured operad), and invertibility declarations.

**Known (III): the counit and a conditional derived adjunction.**
- **The underived counit fails.** For every gaunt strict C with C → 1 a folk weak equivalence there is a contractible algebraically fibrant Y with S₀(Y) ≅ C (take Y = cosk₀ of the right Kan extension of C's reflexive globular set from the globes to Θ_ω, with fillers chosen to make NC → Y algebraic). For C = E this gives a non-constant S₀(Y) → E, so ε_Y : 𝓛_R S₀ Y → Y is not an equivalence. This is compatible with an equivalence 𝓗_CaTT ≃ Cat^coind_ω, since E ≃ 1 in 𝓗_CaTT and Q(NE) ≃ 1.
- **A conditional derived adjunction.** Let K be the computadic cofibrant-replacement comonad on Mod(CaTT) (Markakis, arXiv 2303.11978, Prop. 19, Cor. 21–22; its counit q and comultiplication are in W and K preserves and reflects W, Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara arXiv 2406.13240 Prop. 4.2.2). Assume

  ```
  (H)  f : B → B′ in W between computad-cofibrant models  ⇒  QU𝓛_R(f) is an equivalence.
  ```

  Then the co-Kleisli category of K (weak maps, Bourke–Garner) gives an adjunction Λ : 𝓗_CaTT ⇄ Cat^coind_ω : S̄ with Λ[A] ≃ QU𝓛_R(KA).
- **The reconstruction condition.** For Y presenting jC, the derived counit is QU(d_Y), with d_Y = ε_Y ∘ 𝓛_R(q_{S₀Y}). Since a map into a coinductively complete target is inverted by Q iff it is ∞-surjective, the counit condition is

  ```
  (R)  U𝓛_R K S₀ Y → UY is ∞-surjective.
  ```

- **The equivalence conjecture.** (H), (R) for every coinductively complete C, and the unit condition "A ← KA → S₀𝓛_R KA is an equivalence in 𝓗_CaTT" (for instance η_{KA} ∈ W) together imply 𝓗_CaTT ≃ Cat^coind_ω.
- **A correction.** Full faithfulness of S̄ restricted to a full subcategory 𝓑 implies that the counits at 𝓑 are equivalences only if Λ S̄(𝓑) ⊆ 𝓑.
- **Directed tensor arities.** For L, binary global operations through global dimension 3 have hom tensor domains D⁰ ⊗ D⁰, D¹ ⊗ D⁰, D⁰ ⊗ D¹, D² ⊗ D⁰, D⁰ ⊗ D² and D¹ ⊗ D¹. The last gives the directed interchanger. For α : f₀ ⇒ f₁ : x → y and β : g₀ ⇒ g₁ : y → z, in execution order, it goes (f₀◁β) ; (α▷g₁) ⇛ (α▷g₀) ; (f₁◁β): β first, then α, to α first, then β. In global dimension 4, the interchanger's naturality and the cube of three interchangers are 4-cells in an untruncated theory but equalities in a strict 3-truncation.

**Known (IV): units on graphs, detection, and the reduction to finite computads.**
- **Units on representables and graphs.** α_G : F_w G → F_s G is a globular trivial fibration for every globular set G, since B → T is cartesian and contractible. Hence η_{h_P} ∈ W for every coherator representable h_P = F_w G_P, and η_{F_w G} ∈ W for every graph G. The graph case uses 𝓛_R F_w G ≅ F|G| ≃ N Path(G).
- **Detection.** For f : X → Y in 𝒜, S₀(f) ∈ W ⇒ QU(f) is an equivalence. Algebraic identities and composites agree with geometric ones relative to boundaries, so algebraic invertibility gives geometric coinductive invertibility (ORW Prop. 5.42), and Q inverts the resulting maps (ORW Prop. 5.30).
- **One assertion suffices.** Let (U) be the assertion η_B ∈ W for every computad-cofibrant B. Then (U) implies (H), (R) and 𝓗_CaTT ≃ Cat^coind_ω. For (R), S₀(d_Y)η_{KA} = q_A. Moreover (U) reduces to
  ```
  (11)  η_{A_𝔠} ∈ W for every finite computad 𝔠,
  ```
  by filtered colimits of finite subcomputads (the algebraic colimit is W-equivalent to S₀ of the 𝒜-colimit; FHM Prop. 3.4.4). (11) is an infinite family.
- **The (∞,1) fragment.** Free models on computads with bounded generator dimensions and invertible cells above dimension 1 are free on graphs, so (H) holds between them. (R) for general Kan complexes or quasi-categories is not obtained: finite subcomputads of K S₀ Y can omit the inverse witnesses.
- **L, ordinary presentation.** The coherator of L is the colimit of free globular extensions, adjoining each admissible head once. Through global dimension 3 the generators are classified by active hom-dimension d:
  - r = 1: operation heads with d = 0;
  - r = 2: operation heads with d = 1, and coherence heads with d = 0;
  - r = 3: operation heads with d = 2, either one factor of dimension 2 or two of dimension 1 (the interchangers), and coherence heads with d ≤ 1.

  Whole-grid interchangers are separate generators. Composites of elementary ones are derived, and the two are linked by dimension-4 coherence heads when formally parallel. In dimension 4, the operation profiles are (3), (2,1), (1,2) and (1,1,1), the last being the three-interchanger cube. No Gray-enriched presentation was given.

**Known (V): the triangle, spaces, saturation and enrichment.**
- **The triangle reduces to relative coherence.** Let 𝔠_△ be the computad x, y, z; f : x → y, g : y → z, h : x → z; α : m(f, g) ⇒ h, with A_△ its free CaTT model and T_△ its free strict 2-category. The Θ-nerve of T_△ is yP ∪ yD₂ glued along yD₁ (P = [2]), a homotopy pushout, and so is the corresponding pushout of R-values; hence 𝓛_R A_△ is weakly equivalent to N T_△ and

  ```
  (4)  η_{A_△} ∈ W  ⟺  π : A_△ → T_△ ∈ W.
  ```

  π has strict boundary lifting in dimensions 0–3: all cells of A_△ above dimension 2 are invertible, and lifting in dimension 3 is the statement that parallel 2-terms with equal strict image are joined by a 3-term, which holds in every CaTT context. What remains is

  ```
  (5)  any two parallel r-terms of the CaTT context (x, y, z, f, g, h, α : f ∗₀ g ⇒ h), r ≥ 3, are joined by an (r+1)-term.
  ```

- **(5) holds for r = 3.** The context has a unique source x and sink z, and the two boundary paths share only their endpoints. A normal-form argument (for a 2-term with strict image α, exactly one 0-column of its arity carries α and spans x → z, all other columns factor literally through a point; normalisers built from this factorisation are compatible with every 3-term by induction on syntax) joins any two parallel 3-terms. So the first open case of (5) is r = 4.
- **Spaces.** For Y presenting a space, Z = QU𝓛_R K S₀ Y is a space, and the reconstruction map Z → Y is bijective on π₀, an isomorphism on π₁ and surjective on every π_n. Injectivity on π_n, n ≥ 2, follows from the **spherical representation condition**: every class of π_n(QU𝓛_R B, x), n ≥ 2, is represented by the interpretation of an algebraic n-cell with iterated identity boundary (only B = K S₀ Y is needed). Cellular approximation does not give this.
- **Saturation.** Units in the saturation W^sat give 𝓗 ≃ 𝒜[V⁻¹] for the class V = {f : S₀ f ∈ W^sat}, which is a further localisation of Cat^coind_ω. Equality with Cat^coind_ω needs

  ```
  (10)  S₀ f ∈ W^sat  ⇒  QU f is an equivalence.
  ```

  Two-out-of-six, retracts and filtered colimits do not formally imply saturation (an eight-element poset with a non-saturated class having all three closure properties). The condition

  ```
  (12)  pushouts of maps in Cof ∩ W remain in W
  ```

  (Cof the computad-cofibrations) would give W = W^sat, via the factorisation w = p i with p a trivial fibration.
- **Enrichment.** The semantic tensor arities for L exist in oriented (∞,ω)-categories (Gepner–Heine enriched suspension and wedge). A *strict* Gray enrichment of the raw coherator of L that represents cells is impossible: it would require the raw equation c(f, i(y)) = f for a composition head c and an identity head i, and CaTT-style syntax has no definitional equations. A weak enrichment is not ruled out.

**Known (VI): degree-2 representation, a saturation criterion and the two-square arity.**
- **(5) for r ≥ 4 is a relative-coherence statement.** It asks that parallel 4-terms of the 2-dimensional triangle context with equal strict image be joined. Extending the r = 3 normalisers needs, for each 4-term θ : a → b and chosen comparisons H_a : a → ν, a 5-cell θ ; H_b ⇒ H_a. This is not asked here.
- **Spherical representation in degree 2.** Let B be free on a computad with every positive-dimensional cell coinductively invertible, and X = g U 𝓛_R B (g = groupoidification). Every class of π₂(X, x) is the interpretation of a cell b : 1_x ⇒ 1_x of B.
  - *Filtration.* 𝓛_R(F_w D_n) = F(yD_n) and 𝓛_R(F_w ∂D_n) = F(∂_Θ D_n), so generator attachments become homotopy pushouts of S^{n−1} → D^n. This gives a relative CW model X₁ ⊆ X₂ ⊆ X with X₁ ≃ |G|, X₂ from the 2-generators and higher cells of dimension ≥ 3, so π₂X₁ = 0 and π₂X₂ → π₂X is onto.
  - *Crossed modules.* The homotopy bigroupoid H₂B (2-cells modulo invertible 3-cells; FHM Prop. 2.4.6, Thm 2.4.8, Cor. 2.4.9) maps to Π₂X by a pseudofunctor s whose coherence is checked in generic shapes. Over the free groupoid F = π₁(X₁, G₀), an algebraic crossed module M_B with ker ∂_x = Aut(1_x) in H₂B maps to π₂(X, X₁, G₀). Composed with j from the free crossed module C ≅ π₂(X₂, X₁, G₀) (Whitehead; Brown–Razak Salleh Thm 5.1), it is the canonical map, checked on generators.
  - *Consequence.* For Y presenting a space, d : QU𝓛_R K S₀ Y → Y is an isomorphism on π₀, π₁ and π₂ and surjective on every π_n. Injectivity uses the literal-boundary lift of a nullhomotopy through the Kan fibration Map(yD₃, UY) → Map(∂_Θ D₃, UY) and the trivial fibration q_A.
- **A saturation criterion.** Let 𝒞ⁿ = F_w D_n and j_n : 𝒞ⁿ → ℰⁿ⁺¹ the source inclusion into the FHM weak walking (n+1)-equivalence, so J = {j_n} has J^⋔ = equifibrations and W ∩ J^⋔ = TF (FHM arXiv 2511.09849, Prop. 5.0.4, Prop. 4.1.5). Consider
  ```
  (FEA)  A_𝔠 → A_𝔠 ∐_{𝒞ⁿ} ℰⁿ⁺¹ lies in W, for every finite computad 𝔠, every n and every map 𝒞ⁿ → A_𝔠.
  ```
  (FEA) implies W = W^sat, hence the detection statement (10). Proof: (FEA) extends to cofibrant sources by filtered colimits. Every relative J-cell complex with cofibrant source is in W. A trivial cofibration with cofibrant source is a retract of such a complex (factor through J, then use W ∩ J^⋔ = TF). This gives the precofibration-category axiom CF3, and Rădulescu-Banu (arXiv math/0610009) Thm 7.2.7 gives saturation from 2-out-of-6. Unconditionally, every J-cofibration i : A → B has a strict retraction (A → 1 is an equifibration). (FEA) contains j_n ∈ W itself, which FHM prove only after strict reflection.
- **The two-square arity of L.** For Γ = ΣΔ₂ ∨ ΣΔ₁ (α₁ : f₀ ⇒ f₁, α₂ : f₁ ⇒ f₂ : x → y, β : g₀ ⇒ g₁ : y → z), U_Γ = Δ₂ ⊗ Δ₁ has a canonical 2-cell ρ : P ⇒ Q with P = (f₀◁β) ; (α₁▷g₁) ; (α₂▷g₁) and Q = (α₁▷g₀) ; (α₂▷g₀) ; (f₂◁β), the paste of two elementary squares through M = (α₁▷g₀) ; (f₁◁β) ; (α₂▷g₁).
  - The raw coherator has an independent whole-grid head G_Γ : P ⇒ Q distinct from the elementary paste E_Γ, and evaluating both in the unchanged strict arity identifies them.
  - Adjoining a second parallel 2-generator g ↦ ρ (U_Γ⁺ → U_Γ) is not a folk weak equivalence: g and ρ have the same image and no 3-cell joins them.
  - Attaching the strict walking 3-equivalence along ρ, Û_Γ = U_Γ ∐_{D²} E_s³, gives a folk trivial cofibration U_Γ → Û_Γ and a folk weak equivalence Û_Γ → U_Γ. Its 2-skeleton is U_Γ⁺, and its new 3-generators supply the global 4-dimensional equivalence between G_Γ and E_Γ.
  - This is a repair of one arity. Substitution maps between resolved arities, weak Gray composition, the augmentation to semantic arities, and the model comparison are not constructed. The coloured-operad Boardman–Vogt route (Berger–Moerdijk) does not apply directly: the directed Gray tensor has no braiding.

**The coh rule of L (two-tier).** Contexts are CaTT pasting contexts. Each decomposes uniquely as Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ, with objects a₀, …, aₙ and Θᵢ the pasting scheme in the hom from aᵢ₋₁ to aᵢ.
- **Gray unfolding.** U_{ij}(Γ) := Θᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ Θⱼ (factors in execution order) is the Steiner tensor of the Steiner complexes λ(Θ). Its generators are tensors of generators, dimensions add, and the boundary is the Leibniz formula. It is strongly loop-free, so its cells are determined by their supports.
- **Supports.** For a term t : A with endpoints aᵢ, aⱼ, supp(t : A) is the downward-closed set of generators of U_{ij}(Γ) used by the strict image of t and of the cells in its type, computed in the free strict Gray-enriched ω-category on Γ.
- **The rule.** coh_{Γ, t →_A u}[σ] is well formed when either
  - (operation) supp(t : A) = ∂⁻U_{ij}(Γ), supp(u : A) = ∂⁺U_{ij}(Γ), and dim A = dim U_{ij}(Γ) (so the cell has dimension dim U_{ij}(Γ) + 1); or
  - (coherence) supp(t : A) = U_{ij}(Γ) = supp(u : A).

  **Here (i, j) = (0, n) is required** (proper intervals would let a 1-head over ΣD⁰ ∨ ΣD^{m−1} depend formally on an m-dimensional argument, destroying the filtration by output dimension).
- **Consequences.**
  - On a single hom (n = 1) this is CaTT.
  - For Γ = ΣD¹ ∨ ΣD¹ there is no horizontal composite of α and β. There is an operation-case 3-cell (f◁β) ; (α▷g′) ⇛ (α▷g) ; (f′◁β), and none in the reverse direction.
  - The intended strict model is ∞-Cat_lax (strict ω-categories with lax transformations), where the rule is sound.
  - "Two-tier" means interchange is invertible inside a hom and directed across objects.

**Known (VII): torsion-free computads, and the homotopy hypothesis.**
- **Torsion-free computads reduce to relative coherence.** Let 𝔠 be a finite computad coming from a torsion-free complex in Campion's sense (arXiv 2311.00200; this includes strongly loop-free Steiner complexes), with A = A_𝔠, T = F(𝔠) and π : A → T. Then
  ```
  η_A ∈ W  ⟺  π ∈ W  ⟺  π is a globular trivial fibration  ⟺  RC,
  ```
  where RC says that any two parallel n-cells of A with equal image in T are joined by an (n+1)-cell, for every n. Proof:
  - S₀(e_𝔠) ∘ η = π, where e_𝔠 : 𝓛_R A → NT is the adjunct of π. It is a semantic equivalence by cellular induction, since Campion's Thm 5.11 says the strict attachments of a torsion-free complex are homotopy pushouts.
  - Endo-cells of T are identities (Campion Def. 1.8, Prop. 1.10), so RC fillers map to identities.
  - A boundary-correction lemma whiskers by such fillers one dimension at a time. Together with initial lifts by induction on strict expressions, this gives boundary lifting.
  - Conversely, FHM essential surjectivity at 1_{πa} gives RC.

  So (11) for torsion-free computads is exactly RC in all dimensions. For the triangle this is Known (V), (4)–(5).
- **(11) implies the homotopy hypothesis for a specified coherator.** Let 𝕋^bi be Lanari's theory 𝕋^W (arXiv 1809.07923, Def. 4.3): the CaTT coherator with freely adjoined, independent left and right inverses and cancellation cells for every positive-dimensional cell, J ⊣ V : Mod(𝕋^bi) → Mod(CaTT). Write (U_gpd) for "η_B ∈ W for every groupoidal computad-cofibrant B, infinite computads included". Then
  ```
  (11) for all finite computads  ⟹  (U_gpd)  ⟹  𝕋^bi is a Grothendieck groupoid coherator satisfying Henry's pushout condition,
  ```
  and Mod(𝕋^bi)[W_bi⁻¹] ≃ Mod(CaTT)_gpd[W_gpd⁻¹] ≃ 𝒮 (spaces). Proof outline:
  - A reflection principle: for groupoidal computad-cofibrant B, B′, a map f is in W iff its realization E(f) = gQU𝓛_R(f) is an equivalence. This uses a completion map that preserves algebraic fillers.
  - The inverse-data attachments β_k : 𝔻_k → I_k are inverted by E: the fibre is a product of two contractible homotopy fibres of composition with a path.
  - Bourke's algebraic-injectivity factorizations (arXiv 1811.09532, Thms 4.3–4.4).
  - Henry's Thm 5.3.5 and Cor. 5.3.13 (arXiv 1609.04622).
  So (11) in general is at least as hard as the homotopy hypothesis for 𝕋^bi.
- **The groupoidal restriction is vacuous on finite computads.** A finite ordinary computad has a groupoidal free model only if it has no positive-dimensional generators: a top generator has nonempty support, so it is not invertible (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2406.12127, Prop. 37). So the infinite quantifier in (U_gpd) is essential.
- **Not known:** a converse for (11) itself (the converse for (U_gpd) is Known (VIII)(A)); the comparison of 𝕋^bi with an arbitrary Grothendieck groupoid coherator; (11) for any finite computad with torsion beyond those in Known (IV)–(VI).


**Known (VIII): the groupoidal equivalence, the semi-model structure, and the two missing comparisons.**
- **(A) The groupoidal condition is exactly the homotopy hypothesis for 𝕋^bi.**
  ```
  (U_gpd)  ⟺  𝕋^bi is a Grothendieck groupoid coherator satisfying Henry's pushout condition.
  ```
  The converse uses: an algebraic completion map r_Z (factor UZ → Z^c and extend Z's algebraic fillers along the monomorphism), giving S₀(f) ∈ W ⟺ QU(f) equivalence; Φ(B) is a space for groupoidal cofibrant B (every characteristic globe factors through the core); v_B ∈ W by Lanari's Prop. 4.5 with c_Z = ε^{bi}_Z ∘ J q_{VZ}; and identification of the realization with Henry's comparison (a colimit-preserving endofunctor of spaces fixing the point is the identity). So the groupoidal half of Q1 is equivalent to a named open problem.
- **(B) The directed implications.**
  ```
  (U)  ⟹  (FEA)  ⟺  the left semi-model structure (Cof = I-cof, Fib = J^⋔, WE = W) on Mod(CaTT) exists  ⟹  (H).
  ```
  U ⇒ FEA by naturality of η and the semantic calculation of ORW (arXiv 2603.11005, Construction 5.35, Lemmas 5.38, 5.23); FEA ⇒ semi-model axioms by the retract argument with W ∩ J^⋔ = I^⋔ (FHM); FEA ⇒ (H) since Φ := QU𝓛_R inverts relative J-cell complexes.
- **(N) What FEA ⇒ U needs.** For a generating cellular attachment B′ = B ∐_C D of computad-cofibrant models, FEA makes the square a homotopy pushout in 𝓗_CaTT. Cellular induction on η then needs, in addition,
  ```
  (N)  hocolim( S̄ΦB ← S̄ΦC → S̄ΦD )  →  S̄( ΦB ∐_{ΦC} ΦD )   is an equivalence in 𝓗_CaTT,
  ```
  i.e. the right adjoint S̄ preserves the generating homotopy pushouts (here C = F_w∂Dₙ, D = F_wDₙ). Left properness on cofibrant objects does not give this; Caviglia–Horel's unit induction assumes the analogous preservation by the nerve, and Barwick–Schommer-Pries separate homotopical density from cellular decompositions.
- **(O) Groupoidal comparison does not reach directed homs.** Extending (A) by taking homs needs Φ(B(x, y)) → Φ(B)(ηx, ηy) to be an equivalence with boundaries retained. Groupoidification loses this already for the walking arrow (g Hom_{[1]}(1, 0) = ∅, Hom_{g[1]}(1, 0) ≃ ∗).
- **Homotopical computads.** If the polygraph of 𝔠 is a homotopy polygraph (its strict free ω-category F(𝔠) is also its free (∞,ω)-category), the argument of Known (VII) applies verbatim: η_{A_𝔠} ∈ W ⟺ π : A_𝔠 → F(𝔠) ∈ W ⟺ any two parallel n-cells of A_𝔠 with equal image in F(𝔠) are joined by an (n+1)-cell, for every n. A finite 2-dimensional polygraph is homotopical iff F(𝔠) has no non-identity 2-cell 1_x ⇒ 1_x (a *bubble*). So for every bubble-free finite 2-dimensional computad, (11) is a relative-coherence statement. For a computad with a bubble, 𝓛_R A_𝔠 and F(𝔠) differ (B²ℕ groupoidifies to K(ℤ,2), the free (∞,2)-category on one bubble to S²), and (11) is not of that form.
- **Free strict ω-categories on finite polygraphs are gaunt** (top-dimensional generator content is additive under every composition and zero on identities; induct downward from the top dimension). Hence, for **every** finite computad 𝔠 (no torsion-freeness), π_𝔠 ∈ W ⟺ π_𝔠 is a globular trivial fibration ⟺ RC_id(𝔠): any two parallel n-cells a, b of A_𝔠 with π a = π b are joined by an (n+1)-cell u with π u = 1 (the boundary-correction and initial-lift arguments of Known (VII) use only this). With e_𝔠 : 𝓛_R A_𝔠 → N F(𝔠) the adjunct of π_𝔠, S₀(e_𝔠) ∘ η = π_𝔠 and S₀(e_𝔠) ∈ W ⟺ QU(e_𝔠) is an equivalence ⟺ 𝔠 is homotopical (after completion). So on homotopical 𝔠, (11)_𝔠 ⟺ RC_id(𝔠); on non-homotopical 𝔠, (11)_𝔠 ⇒ ¬RC_id(𝔠), and **a non-homotopical finite computad satisfying RC_id is a counterexample to (11)**.
- **L: an unenriched realization exists in every dimension.** Let 𝕋_L be the raw coherator of L (free globular extensions adjoining every admissible head once). Gepner–Heine (arXiv 2510.10504, Thm 2.4.8) prove: for a presentably monoidal 𝒲 and a small dense full monoidal V ⊂ 𝒲, the wedges of suspensions Θ(V) ⊂ 𝒲-Cat are dense, and a presheaf on Θ(V) is a nerve iff it satisfies the wedge Segal condition and its morphism presheaves are 𝒲-nerves. Take 𝒲 = (∞Cat, lax Gray ⊠), oriented categories being left ⊠-enriched (their §1.6), and V = W_Θ, the closure of Θ under Gray products. By Campion (arXiv 2311.00205, Thm A: the Gray tensor is the unique biclosed monoidal structure making the Gray-cube inclusion strong monoidal; Cor. 3.5: strongly loop-free Steiner complexes include strong monoidally), W_Θ consists of the strict Steiner products, and Gepner–Heine's ⊠ (descended from cubes, their Cor. 3.1.1) is Campion's, up to matching their (∞,ω) notion and cube orientation. Put Θ^or = Θ(W_Θ). Every pasting context Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ lies in Θ^or with hom from aᵢ to aⱼ equal to Θᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ Θⱼ (their Cor. 2.2.8). Θ^or is a 1-category (strict oriented categories embed fully faithfully, their Rem. 3.5.4), so representables yΓ are discrete and fibrant in the localised injective model structure 𝓜_or on simplicial presheaves on Θ^or, and spine inclusions Sp(Γ) → yΓ are weak equivalences. With 𝒜_or its algebraically fibrant objects (Nikolaus) and F ⊣ U, F(Sp Γ) → yΓ is a trivial fibration. L's strict soundness (every head over Γ has a strict filler in the strict Gray-category Γ: the top cell for an operation head, an identity for a coherence head) then gives, by lifting head by head exactly as for R in Known (I), a functor R_L : 𝕋_L → 𝒜_or with R_L(Γ) = F(Sp Γ), preserving globular sums, in every dimension. So S_L(Y) := 𝒜_or(R_L(−), Y) is an L-model with strict substitution and S_L(Y)ₙ = (UY)(Dₙ)₀. No Gray enrichment of 𝕋_L and no resolved tensor arities are used: the whole-grid head and the elementary paste of Known (VI) are two lifts over the same strict cell, joined by the contractible fibres of F(Sp Γ) → yΓ (and by coherence heads in 𝕋_L). Freely adjoining relative fillers, and the strict-enrichment obstruction of Known (V), are therefore not on this route.

**Known (IX): two obstacles located, and the bubble's strict comparison.**
- **The ordinary directed adjunction.** 𝓛_{R_L}(A) = ∫^{P ∈ 𝕋_L} A(P) · R_L(P) is left adjoint to S_L (coend calculation). Its homotopical properties are not known.
- **What a proof of (N) by realizing nerve maps needs.** Assume (FEA) and try to realize a map α : N_𝓗A → N_𝓗B of derived arity nerves (N_𝓗B(P) = Map_{𝓗_CaTT}(h_P, B)) by a strict map f : A → B, by choosing images of generators and comparisons δ_t : f(t) ⇝ α(t) term by term. For a composite term t = σh (h : G_n → h_P, σ : h_P → A), naturality gives a candidate δ_t. It must restrict along i_n : ∂G_n → G_n to the comparison δ_{∂t} already chosen for the literal boundary of t, relative to all lower faces. The two agree only up to a loop in Map(∂G_n, B). Strict substitution fixes f(t) = coh(P, T, fσ), so path lifting in Map(G_n, B) → Map(∂G_n, B) cannot absorb that loop: compare the covering ℝ → ℝ/ℤ, where the constant path and the loop s ↦ s mod ℤ have lifts with different endpoints. What is needed is a system of comparison homotopies compatible with every term operation and every substitution, with all higher relative compatibilities.
- **What 2-out-of-6 for directed weak equivalences needs.** The intrinsic candidate W_L (essentially surjective on objects up to coinductively invertible 1-cells, FHM weak equivalences on all homs) needs the *transport lemma*: for an invertible 1-cell e : x → y, e^* : X(y, z) → X(x, z) (and its right-whiskering counterpart) is essentially ω-surjective. For CaTT this is Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara (arXiv 2406.13240) Thm 3.2.14, whose step (3.2.20) → (3.2.21) moves an inverse-unit witness past a higher cell by an invertible comparison. In L that step needs "an interchanger with an invertible input is invertible", as Chanavat proves for Gray products of diagrammatic sets (arXiv 2505.01387, Thm 3.22). That in turn needs, for all hom-dimensions p, q, literally typed elementary tensor terms τ_{p,q}(u, v) and literally typed comparisons between τ_{p,q}(u′ ∘ u, v) and the paste of τ_{p,q}(u, v) and τ_{p,q}(u′, v). Once boundary paddings make the two parallel, their strict images have full support in (D_p ∐_{D_{p−1}} D_p) ⊗ D_q and L's coherence rule applies. The paddings are not available uniformly. Naturality of CaTT coherences is known only for varied variables of depth ≤ 1 (Benjamin–Markakis–Offord–Sarti–Vicary, arXiv 2501.11620).
- **The bubble's strict comparison fails.** For 𝔠 = (x; σ : 1_x ⇒ 1_x), RC_id(3) fails: with a : 1_x ⇒ 1_x a literal lift of σ, the Eckmann–Hilton 3-term eh(a, a) and 1_{a∗₁a} are parallel with identity strict image, and they take values ±1 and 0 in the CaTT model given by the Gray category doubly delooping the braided monoidal groupoid (ℕ, End = ℤ, β_{p,q} = pq). So π_𝔠 ∉ W. By the homotopical-computads bullet of Known (VIII) this is consistent with (11)_𝔠 and does not decide it.

## Questions

**Q1 (main: decide (U)).** Decide

```
(U)  η_B ∈ W for every computad-cofibrant CaTT model B   (equivalently (11) for every finite computad).
```

- **(a) Proof via (N).** Prove (N) for the generating attachments, and prove (FEA). Then (U) follows by cellular induction. A proof of (N) alone, giving (FEA) ⟺ (U), is also a substantial answer. Known (IX) locates the step: a coherent system of comparison homotopies, compatible with every term operation and substitution. A route that avoids it (for instance computing S̄ on the semantic pushouts F(∂_Θ Dₙ) → F(yDₙ) through the algebraic completion map of Known (VIII)(A), or a resolution of the coherator in which comparison data are part of the structure) is equally welcome.
- **(b) Counterexample.** A counterexample must lie outside the homotopical polygraphs (otherwise it is a failure of relative coherence, a separate problem). The natural candidate is the bubble (x; σ : 1_x ⇒ 1_x): decide whether η_{A_σ} ∈ W, by comparing A_σ with S₀ of the free (∞,ω)-category on one bubble (whose groupoidification is S²). Known (IX) shows π_σ ∉ W, so the answer turns on S₀(e_σ) ∘ η = π_σ with S₀(e_σ) ∉ W.
- **(c) Equivalence.** Prove (U) ⟺ (FEA), or (U) ⟺ a named open statement such as Henry's pushout condition for a directed coherator, or Taylor's free pushout condition (arXiv 2604.09867).
- **(d) Fallback, if (U) is out of reach: reconstruction at free strict targets.** Let K be the computadic replacement and d_C : 𝓛_R(K C) → N C the reconstruction map at a strict ω-category C. Show that Q U(d_C) is an equivalence whenever C = F(Γ) is the free strict ω-category on a finite polygraph Γ. This is a consequence of (U) (Known (III)), and it has its own payoff: if Γ satisfies RC_id(∞), the homotopicality comparison e_Γ : 𝓛_R A_Γ → N F(Γ) is a retract of d_{F(Γ)}, so (d) gives "RC_id(∞) ⇒ Γ homotopical" for every finite polygraph. The inductive form is false and should not be attempted: d_C itself is never an equivalence for nonempty C (test against a target D with a free loop: two maps 𝓛_R K 1 → N D land in different components, and d_1 is a retract of d_C). Only the coinductive completion Q U(d_C) is asked. Known: for every finite free C (and every strict C whose discrete nerve is coinductively complete), Q U(d_C) exhibits N C as the 0-truncation of Q U 𝓛_R(K C), in the mapping-space sense (maps into every discrete, coinductively complete target are already matched). So (d) is exactly the vanishing statement π_j Map(𝔻ⁿ, Q U 𝓛_R(K C)) = 0 for all n ≥ 0 and j ≥ 1, at C = F(Γ); this vanishing is what we ask.

**Q2 (the directed comparison in every dimension).** With R_L, S_L and 𝓛_{R_L} as above, build the directed analogue of the whole comparison: a class W_L of weak equivalences on L-models with 2-out-of-6, the homotopical realization adjunction, and the reduction of an equivalence Mod(𝕋_L)[W_L⁻¹] ≃ OrCat (oriented categories, coinductively completed as needed) to a unit condition on finite computads, as Known (IV) does for CaTT. Then decide that unit condition, at least for every finite computad whose strict free Gray-category is its free oriented category. The first step is the transport lemma of Known (IX). One candidate route: for an invertible 1-cell α (whose inverse α⁻¹ is a term), show that the composite of the interchangers χ_{α,β} and χ_{α⁻¹,β} is joined to identities by coherence heads whose supports are full, so that interchange with an invertible input is invertible. Alternatively, show that S_L cannot be part of such a comparison, and say exactly which step fails.

## What would count as a solution

A proof of (N), (FEA) or (U); a counterexample to (11) with a separating invariant; an equivalence of (U) with (FEA) or with a named open problem; the directed comparison for L (or a precise failure of S_L); or a published reference.

# Researcher Response 1

**The unit is an FHM weak equivalence for every groupoidal computad-cofibrant CaTT model, including infinite computads. The completed reconstruction map is an equivalence at every space.** For a general strict target the reconstruction map induces an equivalence after groupoidification. On the directed side, the singular functor carries semantic weak equivalences into the proposed intrinsic class. The unrestricted unit in Q1 and the directed equivalence in Q2 remain open.

## The groupoidal unit and the counit at spaces

Write \(E(B)=gQU\mathcal L_R B\), where \(g\) denotes groupoidification. Let \(X(B)\) be the classifying space of the simplicial category of labelled paths in \(B\). We use two natural comparisons proved in the companion problem. For computad-free \(B\), its category of labelled weak arities gives a natural zigzag \(X(B)\simeq E(B)\). For groupoidal \(B\) and vertices \(x,y\), the labelled-path comparison gives

\[
 X(B(x,y))\simeq \operatorname{Path}_{x,y}X(B).
\]

Every component of \(X(B)\) contains a vertex. Two vertices in the same component of a groupoidal algebra are joined by an actual invertible cell. The computation of components uses the category of one-cells modulo two-cells and composition of the resulting arrows. The same statements apply inside each successive fixed hom. [Path-space comparison](../answered/relative-coherence-all-dimensions-r7-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-81) · [Natural arity comparison and the two-endpoint version](../answered/relative-coherence-all-dimensions-r7-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-82)

Consequently, for a map \(f:B\to B'\) between groupoidal computad-free algebras, \(E(f)\) an equivalence implies \(f\in W\). Indeed \(X(f)\) is an equivalence. Taking path spaces between any two source vertices gives an equivalence on \(X\) of their fixed homs. Repeat at every prescribed pair of poles. Surjectivity on components at each stage gives precisely the successive essential-surjectivity conditions of FHM. Naturality of the arity zigzag identifies the map used in the first step with \(E(f)\). This proves the reflection implication used in Known (VII) independently of the unit. If computad-cofibrant objects are taken to include retracts, the natural comparison of the two functors on free objects extends to their retracts before applying the groupoidality hypothesis. [FHM, Definition 3.1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13240)

Apply this to Lanari's theory \(\mathbb T^{\rm bi}\), with adjunction \(J\dashv V\) as above. We use the inverse-data calculation of Known (VII). The map \(A\to VJA\) is cellular in the independent inverse-data inclusions \(\beta_k\). Realization in spaces inverts each \(\beta_k\) and its cellular composites with cofibrant source, since these attachments realize as homotopy pushouts. For a pasting arity \(P\), the underlying algebra \(B_P=VJh_P\) is groupoidal and computad-free. Thus \(X(B_P)\simeq E(h_P)\simeq *\). Taking path spaces repeatedly shows that every prescribed parallel pair of cells in \(B_P\) admits a filler. By the adjunction and Yoneda its cells are the arrows \(D_n\to P\) in \(\mathbb T^{\rm bi}\). This gives contractibility at every arity. The theory is cellular by construction and hence is a groupoid coherator. [Inverse-data calculation and cellularity](../answered/catt-models-unit-condition-2026-11-19.md) · [Bourke, Theorems 4.3–4.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09532)

For Henry's condition take any cellular \(\mathbb T^{\rm bi}\)-model \(Z\) and cell \(\mathbb D_n\to VZ\). Form the source-face expansion \(h:Z\to Z'=Z\amalg_{J\mathbb D_n}J\mathbb D_{n+1}\). Bourke's factorization on underlying algebras is

\[
 VZ\longrightarrow VZ\amalg_{\mathbb D_n}\mathbb D_{n+1}
 \longrightarrow VZ'.
\]

The first map becomes a homotopy pushout of a map between contractible spaces under \(E\). The second is \(\beta_k\)-cellular with cofibrant source. Hence \(E(Vh)\) is an equivalence. The endpoints of \(Vh\) are groupoidal and computad-free, so reflection gives \(Vh\in W\). On groupoidal models essential surjectivity at every pair of poles is the globular characterization of Grothendieck weak equivalence. This proves Henry's pushout condition. Known (VIII)(A) now gives \((U_{\rm gpd})\) with its full quantifier. [Ara, Theorem 4.18](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.2941) · [Henry, §5.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.04622) · [Converse for the specified unit](../answered/catt-models-unit-condition-r2-2026-11-19.md)

There is a corresponding case of (FEA) in every attachment rank. Let \(B\) be groupoidal and computad-cofibrant and put \(B'=B\amalg_{\mathcal C^n}\mathcal E^{n+1}\) at any cell of \(B\). The underlying attachment is cellular. Its forward cell is coinductively invertible by the defining tower, as are the inverse and cancellation witnesses in the tower. When \(n>0\), the new parallel \(n\)-cell is equivalent to the old invertible one via the forward cell and is therefore invertible. When \(n=0\) the duplicate is an object. All positive-dimensional generators now have invertible images, and closure of invertibility under weak composition gives groupoidality of \(B'\). The semantic calculation in Known (VIII)(B) inverts \(B\to B'\) under \(\Phi\), hence under \(E\). Reflection proves \(B\to B'\in W\). This includes \(j_0\) by taking the free point as source. [Invertibility under composition and equivalence, FHM §2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13240) · [Semantic walking-equivalence calculation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11005)

Now let \(Y\) present any space and put \(A=S_0Y\). Known (II) shows that \(A\) is groupoidal. The weak equivalence \(q_A:KA\to A\) reflects invertibility, so \(KA\) is groupoidal. Apply the unit just proved to the actual comparison identity

\[
 S_0(d_Y)\,\eta_{KA}=q_A .
\]

Two-out-of-three gives \(S_0(d_Y)\in W\), and detection in Known (IV) gives \(QU(d_Y)\) an equivalence. In particular this checks Q1(d) at the terminal category and at every discrete category. [FHM, Proposition 3.1.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13240)

## What the strict reconstruction map computes

For a free strict target \(C\) in Q1(d), the replacement \(q_C:KC\to C\) lifts every prescribed globular boundary. ## A space-valued test that retains the prescribed poles

There is an exact test for what the groupoidal argument still needs in Q1. For a strict CaTT map \(f:A\to B\), let \(H(f)\) range over \(f\) and the maps obtained by taking any finite succession of homs at a pair of objects of the current source and their two images. Write \(kD\) for the core of a weak category, consisting of its objects and hereditarily invertible cells. Then

\[
 f\in W\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 X(kH(f))\text{ is a weak homotopy equivalence for every such }H.
\]

For the forward implication, each \(H(f)\) is in \(W\) by the prescribed-boundary test. The core preserves this property. Indeed a target cell in the core at a prescribed source boundary lifts up to an invertible comparison. Its lift is invertible by invariance under invertible comparison and reflection of invertibility. The prescribed boundary then puts the lift in the core. On groupoidal algebras a weak equivalence gives a bijection on components and on each based homotopy group. In a fixed hom, surjectivity on classes is essential surjectivity there, and injectivity follows by lifting the comparison between two images at their specified poles. Response 81 now applies to the map of spaces. For the reverse implication only surjectivity on components of these spaces is needed. Every object of each target hom belongs to its core, and a path component in that core gives an invertible comparison to the image of a source object. Running through all depths and poles gives exactly the FHM condition. [Groupoidal cores](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14907) · [Prescribed-boundary equivalences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13240)

In particular this test applies to the actual map \(\eta_B\) without assuming (FEA). Applied to \(\eta_{KC}\), it is also equivalent to Q1(d) at \(C\). Indeed \(S_0(d_C)\eta_{KC}=q_C\in W\), and Known (VIII)(A)'s detection statement identifies \(S_0(d_C)\in W\) with equivalence of \(QU(d_C)\). The target homs in this test are taken in \(S_0\mathcal L_R(KC)\). The comparison of Response 82 with the strict homs of \(C\) leaves precisely these spaces to compute.

## The directed singular functor preserves semantic weak equivalences

Write \(W_L\) for the candidate in Q2. Let \(f:Y\to Z\) be a semantic weak equivalence in \(\mathcal A_{\rm or}\). Choose a path object \(Z\xrightarrow{\sim}PZ\xrightarrow{(d_0,d_1)}Z\times Z\) with endpoint map a fibration. Put \(T=Y\times_{Z,d_0}PZ\), write \(r:T\to Y\) for its projection, \(i:Y\to T\) for the constant-path section, and \(p:T\to Z\) for the second endpoint. Both \(r\) and \(p\) are trivial fibrations. Indeed \(d_0\) is a trivial fibration since \(Z\) is fibrant, and \(T\to Y\times Z\) is a base change of the endpoint fibration. The identities \(ri=1\) and \(pi=f\) then give the weak equivalences by semantic two-out-of-three.

The singular image of either trivial fibration lifts every strict globular boundary by adjunction, since the realizing maps \(F(\partial D_n)\to F(D_n)\) are cofibrations. At fixed poles it therefore restricts to a globular trivial fibration of ordinary CaTT homs. For each \(x,y\) of \(S_LY\), the restriction of \(S_Lr\) at \((ix,iy)\) lies in the ordinary FHM class and splits the restriction of \(S_Li\). Two-out-of-three **in CaTT** and composition with the corresponding restriction of \(S_Lp\) give the local FHM condition for \(S_Lf\). [FHM, Corollary 3.3.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13240)

Suppress \(S_L\) on objects and arrows. Given \(b\in S_LZ\), lift it along \(p\) to \(v\in S_LT\) and put \(a=rv\). Lift the identity at \(a\) along \(r\) with prescribed endpoints \(ia,v\). The resulting arrow \(e:ia\to v\) is invertible. To see this, take the identity's tower of inverses and cancellations, supplied by the point-arity coherences. Lift the chosen inverse once and use that same lift on both sides. Lift the left and right cancellation witnesses with the literal composite boundaries formed from these choices. Repeat for the cancellations in every higher rank. Strict preservation of the operations by \(r\) makes each lifting square commute. Strict preservation by \(p\) sends this tower to one for \(pe:f(a)\to b\). This proves essential surjectivity, hence \(S_Lf\in W_L\). Consequently \(S_L\) induces a functor out of the semantic localization into \(\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbb T_L)[W_L^{-1}]\).

This establishes the forward preservation property in every dimension. The path-object argument supplies inverse witnesses inside fibres of globular trivial fibrations. For Q2 one still needs transport along an arbitrary invertible arrow of an arbitrary \(L\)-model, or an independent proof of two-out-of-six there, and the homotopical realization and finite-unit comparison. ## Comparing transports across different raw labellings

Fix the minimal strong Steiner context $S$, the strict composite $c$ of rank $m$, and a nondegenerate subdivision $f:T↪F(S)$ of $c$ as in Response 9. Write $d$ for the total of $T$. A frame over $f$ fixes the raw labels below rank $m$ and the strict labels of the $m$-variables. Its top raw labels can vary from one end of a path to the other. Write $T_C(v,w)$ for transport in such a frame $C$ between two vertices in its image. This is well-defined even when a vertex has several names. [Frame transport and its dependence on evaluated vertices, Responses 10–11](../answered/relative-coherence-all-dimensions-r8-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-10)

**Theorem.** In $G_c$, the transports of *all* frames over the fixed embedding $f$ generate a thin groupoid on each component of their union. Thus a loop whose successive edges change the top inputs, the generic operations, and the raw lower labellings still contracts if these transports all use $f$. We first give a change-of-arity formula which also applies to some edges represented by degenerate diagrams.

**Change of arity.** Let $C$ have arity $P$ and strict labelling $fg$ for a strict arity map $g:P→T$ taking the total to $d$. All variable labels of $C$ must have proper support in $S$. Let $M$ be a frame over $f$. Suppose the two raw labellings $a_-,a_+$ used by two names of $C$ factor as
$$
                 a_-=u_- e_-,\qquad a_+=u_+ e_+ .
$$
Here $u_-,u_+$ are full labellings extending $M$, and $e_-,e_+:h_P→h_T$ are weak substitutions over $g$ which agree literally below rank $m$. Juxtaposition means composition of maps of free weak algebras. Then the arrow between the two names in $C$ is the arrow of $M$ between their evaluated vertices.

To prove this, double every top variable of $T$, inserting a formal $(m+1)$-step between its two copies, and do the same in $P$. Write $T^+$ and $P^+$ for the resulting trees. For an $m$-cell $p$ of the strict $T$ write $D(p)$ for its simultaneous step in $T^+$. It is the formal step on a top variable, the identity on either copy of a cell from below, and it respects all compositions below $m$ by strict interchange. Consequently replacing each top variable $i$ of $P$ by $D(g i)$ takes the simultaneous step of its total to $D(d)$, also when $g i$ is composite or identity-padded. [Simultaneous steps on strict pastings](../answered/relative-coherence-all-dimensions-r8-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-11)

Send the two copies of $P$ into $h_{T^+}$ by $e_-$ and $e_+$ followed by their respective copy inclusions. They agree on the common skeleton. For the step at $i$ lift $D(g i)$ at the two assigned literal ends by globular boundary lifting in the free weak tree. This gives a substitution $h_{P^+}→h_{T^+}$. Evaluate $T^+$ in $S$ using $u_-,u_+$ and any proper comparisons between their corresponding entries. The images of the steps of $P^+$ are then permissible comparisons for $C$: their strict values are identities on the proper labels $fg i$. Thus evaluation of a generic transport lift in $P^+$ computes the original transport in $C$, by independence of these comparison choices. In $T^+$ its image is a lift over $D(d)$. Its two ends have common lower poles because $e_-,e_+$ agree below $m$. Normalize these poles to the chosen poles of $T$ using the same lower pads on both sides. These pads preserve the read word and the two vertices. The normalized lift now computes transport in $M$ by the same formula. This proves the assertion with no injectivity hypothesis on $g$.

**Agreement at shared vertices.** Suppose $C,C'$ are two frames over $f$ and both contain $v,w$. At $v$ choose full labellings for the two names. Their images contain the same literal primitive head: strict monicity makes the generic marked head remain primitive after evaluation. By the common-vertex factorization of Response 9, both labellings factor through the labelling taking the shorter raw label at every variable. Do this independently at $w$. The two shorter labellings have *the same lower skeleton*, since those entries depend only on $C,C'$. Denote that frame by $M$. For either original frame, its two factorizing weak substitutions agree below $m$: there they evaluate under the same smaller labels to the same original labels, and that evaluation is injective on raw lower terms. Change of arity with $g=1_T$ proves
$$
                    T_C(v,w)=T_M(v,w)=T_{C'}(v,w).
$$
The argument allows the smaller top tuple as well as the factorizing substitutions in top rank to differ at the two vertices. [Common-vertex factorization](#combining-nondegenerate-labellings-with-a-common-vertex)

**A common lower labelling for a finite path.** For a variable $x$ below rank $m$, restrict a frame to the closed globe of $x$. Order these globe labellings by factorization: $a≤b$ means that $b$ factors through $a$ by a weak substitution over the identity of the strict globe. If $a≤b$ and the top labels differ, the top label of $a$ occurs as a proper subterm of that of $b$, by the support argument in Response 9. Equality of the top labels gives equality on the globe by taking faces.

Any two labellings $a,b$ below a common globe labelling $h$ are comparable. Indeed the top label of $h$ is a common evaluation of two writings with strict value $x$. Parse these writings together, as in Response 9. At a stopped pair with the variable $x$ on one side, the other side represents its label. Such a pair must occur: the writings use $x$ by support, and a stopped branch at a lower variable has strict value that variable and uses only its closed globe on either side. Representability at $x$ extends to its whole globe by taking faces. This proves comparability, including rank zero.

Consider a finite sequence of frames with successive shared vertices. At each $x$ the successive globe labellings are comparable by the same parsing of the shared primitive vertex in the full arity. This sequence has a least member. Inductively keep the least member seen so far. If the next labelling is above the last, keep the least. If it is below the last, it and the current least are below a common labelling and hence comparable. For each $x$ choose the label of its least member. This is a single well-typed lower labelling $L$: restriction of a least member at $x$ to any face $y$ occurs in the list at $y$ and is below all entries there. The restrictions therefore give exactly the selected labels at the faces. Each frame's lower labelling factors through $L$. Choose the expressions witnessing this on each globe. Their boundary equations hold after evaluation by construction and already hold before evaluation by injectivity at $L$. The same reasoning applies to the frame taking pointwise shorter lower labels at an adjacent pair. It gives a factorization of that frame's lower labelling through $L$. Extend $L$ to a frame on $T$ with strict labels prescribed by $f$.

**Contraction.** Write a cycle as arrows in successive frames $C_i$, with transition vertices $v_i$ shared by $C_i,C_{i+1}$, indices cyclic. At $v_i$ take the two full labellings that witness the shared primitive vertex, and their common shorter labelling $u_i$ over the frame $M_i$ just constructed for this pair. Before evaluation by $u_i$, the two factorized primitive vertices in $h_T$ are literally equal by injectivity under $u_i$. We can therefore specialize this equality at any other top tuple for $M_i$.

Choose a weak substitution on the lower skeleton expressing $M_i$ through $L$. Extend it to $h_T→h_T$ over the strict identity, filling at each top variable with the assigned literal boundary by tree contraction. Follow it by any full labelling extending $L$, which exists by proper-support lifting. The resulting full labelling extends $M_i$. Specializing the common primitive vertex there gives a vertex $v_i'$ in all three frames $C_i,C_{i+1},L$. Indeed the two original factorizations reconstruct names with the original lower labellings, and specialization through $L$ gives a name in $L$. Normalization of poles in these names preserves their primitive vertex. This step only needs one new tuple at each transition.

The path from $v_i$ to $v_i'$ has the same value in its two incident frames by agreement at shared vertices. Conjugate each edge by these paths at its ends. The new edge in $C_i$ has both ends in $L$, so it equals transport in $L$ by the same agreement result. Around the cycle the conjugating paths cancel, and the product of the transports in $L$ is the identity. This proves the theorem. Together with Response 9, the remaining ordinary obstruction is a loop in one strict subdivision that requires degenerate expansions or transports through different embedded arities within it, beyond the change-of-arity formula above.

## Directed boundary lifting through hom dimension two

Let $A$ be the free $L$-model on a pasting arity, and let $H$ be one of its forward ordinary CaTT homs. Write $K$ for the strict hom, a tensor of globular trees. For **every strict cell of $K$ in dimensions zero, one and two**, strictification $H→K$ has a lift at every prescribed parallel raw boundary. Moreover, any two parallel writings of the same strict **path** are joined by a coinductively invertible 2-cell over its identity. A path can change direction between tensor factors any number of times. These dimensions are internal to the hom, so a lifted 2-cell is a global 3-cell. The constant and backwards homs are covered by the point and empty cases of Response 7.

**Stationary whiskering in an arbitrary model.** Fix global 1-cells in a prefix and suffix of a composable string in an $L$-model $X$, with one hom between them left variable. There is a globular whiskering map $W$ from that hom to the hom of the whole string. In dimension zero choose a composition head. Construct its rank-$r$ operation on the globe $D^r$ in the variable slot and points in all the stationary slots. At the previously chosen faces, Response 8 lifts the total strict cell. The two restrictions to lower common faces agree because the choices in smaller globes were made first. Substitution thus gives a map in every dimension with literal globular face equations.

This map preserves coinductive invertibility in every positive hom dimension. For two top-composable $r$-cells, compare $W(a*b)$ with $W(a)*W(b)$ in the generic two-globe pasting $D^r∐_{D^{r-1}}D^r$ in the variable slot. They are parallel by the face equations and have the same strict total. Response 8 gives an invertible comparison in either direction. For $W(1_a)$ and $1_{W(a)}$ use the single globe of $a$, now over the identity on its total. These comparisons specialize in every $X$.

Here is the coinduction argument, since the whiskering map need only preserve these operations up to comparison. In the target ordinary hom let $I$ be its invertible cells and let $J$ be the smallest class containing $I$ and the images of the source invertibles, closed under ordinary globular pasting with its top inputs in the class. Write $Φ(J)$ for the cells having an inverse with both cancellations in $J$. The proof of FHM Theorem 3.3.7 establishes that $Φ(J)$ is pasting-closed whenever $I⊆J$ and $J$ is pasting-closed. It contains $I$. If $a$ is source-invertible, use $W(a^{-1})$ as inverse candidate. For either cancellation, first compare the composite of the images with the image of the composite, apply $W$ to the chosen source cancellation, then compare the image of the identity with the identity of the image. The outside cells belong to $I$, the middle cell belongs to $J$ by definition, and the paste belongs to $J$. Hence $Φ(J)$ also contains the images of the source invertibles. Minimality gives $J⊆Φ(J)$, so coinduction gives $J⊆I$. This proves the assertion. [Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara, proof of Theorem 3.3.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.14907)

Write $h_1H$ for the category with all objects of an ordinary weak hom and arrows its 1-cells modulo invertible 2-cells. Transitivity, congruence, associativity and the unit laws follow by pasting and ordinary coherence. Each stationary whiskering induces a functor on these categories, by the preceding preservation and comparison results. As a first consequence for the transport in Q2, whiskering with an invertible global arrow $e:x→y$ is essentially surjective on hom objects. For $t:x→z$ take $e^{-1};t$. Reassociate $e;(e^{-1};t)$, whisker the invertible cancellation $e;e^{-1}⇒1_x$ by $t$, and apply the unitor. The associator and unitor are invertible by the point-factor case of Response 8. The same argument works at the other end of a string. [The transport test, Theorem 3.2.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13240)

**Paths with specified ends.** In $h_1H$, between two objects over the same strict vertex there is a unique arrow over its identity. It is an isomorphism: that vertex is a tensor of points, so Response 8 supplies the arrow, its reverse and comparisons with identities. Likewise there is exactly one arrow over a given generating strict edge at any prescribed lifts of its two ends. Here the relevant box is one generating interval with all other factors points. Choose a raw object at each strict vertex. Any strict path has a representative between specified raw ends, by composing the chosen edge lifts and the isomorphisms over vertices. A Steiner tensor is freely generated as a strict polygraph by its atoms, so its 1-cells are words in its generating edges. [Ara–Maltsiniotis, Theorem 2.12 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.00668)

We show that **every** raw 1-cell gives this arrow in $h_1H$, by induction on its expression, simultaneously in all the homs of the arity. For a variable its strict image is a generating edge, and the assertion follows from edge uniqueness. A coherence head of global dimension two has only point factors, by the dimension and full-support rules. Its strict image, also after substitution, is an identity. For an operation head of that dimension there is exactly one factor of dimension one, an ordinary path $[k]$; all others are points. Its top input labels in that factor are consecutive raw 1-cells $b_1,\ldots,b_k$ of a single inner hom. [Directed dimension and support rules](../../agda-gray-findings.md#precisions-to-pseudo-vs-graymd-3)

First work in the head's own arity. Apply stationary whiskering to each successive edge of $[k]$ and compose the results in the outer hom. The composite has the head's strict path. Its two raw endpoints can differ from the head's chosen endpoints. Insert comparisons over the two endpoint vertices to match them literally. The composite so obtained and the head are parallel over the total of the box with active factor $[k]$. Response 8 compares them invertibly. Specialization expresses the original arrow in $h_1H$ as the composite of the $W(b_i)$ and the endpoint corrections. Each $b_i$ is a proper subexpression. By induction it is a composite of lifts of generating edges and vertex isomorphisms. Functoriality on $h_1$ permits this decomposition inside $W$. Strictly, the stationary cells contribute fixed tuples of vertices on either side. Thus $W$ takes each strict generating edge in the inner block to a generating edge in the enlarged block, and takes each vertex identity to a vertex identity. For an empty inner block only the identity case occurs.

It remains to check independence of the intermediate raw objects. At each intermediate vertex insert the unique isomorphism over its identity to the chosen raw object there and its inverse. Each corrected edge is the unique arrow over that edge at the new ends. Vertex arrows compose to the unique such arrow, including for the empty word. This proves the assertion at the original prescribed ends. In particular $h_1H→K_{\leq1}$, where the latter retains all paths and their compositions, is fully faithful and surjective on isomorphism classes of objects. Equality of arrows in $h_1H$ yields an actual invertible 2-cell between the two parallel writings. Its strict image is an identity: $K$ has no nonidentity invertible positive cells by its strong Steiner basis. Equivalently, the top chains of an inverse pair in the highest occupied dimension must sum to zero, and descent in dimension forces identity at every rank.

**Lifting a strict 2-cell.** Express it as a vertical composite of whiskered 2-atoms in the free strict polygraph. Every atom is a rectangular total, either one 2-generator or two 1-generators in different factors, with vertices elsewhere. If both exterior objects lie over the same vertex, strong loop-freeness allows only an identity path and an identity 2-cell there; the point-box result gives the lift directly.
