# Free higher categories with directed interchange: classifying what lies above the least pasting profile

*A self-contained problem. No background beyond standard higher category theory is assumed. Everything in §2 is proved and is not re-asked. The constructions summarized in (K43)–(K53) are defined in full in the round-5 answers, which are reproduced in the repository file `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r5-2026-11-20.md` (Responses 1–34), with Responses 8–9 of `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r4-2026-11-19.md`. (K52)–(K53) are from `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r6-2026-11-20.md`, (K54)–(K57) from `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r7-2026-11-20.md`, (K58)–(K64) from `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r8-2026-11-20.md` (Responses 1–2), and (K65)–(K73) from `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r9-2026-11-20.md` (Responses 1–5). The summaries below fix notation.*

**What we are asking for.** On free objects of finite pasting diagrams there is a least admissible theory V ((K58)). Its free objects have normal forms by rank over the comparison to T₁₂ ((K65)). The profiles above V are now known to be wild:
- they contain infinite antichains and divisibility ((K70));
- they do not form a lattice, and admissibility is not closed under finite unions ((K71));
- not every profile is V plus laws already typed over V ((K72)).

A finite set of equations true in T₁₂ is always admissible over V ((K73)). A type checker built on this offers four settings: V, V + {R}, V + {inner law} and T₁₂ = V + both, where R is the right split law and the inner law is the inner composite-crossing law of (K73). We now ask for one thing: **decidable equality and reachability in the free objects of these four settings, in every dimension**, with normal forms and computable comparison maps between them. Results for one more shape or one more degree are not what we want; a uniform argument is.

## 1. Setting

**Globular sets and composition.** Work with reflexive globular sets with source and target maps s, t, identities, and for k < n a partial composition ∗ₖ of n-cells along k-cells. Composition is written in **diagrammatic order** (u ∗ₖ v means "first u, then v"), and it is strictly associative and unital. The boundary formulas hold: for k < m and (m+1)-cells u, v,

```
s(u ∗ₖ v) = s(u) ∗ₖ s(v),     t(u ∗ₖ v) = t(u) ∗ₖ t(v).        (B)
```

For k < l < n and n-cells in interchange position put `L(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₗ b) ∗ₖ (c ∗ₗ d)` and `R(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₖ c) ∗ₗ (b ∗ₖ d)`. A strict ω-category requires L = R.

**The Gray case (directed interchange along 0-cells only).** In a category enriched in strict ω-categories with the lax Gray tensor product (Ara–Maltsiniotis, arXiv 1607.00668), for 2-cells α : f ⇒ f′ : x → y and β : g ⇒ g′ : y → z there is a 3-cell

```
χ_{α,β} : (f ◁ β) ∗₁ (α ▷ g′)  ⇛  (α ▷ g) ∗₁ (f′ ◁ β)
```

and nothing in the reverse direction. Composition along k-cells with k ≥ 1 is strict. For a finite globular pasting diagram Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ, the free such structure has homs ν(λΘᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘⱼ). Here λ is Steiner's directed complex, ⊗ is Steiner's tensor with `∂(x ⊗ y) = ∂x ⊗ y + (−1)^{|x|} x ⊗ ∂y`, and ν is Steiner's functor to strict ω-categories (Steiner, HHA 2004). Each hom is generated by tuples of cells, and a cell is determined by the set of generators it uses.

**The aim** is the same for directed interchange along k-cells for **every** k, including inside homs.

## 2. What is already known (all proved)

**(K1) Raw higher interchangers are impossible.** If every pair of n-cells in interchange position had an (n+1)-cell L → R, then applying this to identities and using (B) forces L = R one dimension down. That contradicts directedness.

**(K2) A single comparison for each simultaneous variation is impossible.** The obvious repair of (K1) adds, for 3-cells A : a ⇛ a′, …, D : d ⇛ d′ in interchange position, a 4-cell between the parallel 3-cells

```
L(A,B,C,D) ∗₂ χ_{a′}     and     χ_a ∗₂ R(A,B,C,D).        (N)
```

This cannot be required for every instance. Take Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², with two 3-cells A : α ⇛ α′ and B : β ⇛ β′ side by side along a 0-cell, and the instance (A, 1, 1, B). Its two sides map to the hom 2-cells

```
U = (v₀ ∘₀ u₁) ∘₁ c₁₁,     V = c₀₀ ∘₁ (u₀ ∘₀ v₁)
```

of ν(λD² ⊗ λD²), where `u_j = A ⊗ g_j`, `v_i = f_i ⊗ B` and `c_ij = α_i ⊗ β_j`. There is no 4-cell between U and V in either direction: the degree-3 chain would need a positive coefficient at u₀ (or at v₀), but every basis element of degree 3 has a non-positive coefficient there.

**(K3) The two variables have opposite naturality directions.** The degree-3 basis elements are `r_j = A ⊗ β_j` and `t_i = α_i ⊗ B`. Their differentials are

```
∂r_j = c_1j + u₁ − c_0j − u₀,     ∂t_i = c_i0 + v₁ − c_i1 − v₀.
```

So when only A varies, the Gray structure has a naturality 4-cell from the "χ first" side to the "variation first" side (V ⇛ U). When only B varies, it goes the other way (U ⇛ V). The simultaneous variation is the 5-cell A ⊗ B. It goes between two routes of 4-cells, `S → V → T` ⇛ `S → U → T`, where S and T are the composites through c₀₁ and c₁₀. In short, the higher structure is **tensor-shaped**: one Leibniz-signed naturality cell per variable, and higher cells between routes.

**(K4) Supports must use an expanded basis.** If every composite's support were the union of the supports of its parts, L and R would always have equal support. Then "support determines the cell" would force strict interchange.

**(K5) One finite basis forces finitely many cells.** If one finite set B admits injective support maps in every dimension, the structure has finitely many non-identity cells in total.

**(K6) No single directed complex presents the answer, and neither does a hom-wise family of them.** In ν(K), the two composites defining χ have the same chain table, so they are equal. A description must therefore be an iterated tower: objects, then homs presented again by objects and homs, and so on.

**(K7) The first uniform case through hom-dimension 3.** For Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², directing interchange inside the hom 𝒦(x, z) adds exactly two generators of hom-dimension 3 beyond the 25 tensor generators. They are the interchangers χ₁ of (A⊗g, f′⊗B) and χ₂ of (f⊗B, A⊗g′), the only pairs of hom-2-cells composable along a hom-0-cell. Their two sides have different supports. Neither occurs in the boundary of A ⊗ B.

**(K8) The (K7) 3-skeleton cannot be extended by 4-generators alone.** Write h_i^j = α_i ⊗ g_j, k_j^i = f_i ⊗ β_j, P_ij = k_j^0 h_i^1, Q_ij = h_i^0 k_j^1, so c_ij : P_ij ⇒ Q_ij. The tensor 3-generators are r_j : c_0j ; a_j^+ ⇛ a_j^- ; c_1j and t_i : b_i^- ; c_i1 ⇛ c_i0 ; b_i^+ (a_j^±, b_i^± the whiskered edge cells u_j, v_i), and the interchangers are χ₁ : b_0^+ ; a_1^+ ⇛ a_0^+ ; b_1^+ and χ₂ : a_0^- ; b_1^- ⇛ b_0^- ; a_1^-. The six composite hom-2-cells P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ are S = b_0^- ; c_01 ; a_1^+, V₀ = c_00 ; b_0^+ ; a_1^+, V₁ = c_00 ; a_0^+ ; b_1^+, T = a_0^- ; c_10 ; b_1^+, U₀ = a_0^- ; b_1^- ; c_11 and U₁ = b_0^- ; a_1^- ; c_11. Whiskering gives t̄₀ : S ⇛ V₀, χ̄₁ : V₀ ⇛ V₁, r̄₀ : V₁ ⇛ T, r̄₁ : S ⇛ U₁, χ̄₂ = χ₂ ∗₁ 1_{c₁₁} : U₀ ⇛ U₁ and t̄₁ : U₀ ⇛ T. Since ∂(A⊗B) = t₁ − t₀ + r₁ − r₀, requirement 4 forces a 4-cell between the route t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀ from S to T and a route from S to T using r̄₁ and t̄₁. In the free 3-skeleton with exactly the 25 tensor generators plus χ₁, χ₂ and no 2-equations, the first route exists, but the second would have to go S ⇛ U₁ and then U₁ ⇛ U₀ against χ̄₂: in the directed graph of these composites the only S → T path is t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀. So no list of 4-generators recovers A ⊗ B. The naturality cells of χ₁, χ₂ are trivial here (the edge homs have no 3-generators).

The repair must change dimension-3 data: an extra 3-generator such as h : S ⇛ U₀ whose image under the Gray quotient is r̄₁, or a 2-equation such as U₀ = U₁. Inverting χ₂ would also work but breaks requirement 3.

**(K9) The orientation of χ₁, χ₂ in (K7) is forced by substitution (proved).** One might try to avoid (K8) by orienting every inner interchanger by the factor order of the enclosing tensor (always "Θ₂-cell first ⇛ Θ₁-cell first"), which keeps χ₁ and reverses χ₂. This fails requirement 5. The free object on Σ(D² ∨ D²) has, inside its hom, the interchanger of its two 2-cells A′ (left) and B′ (right), oriented as in the Gray case: "B′ first ⇛ A′ first". The assignment A′ ↦ f₀ ⊗ B and B′ ↦ A ⊗ g₁ (with the hom-0-cells and hom-1-cells sent to f₀⊗g₀, f₀⊗g₁, f₁⊗g₁ and to the matching edges) respects all boundaries, so substitution sends that interchanger to a 3-cell from "A ⊗ g₁ first" to "f₀ ⊗ B first", which is exactly χ₂ in its (K7) orientation. The pair χ₁ is forced the same way via A′ ↦ A ⊗ g₀, B′ ↦ f₁ ⊗ B. So χ₂ cannot be reversed: adding the reversed cell as well breaks requirement 3, which is the "inverting χ₂" option above. Inner interchangers are oriented by position (right cell first ⇛ left cell first), as at the outer level, and some repair of dimension-3 data is needed (see (K10)).

**(K10) The smallest case is repaired by a factorization.** The six hom-2 composites P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ of (K8) are the maximal paths of an oriented 3-cube with coordinates A (edges a_j^±), B (edges b_i^±) and C (edges c_ij): S = BCA, V₀ = CBA, V₁ = CAB, T = ACB, U₀ = ABC, U₁ = BAC. The six whiskered hom-3 cells t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀, r̄₁, χ̄₂, t̄₁ are its six square faces (adjacent transpositions); every proper interval carries at most one of them. Add one hom-3 generator and one hom-3 equation:

```
h : S ⇛ U₀,        r̄₁ = h ∗₂ χ̄₂.        (F)
```

Then the maximal hom-3 interval is the free category on S → V₀ → V₁ → T, S → U₀ → U₁, U₀ → T, with 11 non-identity cells with pairwise distinct label sets, two routes S → T (t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ and h ; t̄₁), and no reverse of χ̄₁ or χ̄₂. Killing the inner interchangers gives exactly the free Gray diamond with q(h) = q(r̄₁), and A ⊗ B lifts to one 4-generator W : t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ ⇛ h ; t̄₁ with no non-identity contexts. Supports use one label per whiskered context, so r̄₁ is no longer a basis element (its support is that of h χ̄₂). The equation (F) cannot be dropped or replaced by a 4-cell h χ̄₂ ⇛ r̄₁ without a new class of cells killed by the quotient. h is not an interchanger: S and U₀ each contain a c_ij, and every hom-2 path containing a c_ij spans the whole external hom-0 interval.

h is **not forced**. The mirror repair k : U₁ ⇛ T with t̄₁ = χ̄₂ ∗₂ k works equally well, and so does the symmetric repair with both h and k and both equations (12 cells, second route h ; χ̄₂ ; k, same exact Gray quotient). The alternative 2-equation U₀ = U₁ makes χ̄₂ an endomorphism and needs further equations (at least χ̄₂² = χ̄₂ under set supports), so it is not preferred.

**(K11) The first larger case, ΣD³ ∨ ΣD², is completed through its top cell.** Write the first factor as α₀, α₁ : f₀ → f₁, A⁰, A¹ : α₀ ⇒ α₁, Ω : A⁰ ⇛ A¹, and the second as β₀, β₁ : g₀ → g₁, B : β₀ ⇒ β₁. - **Hom-4 faces.** W^ε = A^ε ⊗ B : a^ε b^ε c^ε ⇛ h^ε f^ε. R_j = Ω ⊗ β_j. The naturalities N₁, N₂ of χ₁, χ₂ have opposite directions: Ω varies the left input of χ₁ and the right input of χ₂. Two inner interchangers along a hom-1 cell, 𝖠 : z_S a¹ ⇛ a⁰ z_{V₀} (of t₀ with Ω) and 𝖥 : f⁰ z_T ⇛ z_{U₀} f¹. Writing 𝖡, 𝖢, 𝖣, 𝖤 for the whiskered N₁, R₀, R₁, N₂, the maximal hom-4 interval has the seven hom-3 paths x₁ … x₇ from S⁰ to T¹.
- **Added data.** Two hom-4 generators and two equations:
  ```
  H : h⁰ z_{U₀} ⇛ z_S h¹,                H e¹ = (h⁰ 𝖤) ; 𝖣                 (variation of the factorization)
  K : a⁰ z_{V₀} b¹ c¹ ⇛ z_S h¹ f¹,       z_S W¹ = (𝖠 b¹ c¹) ; K            (k-type factorization in hom-dimension 4)
  ```
  Then there are exactly two routes x₁ ⇛ x₇: L = (W⁰ z_T) ; (h⁰ 𝖥) ; (H f¹) and R = (a⁰ b⁰ 𝖢) ; (a⁰ 𝖡 c¹) ; K. The top generator Z = Ω ⊗ B : L ⇛ R has the orientation of ∂(Ω ⊗ B) = W¹ − W⁰ − R₁ + R₀.
- **Checks.** On the maximal interval there are 68 hom-3 cells and 98 hom-4 cells, all with distinct supports; the graphs are acyclic, and the reductions have no overlaps. The Gray quotient is exact: q(H) = q(R₁ whiskered), q(K) = q(z_S W¹), and the surviving tensor generators are R₀, R₁, W⁰, W¹.
- **Forcing substitution.** A⁰, A¹ ↦ A, Ω ↦ 1_A sends H ↦ 1_h, K ↦ W and Z ↦ 1_W, so K is not a cell that every degeneration kills.
- **The source/sink pattern.** In both the h-repair and the k-repair of (K10), the face 𝖠 (whose pair flips relative to the tensor faces) creates a second source and a second sink one dimension up. With source-joining (h) in hom-dimension 3, the one-face joining that works in hom-dimension 4 is sink-joining (K); a source-joining alternative would have to factor a composite. Each existing factorization also needs a variation cell and equation under each higher variable (H).

**(K12) The source/sink count fails in the ambient graph.** Reversing one arrow u → v of a finite directed graph changes the number of sources by 1[d⁻(v)=1] − 1[d⁻(u)=0], and the number of sinks by 1[d⁺(u)=1] − 1[d⁺(v)=0].
- Take the interval of λD² ⊗ λD² ⊗ λD² with hom-1 boundary (β₀α₀)γ₀ → (α₁β₁)γ₁. Its hom-2 paths are the 24 orderings of A, B (updates of the first two factors), C (the crossing βα → αβ) and D (the third-factor update).
- Its arrows are BC → CB, CA → AC and DX → XD. The arrow between A and B is positional: AB → BA when C follows the pair, and BA → AB when C precedes it.
- Compare with the graph that uses BA → AB everywhere, which has one source, DBCA, and one sink, ACBD. The positional graph has sources {DBCA, DABC} and sinks {ACBD, BACD}.
- Of the three reversed arrows, BADC → ABDC creates neither a source nor a sink.

So a flipped face does not create one extra source/sink pair in the full comparison graph. It does so only for an isolated polygon.

**(K13) Local factorization does not force alternation.** For a simple oriented polygon with arms pᵢ : sᵢ → tᵢ and qᵢ : sᵢ₊₁ → tᵢ (indices mod r), add hᵢ : s₀ → sᵢ for 1 ≤ i < r, with p₀ = h₁;q₀ and hᵢ;pᵢ = hᵢ₊₁;qᵢ for 1 ≤ i < r − 1. The boundary telescopes to h_{r−1}+p_{r−1}−q_{r−1}, a pair of parallel paths s₀ → t_{r−1}. So source-joining alone always works locally. For r = 2 this is (K10). For r ≥ 3 the equations identify two composites; this is harmless locally (K16).

**(K15) The interchanger-of-a-composite convention.** The substitution in (K14) assumes that crossing against a composite hom-1 path is the composite of the pairwise crossings. That holds in Gray categories, but it does not follow from (B), associativity and units alone.

**(K16) Source-only polygons keep injective supports (proved).** In (K13), every relation runs from the unique source s₀ to a sink, so no relation has a context. The presented category is thin except at (s₀, t_{r−1}), which has two arrows. Orienting the relations gives a one-step normal form, and the edge set of the normal form is an injective support for every r and every arm length. A union-preserving injective invariant with 2|V| + 1 labels also exists. Caveat: overlapping systems such as a;c = b;d, a;d = b;c have no injective union-support.

**(K17) Only one sink matters, and the top cell lifts (proved).**
- *The sink.* Undirected inner interchanges split the 240 words of (K14) into 54 classes. These are the fibres of the tensor hom-2 chain. Only one of the ten sinks, T = apqbrc, lies in the class of the top atom's hom-2 target. The source is S = crqbpa.
- *Paths.* The words reaching T carry 244 arrows and 1,226 paths S → T, with 25 distinct tensor hom-3 chains.
- *The lift.* Keeping all hom-3 data, the top cell lifts as follows:
  - 24 hom-4 operations A_{ij} : P_i ⇒ P_j between chosen paths, 15 of them new and 9 literal whiskerings of the W-cells;
  - six parallel hom-4 routes R₀ … R₅ : P₂₄ ⇒ P₀;
  - six hom-5 generators F_x^±, F_y^±, F_z^± between those routes;
  - one hom-6 generator Ω : F_x^- F_y^- F_z^- ⇒ F_z^+ F_y^+ F_x^+, whose image is the atom x₂ ⊗ y₂ ⊗ z₂ in every dimension.
- *Scope.* This is an extension with a map to ν(K), not yet one whose inner-interchange quotient is ν(K).

**(K18) Source-only completion of a comparison graph (proved).** Let Q be a finite quiver whose generating arrows are split into inner arrows E and positive arrows P, with E acyclic and a rank φ : V(Q) → ℤ constant along E and strictly increasing along P. For every α : s → t in P and every literal directed E-path R : u → t, adjoin j_{α,R} : s → u (with j_{α,1_t} = α), and impose j_{α,e;R} ; e = j_{α,R} for e : u → v in E and R : v → t. Then:
- the set of generators is finite and does not depend on the order of saturation;
- read as contractions (positive arrow followed by an inner arrow), the equations are confluent and terminating, so every path has a unique normal form;
- the completion is acyclic, normal forms have injective edge-set supports, the original path category embeds, and killing E gives exactly the original inner quotient.

On the 240-word interval of (K14) a rank with values 0, …, 7 exists; there are 264 positive seed arrows and 877 positive generators after completion (613 new, 1,117 arrows in all), and 54 inner classes. The completion is natural under maps of comparison categories that send positive seeds to positive seeds and inner arrows to inner paths.

**(K19) The completion is not natural under generator-to-composite maps (proved).** In (K14), take x = bacrqp, u = abpcqr, w₁ = abcpqr, w₂ = bapcqr, v = bacpqr, with inner arrows p : u → w₁, e : w₁ → v, q : u → w₂, f : w₂ → v, and the tensor arrow τ : x → v with its factorization τ = h_z ; f. The completion has j_{τ,e} : x → w₁ with j_{τ,e} ; e = τ. The comparison-category map fixing inner arrows and sending τ ↦ h_z ; f does not extend: an image H : x → w₁ with H ; e = h_z ; f would be j_{h_z,R} ; P with inner paths R, P and P ; e = R ; f as literal inner words, impossible since e ≠ f (contractions preserve the ordered list of positive seeds). This map has not been realised as a substitution of globular generators, so it does not refute requirement 5.

**(K20) Two-sided division is not confluent (proved).** In (K14), with tensor arrows α : bapcqr → bapqcr and β : abpqcr → abpqrc and the inner arrow e : abpqcr → bapqcr, adjoining both a source division h : bapcqr → abpqcr (h ; e = α) and a sink division k : bapqcr → abpqrc (e ; k = β) gives the critical pair h ; e ; k → α ; k and → h ; β, both irreducible and distinct. The presented category satisfies the composite–composite equation α ; k = h ; β. It is not of the crossed shape a;c = b;d, a;d = b;c.

**(K21) Hom-4 comparisons and exactness through hom-dimension 3 (proved).**
- *Disjoint operations.* For two hom-3 operations α (left) and β (right) on disjoint intervals of a hom-2 word, D_{α,β} : (β ; α^β) ⇛ (α ; β^α), right region first ⇛ left region first. On the 240-word interval there are 258 distinct such boundary pairs (50 need eliminated operations expanded through the factorization equations of (K14)).
- *Inner naturality.* For a hom-3 operation α : U ⇛ V and an independent hom-2 update z, comparing "slide z across U, then α" with "α, then slide z across V": for factor degrees (2,1) inner crossing first ⇛ operation first, for (1,2) the reverse. This agrees with the Leibniz signs of (K3). There are 62 distinct local comparisons N. They use (K15) as a convention.
- *Exactness.* On the top atom's hom-1 boundary, imposing I = D = N = 1 (I the inner interchangers) gives 54 hom-2 classes and 874 hom-3 path classes, which are exactly the numbers of 2-cells and 3-cells of ν(λD² ⊗ λD² ⊗ λD²) with that 1-boundary (identities included). Without N it is not exact: the single-arrow equations leave 152 generator classes over 116 tableaux, and two-arrow equations cannot merge them.
- The D and N comparisons introduce no reverse path in the comparison graph they generate on the raw hom-3 paths.

## 3. Requirements

A **tensor-shaped lax-interchange ω-category** has, for every k < l, elementary interchangers χ : L ⇛ R for (l+1)-cells with no reverse cell in free objects; one naturality cell per varying argument, oriented by the Leibniz sign; higher cells between routes of lower naturality cells; and coherence equations to be determined. The free objects F(Γ) must satisfy:
1. an explicit combinatorial description by generators with explicit boundaries;
2. two cells of the same dimension using the same generators, in the expanded basis, are equal;
3. every elementary interchanger exists and its reverse does not;
4. making all interchangers with k ≥ 1 identities gives the free Gray-enriched category, and making all interchangers identities gives the free strict ω-category;
5. closure under substitution of cells for generators.

**Packing laws of one output degree.** Any finite list of initially parallel equations of one positive output degree \(d\) is effectively equivalent over \(V\) to a *single* initially parallel pasting-diagram equation of degree \(d\). Conversely a tuple on the interval extends by constant tuples on the exterior slots. This proves equivalence on all \(V\)-models. In an empty outer interval the free terms are iterated identities at the vertex, so its law already holds. An empty list can be represented by a reflexive equation.

\[
              u_1 *_0 \cdots *_0 u_h = v_1 *_0\cdots *_0 v_h .

The full-interval condition supplies the outer matches, and parallelism of each pair supplies parallelism of the pastes. The component equations imply this equation. ### The maximal size function

Here is one exact series for the maximum as a function of \((\Gamma,d)\), allowing \(+\infty\) for finite fibres of unbounded size. It also specifies the effectivity of this description. Number the finite proposed *canonical* decorated tilings at this arity and degree by distinct natural codes, including the target normal form. Validity of a code is decidable by the finite cut and pattern tests. Number recursively at lower ranks, fixing all edge orders, so each cell has a unique code. For the valid codes among \(1,\ldots,N\), form the zero-one matrix \(K_N\) with entry one when their target normal forms agree. Put \((X)_j=X(X-I)\cdots(X-(j-1)I)\). Define
\[
  C_j(\Gamma,d)=\sum_{N\ge1}2^{-N^2}\operatorname{tr}((K_N)_j)/j!,
  \qquad
  M(\Gamma,d)=\sum_{j\ge1} {\bf1}_{\,C_j(\Gamma,d)>0}.
\]
This formula takes only the diagram and degree as input. Indeed a block of \(k\) codes with the same target is the matrix of all ones; its contribution to the trace divided by \(j!\) is \(\binom{k}{j}\). All coefficients are nonnegative and the tail is bounded by \(\sum_{N>L}2^{-N^2}2^N\), independently of \(j\). Thus the \(C_j\) are uniformly computable reals. Their positive indices form an initial segment: \(C_j>0\) exactly when one can exhibit \(j\) distinct valid decorations of a common target. Every finite set of decorations occurs at a finite \(N\). This proves the formula, including when no single cell attains a largest size. Each proposed lower bound is semidecidable; the formula is a limit description and supplies no general finite stopping rule for declaring a computed finite maximum final.

Through the common 3-skeleton the maximum is one. On the suspended strip of (K66) in degree four it is \(2^{m-1}\): a path on any subinterval crosses at most \(m\) advances, its stops have at most that many cut subsets, and the full interval attains the bound. For \(m\ge1\) the same bound holds on this strip in every higher degree. A degree-five positional square would need two positive 4-cells on consecutive 3-intervals, both using the unique left advance, whereas the two intervals have disjoint advances. At the branching spatial cut, a positive 4-cell requires both short spans and leaves no active partner for a variation or join. Outer operations have no second span and the strip has no 5-globe. Hence there are only identities at five and above, by the positive immediate-face property of the higher declarations. ### Finite shape obstructions and a uniform injectivity test

For any fixed \(d\) and \(k\) there is a finite set of shape obstructions for \(M(\Gamma,d)\ge k\). There is also a finite set, for each \(d\), for \(M(\Gamma,d)=+\infty\). To see this, truncate the nesting of a tree just far enough to retain its generating globular set through \(d\). At depth \(d\) retain the vertex string, replacing each suspended child there by the suspension of a point. This tree of depth at most \(d+1\) has exactly the same generators and faces through \(d\). The native path presentations at the retained ranks use only that data, since the guarded equations already hold there and adding free globular generators in higher degrees imposes no lower relation.

Order these bounded-depth shapes recursively by taking a subsequence of slots at each vertex and applying the same order to the retained subtrees. If \(\Delta\) is obtained from \(\Gamma\) this way, collapsing the other slots has a structural section. In each gap choose a lowest-dimensional arrow from each omitted slot. Group the string into blocks each containing one retained slot, pad its lifted generators by the chosen arrows in that block, and recurse inside the retained slots. If all slots are omitted choose a vertex. The composite with collapse is identity on the generators. Evaluating these same structural substitutions in both theories therefore embeds each fibre on \(\Delta\) into a fibre on \(\Gamma\). [Structural section on a pruned tree](../answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r8-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-2)

The bounded-depth order is a well-quasi-order by induction: finite strings over a well-quasi-order are well-quasi-ordered by subsequence embedding. Both stated properties are upward closed, hence each is generated by finitely many shapes. For the first property one can compute a basis uniformly with the halting oracle. Starting with no shapes, ask whether some diagram outside their upward closures exhibits \(k\) distinct accepted decorations of one target. This is an existential search through finite witnesses. When a witness exists, append its diagram. An infinite iteration would give a sequence with no earlier diagram below a later one. On termination, keep the minimal shapes. For infinity replace the test on a proposed diagram by \(\forall k\,\exists\) a \(k\)-witness; searching outside the present closures now uses at most the third Turing jump. This gives pattern tests and oracle bounds independent of any convergence rate in the series. [Higman's subsequence theorem](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s3-2.1.326)

Equality of the two 4-words is decidable in \(F_V(\Gamma)\). A failing instance gives distinct lifts of the common \(T_{12}\)-value, as do their iterated identities in every higher rank. ## The exact maximum on every ordinary two-chain grid

Write \(M_d(\Gamma)\) for the supremum of the sizes of the whole degree-\(d\) comparison fibres. An unbounded supremum does not imply that an individual fibre is infinite, by (K65). For \(\Gamma_{m,n}=\Sigma[m]\vee\Sigma[n]\) the answer is
\[
M_d(\Gamma_{m,n})=
\begin{cases}
1,&d\le3\text{ or }\min(m,n)\le2\text{ or }m=n=3,\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
In the infinite cases there are fibres of size at least \(2^j\) over powers of one rank-four cell on this *fixed* diagram; all the lifts have the same immediate faces. For \(d>4\), their iterated identities give the same lower bound on whole fibres.

The two theories have the same three-skeleton. On a grid every positive 4-letter is a contextual \(\iota(F,G)\) on spatially disjoint 3-histories. A history using only one row or one column is, up to exterior stationary letters, the literal crossing of that row or column with a contiguous block; its order is forced. To split an operand whose partner has this form, move the outside padding out of \(\iota\) and the intervening gap to the operand being split, by (9). Now the partner is literal and the restricted split applies. With at most two rows (or two columns) every proper spatial pair has one available row (or one column) per argument. With at most three of each, a nonliteral partner would need at least two rows and two columns, leaving at most one of each for the other argument, so the latter cannot split. Thus all missing unconstrained rank-four splits hold under every grid assignment in the finite cases. The round-5 presentation only has the two optional positive splits at rank four; at higher ranks the extra outer unguarded equations belong to \(B\) and the guarded tests already hold in \(F_B\) by (K58). Therefore the whole free grid table in these cases is itself a \(T_{12}\)-model; its free comparison is an isomorphism.

**The threshold grid \(3\times4\).** In (K34), take the first row-group to be \(a_1\), the second to be \(a_2a_3\), and the column-groups \(b_1b_2,b_3b_4\). The crossing in the spatially first middle operand \(F=\chi(a_1,b_1b_2)\) has two events \(f_1,f_2\) in that order. The other middle operand \(G=\chi(a_2a_3,b_3b_4)\) has two atomic schedules \(pyxq\) and \(pxyq\), with \(x,y\) the opposite middle corners of its \(2\times2\) square. The first and last macro-corners of (K34) supply fixed 3-prefix and suffix. With these pads, any actual sequence moving \(F\) past \(G\) by positional exchanges, from right operand first to left operand first, is a loop at the same full block in \(V\) by outer (K15).

Make the first loop \(L_0\) by processing \(G\)'s events in the order \(q,x,y,p\). At each event move \(f_1\) and then \(f_2\) past it. ## A co-r.e. criterion for finite equation lists preserving effective rank

## Uniform normalization with a halting oracle

For an arbitrary finite equation list enumerate the finite typing and equality derivations of \(V+S\). At stage \(j\) take the incidence matrix on the terms and valid equality edges enumerated so far, retaining earlier entries. Equality of two given typed terms is equivalent to their vertex difference belonging to the column span at some finite stage. ## Shared reachability is coNP-complete on a fixed doubled-loop grid

For **each of the four settings**, deciding whether a 5-cell exists between shared chronological products of two specified 4-loops on the fixed diagram \(\Gamma_{2,4}\) is coNP-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions. Consequently the uniform shared-input reachability problem for each setting is coNP-hard. On exactly the same inputs equality has a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm.

Let \(E,L\) be the early and late copies of \(H\), based by whiskering at the composite of the two consecutive doubled blocks. The doubled-grid theorem says that words in these loops are free and that the 5-reachability relation on them is generated by the adjacent forward move \(LE\to EL\). This is a statement about all ambient witnesses, so allowing a search to visit arbitrary intermediate cells does not create shortcuts. The two-row normal forms identify the endpoint theories through degree six. Each intermediate setting maps between them, with the same normalized 4-letters and the same degree-five operation list. [Doubled-grid reachability theorem](../answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r5-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-2) · [Comparison through degree six](../answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r9-2026-11-20.md#the-doubled-grids-through-hom-degree-five)

For equal multiplicities, \(u\to v\) holds precisely if every prefix of \(v\) contains at least as many \(E\)'s as the equally long prefix of \(u\). Each directed swap raises one prefix count. Conversely the condition places the \(j\)-th \(E\) of \(v\) weakly before the \(j\)-th of \(u\) for every \(j\). Working from left to right, slide each successive \(E\) across the intervening \(L\)'s to its required position. With an SLP, precompute the length and the \(E\)-count at each node by addition. A guessed bad prefix index has polynomial bit length. A descent from the root, adding the counts of the skipped left children, verifies it in polynomial time. A mismatch of total multiplicities is decidable directly. This gives coNP membership on the stated inputs. Freeness and deterministic SLP word equality give the equality upper bound.

**Hardness.** Take binary nonnegative subset-sum data \(w_1,\ldots,w_h,t\), and put \(M=1+\max(t,\sum w_i)\). Use two binary strings of length \(2^h M\), viewed as \(2^h\) blocks of width \(M\). In block \(j\) place a single 1 in the first string at offset \(s(j)\), the sum selected by the bits of \(j\) (least significant bit selects \(w_1\)). In the second string place it at offset \(t\) in every block. The strings have a common 1 position exactly on yes instances. This is the subset-marker construction for compressed strings. [Bertoni, Choffrut and Radicioni, *Literal Shuffle of Compressed Words*](https://dl.ifip.org/index.html/db/conf/ifipTCS/ifipTCS2008/BertoniCR08.pdf)

Here are the actual grammar sizes. Start with \(W_0=1\). Having built the first \(2^i\) markers up to and including the last one, set
\[
 W_{i+1}=W_i\,0^{\,2^iM+w_{i+1}-|W_i|}\,W_i .
\]
The second copy starts at its prescribed shift. The exponent is nonnegative since \(|W_i|=(2^i-1)M+\sum_{k\le i}w_k+1\). Pad \(W_h\) with trailing zeros to the prescribed length. The second grammar is the power of \(0^t1\,0^{M-t-1}\). Binary powering and sharing the two references to \(W_i\) give total size polynomial in the binary instance.

For the source loop word replace a first-string 1 by \(EL\) and a 0 by \(LE\). For the target replace a second-string 1 by \(LE\) and a 0 by \(EL\). Every even prefix has equal counts. At the midpoint of a two-letter block the source exceeds the target precisely when both bits are 1. Thus a directed filler exists exactly on **no** instances of subset sum. The translation sends each grammar join to chronological composition at one fixed boundary and each terminal to two fixed loops. No equation, generator, dimension or diagram depends on the arithmetic instance. This proves the hardness assertion. The prefix witness certifies failure of reachability. It does not certify failure of any proposed polynomial equality algorithm for the full signature.

## Shared immediate whiskering on rigid paths with free lower pads

There is a deterministic polynomial equality algorithm for a class of constructor DAGs in which expanding the structural operations can create exponentially many **distinct** contextual letters. It works in any of the four settings and any degree \(d\ge5\). Take parallel inputs built from identities, proper rigid letters with classified intrinsic data, chronological composition, and fixed left or right whiskering at the immediately lower chronological level (cut \(d-2\)). Assume the fixed lower pads and the corresponding initial pad records are SLP words in a faithful free path alphabet: two such words of matching type describe the same lower pad exactly when their spellings agree. The empty pad is typed at its incident face. Intrinsic records include all the data interior to these two immediate pads. A supplied SLP for a lower word may be shared throughout. This extends Response 2's rigid-only procedure to actual whiskering nodes. The number of distinct expanded contextual letters need not be polynomial.

In the structural normal records, surrounding a path by pads \(a,b\) sends the record \((p,g,s)\) of each letter to \((ap,g,sb)\). This is functoriality of fixed whiskering, with immediate pads on each side concatenated. Use the reversed spelling of \(s\) as the second coordinate; both coordinates then change by prepending a fixed word. Rigid factorization gives equality precisely when the resulting sequences of records agree. With parallel roots the empty cases are determined by positivity and the common boundary. [Structural contextual records on the grid](../answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r9-2026-11-20.md#the-doubled-grids-through-hom-degree-five)

For two successive values \(x,y\) of one pad coordinate, cancel their longest common prefix, obtaining tails \((r,t)\). Prepending the same pad to \(x\) and \(y\) preserves this pair. Conversely, given \(x\) and the pair from a valid record sequence, remove its suffix \(r\) and append \(t\) to recover \(y\). Consequently the first pad pair, the intrinsic letters in order and these two-coordinate changes between successive letters are a complete invariant. The types of empty component words are recovered from the intrinsic record and the matching boundary. Only word equality, cancellation in the supplied free alphabet, and equality of the intrinsic labels are used.

At each DAG node store the first and last pad pairs as word SLPs and the internal record sequence as a token SLP. An atom contributes its intrinsic name. A whisker updates the four end words and reuses the whole token SLP. A chronological join inserts **one** intervening token for the changes from the last pair on the left to the first pair on the right. Classify these at most linearly many tokens by equality of their four tail words. Empty factors require no token. It remains to compare the first pair and the two token SLPs at the roots.

The end-word grammars before slicing have linear size. For each join, binary search on prefix length computes a longest common prefix using compressed word equality. An interval of a grammar word is an ordered concatenation of fully covered subtrees and at most two partial boundary descents, giving \(O(h)\) pieces at grammar height \(h\). Building the prefix and tail SLPs costs polynomial time. All length integers have polynomial bit length, and comparison of two token or pad SLPs is deterministic polynomial time. Computing all comparisons in the same workspace is sound because only the ancestors of each queried root are needed. [Compressed-string equality](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CPM.2017.2)

**A typed example.** On the doubled grid of Response 4 use its square \(D:LE\to EL\). Start with \(P_0=D\); given \(P_i:u_i\to v_i\), take \(P_{i+1}=(P_i\,u_i);(v_i\,P_i)\). Its lower faces are \(u_{i+1}=u_i^2, v_{i+1}=v_i^2\), all loops at one 3-cell. Freeness of the two lower loop letters supplies the pad hypothesis. The \(j\)-th occurrence in a sweep of \(N=2^i\) sites, indexed from zero, has left pad \((EL)^j\) and right pad \((LE)^{N-1-j}\). All \(N\) contextual letters differ. Grouping the same sweep into quarters gives the same value; doing the right batch first gives a different rigid sequence at the same external faces.

## Polynomial equality on two-chain grids by contracting crossings

Put \(\Gamma_{m,n}=\Sigma[m]\vee\Sigma[n]\), with the two chains given explicitly. In all four settings, equality of shared terms through degree three on \(\Gamma_{m,n}\) takes deterministic polynomial time in \(m+n\) and the input size. In degree four on \(\Gamma_{2,n}\) the same uniform bound holds for straight-line programs of chronological products of contextual crossings. For arbitrary degree-at-most-four constructor DAGs on \(\Gamma_{2,n}\) the bound is \(2^{O(n)}\operatorname{poly}(s+n)\), where \(s\) is the input size. In particular the latter test is polynomial on each fixed two-row grid. The claim includes subintervals and parallel inputs with loops.

**Degree three.** Record a crossing of a block of consecutive \(b\)'s past a block of consecutive \(a\)'s by the two nonempty intervals of named advances. The starting shuffle and these intervals determine the entire contextual move. Orient each binary subdivision of (K15) toward the single crossing. On these two chains the only proper 3-operations are crossings. Moving the fixed spatial padding outside a crossing gives exactly these records. Two successive same-axis contractions in a three-letter overlap join by merging all three consecutive pieces of the one interval. To see why different axes cannot overlap, write the shuffle after a left split as \(U A_1 A_2 B W\), with last move \(B A_2\mapsto A_2 B\). For that move to be first in a right split the following move must slide \(A_2\) past a new \(b\)-block immediately on its left. The letter there belongs to \(A_1\). After a right split the shuffle instead ends locally in \(A B_1 B_2\), with last move \(B_1 A\mapsto A B_1\). A following left split would need an \(a\)-block immediately after \(B_1\), where \(B_2\) stands. The two kinds of contraction also cannot apply to the same pair, since exactly one of the two nonempty named intervals changes. Disjoint replacements commute. Every contraction decreases length, so induction on length joins any two reductions and gives a unique irreducible block word. Its expansions recover the cell by (K15). [Two-chain presentation](lax-interchange-every-dimension-r9-2026-11-20.md#the-doubled-grids-through-hom-degree-five)

Every atomic crossing takes one named pair from \(ba\) to \(ab\). That pair never crosses again along a 3-history. Hence a history has at most \(mn\) atomic events even if supplied as a shared term. Evaluate the syntax bottom-up, representing each lower cell by its endpoints and shuffle, and each history by its starting shuffle and a spelling of at most \(mn\) events. Products concatenate with their indicated spatial pads, whiskerings change those pads, and a block crossing can be expanded by sweeping its rectangle once. Units produce empty histories. The Godement formula has bounded depth through degree three. At each node these operations and the contractions take polynomial time. This orientation works on the alphabet of block crossings. The overlaps counted by (K52) concern its shortlex completion, which can compare different words of equal length. [Independent shortlex computation](lax-interchange-every-dimension-r6-2026-11-20.md#validation)

**Degree four.** On the two-row grid every proper letter has an active single-row history \(F\) on row one and \(G\) on row two. Each runs through a nonempty consecutive interval of columns in descending order. Write its record as \((P,F,G,Q)\) with initial and final 3-history pads, keeping the actual spatial word and stationary letters. In the formulas below row events carry their positions in that word. The record has source \(P G F Q\) and target \(P F G Q\). The two binary subdivisions have records
\[
\begin{aligned}
 F=F_1 F_2:\quad &(P,F_1,G,F_2 Q),\ (P F_1,F_2,G,Q),\\
 G=G_1 G_2:\quad &(P G_1,F,G_2,Q),\ (P,F,G_1,G_2 Q).
\end{aligned}
\]
Compute every displayed pad as a 3-cell. Both subdivisions already hold in \(V\) on this grid. Each opposite operand is, up to stationary spatial padding, one literal crossing of its row past a column interval. Balancing moves that padding outside or onto the moving operand, after which the restricted split in \(V\) applies. These subdivisions, lower equality and the indicated structural identifications present all 4-equations here. [Derivation and completeness on two-row subintervals](lax-interchange-every-dimension-r9-2026-11-20.md#the-doubled-grids-through-hom-degree-five)

Again contract toward one letter. In a left-left critical triple, the first record supplies the exterior prefix, the last supplies the suffix, and the three consecutive row-one intervals concatenate. Both contractions continue to that record by associativity of lower composition. The right-right case concatenates the three row-two intervals in reverse processing order. For a left-right overlap, the middle record would have a prefix with spellings ending in different rows, \(P F_1=P'G_1'\). Each representative of a nonempty 3-history has the same last named event. Indeed every atomic expansion of a rectangle has the same last corner, the crossing of the last \(a\) with the first \(b\), by induction on its cuts. Replacing a contextual expansion preserves that last event. For a right-left overlap use the suffix and the corresponding first corner. Equal records cannot contract on both axes for the same pair, since the changing active intervals specify the axis. The combined letter in a contraction is uniquely determined by the unchanged exterior pads and consecutive intervals. Termination and confluence now give a complete normal form. No cancellation of a lower pad was used.

**A product on shared normal words.** Give \((P,F,G,Q)\) area \(|F||G|\). It is positive, at most \(n^2\), and additive under contraction. In joining two irreducible words, the only possible first contraction crosses the junction. Thereafter every new contraction includes the letter just formed, since the unchanged prefix and suffix were already irreducible. The center letter therefore consumes at most \(n^2\) letters from the two input words in total. Each candidate contraction can be tested directly by the two displayed formulas, concatenating intervals, checking the common spatial placement and comparing normalized pads of at most \(2n\) atomic events. This requires no enumeration of the contextual alphabet.

For each production of the input grammar store its irreducible result as a binary concatenation DAG. Locate neighbouring letters by descending with the stored lengths. After the junction computation retain the untouched prefix and suffix by cutting the two original trees once each and linking those pieces to the center. A prefix or suffix cut copies at most one boundary descent and has height at most that of the original tree. The new root increases the maximum height by at most two per production. Thus the resulting grammar has polynomial size, even when the normal word has exponential length. Lengths have polynomially many bits, since contraction never increases the number of expanded letters. Intern only the polynomially many terminals actually produced, testing their lower records by the degree-three algorithm. Equality of the final parallel words is deterministic polynomial-time SLP equality. At length zero compare the base histories. [Jeż, *Recompression of SLPs*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CPM.2017.2)

For the constructor-DAG bound, distribute each structural whisker through chronological products and memoize a subterm together with its accumulated structural context **before** evaluating its product. This also accommodates lower equalities exposed by a new pad. A context has one pair of pads at each of the finitely many lower ranks: a new whisker of the same rank composes pads, and one of lower rank acts on the existing higher pads by functoriality. The possible objects, 1-cells and 2-shuffles number polynomially in \(n+1\) on this grid (there are at most two row letters). For a 3-pad choose its starting shuffle and its sequence of row indices. The sequence has length at most \(2n\) and determines every column crossed. Consequently each pad, and hence the entire fixed-rank context, ranges over at most \(2^{O(n)}\operatorname{poly}(n+1)\) values. Pad operations have polynomial cost by the degree-three result. Input faces may be evaluated along with each node, keeping its lower boundaries. The fixed-depth Godement formula introduces only a bounded number of structural products and face actions per constructor in these degrees. Proper nonstructural 4-heads give single contextual records. Each memoized product now uses the preceding grammar algorithm. Taking a fixed polynomial in the total number of memoized entries gives the stated bound. This argument controls arbitrary fixed lower whiskerings for bounded row count in degree four. General diagrams and degrees can also have positive unary and mixed top equations.
