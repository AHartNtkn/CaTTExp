# CaTT and its directed variant L as presentations of (∞,ω)-categories: strict presentability, explicit units and the two model structures

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Everything under the "Known" headings is proved and is not re-asked.*

**What we are asking for.** Both comparisons are proved (Known (X)), and they are now related to each other and to the literature (Known (XI)). There is a map of coherators t : 𝕋_L → 𝕋. Restriction along it presents the inclusion of ordinary into oriented higher categories. 𝒪 is Gepner–Heine's theory, completed. Both model categories carry full model structures in which every object is fibrant. Strict presentability has a recognition test. We ask for the whole remaining line: an intrinsic description of which objects are strictly presentable and whether this is decidable, bounds on the witnesses of the unit for each lifting problem, and the further structure of the two model categories. Special computads or low dimensions are not what we want.

## Background

**CaTT.** CaTT (Finster–Mimram, LICS 2017) is a dependent type theory with one sort former, `⋆` and `arr A t u` (cells of type A from t to u), and one term former, `coh`. For a *pasting context* Θ (a context presenting a globular pasting diagram) and two parallel terms u, v over Θ satisfying a support (free-variable) condition, `coh Θ (u → v) σ` is a cell, for every substitution σ into Θ. Substitution is strict and syntactic. The models of CaTT are exactly Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis weak ω-categories: presheaves on the CaTT coherator sending globular sums to globular products (Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, arXiv 2106.04475, §3).

**A directed variant.** A variant L of CaTT keeps the same syntax but changes the side condition of `coh` so that, across objects, interchange is a directed cell (lax, not invertible). Its intended strict models are strict ω-categories with lax transformations (Ara–Maltsiniotis's ∞-Cat_lax, arXiv 1607.00668, Ex. C.18), and its intended homotopical models are oriented (∞,ω)-categories in the sense of Gepner–Heine (arXiv 2510.10504), whose homs carry Campion's Gray tensor (arXiv 2311.00205). The strict soundness of L is proved, using that cells of free strict ω-categories on Steiner complexes are determined by their supports.

**What is known about the homotopy theory.**
- Strict ω-categories have the Lafont–Métayer–Worytkiewicz model structure, cofibrantly generated by the boundary inclusions ∂Oₙ → Oₙ (arXiv 0712.0617, §4).
- Leinster's operad is the universal cofibrant replacement of the strict ω-category operad for that weak factorisation system (Garner, arXiv 0804.2663). Markakis extends this to generalised signatures: free algebras on computads are the cofibrant objects (arXiv 2303.11978).
- For Batanin–Leinster weak ω-categories, Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara prove 2-out-of-3 for ω-weak equivalences and call a (left semi-)model structure future work (arXiv 2406.13240, introduction). A full model structure on CaTT models is now known (Known (XI)).
- Cat_ω (Loubaton, arXiv 2406.05425, the inductive notion lim Cat_n) is presentable and cartesian closed (Prop. 2.2.1.54). Campion's Gray tensor is built on flagged (∞,∞)-categories and transferred to Cat_n for n ≤ ω and to the version with coinductive equivalences.
- CaTT's invertible cells are the coinductively invertible ones (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2406.12127).

**Known (I): the relative category and a strict coherent choice.**
- **A relative category.** Models of CaTT are equivalent to Batanin–Leinster weak ω-categories (Benjamin–Markakis–Sarti, arXiv 2405.00398, §7). With W the Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara ω-weak equivalences (2-out-of-6, arXiv 2406.13240 Thm 3.3.3, Cor. 3.3.4), 𝓗_CaTT := Mod(CaTT)[W⁻¹] exists. FHM (arXiv 2511.09849) characterise trivial fibrations as W ∩ ω-equifibrations (Prop. 4.1.5) and equifibrations as J-injectives (Thm 4.3.1). No (semi-)model structure is known.
- **Strict coherent choice, one direction.** Let 𝓜 be the injective complete Segal Θ_ω-space model category and 𝒜 its algebraically fibrant objects (Nikolaus). For each globular pasting shape P, yP is discrete and fibrant, so F Sp(P) → yP is a trivial fibration in 𝒜. Lifting the CaTT coherator's cell attachments against these gives R : 𝕋 → 𝒜 preserving globular sums, and S(Y) := 𝒜(R(−), Y) is a CaTT model with strict substitution and S(Y)_n = (UY)(D_n)₀. S sends weak equivalences to W, so it induces Cat_ω → 𝓗_CaTT. It has a formal left adjoint 𝓛_R(A) = ∫^P A(P) · R(P).
- **(2,2)-categories are presented** (Campbell, arXiv 1907.01999: 2-truncated (∞,2)-categories are bicategories; strictify).
- **Obstruction to the raw inductive nerve.** The coherent walking ω-equivalence E (Hadzihasanovic–Loubaton–Ozornova–Rovelli, arXiv 2404.14509, Thm 1.33) is a gaunt strict ω-category with E → 1 a folk weak equivalence, so no functor 𝓗_CaTT → Cat^ind_ω agrees with the raw inductive nerve on strict ω-categories. The Street nerve marking coinductively invertible cells does send E → 1 to an equivalence (Henry–Loubaton, arXiv 2301.11424, Prop. 4.58).
- **Free shapes agree.** Finite globular pasting shapes and their Gray products are the same free objects in the inductive and coinductive theories (Campion, arXiv 2311.00205, Cor. 3.5, Thm 3.14, Cor. 3.15).

**Known (II): coinductive completion and reconstruction tests.**
- **S factors through coinductive completion.** Write Q : Cat^ind_ω ⇄ Cat^coind_ω : j for coinductive completion. By Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde (arXiv 2603.11005: Lemma 5.9, Prop. 5.29 and 5.30, Cor. 5.31), completion is the localisation at ∞-surjective maps, and its units are ∞-surjective. S₀ sends every ∞-surjective map to W, because for an 𝒜-fibration, π₀-surjectivity of the Kan fibration Map(yDₙ, UY) → Map(Bₙ, UY) ×_{Map(Bₙ,UZ)} Map(yDₙ, UZ) gives strict globular boundary lifts, which are FHM trivial fibrations. Hence S ≃ S̄ ∘ Q with S̄ = S j.
- **The underived left adjoint is not relative.** The coend 𝓛_R ⊣ S₀ is an ordinary adjunction. For gaunt strict C with discrete nerve NC (canonically algebraically fibrant), π₀ Map(𝓛_R A, NC) ≅ Mod(CaTT)(A, C). Applied to the coherent walking equivalence w : E → 1, this would make Mod(1, E) → Mod(E, E) a bijection if 𝓛_R(w) were an equivalence, which is false. So 𝓛_R does not send W to inductive equivalences.
- **S₀ preserves categorical level.** If Y presents an (∞,n)-category, every cell of S₀(Y) above dimension n is coinductively invertible. The proof extends the cell strictly over N(Σ^{k−1} I), then uses that FHM weak equivalences reflect invertibility.
- **A necessary test for reconstruction.** If the ordinary counit ε_Y : 𝓛_R S₀(Y) → Y is an equivalence, then 𝒜(Y, NC) → Mod(CaTT)(S₀Y, C) is bijective for every gaunt strict C. In particular, for contractible Y every strict CaTT map S₀(Y) → E must be constant.
- **Marked targets.** Marking the coinductive equivalences of C does not change C. Localising at them gives QC (ORW Prop. 5.29).
- **Directed version, conditional.** Suppose the coherator of L has a Gray-enriched cellular presentation, and suppose the hom maps K(Q_a, Q_b) → K(Q_a, Y_b) are trivial fibrations. Then lifting into the enriched category of commuting squares gives an enriched R_L that strictly preserves composition. Still to be supplied are arity colimits in the semantic category, ordered tensor domains D^r ⊗ D^s (for example via a coloured operad), and invertibility declarations.

**Known (III): the counit and a conditional derived adjunction.**
- **The underived counit fails.** For every gaunt strict C with C → 1 a folk weak equivalence there is a contractible algebraically fibrant Y with S₀(Y) ≅ C (take Y = cosk₀ of the right Kan extension of C's reflexive globular set from the globes to Θ_ω, with fillers chosen to make NC → Y algebraic). For C = E this gives a non-constant S₀(Y) → E, so ε_Y : 𝓛_R S₀ Y → Y is not an equivalence. This is compatible with an equivalence 𝓗_CaTT ≃ Cat^coind_ω, since E ≃ 1 in 𝓗_CaTT and Q(NE) ≃ 1.
- **A conditional derived adjunction.** Let K be the computadic cofibrant-replacement comonad on Mod(CaTT) (Markakis, arXiv 2303.11978, Prop. 19, Cor. 21–22; its counit q and comultiplication are in W and K preserves and reflects W, Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara arXiv 2406.13240 Prop. 4.2.2). Assume

  ```
  (H)  f : B → B′ in W between computad-cofibrant models  ⇒  QU𝓛_R(f) is an equivalence.
  ```

  Then the co-Kleisli category of K (weak maps, Bourke–Garner) gives an adjunction Λ : 𝓗_CaTT ⇄ Cat^coind_ω : S̄ with Λ[A] ≃ QU𝓛_R(KA).
- **The reconstruction condition.** For Y presenting jC, the derived counit is QU(d_Y), with d_Y = ε_Y ∘ 𝓛_R(q_{S₀Y}). Since a map into a coinductively complete target is inverted by Q iff it is ∞-surjective, the counit condition is

  ```
  (R)  U𝓛_R K S₀ Y → UY is ∞-surjective.
  ```

- **The equivalence conjecture.** (H), (R) for every coinductively complete C, and the unit condition "A ← KA → S₀𝓛_R KA is an equivalence in 𝓗_CaTT" (for instance η_{KA} ∈ W) together imply 𝓗_CaTT ≃ Cat^coind_ω.
- **A correction.** Full faithfulness of S̄ restricted to a full subcategory 𝓑 implies that the counits at 𝓑 are equivalences only if Λ S̄(𝓑) ⊆ 𝓑.
- **Directed tensor arities.** For L, binary global operations through global dimension 3 have hom tensor domains D⁰ ⊗ D⁰, D¹ ⊗ D⁰, D⁰ ⊗ D¹, D² ⊗ D⁰, D⁰ ⊗ D² and D¹ ⊗ D¹. The last gives the directed interchanger. For α : f₀ ⇒ f₁ : x → y and β : g₀ ⇒ g₁ : y → z, in execution order, it goes (f₀◁β) ; (α▷g₁) ⇛ (α▷g₀) ; (f₁◁β): β first, then α, to α first, then β. In global dimension 4, the interchanger's naturality and the cube of three interchangers are 4-cells in an untruncated theory but equalities in a strict 3-truncation.

**Known (IV): units on graphs, detection, and the reduction to finite computads.**
- **Units on representables and graphs.** α_G : F_w G → F_s G is a globular trivial fibration for every globular set G, since B → T is cartesian and contractible. Hence η_{h_P} ∈ W for every coherator representable h_P = F_w G_P, and η_{F_w G} ∈ W for every graph G. The graph case uses 𝓛_R F_w G ≅ F|G| ≃ N Path(G).
- **Detection.** For f : X → Y in 𝒜, S₀(f) ∈ W ⇒ QU(f) is an equivalence. Algebraic identities and composites agree with geometric ones relative to boundaries, so algebraic invertibility gives geometric coinductive invertibility (ORW Prop. 5.42), and Q inverts the resulting maps (ORW Prop. 5.30).
- **One assertion suffices.** Let (U) be the assertion η_B ∈ W for every computad-cofibrant B. Then (U) implies (H), (R) and 𝓗_CaTT ≃ Cat^coind_ω. For (R), S₀(d_Y)η_{KA} = q_A. Moreover (U) reduces to
  ```
  (11)  η_{A_𝔠} ∈ W for every finite computad 𝔠,
  ```
  by filtered colimits of finite subcomputads (the algebraic colimit is W-equivalent to S₀ of the 𝒜-colimit; FHM Prop. 3.4.4). (11) is an infinite family.
- **The (∞,1) fragment.** Free models on computads with bounded generator dimensions and invertible cells above dimension 1 are free on graphs, so (H) holds between them. (R) for general Kan complexes or quasi-categories is not obtained: finite subcomputads of K S₀ Y can omit the inverse witnesses.
- **L, ordinary presentation.** The coherator of L is the colimit of free globular extensions, adjoining each admissible head once. Through global dimension 3 the generators are classified by active hom-dimension d:
  - r = 1: operation heads with d = 0;
  - r = 2: operation heads with d = 1, and coherence heads with d = 0;
  - r = 3: operation heads with d = 2, either one factor of dimension 2 or two of dimension 1 (the interchangers), and coherence heads with d ≤ 1.

  Whole-grid interchangers are separate generators. Composites of elementary ones are derived, and the two are linked by dimension-4 coherence heads when formally parallel. In dimension 4, the operation profiles are (3), (2,1), (1,2) and (1,1,1), the last being the three-interchanger cube. No Gray-enriched presentation was given.

**Known (V): the triangle, spaces, saturation and enrichment.**
- **The triangle reduces to relative coherence.** Let 𝔠_△ be the computad x, y, z; f : x → y, g : y → z, h : x → z; α : m(f, g) ⇒ h, with A_△ its free CaTT model and T_△ its free strict 2-category. The Θ-nerve of T_△ is yP ∪ yD₂ glued along yD₁ (P = [2]), a homotopy pushout, and so is the corresponding pushout of R-values; hence 𝓛_R A_△ is weakly equivalent to N T_△ and

  ```
  (4)  η_{A_△} ∈ W  ⟺  π : A_△ → T_△ ∈ W.
  ```

  π has strict boundary lifting in dimensions 0–3: all cells of A_△ above dimension 2 are invertible, and lifting in dimension 3 is the statement that parallel 2-terms with equal strict image are joined by a 3-term, which holds in every CaTT context. What remains is

  ```
  (5)  any two parallel r-terms of the CaTT context (x, y, z, f, g, h, α : f ∗₀ g ⇒ h), r ≥ 3, are joined by an (r+1)-term.
  ```

- **(5) holds for r = 3.** The context has a unique source x and sink z, and the two boundary paths share only their endpoints. A normal-form argument (for a 2-term with strict image α, exactly one 0-column of its arity carries α and spans x → z, all other columns factor literally through a point; normalisers built from this factorisation are compatible with every 3-term by induction on syntax) joins any two parallel 3-terms. So the first open case of (5) is r = 4.
- **Spaces.** For Y presenting a space, Z = QU𝓛_R K S₀ Y is a space, and the reconstruction map Z → Y is bijective on π₀, an isomorphism on π₁ and surjective on every π_n. Injectivity on π_n, n ≥ 2, follows from the **spherical representation condition**: every class of π_n(QU𝓛_R B, x), n ≥ 2, is represented by the interpretation of an algebraic n-cell with iterated identity boundary (only B = K S₀ Y is needed). Cellular approximation does not give this.
- **Saturation.** Units in the saturation W^sat give 𝓗 ≃ 𝒜[V⁻¹] for the class V = {f : S₀ f ∈ W^sat}, which is a further localisation of Cat^coind_ω. Equality with Cat^coind_ω needs

  ```
  (10)  S₀ f ∈ W^sat  ⇒  QU f is an equivalence.
  ```

  Two-out-of-six, retracts and filtered colimits do not formally imply saturation (an eight-element poset with a non-saturated class having all three closure properties). The condition

  ```
  (12)  pushouts of maps in Cof ∩ W remain in W
  ```

  (Cof the computad-cofibrations) would give W = W^sat, via the factorisation w = p i with p a trivial fibration.
- **Enrichment.** The semantic tensor arities for L exist in oriented (∞,ω)-categories (Gepner–Heine enriched suspension and wedge). A *strict* Gray enrichment of the raw coherator of L that represents cells is impossible: it would require the raw equation c(f, i(y)) = f for a composition head c and an identity head i, and CaTT-style syntax has no definitional equations. A weak enrichment is not ruled out.

**Known (VI): degree-2 representation, a saturation criterion and the two-square arity.**
- **(5) for r ≥ 4 is a relative-coherence statement.** It asks that parallel 4-terms of the 2-dimensional triangle context with equal strict image be joined. Extending the r = 3 normalisers needs, for each 4-term θ : a → b and chosen comparisons H_a : a → ν, a 5-cell θ ; H_b ⇒ H_a. This is not asked here.
- **Spherical representation in degree 2.** Let B be free on a computad with every positive-dimensional cell coinductively invertible, and X = g U 𝓛_R B (g = groupoidification). Every class of π₂(X, x) is the interpretation of a cell b : 1_x ⇒ 1_x of B.
  - *Filtration.* 𝓛_R(F_w D_n) = F(yD_n) and 𝓛_R(F_w ∂D_n) = F(∂_Θ D_n), so generator attachments become homotopy pushouts of S^{n−1} → D^n. This gives a relative CW model X₁ ⊆ X₂ ⊆ X with X₁ ≃ |G|, X₂ from the 2-generators and higher cells of dimension ≥ 3, so π₂X₁ = 0 and π₂X₂ → π₂X is onto.
  - *Crossed modules.* The homotopy bigroupoid H₂B (2-cells modulo invertible 3-cells; FHM Prop. 2.4.6, Thm 2.4.8, Cor. 2.4.9) maps to Π₂X by a pseudofunctor s whose coherence is checked in generic shapes. Over the free groupoid F = π₁(X₁, G₀), an algebraic crossed module M_B with ker ∂_x = Aut(1_x) in H₂B maps to π₂(X, X₁, G₀). Composed with j from the free crossed module C ≅ π₂(X₂, X₁, G₀) (Whitehead; Brown–Razak Salleh Thm 5.1), it is the canonical map, checked on generators.
  - *Consequence.* For Y presenting a space, d : QU𝓛_R K S₀ Y → Y is an isomorphism on π₀, π₁ and π₂ and surjective on every π_n. Injectivity uses the literal-boundary lift of a nullhomotopy through the Kan fibration Map(yD₃, UY) → Map(∂_Θ D₃, UY) and the trivial fibration q_A.
- **A saturation criterion.** Let 𝒞ⁿ = F_w D_n and j_n : 𝒞ⁿ → ℰⁿ⁺¹ the source inclusion into the FHM weak walking (n+1)-equivalence, so J = {j_n} has J^⋔ = equifibrations and W ∩ J^⋔ = TF (FHM arXiv 2511.09849, Prop. 5.0.4, Prop. 4.1.5). Consider
  ```
  (FEA)  A_𝔠 → A_𝔠 ∐_{𝒞ⁿ} ℰⁿ⁺¹ lies in W, for every finite computad 𝔠, every n and every map 𝒞ⁿ → A_𝔠.
  ```
  (FEA) implies W = W^sat, hence the detection statement (10). Proof: (FEA) extends to cofibrant sources by filtered colimits. Every relative J-cell complex with cofibrant source is in W. A trivial cofibration with cofibrant source is a retract of such a complex (factor through J, then use W ∩ J^⋔ = TF). This gives the precofibration-category axiom CF3, and Rădulescu-Banu (arXiv math/0610009) Thm 7.2.7 gives saturation from 2-out-of-6. Unconditionally, every J-cofibration i : A → B has a strict retraction (A → 1 is an equifibration). (FEA) contains j_n ∈ W itself, which FHM prove only after strict reflection.
- **The two-square arity of L.** For Γ = ΣΔ₂ ∨ ΣΔ₁ (α₁ : f₀ ⇒ f₁, α₂ : f₁ ⇒ f₂ : x → y, β : g₀ ⇒ g₁ : y → z), U_Γ = Δ₂ ⊗ Δ₁ has a canonical 2-cell ρ : P ⇒ Q with P = (f₀◁β) ; (α₁▷g₁) ; (α₂▷g₁) and Q = (α₁▷g₀) ; (α₂▷g₀) ; (f₂◁β), the paste of two elementary squares through M = (α₁▷g₀) ; (f₁◁β) ; (α₂▷g₁).
  - The raw coherator has an independent whole-grid head G_Γ : P ⇒ Q distinct from the elementary paste E_Γ, and evaluating both in the unchanged strict arity identifies them.
  - Adjoining a second parallel 2-generator g ↦ ρ (U_Γ⁺ → U_Γ) is not a folk weak equivalence: g and ρ have the same image and no 3-cell joins them.
  - Attaching the strict walking 3-equivalence along ρ, Û_Γ = U_Γ ∐_{D²} E_s³, gives a folk trivial cofibration U_Γ → Û_Γ and a folk weak equivalence Û_Γ → U_Γ. Its 2-skeleton is U_Γ⁺, and its new 3-generators supply the global 4-dimensional equivalence between G_Γ and E_Γ.
  - This is a repair of one arity. Substitution maps between resolved arities, weak Gray composition, the augmentation to semantic arities, and the model comparison are not constructed. The coloured-operad Boardman–Vogt route (Berger–Moerdijk) does not apply directly: the directed Gray tensor has no braiding.

**The coh rule of L (two-tier).** Contexts are CaTT pasting contexts. Each decomposes uniquely as Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ, with objects a₀, …, aₙ and Θᵢ the pasting scheme in the hom from aᵢ₋₁ to aᵢ.
- **Gray unfolding.** U_{ij}(Γ) := Θᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ Θⱼ (factors in execution order) is the Steiner tensor of the Steiner complexes λ(Θ). Its generators are tensors of generators, dimensions add, and the boundary is the Leibniz formula. It is strongly loop-free, so its cells are determined by their supports.
- **Supports.** For a term t : A with endpoints aᵢ, aⱼ, supp(t : A) is the downward-closed set of generators of U_{ij}(Γ) used by the strict image of t and of the cells in its type, computed in the free strict Gray-enriched ω-category on Γ.
- **The rule.** coh_{Γ, t →_A u}[σ] is well formed when either
  - (operation) supp(t : A) = ∂⁻U_{ij}(Γ), supp(u : A) = ∂⁺U_{ij}(Γ), and dim A = dim U_{ij}(Γ) (so the cell has dimension dim U_{ij}(Γ) + 1); or
  - (coherence) supp(t : A) = U_{ij}(Γ) = supp(u : A).

  **Here (i, j) = (0, n) is required** (proper intervals would let a 1-head over ΣD⁰ ∨ ΣD^{m−1} depend formally on an m-dimensional argument, destroying the filtration by output dimension).
- **Consequences.**
  - On a single hom (n = 1) this is CaTT.
  - For Γ = ΣD¹ ∨ ΣD¹ there is no horizontal composite of α and β. There is an operation-case 3-cell (f◁β) ; (α▷g′) ⇛ (α▷g) ; (f′◁β), and none in the reverse direction.
  - The intended strict model is ∞-Cat_lax (strict ω-categories with lax transformations), where the rule is sound.
  - "Two-tier" means interchange is invertible inside a hom and directed across objects.

**Known (VII): torsion-free computads, and the homotopy hypothesis.**
- **Torsion-free computads reduce to relative coherence.** Let 𝔠 be a finite computad coming from a torsion-free complex in Campion's sense (arXiv 2311.00200; this includes strongly loop-free Steiner complexes), with A = A_𝔠, T = F(𝔠) and π : A → T. Then
  ```
  η_A ∈ W  ⟺  π ∈ W  ⟺  π is a globular trivial fibration  ⟺  RC,
  ```
  where RC says that any two parallel n-cells of A with equal image in T are joined by an (n+1)-cell, for every n. Proof:
  - S₀(e_𝔠) ∘ η = π, where e_𝔠 : 𝓛_R A → NT is the adjunct of π. It is a semantic equivalence by cellular induction, since Campion's Thm 5.11 says the strict attachments of a torsion-free complex are homotopy pushouts.
  - Endo-cells of T are identities (Campion Def. 1.8, Prop. 1.10), so RC fillers map to identities.
  - A boundary-correction lemma whiskers by such fillers one dimension at a time. Together with initial lifts by induction on strict expressions, this gives boundary lifting.
  - Conversely, FHM essential surjectivity at 1_{πa} gives RC.

  So (11) for torsion-free computads is exactly RC in all dimensions. For the triangle this is Known (V), (4)–(5).
- **(11) implies the homotopy hypothesis for a specified coherator.** Let 𝕋^bi be Lanari's theory 𝕋^W (arXiv 1809.07923, Def. 4.3): the CaTT coherator with freely adjoined, independent left and right inverses and cancellation cells for every positive-dimensional cell, J ⊣ V : Mod(𝕋^bi) → Mod(CaTT). Write (U_gpd) for "η_B ∈ W for every groupoidal computad-cofibrant B, infinite computads included". Then
  ```
  (11) for all finite computads  ⟹  (U_gpd)  ⟹  𝕋^bi is a Grothendieck groupoid coherator satisfying Henry's pushout condition,
  ```
  and Mod(𝕋^bi)[W_bi⁻¹] ≃ Mod(CaTT)_gpd[W_gpd⁻¹] ≃ 𝒮 (spaces). Proof outline:
  - A reflection principle: for groupoidal computad-cofibrant B, B′, a map f is in W iff its realization E(f) = gQU𝓛_R(f) is an equivalence. This uses a completion map that preserves algebraic fillers.
  - The inverse-data attachments β_k : 𝔻_k → I_k are inverted by E: the fibre is a product of two contractible homotopy fibres of composition with a path.
  - Bourke's algebraic-injectivity factorizations (arXiv 1811.09532, Thms 4.3–4.4).
  - Henry's Thm 5.3.5 and Cor. 5.3.13 (arXiv 1609.04622).
  So (11) in general is at least as hard as the homotopy hypothesis for 𝕋^bi.
- **The groupoidal restriction is vacuous on finite computads.** A finite ordinary computad has a groupoidal free model only if it has no positive-dimensional generators: a top generator has nonempty support, so it is not invertible (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2406.12127, Prop. 37). So the infinite quantifier in (U_gpd) is essential.
- **Not known:** a converse for (11) itself (the converse for (U_gpd) is Known (VIII)(A)); the comparison of 𝕋^bi with an arbitrary Grothendieck groupoid coherator; (11) for any finite computad with torsion beyond those in Known (IV)–(VI).


**Known (VIII): the groupoidal equivalence, the semi-model structure, and the two missing comparisons.**
- **(A) The groupoidal condition is exactly the homotopy hypothesis for 𝕋^bi.**
  ```
  (U_gpd)  ⟺  𝕋^bi is a Grothendieck groupoid coherator satisfying Henry's pushout condition.
  ```

For every sphere s of P_X and every cell d of S_X/R with boundary the image of s, some generator with boundary exactly s evaluates to d. In degree zero the test requires surjectivity on objects. ## Questions

**Q1 (main: which objects are strictly presentable).** The test of Known (XI) quantifies over all relation sets on a set of size κ.
- Give an intrinsic characterization of the coinductively complete (∞,ω)-categories, and of the objects of 𝒪, that are equivalent to a strict ω-category (respectively a strict Gray-category). It may be homotopical, for example in terms of homotopy-type invariants of the homs, or structural, for example in terms of the image of the nerve.
- For objects presented by finite computads, decide whether strict presentability is decidable, and give the exact complexity if it is not.
- State how the ordinary and directed answers relate along i. Is a strictly presentable object of V strictly presentable in 𝒪, and is the converse true for objects in the image of i?
- Settle the gaunt and the groupoidal cases. Simpson's obstruction excludes P₃S²; say exactly which n-types are strictly presentable in each case.

**Q2 (bounds for each lifting problem).** For a finite computad B and a single lifting problem for η_B (a strict cell d of S₀𝓛_R B with a prescribed raw boundary), give an explicit raw lift, with a bound on its size and dimension in terms of d and its boundary. Say whether a bound polynomial in the size of d exists, and do the same for the directed unit.

**Q3 (the two model categories).**
- Are the two model structures left or right proper, simplicial, combinatorial and tractable?
- Is the model structure on L-models monoidal for a Gray tensor?
- Are they Quillen equivalent, by explicit Quillen functors, to published model structures for (∞,ω)-categories (complicial sets, or Campion–Kapulkin–Maehara's cubical model) and for oriented ones?
- Describe syntactically the L-models in the image of t*, up to weak equivalence. For instance, is it the L-models in which every interchanger is coinductively invertible?

## What would count as a solution

An intrinsic characterization of strictly presentable objects, with its decidability for finite computads; an explicit lift with a size bound for each unit lifting problem; properness, monoidality or a Quillen equivalence for either model structure; or a published reference.

# Researcher Response 1

## The unit need not lift even one prescribed vertex

For Q2 there are two distinct lifting properties. Membership of the unit in $W$ gives a raw cell at the prescribed raw boundary **and an invertible comparison** to the specified target cell. An exact lift would give equality with that cell. The latter fails in dimension zero for the computad consisting of one vertex, in both theories.

Indeed, let $B=h_{D_0}$. By representability $L B=R(D_0)=F(yD_0)$, with $F$ the free algebraically fibrant object functor. The presheaf $yD_0$ is terminal: every strict arity has a unique map to the point. The set of target objects of the unit is $(UF(yD_0))(D_0)_0$, and the image of the unique raw object is the original vertex. This calculation uses the actual unit.

Here is why there is another vertex. Let $J_{\rm alg}$ denote the generating trivial cofibrations chosen for algebraic fibrancy in the injective localized simplicial presheaf model. Some $A\hookrightarrow A'$ in this set adds a vertex at $D_0$. Otherwise the entire weakly saturated class would be bijective on vertices there, since evaluation and taking vertices preserve colimits and retracts. But $yD_0\times\{0\}\to yD_0\times\Delta^1$ is an objectwise trivial monomorphism and adds such a vertex. Fix $v\in A'(D_0)_0\setminus A(D_0)_0$. At the first stage of the free algebraic fibrant replacement there is a copy of $A'$ attached along the unique $A\to yD_0$. Its vertex $v$ gives a new vertex $d$. All subsequent maps are monomorphisms, so $d$ is distinct from the original vertex in $UF(yD_0)$. This is the free construction even when the input already admits fillers. One can in particular include the displayed interval inclusion among the generators and take $d$ to be its second endpoint. The proof applies equally on the oriented arity site. If algebraic fibrancy is specified only by a family detecting fibrant objects, adjoining this interval still detects exactly the same objects and gives a permitted choice of the actual unit. For that more general convention the counterexample refutes a bound uniform in the choices; the weak-saturation argument applies to every full generating set of trivial cofibrations. [Nikolaus, construction of the free object in §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1342)

The boundary of $d$ is empty. No raw object maps to it. Thus a size bound, polynomial or otherwise, for a literal lift fails for a single finite input. For this example the lift *up to equivalence* uses the original object. A complexity bound for producing that weaker kind of witness would have to specify representations of algebraic fillers, of the realization choices, and of the invertibility data to be output.

## Finite ordinary computads and the strictification test

For a finite typed ordinary computad $P$, the following three properties are equivalent.

1. Its free weak algebra $A_P$ has some strict presentation.
2. Its canonical strictification $\pi:A_P\to F_sP$ is in $W$.
3. Its strict polygraph is homotopical.

The nonformal direction is $1\Rightarrow2$. By Known (XI), $A_P$ is cofibrant and a strict presentation $C$ is fibrant. Represent the isomorphism in the homotopy category by an actual raw map $f:A_P\to C$ in $W$. Strict evaluation gives a factorization $f=v\pi$. Given parallel $a,b$ with $\pi a=\pi b$, apply the relative essential-surjectivity test for $f$ at $a,b$ to the identity of $f a=f b$. The resulting $u:a\to b$ has $f u$ invertibly comparable to that identity, so $f u$ is invertible. FHM weak equivalences reflect invertibility. Thus $u$, and hence $\pi u$, are coinductively invertible. Gauntness of the finite free strict target in Known (VIII) gives $\pi u=1$. This proves $\mathrm{RC}_{\rm id}(P)$ at every pair, and Known (VIII) then gives 2. Assertion 2 itself supplies the strict presentation, and its equivalence with 3 follows from Known (VIII) and (X).

In particular **strict presentability is undecidable**, already for finite 2-dimensional ordinary computads. In this rank it is exactly bubble-freeness and is $\Pi^0_1$-complete. Existence of a nonidentity bubble is recursively enumerable by enumerating nonempty closed rewrite diagrams at an empty path. For the reverse reduction take positive semi-Thue reachability $u\Rightarrow^*v$ on alphabet $A$. Put the letters at a vertex $Q$, with $L:P\to Q$, $R:Q\to P$ for a second vertex $P$. Add the rewrites at $Q$ and the rules $1_P\Rightarrow LuR$, $LvR\Rightarrow1_P$. From $1_P$ a path consists of a string of delimited blocks. Insertions occur between blocks, rewrites stay inside a block, and a deletion consumes a whole block. A nonempty diagram returning to $1_P$ must close an inserted block and hence witnesses $u\Rightarrow^*v$. Such a derivation also suffices, using just one block. No rule starts at $1_Q$. Each attaching word can be given a fixed binary bracketing to produce a finite raw computad. This proves the stated many-one degree with explicit input conversion. The last assertion distinguishes the images of the two equal pastings.

```python
from itertools import permutations

paths = {''.join(p) for p in permutations('VVHH')}
rows = [('a1', 'a0', 'a0'), ('b1', 'b1', 'b0')]

def exchanges(path):
    for i in range(len(path) - 1):
        if path[i:i + 2] == 'VH':
            column = path[:i].count('H')
            row = path[:i].count('V')
            before, after = rows[column][row + 1], rows[column][row]
            yield path[:i] + 'HV' + path[i + 2:], (before, after)

edges = {p: list(exchanges(p)) for p in paths}
assert len(paths) == 6 and sum(map(len, edges.values())) == 6
changes = {(first, second) for mid, first in edges['VHVH']
           for end, second in edges[mid] if end == 'HVHV'}
alpha, beta = ('a0', 'a1'), ('b0', 'b1')
assert changes == {(alpha, beta), (beta, alpha)}
```

No raw object maps to d, so the unit has no literal lift even at an empty boundary. A bound can only concern lifts up to invertible comparison.
- **Q1, finite ordinary computads.** A finite typed ordinary computad has some strict presentation iff its strictification π is in W iff it is homotopical. So strict presentability is undecidable. In dimension 2 it is bubble-freeness and is Π⁰₁-complete, by a reduction from semi-Thue reachability with delimited blocks. In every finite dimension it is in Π⁰₂.
- **Q1, finite directed computads.** Free bipointed suspension reduces the ordinary problem, so the directed problem is Π⁰₁-hard. A uniform upper bound is lightface Σ¹₁.
- **Q1, recognition on strict arities.** X is strictly presentable iff it is the colimit of a 0-truncated diagram on Θ (or on the strict wedge site Θ(V₀)) satisfying the spine (wedge) bijections. This settles the gaunt case: X is strictly presentable when its arity mapping spaces are discrete. Groupoidal and n-type versions follow.
- **Q1, simply connected n-types.** A simply connected ordinary n-type is strictly presentable iff it is a product of K(A_j, j), 2 ≤ j ≤ n. This extends Ara's result from strict to weak inverses. P₄S³ has no directed strict presentation, because its loop space would split and the square of the degree-two class is zero on ΩS³.
- **Q1, along i.** An ordinary strict presentation stays strict in 𝒪. The converse fails: the double delooping of the braided monoidal groupoid on ℤ with β_{p,q} = pq is a strict Gray presentation of an object iX with X not ordinarily strictly presentable.
- **Q3 (t*).** An L-algebra is in the image of t* up to W_L iff every elementary mixed term τ_{p,q} evaluates to a coinductively invertible cell. This answers the last bullet of Q3 positively.
- **Q3 (model structures).** Both raw model categories are combinatorial, tractable, left proper and right proper. There are explicit Quillen zigzags to the localized complete Segal Θ-space, complicial and comical models. The discrete enrichment fails the simplicial axiom.
- **Q3 (no Gray monoidal structure).** A homwise Gray tensor on L-models has no middle-four map. No Quillen bifunctor's derived tensor can extend the ordinary Gray tensor on embedded Gray cubes, because the two one-column strips of the 2 × 2 grid have no pushout i([2] ⊗ [2]) in 𝒪.
- **Q3 (not Quillen equivalent to unlocalized models).** The terminal object is sequentially compact in saturated complicial and comical sets. It is not in the coinductive raw model, as the finite stages of the walking coinductive equivalence show.

**Checked by hand.**
- **The semi-Thue reduction.** A rewrite source is a word in the letters, so it never contains L or R and stays inside one block. A nonidentity 2-cell 1_P ⇒ 1_P must insert a block and delete it, so it contains a derivation u ⇒* v. No rule has source 1_Q, so there are no bubbles at Q.
- **Group completion.** BT → B(T^gp) is a weak equivalence for a discrete commutative monoid T (group completion theorem). A simplicial abelian group is a product of Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces. The K(A_j, j) splitting follows.
- **P₄S³.** ΩP₄S³ is simply connected with π₂ = ℤ and π₃ = ℤ/2. ΩS³ → ΩP₄S³ is 4-connected. In H*(ΩS³; ℤ) the square of the degree-two generator is twice a generator, so it is zero mod 2. On K(ℤ, 2) the square is nonzero. The contradiction holds.
- **The braided example.** β_{p,q} = pq satisfies naturality (the group is commutative) and both hexagons (bilinearity). The self-braiding β_{1,1} = 1 is nonzero, which is the obstruction the response uses.
- **The strip obstruction.** By naturality on retracts, the derived tensor would send the pushout iI ∨ iI = i[2] to a pushout of the two strips. Maps from the strips into the gaunt W = ΣI ∨ ΣI agree on the common edge but do not extend to the grid, since in W(0, 2) = I ⊗ I the two orders are distinct 1-cells.
- **Right properness** follows from every object being fibrant. **Left properness** is the cylinder argument with separated presheaves, which extends the round-5 rectification relative to a map.

**Caveats.**
- The Q2 counterexample depends on the convention that the unit is the free algebraic fibrant replacement. It refutes literal lifts only. A bound for lifts up to invertible comparison is still open, and the response says that it needs a representation of fillers to be specified first.
- The recognition criterion on strict arities is structural, not homotopical. It quantifies over diagrams D. A homotopy-invariant answer is given only for simply connected groupoidal n-types (ordinary) and for one directed example.
- The computability degree is exact only in dimension 2 (ordinary) and on suspensions (directed). For unrestricted finite computads the ordinary problem lies between Π⁰₁ and Π⁰₂, and the directed one between Π⁰₁ and Σ¹₁.
