# Which polygraphs are homotopy polygraphs? A finite, generator-level criterion in every dimension

*A self-contained problem. It assumes familiarity with polygraphs (computads), strict ω-categories and (∞,ω)-categories, and nothing else. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked, except item 17, which is marked as evidence.*

**What we are asking for.** An exact characterisation of finite homotopy polygraphs is now known in a form that only uses strict cells (items 19–20): Γ is homotopical iff it is bubble-free in every dimension and certain finite complexes built from strict cells are contractible. What is missing is a criterion that is **finite and stated at the level of generators**, in every dimension: a test one can run on the finite list of generators, or a proof that none exists. Isolated low-dimensional examples are of little use to us.

## 1. Setting

- A **polygraph** Γ (computad) is a set of generators of each dimension, each n-generator having a source and a target which are parallel (n−1)-cells of the free strict ω-category on the lower generators. All polygraphs here are finite.
- `F(Γ)` is the free strict ω-category on Γ.
- `Free(Γ)` is the free (∞,ω)-category on Γ: generators are attached one at a time along their boundaries by pushouts of `∂Dⁿ → Dⁿ` in (∞,ω)-categories (Loubaton's Cat_ω, arXiv 2406.05425, or Campion's framework, arXiv 2311.00200). Strict ω-categories are (∞,ω)-categories, so there is a canonical comparison `c_Γ : Free(Γ) → F(Γ)`.
- Γ is a **homotopy polygraph** (or *homotopical*) when c_Γ is an equivalence, i.e. when every strict pushout attaching a generator is also a homotopy pushout.
- A 2-cell `1_x ⇒ 1_x` of F(Γ) other than the identity is a **bubble**.
- Γ is **positive in every dimension** when every generator of dimension n ≥ 2 has a source and a target that are not identity cells. (For n = 2: both boundary paths are nonempty.)
- More generally, a non-identity n-cell `e : 1^{n−1−k}_c ⇒ 1^{n−1−k}_c` (n ≥ 2, source and target both the (n−1)-dimensional iterated identity of a k-cell c, k ≤ n−2) is an **iterated-identity endo-cell**. Γ is **bubble-free in every dimension** when F(Γ) has none. For n = 2, k = 0 these are the bubbles.
- A strict ω-functor p : C → D is a **discrete Conduché functor** when every factorisation of p(x) in D lifts uniquely to a factorisation of x in C.

## 2. Why it matters

Homotopy polygraphs are exactly the computads whose free (∞,ω)-category can be computed strictly. Finite presentations of Grothendieck constructions over a free base, the comparison of weak and strict free objects in type theories for weak ω-categories, and pasting theorems for non-globular shapes all need this hypothesis, and at present each use checks it by hand. Dimension 2 is settled exactly (item 5 below), necessity of bubble-freeness is settled in every dimension (item 14), and bubble-freeness alone is not sufficient from dimension 3 (item 18).

## 3. What is known

1. **Pasting theorems.** For a strongly loop-free Steiner complex K, ν(K) is the free (∞,ω)-category on its atoms (Campion, *Pasting diagrams in higher categories are free*, arXiv 2311.00200). Chanavat (*A strengthened (∞,n)-categorical pasting theorem*, arXiv 2603.22242) extends this to regular directed complexes with frame-acyclic molecules (Thm 3.17), and deduces that positive 2-polygraphs (Cor. 3.39) and regular 3-polygraphs in Henry's sense (Cor. 3.38) are homotopical. Comment 3.40 there describes the extension to arbitrary regular and positive polygraphs as expected, without proof.
2. **Bubbles obstruct.** If F(Γ) has a bubble q, sending every 2-generator to 1 gives r : F(Γ) → B²ℕ with r(q) = k > 0, and q defines i : B²ℕ → F(Γ) by Eckmann–Hilton; ri = [k] acts by kʲ on H_{2j}(K(ℤ,2); ℚ), so the groupoidification of F(Γ) has nonzero rational homology in every even degree, while that of Free(Γ) is a finite CW complex. Example: (x; σ : 1_x ⇒ 1_x), F(Γ) = B²ℕ ≃ K(ℤ,2) after groupoidification, Free(Γ) ≃ S².
3. **Bubble-freeness is undecidable**, already for one-vertex 2-polygraphs with no generating bubble (reduction from reachability in positive semi-Thue systems via an insertion rule 1 ⇒ L w Z and a deletion rule L v Z ⇒ 1).
4. **Dimension 2, combinatorial core.** For a progressive one-colour string diagram G every component of which meets the boundary, the cell complex K_G whose vertices are the *tier encodings* of G (decompositions into layers of simultaneous generators) and whose faces are products of permutohedra (splitting a layer) has contractible components. Proof: right-exchange runs (which terminate for boundary-connected diagrams, Delpeuch–Vicary arXiv 1804.07832, Cor. 2.16) give a unique maximal cell F(v) with source v; deleting vertices in topological order removes a closed star along its antistar, a ball. Two floating scalars give a circle, so the boundary condition is needed.
5. **Dimension 2, exact answer.** A finite 2-polygraph is homotopical **iff it is bubble-free**. The "if" direction uses item 4 and Heidemann's mesh model of (∞,2)-categories (arXiv 2411.04870): a presheaf X_Γ on meshes with L₂ j*X_Γ ≃ Free(Γ), and an objectwise equivalence j*X_Γ → N₂F(Γ) computed as X_Γ(Q) ≃ B(Q↓t) ≃ ⊔_G BP_G over the diagrams G of shape Q, with each BP_G contractible by item 4 (a bubble-free polygraph has no boundary-disjoint diagram component and no diagram automorphisms). Consequences: homotopicality of finite 2-polygraphs is undecidable (item 3); the finite case of Guetta's conjecture (homologically coherent ⟺ bubble-free, *Homology of strict ω-categories*, arXiv 2104.12662, Conj. 6.6.5) holds; every 2-polygraph whose 2-generators all have non-empty target (or all non-empty source) is homotopical, with any number of vertices.
6. **Suspensions.** Σ^k Γ is homotopical iff Γ is (Hom_{ΣC}(0,1) ≃ C, and suspension preserves and detects the comparison). So for 2-polygraphs, Σ^k Γ is homotopical iff Γ is bubble-free.
7. **Graph assembly, every dimension.** For a finite directed graph Q (loops and parallel edges allowed) and finite polygraphs P_e on its edges, the polygraph B_Q(P_•) with vertices Q₀ and (r+1)-generators ⊔_e (P_e)_r (the free category on Q enriched in the F(P_e)) is homotopical iff every P_e is. In particular **adjoining a 1-generator preserves homotopicality in every dimension**.
8. **Discrete Conduché descent (Theorem A).** If p : Γ → Δ is a polygraph map with F(p) discrete Conduché and Δ homotopical, then Γ is homotopical. Surjectivity on generators alone is not enough ([1] → F(loop)).
9. **Typing into positive templates (Theorem B).** A polygraph map Γ → Δ into a homotopical Δ that is source-positive or target-positive in every dimension makes Γ homotopical (Henry's cartesianness for such polygraphs makes F(p) discrete Conduché).
10. **Mapping cylinders (Theorem C).** Strict double mapping cylinders of finite homotopical presentations along arbitrary strict functors have finite homotopical presentations, with generators Δ_n ⊔ ℰ_n ⊔ Γ_{n−1}; Gray cylinders, cones and suspensions are instances (Loubaton arXiv 2406.05425 Thm 2.3.3.22, Campion's pasting theorem for Dⁿ ⊗ [1]). Items 7–10 give a sound finite-certificate system, but typing into templates from a good class cannot leave that class, so it does not reach all positive polygraphs.
11. **Positivity is not necessary.** (∗; t; η : 1 ⇒ t) is homotopical (Blans–Linskens, arXiv 2605.03150), and so is its suspension, a non-positive 3-polygraph. Regularity is not necessary either: the positive non-regular 3-polygraph x, y, z; p, q : x → y, r : y → z; α, β : p ⇒ q; ζ : α ▷ r ⇛ β ▷ r is homotopical.
12. **Positivity in the top dimension alone is not enough.** One vertex, b : 1 ⇒ 1, Ω : b ⇛ b: Ω has non-identity boundaries, but b is a bubble.
13. **Strict versus "stricter".** Chanavat (*Homotopy theory of stricter n-categories*, arXiv 2509.26563, Comment 2.67) gives a 4-dimensional regular directed complex whose molecules do not form a polygraph under strict composition. This is **not** a counterexample to the positive case: the extra equations appear in the passage from the free strict category to molecules, not in the free strict category itself.

14. **Higher bubbles obstruct in every dimension.** If a nonidentity n-cell is an endomorphism of an iterated identity on any k-cell, k ≤ n − 2, then the strict n-skeleton has nonzero mod-p cohomology in arbitrarily high degrees for some prime p. The free cellular n-skeleton has cohomological dimension at most n, and homotopicality passes to skeleta. This covers composite bubbles and positive-dimensional bases. (Proof: the inclusion of the n-skeleton is discrete Conduché, so homotopicality passes to it; counting n-generators gives r : F(Γ_{≤n}) → Bⁿℤ, the endo-cell q gives i : Σ^{n−2}B²ℕ → F(Γ_{≤n}) with r i of degree m = r(q) > 0 on the bottom class; for an odd prime p ∤ m the iterated reduced powers P^{p^{j−1}}⋯P^1 of the pulled-back fundamental class restrict to m σ^{n−2}(x^{p^j}) ≠ 0.) So bubble-freeness in every dimension is necessary.

15. **Groupoidification does not recognise homotopicality from dimension 3.** Π = (x; a : 1_x ⇒ 1_x; ε : a ⇛ 1_{1_x}) is not homotopical (its 2-skeleton has the bubble a, and homotopicality passes to skeleta), yet both groupoidifications are contractible. Free(Π) groupoidifies to S² ∪_{deg 1} D³. In F(Π) the hom on 1_{1_x} is the free commutative monoid in Cat on (a, ε : a → 0), whose morphisms reduce to ε^{⊗k} ⊗ 1_{a^n}, so it is the poset (ℕ, ≥) with terminal object 0. Π is also homologically coherent. Suspensions give such examples in every dimension ≥ 3.
16. **Positive-to-one polygraphs are homotopical.** If every d-generator (d ≥ 2) has a generating (d−1)-cell as target and a non-identity source, Γ is homotopical. The proof: Zawadowski (arXiv 0708.2658, Cor. 13.5) identifies such polygraphs with presheaves on positive opetopes, which are acyclic atoms forming a full sieve (Leclerc; Hadzihasanovic); then Chanavat's Cor. 3.27 applies (directed complexes with acyclic atoms generate homotopy polygraphs). The same holds after choosing, independently in each dimension, source or target as the generator side (globular opposites). This gives a decidable sufficient test.
17. **The combinatorial core in dimension 3 (evidence, not proved).** Following item 4 one dimension up, fix a 3-dimensional diagram G (a strict 3-cell of F(Γ) together with a representing composite). Its *3-tier sequences* (decompositions into layers of simultaneous 3-steps) are ordered by refinement; call the poset Π_G. When no 3-step has an idle boundary wire (every wire in the source region of a step is touched by it), Π_G appears to be the poset of braid-fan cells inside an open convex cone cut out by a partial order on the 3-steps, hence contractible: checked by computer on about 550 random diagrams of that kind. The step "two 3-steps that can each fire first can fire together" fails in general (item 18). The true fibre also contains *interchange slices* (2-dimensional exchanges between 3-steps), which make it an infinite category. Truncating at K interchange slices is not homotopy-correct (K = 1 already gives a wedge of circles).

18. **Bubble-free does not suffice in dimension 3.** Objects 0 → 1 → 2 → 3, edges f₀, f₁ : 0 → 1, g : 1 → 2, h₀, h₁ : 2 → 3, 2-generators α₀, α₁ : f₀ ⇒ f₁, β₀, β₁ : h₀ ⇒ h₁, 3-generators A : α₀g ⇛ α₁g and B : gβ₀ ⇛ gβ₁. Γ is positive in every dimension, has an acyclic 1-graph and is bubble-free in every dimension (occurrence counts), but the fibre of Free(Γ) over the unique strict 3-cell α₀gβ₀ ⇛ α₁gβ₁ is S¹: the hom from 0 to 3 is (D × Q) ⊔_{Q×Q} (Q × D), with Q the walking parallel pair and D the walking 2-cell. In item 17's terms, A and B overlap on the idle wire g, so each can fire first but they cannot fire together, and the two serial orders are joined by two different exchanges. Further known facts for positive 3-polygraphs (all generating faces non-identity):
    - If no idle right suffix of a generating 3-face equals an idle left prefix of a generating 3-face, every fibre is contractible, so Γ is homotopical. For width-preserving generators this matching test is exact.
    - Every weight-two fibre is a point, an interval or a circle, and a circle occurs exactly when some path is an idle suffix of **both** faces of one generator and an idle prefix of **both** faces of another.
    - For every k ≥ 2 there is a positive 3-polygraph with a full-support fibre ≃ S^{k−1} ("seam rings") whose weight-two fibres are all contractible, so no bounded-weight test is exact.

19. **An exact criterion from strict cells, every dimension.** For n ≥ 3 let P be the set-valued rectangular n-fold array NF(Γ_{<n}) ⊔_{∐_z N∂Dⁿ} ∐_z NDⁿ (z ranging over the n-generators). Iterating the necklace formula of Barkan–Steinebrunner (arXiv 2301.08650, Thm A and Prop. 2.16) one direction at a time, with Thomason's theorem, gives ordinary categories A(c) over each new strict n-cell c whose classifying spaces are the fibres of Free(Γ) → F(Γ). So Γ is homotopical iff it is bubble-free in every dimension and every A(c) has connected, simply connected and acyclic nerve (a finite edge-word and integral-cycle test per challenge). This puts homotopicality in Π⁰₂. It is decidable with the jump of a bubble-freeness oracle; whether the bubble-freeness oracle alone suffices is open.

20. **A finite last-step criterion in dimension 3.** For a bubble-free 3-polygraph, introduce a marker h_z parallel to each 3-generator z. A chart is a strict 2-cell Q over the 2-skeleton and the markers; [Q] performs all marked changes. For a strict 3-cell c of positive weight, L(c) has one simplex with vertex set I for each strict factorization c = [Q] ∘ p with slot set I ≠ ∅, and its face on J ⊆ I performs I∖J first (appended to p) and keeps J. Then **a finite 3-polygraph is homotopical iff it is bubble-free in every dimension and every L(c) is contractible** (connected, trivial edge-path group, zero reduced integral homology). Each L(c) is finite and effectively computable, non-positive generators are allowed, and the proof is Quillen's Theorem A applied to "last chart and prefix" from the category of chart strings, by induction on weight. Examples: the middle wire of item 18 has L(c) a circle; the two- and three-seam rings give a square boundary and an octahedron boundary; a repeated deletion a a → 1 has two distinct prefixes joined by an edge (so the prefix must be kept).

21. **A sufficient condition for positive polygraphs, every dimension.** For a positive presentation of dimension n = d + 1 ≥ 3, form the d-skeleton with one parallel d-marker per top generator; its slots are canonical by Henry's representation theorem (arXiv 1711.00744). If the marked lower presentation passes recursively and, for every positive-weight strict c, the one-slot final charts with their prefixes are the vertices of a single simplex of final choices (every nonempty subset occurs for exactly one (p, Q)), then Γ is homotopical; more generally it suffices that every L(c) is contractible. This recovers item 16 (positive-to-one) and the no-matching case of item 18.

22. **No bounded test.** A uniform weight bound on the strict cells to test would contradict the seam rings of item 18, so any finite criterion must be at the level of generators (or of some finite structure other than weight-bounded cells).

## 4. Questions

**Question 1 (main: a generator-level criterion in every dimension).** Find a condition C on the finite list of generators of Γ (for example: no "seam ring", that is, no cyclic configuration of idle overlaps among top generators, suitably defined for n-generators and lower-dimensional idle sub-diagrams) such that Γ is homotopical iff F(Γ) is bubble-free in every dimension and Γ satisfies C, and prove it in every dimension. Items 19–20 give the exact criterion in terms of strict cells; the task is to replace "every L(c) (or A(c)) is contractible" by a test on generators. If no such finite test exists, prove that, for example by showing that homotopicality is not decidable relative to a bubble-freeness oracle.

**Question 2 (decidability).** Is homotopicality of finite bubble-free polygraphs decidable, or decidable relative to a bubble-freeness oracle? Item 19 gives decidability relative to the jump of that oracle. A proof in dimension 3 using item 20 would already be useful, but please aim for every dimension.

**Question 3 (fallback: last steps in every dimension).** Extend item 20 to every dimension for all bubble-free polygraphs, positive or not: Γ is homotopical iff every finite last-step complex L(c) is contractible, with L(c) built from strict d-cells over the marked d-skeleton. Item 21 does this for positive presentations; the obstacle in general is the homotopicality of the marked lower presentations and canonical slots without Henry's theorem.

## 5. What counts as a solution

A proof of Question 1, 2 or 3; a counterexample to a proposed generator-level criterion with a proof of non-homotopicality; a decidability or undecidability theorem; or a published reference.

# Researcher Response 1

## No computable generator test, even with positivity promised

**Theorem.** Homotopicality is undecidable on finite positive 3-polygraphs with just five 3-generators, even when each generator can occur at most once in any strict 3-cell. For each fixed dimension $n\ge3$ it is undecidable on finite positive $n$-polygraphs. Consequently the finite algorithm requested in Question 1 does not exist. The reduction gives an exact co-r.e.-complete family on the bubble-free promise.

**The two-seam presentation.** Use the two-seam ring of item 18. We recall the data needed for the reduction, in particular the statement about *all* its labels. Its five consecutive lanes are $s_0,p_1,s_1,p_2,s_2$. Each lane connects its own two consecutive objects. On $s_i$ there are three signal gates $L_i,M_i,R_i:u_i\Rightarrow v_i$. On $s_1$ there is also $t:q\Rightarrow u_1$. On each seam $p_i$ there are $t:q\Rightarrow p$ and $d_i,e_i:p\Rightarrow p$. Letters on different lanes have distinct types. The top changes are as follows. A pair of entries in a row denotes their horizontal composite.

| Change | Lanes | Before | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| $a_i$ | $s_{i-1},p_i$ | $M_{i-1},d_i$ | $R_{i-1},1_p$ |
| $b_i$ | $p_i,s_i$ | $1_p,L_i$ | $e_i,M_i$ |
| $z$ | whole line | $L_0,1_q,1_q,1_q,M_2$ | $M_0,1_q,1_q,1_q,R_2$ |

Call this presentation $S$. There is a label $c_*$ using all five changes whose space of chart strings is a circle. Every label omitting any one of the five changes has contractible space of chart strings. These are the full-support and proper-support parts of the same ring calculation. Briefly, a history uses each change at most once because each signal lane carries at most one signal gate and it progresses $L_i,M_i,R_i$. At a common gap of $a_i,b_i$ choose the spatial order of the two markers. For fixed choices the charts are the ordered partitions of the events respecting the signal precedences. Where the two choices agree one imposes the additional temporal precedence $a_i<b_i$. Intersections of the resulting sheets are contractible whenever these precedences are consistent. The only possible directed cycle uses all five changes, in the order $a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2,z,a_1$. With a missing change even the full intersection is contractible. For the standard label with both gaps common, the sign cover gives the boundary of a square. The proof tracks the specific gap when a lane contains repeated seam letters. [Two-seam specialization of the ring proof, including all other labels](../answered/relative-coherence-all-dimensions-r6-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-8)

**A passive strip.** Take a finite positive semi-Thue system $T$ on alphabet $E$ and two nonempty words $w,v$. Insert a strip between $p_1$ and $s_1$ by splitting their common object into $P_-,P_+$. Give the strip two edges $Q,U:P_-\to P_+$, a private vertex $m$, edges $l:P_-\to m$ and $r:m\to P_+$, and a loop at $m$ for each letter of $E$. Its ordinary 2-gates are
$$
        Q\Rightarrow lwr,\qquad x\Rightarrow y\quad(x\to y\in T),
          \qquad lvr\Rightarrow U .
$$
There are no further gates in the strip. Of the five old 3-generators only $b_1$ and $z$ span it. Insert $1_U$ on both faces of $b_1$ and $1_Q$ on both faces of $z$. This specifies the whole finite presentation $G(T,w,v)$. All generating faces are nonidentity. In a positive polygraph a nonidentity cell of dimension at least two has nonidentity source and target: serialize the top occurrences and inspect the first source and last target, where the generating face survives whiskering by additivity of occurrence counts. Thus every instance is bubble-free.

The passive strip has a strict diagram $Q\Rightarrow U$ exactly when $w\to_T^*v$. Indeed a serialization from $Q$ has to open the strip by the first gate, rewrite the tape word between the private ports, and close by the last gate. A derivation supplies the converse diagram. In any fixed passive diagram $K$ there is at most one place, up to interchange, to read the entire edge $Q$, and likewise for $U$. The $Q$ cut must be the initial cut, since no gate produces $Q$ and there is no way to act on a proper part of that single edge. The $U$ cut must be final by the dual argument. In the identity diagram on either edge its one gap serves as the corresponding cut.

**Erasure lemma.** Erase the strip by collapsing its edges and gates to identities. For every strict positive-weight label $c$ of $G$, erasure identifies its chart-string complex, with all face incidences, with that of its image $\bar c$ in $S$.

To prove this, fix the outer paths and the source 2-diagram of $c$. Ordinary 2-diagrams split into a diagram in each lane, including a fixed diagram $K$ in the strip: each ordinary gate acts wholly in its own lane, and different lanes commute. The outside graph traverses the strip at most once. If a path starts or ends inside it, one obtains the corresponding partial strip diagram. Along every top change $K$ stays fixed. In particular an occurrence of $z$ requires the $Q$ cut and an occurrence of $b_1$ requires the $U$ cut. Their occurrence counts are preserved by strict equality in $S$. Every string over $\bar c$ consequently requires only cuts available in this same $K$.

Lift a tier of such a string by restoring $K$. A marker for $z$ reads its initial $Q$ and a marker for $b_1$ reads its final $U$. If both occur in one tier, their order is already forced *in the erased chart*: on $p_1$ the marker for $z$ reads $q$ and the marker for $b_1$ reads $p$, with the gate $t:q\Rightarrow p$ between them. Restore a serialization of $K$ between the two markers. With just one marker put $K$ on its prescribed side, and with neither insert $K$ independently. This constructs a typed tier even if the tape rewrites change word lengths. It gives a unique strict tier: its restriction to the old boxes is fixed, the placements of the two possible reads relative to every box of $K$ are fixed, and every other old box is disjoint from $K$. Formally, two serializations of the same old diagram differ by interchanging independent boxes. Such a swap lifts after commuting disjoint tape boxes out of the way; the two reads cannot be swapped with each other when both are present. Serializations of $K$ itself differ by its strict interchanges. For partial strips no spanning marker can occur, and restoring the fixed partial diagram gives the same uniqueness.

Splitting a tier substitutes identities on the passive reads, hence the lift commutes with every ordered split, including its incidence. Initial and final diagrams restore with the same $K$. All lifted strings have label $c$: strings of a fixed strict label are connected by splits and their reverses (strict exchange in two-hole contexts), and these moves lift starting at the original string. Erasure and restoration are inverse on strings and their incidences. This proves the lemma. The use of actual chart strings to compute geometric fibres under the bubble-free hypothesis is the chart model in the [proof of item 20](../answered/homotopy-polygraphs-r3-2026-11-20.md#the-finite-last-step-criterion-in-dimension-three).

If $w\to_T^*v$, choose a witnessing $K:Q\Rightarrow U$. Restore the standard label $c_*$ by the tier construction. Its fibre is a circle, proving non-homotopicality. If $w\not\to_T^*v$, no $K$ can supply both cuts. Every label therefore omits $b_1$ or $z$. The erasure lemma and the proper-support calculation give contractibility for **every** positive-weight fibre. Weight zero gives identity points, and the lower skeleton is positive and homotopical by item 5. We have proved
$$
             G(T,w,v)\text{ is homotopical}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
             w\not\to_T^*v .
$$

Positive semi-Thue reachability is r.e.-complete (the input problem of item 3). For instance, encode a Turing configuration by a delimited tape word with one state letter immediately before its scanned symbol. Local nonempty-word rules implement head moves, including inserting a blank at a tape boundary. Only a halting state enters a cleanup phase that consumes adjacent letters one at a time and replaces the final delimited state by a fixed nonempty accept word. Conversely every derivation from the encoded initial word follows a machine transition until cleanup. Enumeration of derivations gives the r.e. upper bound. Thus the displayed equivalence proves the stated completeness for the parameterized family. It also rules out a sound and complete recursively enumerable system of finite positive certificates of homotopicality on this promise: enumerating certificates would semidecide the complement of reachability. Erasure preserves the five counts, so the top weight of *every* strict 3-cell is at most five. The unbounded information is carried by its lower diagram. Iterated suspension is effective, preserves positivity, and preserves and reflects the comparison by item 6. This proves the theorem in each asserted dimension.

**Oracle scope.** This answers the ordinary finite-test question negatively even when bubble-freeness of the input is promised. Access to a bubble-freeness oracle on *other* finite presentations is stronger. On this reduction family one such query suffices, by applying item 3 to $(T,w,v)$. For the unrestricted promise problem the reduction gives $0'$-hardness and item 19 gives a $0''$ upper bound. Whether $0'$ suffices in general is a separate issue.

## A unary obstruction to the last-step equivalence in dimension four

**Theorem.** There is a finite positive 4-polygraph $G$ whose ordinary lower skeleta are homotopical and whose $L(c)$ is a single vertex for every positive-weight strict 4-cell. One of those cells has geometric fibre $S^1$. Thus the equivalence in Question 3 fails even with canonical slots. The hypothesis on the marked lower presentation in item 21 has mathematical content.

Use the four objects, the edges $f_0,f_1,g,h_0,h_1$, and the gates $\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\beta_0,\beta_1$ of the middle-wire example in item 18. Give $G$ the following higher generators, with juxtaposition by $g$ denoting whiskering:
$$
 A_0,A_1:\alpha_0\Rightarrow\alpha_1,\qquad
 B:g\beta_0\Rightarrow g\beta_1,\qquad
 Z:A_0g\Rightarrow A_1g .
$$
Here the $A_i$ live on the first edge alone, $B$ spans the last two edges, and $Z$ spans the first two. Everything is positive. The 3-skeleton satisfies the no-matching test of item 18: none of its generating 3-faces has a nonempty idle right suffix. All lower skeleta are therefore homotopical.

At time index $k$, the marked 3-presentation $M_k$ has the old generators and the extra generators
$$
       C_i:\alpha_0 g\Rightarrow\alpha_1 g,\qquad 1\le i\le k.
$$
A strict 2-diagram has at most one $\alpha$ gate: its path visits the first lane at most once and its only change there is $f_0\Rightarrow f_1$. Hence a strict 3-cell of $M_k$ contains at most one occurrence from the entire set $\{A_0,A_1,C_1,\ldots,C_k\}$; each consumes $\alpha_0$ and produces $\alpha_1$. There are no multiple-slot charts. Moreover a unary change replaces the single $A_0$ in its 3-source by $A_1$, and can never be followed by another change. Thus every nonidentity strict 4-cell has weight one.

For clarity, distinct strict unary charts give distinct strict 4-cells in this argument. In an arbitrary free strict top extension one can compose the elementary one-hole contexts serially, with relations given by equality of lower marked contexts and exchange in two-hole contexts. This presentation follows by serializing a strict expression and expanding each interchange; equivalently, applying the ordinary fundamental category functor to the change-index simplicial set imposes the two-slot square relations. Here there are no two-slot contexts or composable pairs of changes. Thus each nonidentity 4-cell has exactly its one unary chart, and its only strict prefix in the last-step complex is the identity. [Ara et al., Appendix D on the free strict extension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00429)

**The geometric fibre.** Horizontally at time $k$, the cellular attachment for $G$ presents the *free geometric* 3-category on $M_k$. Indeed the two constant maps $[k]\to[1]$ give the two faces of the 4-globe, and the $k$ nonconstant maps give exactly the $k$ marked 3-globes. Starting with the strict old 3-skeleton here is legitimate because it is homotopical. Restricting a nonconstant change to its crossing edge just renames that marker. These assertions follow by horizontal Segalification of the cellular pushout, before the fourth-direction Segalification, in the order of [Barkan–Steinebrunner, Proposition 2.16](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08650).

In $M_1$ consider the strict 3-cell $d$ that uses $C_1$ and $B$ from $\alpha_0g\beta_0$ to $\alpha_1g\beta_1$. Its horizontal fibre is the middle-wire circle of item 18. To see why the two extra old generators $A_0,A_1$ have no effect, grade horizontal diagrams by their counts of these generators. Both counts in $d$ vanish. In the strict array for attaching the 3-generators, the zero-count subarray is exactly the attachment with just $C_1,B$ on the common 2-skeleton. In each successive horizontal necklace calculation all bead slots over a zero-count composite have zero counts by additivity. Restricting to this summand therefore gives its original fibre at each reflection. The same proof applies to the label $d_i$ with just $C_i,B$ in $M_k$, including the exclusion of every $C_j$ for $j\ne i$.

It remains to check that the circle survives composition in time. Evaluate the horizontally reflected array on the horizontal 3-globe, giving a simplicial space in the last direction. Its vertex space is the discrete set of old 3-cells up to equivalence. In degree $k$ its summands with no change are the old discrete labels up to equivalence, by the same zero-count argument and homotopicality of the old skeleton. Every remaining summand has one marker with a unique time index $i$. Restriction to edge $i$ is an equivalence onto the corresponding unary summand of degree one: over these labels the horizontal calculation uses the old generators and just $C_i$, and the restriction identifies this presentation with $M_1$ by renaming. All other edge restrictions give identities of the forced old endpoints. Conversely a composable tuple of edges over these discrete vertices contains at most one change, by the $A_0,A_1$ count. The Segal map to the iterated edge product is thus an equivalence on every summand. Only identity components can be equivalences since a change consumes the sole $A_0$; the row is also complete. The last reflection fixes this row. Lower globes have their old flags, as is also seen by putting a zero entry in the horizontal rectangle. Consequently the 4-cell specified by the unary chart $d$ has actual fibre $S^1$ over its strict label. This proves the theorem.

Suspending this example gives the same discrepancy in each fixed top dimension above four. The forward hom recovers the original presentation, its marked charts and their incidence maps. All other outer homs have no top changes. By item 6 the circle persists. In particular, inspecting actual top-step cycles in each ordinary skeleton misses a lower-dimensional interchange occurring *inside one marked step*. A recursive test that also inspects the homotopicality of the auxiliary marked presentations would inspect a different collection of labels.
