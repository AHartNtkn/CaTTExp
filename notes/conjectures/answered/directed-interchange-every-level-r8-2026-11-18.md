# Free higher categories with directed interchange in every dimension (eighth round)

*A self-contained problem. No background beyond standard higher category theory is assumed. Everything in §2 is proved and is not re-asked. Questions are in §4, smallest first.*

## 1. Setting

**Globular sets and composition.** Work with reflexive globular sets with source and target maps s, t, identities, and for k < n a partial composition ∗ₖ of n-cells along k-cells. Composition is written in **diagrammatic order** (u ∗ₖ v means "first u, then v"), and it is strictly associative and unital. The boundary formulas hold: for k < m and (m+1)-cells u, v,

```
s(u ∗ₖ v) = s(u) ∗ₖ s(v),     t(u ∗ₖ v) = t(u) ∗ₖ t(v).        (B)
```

For k < l < n and n-cells in interchange position put `L(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₗ b) ∗ₖ (c ∗ₗ d)` and `R(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₖ c) ∗ₗ (b ∗ₖ d)`. A strict ω-category requires L = R.

**The Gray case (directed interchange along 0-cells only).** In a category enriched in strict ω-categories with the lax Gray tensor product (Ara–Maltsiniotis, arXiv 1607.00668), for 2-cells α : f ⇒ f′ : x → y and β : g ⇒ g′ : y → z there is a 3-cell

```
χ_{α,β} : (f ◁ β) ∗₁ (α ▷ g′)  ⇛  (α ▷ g) ∗₁ (f′ ◁ β)
```

and nothing in the reverse direction. Composition along k-cells with k ≥ 1 is strict. For a finite globular pasting diagram Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ, the free such structure has homs ν(λΘᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘⱼ). Here λ is Steiner's directed complex, ⊗ is Steiner's tensor with `∂(x ⊗ y) = ∂x ⊗ y + (−1)^{|x|} x ⊗ ∂y`, and ν is Steiner's functor to strict ω-categories (Steiner, HHA 2004). Each hom is generated by tuples of cells, and a cell is determined by the set of generators it uses.

**The aim** is the same for directed interchange along k-cells for **every** k, including inside homs.

## 2. What is already known (all proved)

**(K1) Raw higher interchangers are impossible.** If every pair of n-cells in interchange position had an (n+1)-cell L → R, then applying this to identities and using (B) forces L = R one dimension down. That contradicts directedness.

**(K2) A single comparison for each simultaneous variation is impossible.** The obvious repair of (K1) adds, for 3-cells A : a ⇛ a′, …, D : d ⇛ d′ in interchange position, a 4-cell between the parallel 3-cells

```
L(A,B,C,D) ∗₂ χ_{a′}     and     χ_a ∗₂ R(A,B,C,D).        (N)
```

This cannot be required for every instance. Take Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², with two 3-cells A : α ⇛ α′ and B : β ⇛ β′ side by side along a 0-cell, and the instance (A, 1, 1, B). Its two sides map to the hom 2-cells

```
U = (v₀ ∘₀ u₁) ∘₁ c₁₁,     V = c₀₀ ∘₁ (u₀ ∘₀ v₁)
```

of ν(λD² ⊗ λD²), where `u_j = A ⊗ g_j`, `v_i = f_i ⊗ B` and `c_ij = α_i ⊗ β_j`. There is no 4-cell between U and V in either direction: the degree-3 chain would need a positive coefficient at u₀ (or at v₀), but every basis element of degree 3 has a non-positive coefficient there.

**(K3) The two variables have opposite naturality directions.** The degree-3 basis elements are `r_j = A ⊗ β_j` and `t_i = α_i ⊗ B`. Their differentials are

```
∂r_j = c_1j + u₁ − c_0j − u₀,     ∂t_i = c_i0 + v₁ − c_i1 − v₀.
```

So when only A varies, the Gray structure has a naturality 4-cell from the "χ first" side to the "variation first" side (V ⇛ U). When only B varies, it goes the other way (U ⇛ V). The simultaneous variation is the 5-cell A ⊗ B. It goes between two routes of 4-cells, `S → V → T` ⇛ `S → U → T`, where S and T are the composites through c₀₁ and c₁₀. In short, the higher structure is **tensor-shaped**: one Leibniz-signed naturality cell per variable, and higher cells between routes.

**(K4) Supports must use an expanded basis.** If every composite's support were the union of the supports of its parts, L and R would always have equal support. Then "support determines the cell" would force strict interchange.

**(K5) One finite basis forces finitely many cells.** If one finite set B admits injective support maps in every dimension, the structure has finitely many non-identity cells in total.

**(K6) No single directed complex presents the answer, and neither does a hom-wise family of them.** In ν(K), the two composites defining χ have the same chain table, so they are equal. A description must therefore be an iterated tower: objects, then homs presented again by objects and homs, and so on.

**(K7) The first uniform case through hom-dimension 3.** For Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², directing interchange inside the hom 𝒦(x, z) adds exactly two generators of hom-dimension 3 beyond the 25 tensor generators. They are the interchangers χ₁ of (A⊗g, f′⊗B) and χ₂ of (f⊗B, A⊗g′), the only pairs of hom-2-cells composable along a hom-0-cell. Their two sides have different supports. Neither occurs in the boundary of A ⊗ B.

**(K8) The (K7) 3-skeleton cannot be extended by 4-generators alone.** Write h_i^j = α_i ⊗ g_j, k_j^i = f_i ⊗ β_j, P_ij = k_j^0 h_i^1, Q_ij = h_i^0 k_j^1, so c_ij : P_ij ⇒ Q_ij. The tensor 3-generators are r_j : c_0j ; a_j^+ ⇛ a_j^- ; c_1j and t_i : b_i^- ; c_i1 ⇛ c_i0 ; b_i^+ (a_j^±, b_i^± the whiskered edge cells u_j, v_i), and the interchangers are χ₁ : b_0^+ ; a_1^+ ⇛ a_0^+ ; b_1^+ and χ₂ : a_0^- ; b_1^- ⇛ b_0^- ; a_1^-. The six composite hom-2-cells P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ are S = b_0^- ; c_01 ; a_1^+, V₀ = c_00 ; b_0^+ ; a_1^+, V₁ = c_00 ; a_0^+ ; b_1^+, T = a_0^- ; c_10 ; b_1^+, U₀ = a_0^- ; b_1^- ; c_11 and U₁ = b_0^- ; a_1^- ; c_11. Whiskering gives t̄₀ : S ⇛ V₀, χ̄₁ : V₀ ⇛ V₁, r̄₀ : V₁ ⇛ T, r̄₁ : S ⇛ U₁, χ̄₂ = χ₂ ∗₁ 1_{c₁₁} : U₀ ⇛ U₁ and t̄₁ : U₀ ⇛ T. Since ∂(A⊗B) = t₁ − t₀ + r₁ − r₀, requirement 4 forces a 4-cell between the route t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀ from S to T and a route from S to T using r̄₁ and t̄₁. In the free 3-skeleton with exactly the 25 tensor generators plus χ₁, χ₂ and no 2-equations, the first route exists, but the second would have to go S ⇛ U₁ and then U₁ ⇛ U₀ against χ̄₂: in the directed graph of these composites the only S → T path is t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀. So no list of 4-generators recovers A ⊗ B. The naturality cells of χ₁, χ₂ are trivial here (the edge homs have no 3-generators).

The repair must change dimension-3 data: an extra 3-generator such as h : S ⇛ U₀ whose image under the Gray quotient is r̄₁, or a 2-equation such as U₀ = U₁. Inverting χ₂ would also work but breaks requirement 3.

**(K9) The orientation of χ₁, χ₂ in (K7) is forced by substitution (proved).** One might try to avoid (K8) by orienting every inner interchanger by the factor order of the enclosing tensor (always "Θ₂-cell first ⇛ Θ₁-cell first"), which keeps χ₁ and reverses χ₂. This fails requirement 5. The free object on Σ(D² ∨ D²) has, inside its hom, the interchanger of its two 2-cells A′ (left) and B′ (right), oriented as in the Gray case: "B′ first ⇛ A′ first". The assignment A′ ↦ f₀ ⊗ B and B′ ↦ A ⊗ g₁ (with the hom-0-cells and hom-1-cells sent to f₀⊗g₀, f₀⊗g₁, f₁⊗g₁ and to the matching edges) respects all boundaries, so substitution sends that interchanger to a 3-cell from "A ⊗ g₁ first" to "f₀ ⊗ B first", which is exactly χ₂ in its (K7) orientation. The pair χ₁ is forced the same way via A′ ↦ A ⊗ g₀, B′ ↦ f₁ ⊗ B. So χ₂ cannot be reversed: adding the reversed cell as well breaks requirement 3, which is the "inverting χ₂" option above. Inner interchangers are oriented by position (right cell first ⇛ left cell first), as at the outer level, and some repair of dimension-3 data is needed (see (K10)).

**(K10) The smallest case is repaired by a factorization.** The six hom-2 composites P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ of (K8) are the maximal paths of an oriented 3-cube with coordinates A (edges a_j^±), B (edges b_i^±) and C (edges c_ij): S = BCA, V₀ = CBA, V₁ = CAB, T = ACB, U₀ = ABC, U₁ = BAC. The six whiskered hom-3 cells t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀, r̄₁, χ̄₂, t̄₁ are its six square faces (adjacent transpositions); every proper interval carries at most one of them. Add one hom-3 generator and one hom-3 equation:

```
h : S ⇛ U₀,        r̄₁ = h ∗₂ χ̄₂.        (F)
```

Then the maximal hom-3 interval is the free category on S → V₀ → V₁ → T, S → U₀ → U₁, U₀ → T, with 11 non-identity cells with pairwise distinct label sets, two routes S → T (t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ and h ; t̄₁), and no reverse of χ̄₁ or χ̄₂. Killing the inner interchangers gives exactly the free Gray diamond with q(h) = q(r̄₁), and A ⊗ B lifts to one 4-generator W : t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ ⇛ h ; t̄₁ with no non-identity contexts. Supports use one label per whiskered context, so r̄₁ is no longer a basis element (its support is that of h χ̄₂). The equation (F) cannot be dropped or replaced by a 4-cell h χ̄₂ ⇛ r̄₁ without a new class of cells killed by the quotient. h is not an interchanger: S and U₀ each contain a c_ij, and every hom-2 path containing a c_ij spans the whole external hom-0 interval.

h is **not forced**. The mirror repair k : U₁ ⇛ T with t̄₁ = χ̄₂ ∗₂ k works equally well, and so does the symmetric repair with both h and k and both equations (12 cells, second route h ; χ̄₂ ; k, same exact Gray quotient). The alternative 2-equation U₀ = U₁ makes χ̄₂ an endomorphism and needs further equations (at least χ̄₂² = χ̄₂ under set supports), so it is not preferred.

**(K11) The first larger case, ΣD³ ∨ ΣD², is completed through its top cell.** Write the first factor as α₀, α₁ : f₀ → f₁, A⁰, A¹ : α₀ ⇒ α₁, Ω : A⁰ ⇛ A¹, and the second as β₀, β₁ : g₀ → g₁, B : β₀ ⇒ β₁. The maximal hom-3 interval is two copies (ε = 0, 1) of the repaired hexagon of (K10), with arrows a^ε, b^ε, c^ε, h^ε, e^ε, f^ε, joined by six variations z_X : X⁰ → X¹ (Ω whiskered in place of the A-edge of X).
- **Hom-4 faces.** W^ε = A^ε ⊗ B : a^ε b^ε c^ε ⇛ h^ε f^ε. R_j = Ω ⊗ β_j. The naturalities N₁, N₂ of χ₁, χ₂ have opposite directions: Ω varies the left input of χ₁ and the right input of χ₂. Two inner interchangers along a hom-1 cell, 𝖠 : z_S a¹ ⇛ a⁰ z_{V₀} (of t₀ with Ω) and 𝖥 : f⁰ z_T ⇛ z_{U₀} f¹. Writing 𝖡, 𝖢, 𝖣, 𝖤 for the whiskered N₁, R₀, R₁, N₂, the maximal hom-4 interval has the seven hom-3 paths x₁ … x₇ from S⁰ to T¹.
- **Added data.** Two hom-4 generators and two equations:
  ```
  H : h⁰ z_{U₀} ⇛ z_S h¹,                H e¹ = (h⁰ 𝖤) ; 𝖣                 (variation of the factorization)
  K : a⁰ z_{V₀} b¹ c¹ ⇛ z_S h¹ f¹,       z_S W¹ = (𝖠 b¹ c¹) ; K            (k-type factorization in hom-dimension 4)
  ```
  Then there are exactly two routes x₁ ⇛ x₇: L = (W⁰ z_T) ; (h⁰ 𝖥) ; (H f¹) and R = (a⁰ b⁰ 𝖢) ; (a⁰ 𝖡 c¹) ; K. The top generator Z = Ω ⊗ B : L ⇛ R has the orientation of ∂(Ω ⊗ B) = W¹ − W⁰ − R₁ + R₀.
- **Checks.** On the maximal interval there are 68 hom-3 cells and 98 hom-4 cells, all with distinct supports; the graphs are acyclic, and the reductions have no overlaps. The Gray quotient is exact: q(H) = q(R₁ whiskered), q(K) = q(z_S W¹), and the surviving tensor generators are R₀, R₁, W⁰, W¹.
- **Forcing substitution.** A⁰, A¹ ↦ A, Ω ↦ 1_A sends H ↦ 1_h, K ↦ W and Z ↦ 1_W, so K is not a cell that every degeneration kills.
- **The source/sink pattern.** In both the h-repair and the k-repair of (K10), the face 𝖠 (whose pair flips relative to the tensor faces) creates a second source and a second sink one dimension up. With source-joining (h) in hom-dimension 3, the one-face joining that works in hom-dimension 4 is sink-joining (K); a source-joining alternative would have to factor a composite. Each existing factorization also needs a variation cell and equation under each higher variable (H).

**(K12) The source/sink count fails in the ambient graph.** Reversing one arrow u → v of a finite directed graph changes the number of sources by 1[d⁻(v)=1] − 1[d⁻(u)=0], and the number of sinks by 1[d⁺(u)=1] − 1[d⁺(v)=0].
- Take the interval of λD² ⊗ λD² ⊗ λD² with hom-1 boundary (β₀α₀)γ₀ → (α₁β₁)γ₁. Its hom-2 paths are the 24 orderings of A, B (updates of the first two factors), C (the crossing βα → αβ) and D (the third-factor update).
- Its arrows are BC → CB, CA → AC and DX → XD. The arrow between A and B is positional: AB → BA when C follows the pair, and BA → AB when C precedes it.
- Compare with the graph that uses BA → AB everywhere, which has one source, DBCA, and one sink, ACBD. The positional graph has sources {DBCA, DABC} and sinks {ACBD, BACD}.
- Of the three reversed arrows, BADC → ABDC creates neither a source nor a sink.

So a flipped face does not create one extra source/sink pair in the full comparison graph. It does so only for an isolated polygon.

**(K13) Local factorization does not force alternation.** For a simple oriented polygon with arms pᵢ : sᵢ → tᵢ and qᵢ : sᵢ₊₁ → tᵢ (indices mod r), add hᵢ : s₀ → sᵢ for 1 ≤ i < r, with p₀ = h₁;q₀ and hᵢ;pᵢ = hᵢ₊₁;qᵢ for 1 ≤ i < r − 1. The boundary telescopes to h_{r−1}+p_{r−1}−q_{r−1}, a pair of parallel paths s₀ → t_{r−1}. So source-joining alone always works locally. For r = 2 this is (K10). For r ≥ 3 the equations identify two composites; this is harmless locally (K16).

**(K14) The triple tensor through hom-dimension 3.**
- *Words.* Write a, b, c for the hom-2 updates of the three factors. Start from hom-1 order zyx, with crossings p : yx → xy, q : zx → xz and r : zy → yz. The tensor hom-3 generator is τ : rqp → pqr.
- *The maximal interval.* Its hom-2 words are the 240 orderings of a, b, c, p, q, r whose crossings occur as rqp or pqr. Its hom-3 arrows are:
  - the tensor naturalities, where the lower-index update moves earlier past a crossing involving its factor and the higher-index update moves later;
  - the inner interchangers, oriented right-first ⇛ left-first by the current hom-1 order;
  - the contextual instances of τ.

  That makes 240 + 240 + 24 = 504 arrows.
- *Added data.* Six families of new hom-3 arrows, each with an equation old = new ; inner, applied in every typed context:

  | New arrow | Factors | Contexts |
  |---|---|---|
  | h_p : bpa → abp | bpa → bap | 8 |
  | h_q : cqa → acq | cqa → caq | 8 |
  | h_r : crb → bcr | crb → cbr | 8 |
  | λ_x : rqa → arq | rqa → raq | 12 |
  | h_z : crqp → pcqr | the τ-instance crqp → cpqr | 6 |
  | μ : crqa → acrq | cλ_x, that is crqa → carq | 3 |

  μ factors a previously added joining arrow. Without it the interval has two sources, crqbpa and acrqbp, and a composite-valued substitution A ↦ a, B ↦ c;r of the binary h-repair has no image.
- *Result.* The retained basis has 504 arrows, the graph is acyclic, and there are 40,172 non-identity hom-3 paths with pairwise distinct edge sets. The unique source is crqbpa. There are **ten sinks**: apqbrc, apqcbr, bapqrc, braqpc, brcaqp, capqbr, cbapqr, cbraqp, raqpbc and rcaqpb. Killing the inner interchangers gives the same quotient as the unrepaired presentation.
- *Hom-4 lifts.* The eighteen tensor hom-4 generators have typed lifts. Six of them, of pattern (2,2,0), are as in (K10). The other twelve take the three shapes W_x, W_y, W_z of patterns (2,1,1), (1,2,1) and (1,1,2), with four choices each:
  - W_x : rqpa → pqra → pqar → paqr → apqr ⇛ rqpa → rqap → arqp → apqr, through λ_x p;
  - W_y : rqbp → rbqp → brqp → bpqr → pbqr → pqbr ⇛ rqbp → rqpb → pqrb → pqbr;
  - W_z : crqp → rcqp → rqcp → rqpc → pqrc ⇛ crqp → pcqr → pqcr → pqrc, through h_z.

**(K15) The interchanger-of-a-composite convention.** The substitution in (K14) assumes that crossing against a composite hom-1 path is the composite of the pairwise crossings. That holds in Gray categories, but it does not follow from (B), associativity and units alone.

**(K16) Source-only polygons keep injective supports (proved).** In (K13), every relation runs from the unique source s₀ to a sink, so no relation has a context. The presented category is thin except at (s₀, t_{r−1}), which has two arrows. Orienting the relations gives a one-step normal form, and the edge set of the normal form is an injective support for every r and every arm length. A union-preserving injective invariant with 2|V| + 1 labels also exists. Caveat: overlapping systems such as a;c = b;d, a;d = b;c have no injective union-support.

**(K17) Only one sink matters, and the top cell lifts (proved).**
- *The sink.* Undirected inner interchanges split the 240 words of (K14) into 54 classes. These are the fibres of the tensor hom-2 chain. Only one of the ten sinks, T = apqbrc, lies in the class of the top atom's hom-2 target. The source is S = crqbpa.
- *Paths.* The words reaching T carry 244 arrows and 1,226 paths S → T, with 25 distinct tensor hom-3 chains.
- *The lift.* Keeping all hom-3 data, the top cell lifts as follows:
  - 24 hom-4 operations A_{ij} : P_i ⇒ P_j between chosen paths, 15 of them new and 9 literal whiskerings of the W-cells;
  - six parallel hom-4 routes R₀ … R₅ : P₂₄ ⇒ P₀;
  - six hom-5 generators F_x^±, F_y^±, F_z^± between those routes;
  - one hom-6 generator Ω : F_x^- F_y^- F_z^- ⇒ F_z^+ F_y^+ F_x^+, whose image is the atom x₂ ⊗ y₂ ⊗ z₂ in every dimension.
- *Scope.* This is an extension with a map to ν(K), not yet one whose inner-interchange quotient is ν(K).

**(K18) Source-only completion of a comparison graph (proved).** Let Q be a finite quiver whose generating arrows are split into inner arrows E and positive arrows P, with E acyclic and a rank φ : V(Q) → ℤ constant along E and strictly increasing along P. For every α : s → t in P and every literal directed E-path R : u → t, adjoin j_{α,R} : s → u (with j_{α,1_t} = α), and impose j_{α,e;R} ; e = j_{α,R} for e : u → v in E and R : v → t. Then:
- the set of generators is finite and does not depend on the order of saturation;
- read as contractions (positive arrow followed by an inner arrow), the equations are confluent and terminating, so every path has a unique normal form;
- the completion is acyclic, normal forms have injective edge-set supports, the original path category embeds, and killing E gives exactly the original inner quotient.

On the 240-word interval of (K14) a rank with values 0, …, 7 exists; there are 264 positive seed arrows and 877 positive generators after completion (613 new, 1,117 arrows in all), and 54 inner classes. The completion is natural under maps of comparison categories that send positive seeds to positive seeds and inner arrows to inner paths.

**(K19) The completion is not natural under generator-to-composite maps (proved).** In (K14), take x = bacrqp, u = abpcqr, w₁ = abcpqr, w₂ = bapcqr, v = bacpqr, with inner arrows p : u → w₁, e : w₁ → v, q : u → w₂, f : w₂ → v, and the tensor arrow τ : x → v with its factorization τ = h_z ; f. The completion has j_{τ,e} : x → w₁ with j_{τ,e} ; e = τ. The comparison-category map fixing inner arrows and sending τ ↦ h_z ; f does not extend: an image H : x → w₁ with H ; e = h_z ; f would be j_{h_z,R} ; P with inner paths R, P and P ; e = R ; f as literal inner words, impossible since e ≠ f (contractions preserve the ordered list of positive seeds). This map has not been realised as a substitution of globular generators, so it does not refute requirement 5.

**(K20) Two-sided division is not confluent (proved).** In (K14), with tensor arrows α : bapcqr → bapqcr and β : abpqcr → abpqrc and the inner arrow e : abpqcr → bapqcr, adjoining both a source division h : bapcqr → abpqcr (h ; e = α) and a sink division k : bapqcr → abpqrc (e ; k = β) gives the critical pair h ; e ; k → α ; k and → h ; β, both irreducible and distinct. The presented category satisfies the composite–composite equation α ; k = h ; β. It is not of the crossed shape a;c = b;d, a;d = b;c.

**(K21) Hom-4 comparisons and exactness through hom-dimension 3 (proved).**
- *Disjoint operations.* For two hom-3 operations α (left) and β (right) on disjoint intervals of a hom-2 word, D_{α,β} : (β ; α^β) ⇛ (α ; β^α), right region first ⇛ left region first. On the 240-word interval there are 258 distinct such boundary pairs (50 need eliminated operations expanded through the factorization equations of (K14)).
- *Inner naturality.* For a hom-3 operation α : U ⇛ V and an independent hom-2 update z, comparing "slide z across U, then α" with "α, then slide z across V": for factor degrees (2,1) inner crossing first ⇛ operation first, for (1,2) the reverse. This agrees with the Leibniz signs of (K3). There are 62 distinct local comparisons N. They use (K15) as a convention.
- *Exactness.* On the top atom's hom-1 boundary, imposing I = D = N = 1 (I the inner interchangers) gives 54 hom-2 classes and 874 hom-3 path classes, which are exactly the numbers of 2-cells and 3-cells of ν(λD² ⊗ λD² ⊗ λD²) with that 1-boundary (identities included). Without N it is not exact: the single-arrow equations leave 152 generator classes over 116 tableaux, and two-arrow equations cannot merge them.
- The D and N comparisons introduce no reverse path in the comparison graph they generate on the raw hom-3 paths.

**(K22) The fifteen fresh hom-4 lifts of (K17) (proved after the lower quotient).** After I = D = N = 1, every one of the 15 new operations A_{ij} has an expression through an existing W-cell (W_x, W_y, W_z or their (2,2,0)-type analogues W_{xy}, W_{xz}) with prefix and suffix whiskerings and the same quotient boundary. Three of them can be defined before quotienting as a whiskered W-cell followed by forward D-comparisons. A fourth, A_{2,1}, is identified through one new hom-4 generator H and one hom-4 equation whose two sides reduce, after the lower quotient, to H̄ = 1 ∗₂ W̄. The remaining eleven are identified only in the quotient. Support injectivity in hom-dimensions 4 and 5 and exactness through hom-dimension 6 are not established.

**Literature.** No construction and no impossibility result is known.
- Closest: n-sesquicategories, which have no interchange at all (Araújo, arXiv 2202.09293 and 2210.07704; Forest–Mimram, arXiv 2211.16099 and *Rewriting in Gray categories*, MSCS 2022).
- Crans's teisi.
- Bourke–Lobbia, arXiv 2610.31448: their §4.2.6 lax 4-categories have non-invertible outer interchangers, but invertible interchange inside homs.
- Bourke–Gurski's cocategorical obstruction needs hypotheses not assumed here.

**(K23) A substitution-natural source-only completion (proved, machine-checked).** In the setting of (K18), for α : s → t in P and inner paths I : t → m and R : u → m, adjoin j_{α,I,R} : s → u ("α ; I ; R⁻¹"), with j_{α,1,1} = α and the equations j_{α,I,e;R} ; e = j_{α,I,R} and j_{α,I,1} = α ; I. (K18) is the case I = 1. The left-hand sides never overlap, so the oriented system has the diamond property. It is confluent with unique normal forms for any quiver, Path(Q) embeds, seed lists are invariant, every j raises φ (so the completion is acyclic and normal forms have injective supports), killing E gives the original inner quotient, and the generators are finite. Every map of comparison categories that sends inner arrows to inner paths and each seed to a word containing a seed (pre ; β ; post, β the last seed) extends by j_{α,I,R} ↦ pre ; j_{β, post;F(I), F(R)}. ## 3. Requirements

A **tensor-shaped lax-interchange ω-category** has, for every k < l, elementary interchangers χ : L ⇛ R for (l+1)-cells with no reverse cell in free objects; one naturality cell per varying argument, oriented by the Leibniz sign; higher cells between routes of lower naturality cells; and coherence equations to be determined. The free objects F(Γ) must satisfy:
1. an explicit combinatorial description by generators with explicit boundaries;
2. two cells of the same dimension using the same generators, in the expanded basis, are equal;
3. every elementary interchanger exists and its reverse does not;
4. making all interchangers with k ≥ 1 identities gives the free Gray-enriched category, and making all interchangers identities gives the free strict ω-category;
5. closure under substitution of cells for generators.

## 4. Injective normal-form supports through hom-dimension 5

The support argument applies to the specified finite comparison presentations, using the normal-form interpretation of support permitted in K16 and K18.

### 4.1 A finite-DAG lemma removes the need for convergent equation orientations

Let \(Q\) be a finite directed multigraph without directed cycles, and impose any equations between parallel \(Q\)-paths.

There are finitely many paths: a path cannot repeat a vertex, so its length is at most \(|Q_0|-1\). Consequently every congruence class has a computable least representative in a fixed ordering of paths. Congruence is computed by bidirectional equation replacement in all path contexts; this terminates because the path set is finite.

A nonempty path in a DAG is determined by its set of **actual edge IDs**. Its incidence relations determine their unique directed order.

For the higher-dimensional application, define support recursively:
\[
\operatorname{supp}_n(f)=
\operatorname{Edges}_n(\operatorname{NF}(f))
\;\cup\;
\operatorname{supp}_{n-1}(sf)
\;\cup\;
\operatorname{supp}_{n-1}(tf),
\tag{9}
\]
with generator IDs tagged by dimension.

If the top-dimensional edge set is nonempty, it determines the normal path. If it is empty, the cell is an identity, distinguished by its lower support. Thus support is injective.

**No composite graph vertex receives a new support atom.** The labels are actual contextual generating edges and inherited lower-dimensional generators. Support is taken after normalization; it is not asserted to preserve unions of arbitrary expressions.

### 4.2 Application in hom-dimension 4

## 5. Two broader completion attempts expose additional restrictions

These results explain why repairing only the generator-to-composite defect does not solve Q1.

### 5.1 A finite completion can handle positive composite images

For a weak inner class \(K\), define the finite groupoid
\[
G_2(E)(u,v)=
\{g\in\mathbb F_2^{E_K}:\partial g=[u]+[v]\}.
\]
Composition is addition over \(\mathbb F_2\).

For every positive seed \(\alpha:s\to t\) and \(g:t\to u\), add
\[
j[\alpha,g]:s\to u,
\]
with \(j[\alpha,0]=\alpha\), and impose
\[
j[\alpha,g];e=j[\alpha,g+[e]].
\tag{11}
\]

These contractions terminate and are confluent: their left sides have sorts “positive, inner,” so two reductions cannot overlap in one edge. The original path category embeds because an inner path in a DAG is determined by its edge-parity vector. Killing \(E\) identifies every \(j[\alpha,g]\) with \(\alpha\).

This construction is natural for comparison functors sending inner edges to inner paths and positive seeds to **positive composite paths**. The extension transports the final positive factor by the image parity vector.

It still fails the globular degeneration in §1, because that degeneration sends a positive arrow to an identity.

There is also a higher-dimensional obstruction. In the actual inner diamond from K19, write
\[
P=p;e,\qquad Q=q;f,
\]
and retain its existing comparison \(D:P\Rightarrow Q\). With \(A=h_z\), define
\[
a=j[A,[q]],\qquad
b=j[A,[p]+[e]+[f]].
\]
There are distinct normal generators \(X,Y\) satisfying
\[
aP=X,\quad aQ=Y,\qquad
bP=Y,\quad bQ=X.
\tag{12}
\]
Whiskering \(D\) gives
\[
a[D]:X\Rightarrow Y,
\qquad
b[D]:Y\Rightarrow X.
\]

Thus this finite completion introduces opposite **whiskered** comparisons at the next dimension. It cannot inherit the preceding DAG support proof recursively.

### 5.2 Unrestricted free division is infinite

Instead, freely adjoin divisions \(D_e(f)\) for arbitrary positive \(f\), subject only to
\[
D_e(f);e=f.
\tag{13}
\]
Flatten composition into words and contract (13), including in division arguments.

Counting primitive letters and division constructors proves termination: each contraction reduces that count by two. Reductions at disjoint positions commute. A reduction in another reduction’s argument also commutes with it, because the outer rule neither duplicates nor deletes that argument. Hence normal forms are unique.

On the same inner diamond, set
\[
a_0=A,\qquad
a_{n+1}=D_p\!\bigl(D_e(a_n;f)\bigr);q.
\tag{14}
\]
Every \(a_n\) has the same boundary. These expressions are irreducible and have weights \(1+4n\), so they are pairwise distinct.

Therefore **unrestricted free division saturation is already infinite on this finite comparison diagram**. This concerns that specific division rule; it is not an infinitude theorem for every possible lax-interchange theory.

Both constructions and their proofs are supplied in `r8_substitution_notes.md`, with checks in `r8_substitution_check.py`.
