# Limits, colimits, representables and Yoneda in CaTT style

*Design notes, revised 2026-11-18 (§3 and §5.2 rewritten as practical comparisons). Companion to `catt-survey.md` (project files). Scope: representables, Yoneda-style principles, and (weighted, lax, ω-)limits and colimits as CaTT-style rules. Internal hom / Gray tensor and fibrations are handled in other threads; the interfaces are marked **[→ Gray]** and **[→ fibrations]**. Fixed constraints: no universes, the ambient may stay external, and no definitional β/η rules. Universal properties are witnessed by generated cells.*

*Markers. **[paper]**: checked against the cited paper this session. **[recall]**: from background knowledge, not re-checked. **[conj]**: unproved; every such claim has a proof attempt in §10 and, where the attempt stalls, a self-contained problem in `notes/conjectures/`. **[proposal]**: a design choice being put forward. Unproved claims are investigated, never used as reasons to reject an option.*

---

## 0. Summary

1. **Adopt Mikhail's CaTT_lim as the limit core** ([2412.13310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13310)). It already has the right shape: cone and transfor *contexts* generated by judgments, plus one filler schema (`ucone`, `uni`) with free-variable side conditions. Section 2 gives its rules in full, checked against the PDF.
2. **Add markings to diagrams** so some cone cells must be invertible. This gives pseudo and strict (homotopy) limits, pullbacks, equalizers and comma/hom objects. By Abellán–Haugseng–Martini, weighted limits reduce to partially lax ones, so **weighted limits need no separate rule**, only a marking and substitution along a "display" map (§4).
3. **Colimits are the dual rule family**, obtained by reversing the 1-cells as Mikhail remarks. Since the ambient is not self-dual, this is a second, mirrored rule family, not a meta-operation (§4.3).
4. **Representables.** With a terminal object ⊤ (the limit of the empty diagram):
   - *elements* of an object C are 1-cells `⊤ → C`, and their composition is CaTT in the hom `Hom(⊤, C)` via suspension (§5.1);
   - *free ω-categories on contexts* `⟦Γ⟧` can be either a primitive with its own universal property (R-A) or derived as `laxcolim_Γ (const ⊤)` (R-B, **[conj]**). §5.2 compares the two;
   - in both, the Yoneda principle "maps `⟦Γ⟧ → C` are Γ-shaped families of elements of C" is a universal property witnessed by generated cells, with no definitional β/η.
5. **Pseudo or lax ambient.** If the ambient is a model of CaTT, its 2-cells are forced to be *pseudo* transformations, because every coherence, interchange included, is invertible (§3.1). The two ways out, a pseudo ambient (P) and a Gray-style lax ambient (L), are compared in `notes/pseudo-vs-gray.md`. §4–§6 hold under either.
6. **What this reaches.** All finite (lax, pseudo, weighted) limits and colimits, and cotensors by shapes. Hom objects and an ℕ-like object `Hom_{⟦x, s:x→x⟧}(x, x)` with iteration (§6) come from the limit rules under L, and need the Gray thread's lax cotensors under P (§10.1). **What it does not reach:** colimits indexed by an *object* rather than a context (sequential colimits, general Kan extensions) and induction (dependent elimination). Both need internal hom [→ Gray] or fibrations [→ fibrations].
7. **Proof attempts (§10).**
   - Proved: CaTT_lim's universal property forces cone morphisms to be pseudo in a CaTT ambient. So its rules fit L as written, and fit P only with that modification.
   - Proved: marking needs no closure condition.
   - Partly proved: free objects as lax colimits of the point.
   - Reduced to one semantic theorem: weighted limits as marked limits.
   - Solved (external researcher, verified): the ℕ-like object is discrete (§10.5).
   - Remaining gaps are written up as problems in `notes/conjectures/`.

## 1. Conventions

- **The ambient.** We are working "CaTT over ωCat": the base type ⋆ is read as "ω-categories", 1-cells as functors, and higher cells as transfors. `Ob` in Mikhail's paper is our ⋆.
- **Diagrams are substitutions.** A diagram of shape Γ in the ambient is a substitution `Δ ⊢ γ : Γ`, as in CaTT_lim. A *generic* diagram is the identity on Γ, so `lim_Γ` is the limit of the generic diagram and `lim_Γ[γ]` is the limit of γ. This is a big ergonomic win of the context-as-diagram design and should be kept.
- **Orientation.** "Lax" follows Mikhail. For `f : x → y` in Γ, the cone cell is `p_f : p_y → p_x ∗ f` (diagrammatic composition). "Oplax" is the opposite in dimension 1, and other variants come from opposites in other dimensions (Benjamin–Markakis, [2402.01611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01611)).

## 2. Literature: what each source gives

### 2.1 Mikhail, CaTT_lim [paper]

Source: T. J. Mikhail, *A type-theoretic definition of lax (∞,∞)-limits*, [arXiv 2412.13310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13310), Definition 4.15. Rules are transcribed from the PDF with notation lightly normalised.

**Linearity (Def. 3.1).** For `Γ ⊢ t : A` and x ∈ FV(t) with dim x = dim t:

```
(VAR) x ∝ x
(OP)  x ∝ op_{Γ,s→t}[γ]   if there is a unique x_i ∈ FV(Γ) with x ∝ γ_i
(COH) x ∝ coh_{Γ,s→t}[γ]  likewise
```

**Cones as contexts.**

```
(EK)  c : Ob  cone(∅; c)

      Γ, c:Ob, Π  cone(Γ; c)
      Γ, x:X, c:Ob, Π ⊢ s →_A t
      τCond(s:A, x:X, c:Ob, Π)      σCond(t:A, x:X, Π)
(KE)  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
      Γ, x:X, c:Ob, Π, p_x : s →_A t   cone((Γ, x:X); c)
```

- `σCond(t:A, x:X, Π)` says that t:A is "categorical", that x ∝ t, and that
  `FV(t:A) = FV(x:X) ∪ ⋃ { FV(p:T_p) | p ∈ FV(Π), y ∈ FV(σ(x):∂X), y ∝ τ(p) }`.
- `τCond` is the same with `FV(c:Ob)` in place of `FV(x:X)`, and without linearity.
- In words: the target of `p_x` is "x composed after the projections of x's source", and its source is "the projection of x's target". Both are fixed by free-variable conditions, never by naming a composite.

**Universal cone.**

```
(Θ, x:X, Θ') cone(Γ; c)     Γ ⊢uni_Γ κ : Θ     Δ ⊢ γ : Γ
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
           Δ ⊢ ucone_{Γ,x}[γ] : X[κ ∘ γ]
```

Here `lim_Γ := ucone_{Γ,c}`, and `⊢uni` is an auxiliary judgment that builds the universal cone variable by variable.

**Conical transfors** (`ctrf`, the Gray-shaped contexts `Γ ⊗ Dⁿ` adapted to cones) and **postcomposition with a cone** (`star`) are further context judgments. They also have free-variable side conditions; `Star` asks that `FV(u:X)` be the free variables of the appropriate iterated source or target of α, plus the relevant projections.

**Universal property.**

```
K cone(Γ; c)    M ctrf(Γ; c)    dim(α) = n_M
Λ, α:T_α, Δ ⊢_{K,Θ} x : X       Λ, α:T_α, Δ ⊢star_{K;α} w : M
Ω ⊢uni_Γ θ : (K, Θ)             Φ ⊢ ω : Ω
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
J1:  Φ ⊢ uni_{Γ,x}[ω] : X[θ∘ω]         if x = α
J2:  Ω ⊨ uni_{Γ,x}[id] : X[θ]          if x ∈ FV(Δ)
```

**Invertibility judgment.** `Γ ⊨ u : s → t` implies `Γ ⊢ u`, and comes with `inv(u)`, `eta(u) : 1_s → u·inv(u)` and `eps(u) : inv(u)·u → 1_t`, all stable under substitution.

**What the universal property says.** The functor `ucone_* : Hom(c, lim_Γ) → Cones(Γ; c)` is essentially surjective at every dimension (§4.4 of the paper). J1 produces the "witness" cell (the mediating map or higher cell), and J2 produces the invertible cells that say it factors correctly.

**Status [paper].**
- Finite computads only.
- Cones for arbitrary Γ depend on Conjecture 3.6; only the globular case is fully proved (Thm. 3.3).
- Gray shapes are proved to exist only for n = 1 (Thm. 4.4).
- There is no semantics.
- Colimits are only mentioned ("reversing the orientation of all 1-dimensional cells").
- Weighted limits, Yoneda and exponentials are not mentioned.

**Aesthetic verdict.** This is in-style. The cost is the size of the auxiliary machinery (`cone`, `ctrf`, `pctrf`, `star`, `pstar`, `⊢uni`, `⊢_{K,Θ}`), each with its own rules. A user never writes these judgments, but a kernel would have to implement all of them.

### 2.2 Loubaton, categorical theory of (∞,ω)-categories [paper, abstract/ToC level]

Source: [arXiv 2406.05425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05425).

- Gray cylinders and Gray cones are defined as pushouts, via Steiner theory (§1.2).
- Lax transformations are maps `A ⊗ [1] → B`.
- **Lax Grothendieck construction** (Thm 4.1.4.2): lax functors to ω correspond to left cartesian fibrations.
- **Yoneda** (Thm 4.2.1.20): `C → Ĉ := Hom(Cᵗ, ω)` is fully faithful.

Relevance: this is the semantic target, and also a warning. Loubaton's Yoneda lemma is stated with **ω, the (∞,ω)-category of (∞,ω)-categories, as a universe**. Universes are excluded from this design, so we can only have *representability* statements (maps out of a shape correspond to data), not "presheaves are a category." §5 is designed around that restriction.

### 2.3 Abellán–Haugseng–Martini [paper, abstract level]

Source: *Free fibrations, lax colimits and Kan extensions for (∞,2)-categories*, [arXiv 2602.07604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07604).

- **Thm F:** the I-lax colimit of `F : A → Cat` is the total space of the classifying fibration with the cartesian 2-cells and the cocartesian 1-cells over I *inverted*. Dually, the I-lax limit is a category of sections.
- **Weighted (co)limits are partially lax (co)limits** over the fibration classified by the weight, and conversely.
- Kan extensions are I_b-lax limits over comma categories.

Relevance: this justifies **"marking" as the only extra ingredient for weighted limits** in dimension 2. For ω, see §10.4.

### 2.4 Gepner–Heine, oriented colimits [paper, abstract/intro level]

Source: *Colimits in oriented category theory*, [arXiv 2608.02592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02592), Aug 2026.

- They argue that lax colimits are "incompatible with … the Gray tensor product", and propose *oriented* (co)limits, which agree with lax ones up to dimension 1.
- The oriented colimit of a constant functor at A is `A ⊗ I` (Gray), while the lax colimit is `A × I`.
- The higher Grothendieck construction computes oplax colimits, and E-lax colimits are localised Grothendieck constructions.

Relevance: **CaTT_lim's transfors are Gray-shaped, so its limits are oriented ones in their sense.** §10.1 shows its rules as written are sound only for Gray-enriched (L) ambients. This matters for cotensors (§6.3): oriented limits of a constant diagram give Gray-hom functor categories, i.e. *lax* transformations.

### 2.5 Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari [paper, abstract level]

Source: *Fibrations and lax limits of (∞,2)-categories*, [arXiv 2012.04537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04537).

- They build a uniform theory of 2-(co)limits covering lax, weighted and pseudo limits through four variances of fibration.
- They give a cofinality notion, and prove 2-limits are unique when they exist.

Relevance: cofinality is the tool for proving that our derived constructions (§6) agree with the intended ones.

### 2.6 Coherators and representables [recall]

Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis (as made precise for CaTT by BFM, [2106.04475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04475)):
- A weak ω-category is a presheaf on the coherator Θ_CaTT that sends globular sums to limits.
- Read in the other direction: **disks are co-ω-category objects**, and `Hom(D^•, C)` is an ω-category for any C.

This is the semantic content of §5. The CaTT judgment `Γ ⊢ t : A` *is* a co-operation on disks, `⟦Dⁿ⟧ → ⟦Γ⟧`.

### 2.7 Directed Yoneda in simplicial HoTT [recall]

Riehl–Shulman and GWB ([2407.09146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09146), [2602.02218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02218)):
- *Dependent* Yoneda is directed path induction for covariant families.
- It needs type families, so it belongs to the fibrations story [→ fibrations].
- Its non-dependent shadow, "a natural transformation out of a representable is determined by the image of the identity", is what §5 targets.

## 3. Pseudo or lax ambient

### 3.1 The fact that forces a choice

In CaTT every coherence is invertible. If `coh_{Γ, t→u}` is a valid coherence, then so is `coh_{Γ, u→t}`, because the side condition is symmetric in t and u. Benjamin–Markakis ([2406.12127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12127)) prove the stronger statement that coherences are coinductively invertible.

This has a consequence for what the ambient's 2-cells can mean. Take this ambient context:

```
C D : ⋆,   F G : C → D,   α : F → G,          (α is a 2-cell between functors)
x y : ⊤ → C,   f : x → y                       (f is an arrow of C, see §5.1)
```

- The *component* of α at x is the whiskering `α ∘ x : F∘x → G∘x`, which is an arrow of D.
- The two ways of composing α with f, namely `(α∘y)·(F∘f)` and `(G∘f)·(α∘x)`, are both arrows `F∘x → G∘y` of D.
- They have the same free variables, so CaTT gives a coherence (the interchange law) between them, and its inverse.

That coherence is α's naturality square at f. Since it is invertible, **every 2-cell of a CaTT ambient is a pseudo transformation**: its naturality squares commute up to invertible cells. A lax transformation, whose squares are filled by a cell in one direction only, cannot be a 2-cell of the ambient unless the core rule changes.

The semantics agrees **[recall]**:
- 2-categories with lax transformations do not form a tricategory. They form a "lax Gray-category", where interchange across a functor composition is a one-way cell (Gray 1974).
- Loubaton's ω-category of ω-categories has cartesian hom-objects. Lax transformations there are maps out of the Gray cylinder `A ⊗ [1]`, not 2-cells.

So there are two coherent designs: a pseudo ambient (P) and a lax ambient (L).

### 3.2 The comparison lives in `pseudo-vs-gray.md`

The full description of the two designs and their practical comparison is now in `notes/pseudo-vs-gray.md` (PR #5), which is the project's single pseudo-vs-lax note:
- **P:** a pseudo (cartesian) ambient, with lax structure obtained by construction;
- **L:** a lax (Gray-enriched) ambient with a Gray-style coherence rule.

An earlier version of this section made four claims that that note's "Reconciliation" section settles differently:
- **R1.** L's side condition is still equality of supports, computed on the Gray unfolding of the pasting scheme, and it is decidable. Its correctness (lemma S1) is open.
- **R2.** L's contexts stay pasting schemes. The open Gray-shape problem for n > 1 belongs to P's lax function objects, not to L's kernel.
- **R4.** The single-hom fragment of L is CaTT, so part of the existing metatheory carries over.
- **R7.** The cheap direction of embedding is P into L, since P is L plus a marking that makes interchangers invertible.

What stands from this note:
- §3.1's obstruction.
- P and L coincide semantically for functors into 1-categories. Under L this costs a syntactic annotation (R6).

**How §4–§6 read under each option.** §4–§6 hold under either option.
- Under P, lax cells (cone 2-cells, lax naturality) come from the universal properties of lax limits and lax function objects, as written.
- Under L, cone 2-cells and the 2-dimensional universal property use native lax cells (`pseudo-vs-gray.md` §4.4). Lax function objects (§6.3) are then the native homs made into objects.

## 4. Limits and colimits

### 4.1 Base: CaTT_lim as is [proposal]

Take Definition 4.15 of Mikhail wholesale for finite diagrams.

Kernel-level simplifications worth trying, since they are aesthetic, not semantic:
- **Merge the star/pstar/uni bookkeeping into a single "lifting" judgment.** The rule's content is: for every ctrf shape M and every partial filler θ of the "M-shaped cell of cones," produce the next variable. This is a generic *horn-filling* statement: the filler schema is "each conical-transfor horn has a filler, which is invertible except at its top cell." Stating it as a horn judgment `M horn(Γ; c) ⊢ x` would bring it close to how `coh` reads.
- **Linearity (`x ∝ t`)** is the sole new kind of side condition. It is the same concern as the post's "no cancellation," so it fits. Keep it.

### 4.2 Marked diagrams: pseudo, strict and partial laxness [proposal]

**Idea.** A diagram context may *mark* some of its positive-dimensional variables. The cone rule makes the projection cell of a marked variable invertible, by placing it in Mikhail's `⊨` judgment, or in an ICaTT invertibility type ([2602.16602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16602)).

```
Γ⁺ ::= Γ with a subset μ ⊆ Var_{≥1}(Γ) of marked variables

(KE-marked)   as (KE), with conclusion
              Γ, x:X, c:Ob, Π, p_x : s →_A t   cone((Γ,x:X)^{μ}; c)
              and, if x ∈ μ, the extra judgment
              Γ, x:X, c:Ob, Π, p_x ⊨ p_x : s →_A t
```

Side conditions:
- **(M1) No closure condition is needed** (§10.2). Cone cells over composites, identities and coherences of marked generators are invertible automatically.
- **(M2) The universal property quantifies only over cones with the same marking.** That is, `ctrf` shapes inherit the marking, and J2 already produces `⊨` cells.

What it gives:

| Marking | Limit | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| none | lax (oriented) limit | lax limits, comma-like constructions |
| all | pseudo = homotopy limit of the diagram | products (no difference for discrete Γ), pullbacks, equalizers, iso-inserters |
| some | partially lax | comma objects `f ↓ g` (mark one leg), hom objects (§6.2), inserters |

**Weighted limits.**
- In CaTT style a weight is a *display substitution* `π : Ψ → Γ` together with a marking of Ψ: the "Grothendieck construction of the weight."
- `lim^{(Ψ,π)} γ := lim_{Ψ^μ}[γ ∘ π]`.
- AHM show this recovers all weighted limits in dimension 2. For ω, see §10.4.
- The side condition on π ("π is a (co)cartesian display") is the open part. A first version can **omit it**: any π gives *some* partially lax limit, and only the comparison theorem with "weighted limits" needs π to be a fibration.

### 4.3 Colimits [proposal]

Mikhail says colimits are obtained by reversing all 1-cells. Concretely, there is a second rule family `cocone`, `uccone`/`colim_Γ`, `couni`:
- It is identical except that **`p_x` and the apex swap roles in dimension 1**.
- Example: for `f : x → y`, the cocone cell is `q_f : q_x → f ∗ q_y`, with legs `q_x : x → c`.

Opposites are a meta-operation on *terms in one ambient*. They are not a symmetry of the ambient ωCat, since `ωCat^op` is not ωCat. So colimits really are **new rules**, though generated by the same schema. Recommendation: present a single schema parameterised by an orientation `ε ∈ {lim, colim}` × a laxness marking, so the kernel has one implementation.

### 4.4 Orientation variants

- Mikhail notes the other lax/oplax variants come from opposites. With marking, the rule schema needs only {lax, oplax} × {lim, colim}.
- The other orientations come from cotensors by opposite shapes, `[⟦Γ^{op_S}⟧, C]` (§10.6).

## 5. Representables and Yoneda

### 5.1 Elements are maps from the terminal object [proposal; no new rule]

Let `⊤ := lim_∅` (Mikhail Ex. 4.10). Define, purely as notation:

```
x : C          means   x : ⊤ →_⋆ C
f : x →_C y    means   f : x →_{⊤→C} y          (an ambient 2-cell)
...and so on in all dimensions.
```

- **Composition of elements.** The hom `Hom(⊤, C)` is a hom-ω-category of the ambient. Its pasting schemes are *suspensions* `ΣΘ[⊤, C]` of pasting schemes Θ, and its coherences are suspended coherences.
  - So "CaTT inside C" is CaTT in the ambient, restricted to suspended shapes.
  - Benjamin–Markakis's `Σ ⊣ Hom` and "homs of free are free" ([2402.01611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01611)) say this restriction loses nothing.
- **Functor action** is whiskering: `F x := F ∘ x`, `F f := F ∘ f`. Then `F(g·f) ≅ F g · F f`, `F(id) ≅ id`, and all higher functoriality are ambient coherences. This is exactly the post's §"Functors" observation, now with C, D as *variables*.
- **Components** of `α : F ⇒ G` are `α_x := α ∘ x`. Naturality cells are interchange coherences, hence pseudo (§3).

**Semantic check [recall/conj].** In ωCat with cartesian homs, `Hom(1, C) ≃ C`, since `1 × Dⁿ = Dⁿ`. So "elements of C" are elements of C.

**What does not follow without more rules.**
- **Extensionality / density:** "if `F x ≅ G x` naturally in elements x, then `F ≅ G`." That needs `⊤` and the disks to be jointly dense, which is the internal-hom/Gray thread's problem [→ Gray].
- I suggest an explicit rule only if needed. It would read: *a family of components indexed by the generic element, plus naturality, is a transfor.* That is the cylinder rule `Hom(⟦Γ⟧,D)(u,v) ≃ Hom(⟦(Γ ⊗ D¹)^μ⟧, D)` restricted to representable domains. Here `(Γ ⊗ D¹)^μ` is Mikhail's `gray` context with its cylinder cells *marked*: under Option P the ambient's 2-cells are pseudo, so the marked Gray cylinder (≈ Γ × D¹) is the right shape, and the unmarked one classifies lax transformations instead. It is well-formed because the `gray` judgment generates `Γ ⊗ D¹` as a finite context.

### 5.2 Shapes as objects: two kernel options and one surface layer

The goal is an object `⟦Γ⟧` for every finite context Γ that behaves like the free ω-category on Γ. In particular, maps `⟦Γ⟧ → C` should be the same as Γ-shaped families of elements of C. That statement is the Yoneda (corepresentability) principle of this design.

Neither option below uses definitional β or η rules. "Maps out of `⟦Γ⟧` are Γ-families" is witnessed by generated cells (a mediating map plus invertible cells), in the same way as Mikhail's `uni`.

**Option R-A: shape objects as a primitive.**

The kernel gets a new object former and a new universal property:

```
Γ ⊢  (any finite context)
─────────────
⊢ ⟦Γ⟧ : ⋆

C : ⋆,   a Γ-family of elements of C
────────────────────────────────────────
a map ⟦Γ⟧ → C, plus invertible cells saying it restricts to the family,
plus the higher cells making "restriction" essentially surjective at every level
```

- A "Γ-family of elements of C" is a substitution of Γ in which each variable becomes an element of C in the sense of §5.1.
- The second rule has to be stated with the same kind of machinery as Mikhail's `uni`: a shape judgment for families and their higher maps, and a filler schema with invertible witnesses.
- Substitutions act on shapes: `Δ ⊢ σ : Γ` gives `⟦σ⟧ : ⟦Γ⟧ → ⟦Δ⟧` by applying the universal property to "σ, read as a Γ-family in ⟦Δ⟧". Composites `⟦σ∘τ⟧` and `⟦τ⟧∘⟦σ⟧` are related by an invertible generated cell, not identified.

**Option R-B: shape objects as lax colimits of the point.**

The kernel gets nothing new beyond the colimit rules of §4.3:

```
⟦Γ⟧ := colim^{lax}_Γ [ !_Γ ∘ ⟨⊤⟩ ]
```

- `(c:⋆) ⊢ !_Γ : Γ` is the **constant substitution**. It sends each variable `x : A` to an inhabitant of `A[!]` over the one-point context.
  - Such an inhabitant exists because every type over `(c:⋆)` is inhabited by a coherence: `Var = {c}` on both sides, so the `coh` side condition holds.
  - So `!_Γ` sends objects to c, 1-cells to `id_c`, and 2-cells to unitors or identities, as needed. Γ need not be a pasting scheme.
- **What a cocone is.** A lax cocone over const ⊤ with apex C has:
  - legs `q_x : ⊤ → C`, i.e. elements of C;
  - for `f : x → y`, a cell `q_f : q_x → id_⊤ ∗ q_y`, which is an element-level arrow `q_x → q_y` after a unit coherence;
  - and so on in higher dimensions.

  So a cocone is a Γ-family of elements of C, with unit coherences inserted. The colimit's universal property is then the Yoneda principle above.
- **Semantics [conj, §10.3].** The lax colimit of the constant point diagram over I is I itself: either the Grothendieck construction of the terminal functor, or Gepner–Heine's `1 × I = 1 ⊗ I = I`. So `⟦Γ⟧ ≃ Free(Γ)`, possibly up to an opposite in some dimensions depending on orientation. The convention in §1 should be chosen so that no opposite appears. §10.3 gives the proof attempt and where it stalls.

**Structure available under either option:**
- `⟦∅⟧` is initial, and `⟦x:⋆⟧ ≃ ⊤`.
- `⟦Dⁿ⟧` is the walking n-cell.
- A term `Γ ⊢ t : A` of dimension n gives a 1-cell `⟦t⟧ : ⟦Dⁿ⟧ → ⟦Γ⟧`, by reading t as an element of `⟦Γ⟧` through §5.1.
- So **CaTT itself becomes the language of the sub-ω-category of shapes `⟦Γ⟧`**. This is the Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis picture (§2.6) made internal.

**Practical comparison.**

| Practical question | R-A (primitive shapes) | R-B (shapes are colimits) |
|---|---|---|
| New kernel rules | an object former and its universal property (a second `uni`-like schema) | none beyond colimits |
| Available before general colimits are implemented | yes | no |
| Semantic justification | direct: free ω-categories on finite computads exist (Dean et al.) | needs the conjecture `laxcolim(const 1) ≃ Free(Γ)` |
| Size of a typical proof term | small: one shape-specific filler | larger: goes through the general `couni` machinery, with unit coherences in every cocone cell |
| Duplication | the shape universal property overlaps with colimits once those exist | none |
| Uniformity | shapes are a special case | "free" is a colimit like any other, so facts about colimits apply to shapes |

**Surface layer R-C (used with either).** Users write `C : ⋆ | x y : C, f : x →_C y ⊢ …`, a CaTT context fibred over an ambient object. The elaborator translates this to §5.1 notation and to the universal maps of whichever kernel option is used. It adds no kernel rules.

**Staging.** R-A can come first, since it needs no colimits. Once colimits exist, R-B's conjecture says R-A's rules are admissible. They would then become derived rules or elaborator shortcuts, and the kernel would shrink back to R-B. If the conjecture fails, R-A stays as a primitive and the failure locates the mismatch between lax colimits and free categories.

## 6. Derived constructions and test cases

All of these are finite (marked) limits and colimits of finite diagrams.

### 6.1 Basic (co)limits
- Terminal `⊤ = lim_∅`; initial `⊥ = colim_∅`.
- Products: lim over a discrete Γ; coproducts dually.
- Pullbacks and pushouts: the cospan or span with all cells marked.
- Equalizers and coequalizers, and walking-iso quotients (needs ICaTT's walking equivalence as a context).

### 6.2 Hom objects
For `a, b : C` (elements), define `Hom_C(a, b)` as the partially lax limit of the diagram

```
x:⋆, y:⋆, z:⋆, f : x → z, g : y → z     with g marked,
substituted by  x ↦ ⊤, y ↦ ⊤, z ↦ C, f ↦ a, g ↦ b.
```

A cone with apex c is `p_x, p_y : c → ⊤` (trivial), `p_z : c → C`, an invertible `p_g : p_z ≅ b∘!`, and `p_f : p_z → a∘!`. That is a "c-parameterised arrow between b and a" (or a to b, depending on orientation), so this is the comma `a ↓ b` = `Hom_C(a, b)`.

- **Under L:** elements of this object are the arrows between a and b, and its arrows are C's 2-cells. This is the internal counterpart of Benjamin–Markakis's Hom.
- **Under P:** its arrows are only the *invertible* 2-cells (§10.1). The genuine hom object is then a fibre of the lax arrow object `[⟦D¹⟧, C]_lax`, which the Gray thread supplies.

### 6.3 Cotensors by shapes, and lax functor categories
- `[⟦Γ⟧, C] := lim_Γ [ !_Γ ∘ ⟨C⟩ ]`, the lax limit of the constant diagram at C.
- **Under L,** with CaTT_lim's rules as written, this is the **Gray-hom functor category: functors `⟦Γ⟧ → C` and lax transformations**. With all cells marked it is the pseudo functor category.
- **Under P,** cone-style limits give only the pseudo functor category. The lax one needs the Gray tensor (§10.1).
- **Lax transformations under Option P, given lax cotensors.** A lax transformation `F ⇒ G : C → D` is a 1-cell `C → [⟦D¹⟧, D]_lax` whose composites with the two evaluations are F and G.
  - "Whose composites are F and G" is an element of a pullback, so a lax transformation is an element of a finite marked limit.
  - Consequence: **lax transformations between functors with an arbitrary domain need only cotensors by `⟦D¹⟧`, not a general internal hom.** The general internal hom `[C, D]` for an *arbitrary* object C is not a limit [→ Gray].
  - When C is itself built as a colimit of shapes, `[colim_i ⟦Γ_i⟧, D] ≃ lim_i [⟦Γ_i⟧, D]` gives it anyway, once an internal hom exists (§10.6).

### 6.4 An ℕ-like object (test case for "ordinary math")
- Let `L := (x:⋆, s : x → x)`. This is a finite, non-pasting-scheme context.
  - `⟦L⟧` is the free ω-category on a loop, i.e. "Bℕ".
  - Its universal property: maps `⟦L⟧ → C` are pairs (element c, endo-arrow e on c). **This is iteration.**
- `N := Hom_{⟦L⟧}(x, x)` (§6.2). Its elements are the arrows `sⁿ`, so N is the "object of natural numbers."
  - It is **equivalent to the discrete ω-category on ℕ** (§10.5, solved). Each hom of the free weak ω-category on a graph is equivalent to the discrete set of paths.
  - N needs the hom-object construction, which comes from the limit rules under L and from the Gray thread under P (§6.2).
- **Operations:**
  - zero = `id_x`;
  - successor = postcomposition with s, i.e. the functoriality of Hom in its second argument;
  - for (C, c, e), the map `N → Hom_C(c, c)` sending n to eⁿ is the action on homs of the map `⟦L⟧ → C`.
- **What is missing:**
  - Primitive recursion and induction need a *dependent* eliminator, which needs families [→ fibrations].
  - "N is discrete" is a property we can state (all 2-cells invertible, and all parallel arrows equal up to a higher cell) but cannot *prove* inside the theory without an h-level discipline.
- This fits the limited-normalisation constraint: nothing computes, and recursion is a universal property only.
- **Also:** the poset `(ℕ, ≤)` is the slice `x ↓ ⟦L⟧`, a finite comma object (§6.2). So "sequence-shaped" indexing objects exist; what is missing is colimits indexed by them (§7).

### 6.5 Other "acts like" structures
Monoid-like, category-like and module-like objects can be presented by *finite contexts with marked cells* (e.g. a free monoid object `⟦x, m: x→x, …⟧`).

But a structure with *equations*, such as a vector space, needs either:
- (a) colimits that impose equations as invertible cells (coequaliser- or localisation-style), which fits here; or
- (b) a notion of "algebra for a presented theory." The latter skirts Anthony's "no algebraic structure in the meta-theory" rule unless the theory is itself just a context.

Option (a) is the in-style route: a presentation is a context plus a marking, and the presented object is its marked colimit. **This should be explored next, starting with the integers, where cancellation shows up.**

## 7. Limits of this design (what needs other threads)

| Missing | Why it is not a finite (co)limit | Candidate |
|---|---|---|
| Colimits indexed by an *object* I (sequential colimits, Kan extensions along non-shapes) | The diagram is a map `I → C`, not a context. ## 10. Proof attempts

Each unproved claim in this note was attempted. Where an attempt stalls, the remaining gap is a self-contained problem in `notes/conjectures/`.

### 10.1 CaTT_lim's universal property forces pseudo cone morphisms in a CaTT ambient (argument complete modulo one check)

**Claim.** In CaTT_lim, the connecting cells of cone morphisms (n = 1 `ctrf` shapes) over positive-dimensional cone variables are derivably invertible.

**Argument.**
1. Take `u, u' : c → L` with `L = lim_Γ`, and a 2-cell `β : u → u'`.
2. The cone morphism `ucone_*(β)` has, at a 1-cell f of Γ, a connecting cell relating the two composites of `ucone_f` (a 2-cell along `L → ·`) and β (a 2-cell along `c → L`). That cell is an interchange coherence, hence invertible.
3. By the 2-dimensional instance of `uni`, every cone morphism m is related to some `ucone_*(β)` by an invertible 2-transfor.
4. So `m_f` is a composite of invertible cells, hence invertible, using closure of coinductive equivalences under composition (Benjamin–Markakis).

**Consequences.**
- **Under P.** ωCat with pseudo transformations has non-invertible modifications, so it does not satisfy CaTT_lim's rules as written. The P-version of CaTT_lim must put the `ctrf` connecting cells in `⊨`, i.e. use *pseudo morphisms of lax cones*. With that change, Hom(c, lim) is lax cones with pseudo morphisms. This is the correct notion of lax limit in a cartesian-enriched setting.
- **Under L.** Interchange across a 0-cell is one-way, so step 2 fails and the rules as written are the intended ones. CaTT_lim's Gray-shaped `ctrf` is native to L. This agrees with `pseudo-vs-gray.md` §4.4.
- **Hom objects and lax cotensors (§6.2, §6.3) under P.** The comma of `⊤ →a C ←b ⊤` (b-leg marked) has arrows b → a as points, but only *invertible* 2-cells of C as its arrows, since those arrows are cone morphisms. The genuine hom-object is the fibre of the oplax arrow object. Its universal property goes through the Gray tensor (maps `X ⊗ D¹ → C`), not through cones. This matches the (∞,2) fact that mapping categories are fibres of `Ar^oplax(C)`, not of the cartesian `Fun([1], C)` (GHL) **[recall]**.
  - So under P, hom objects and lax cotensors come from the Gray thread's constructions, not from §4.
  - Under L, §6.2 and §6.3 hold as written.

**Remaining check.** Steps 2 and 3 depend on the exact orientation of Mikhail's `ctrf` connecting cells (his σCond/τCond). Problem: `conjectures/catt-lim-pseudo-vs-gray.md`.

### 10.2 Marking closure (§4.2, M1): resolved, no closure condition needed

**Claim.** Marking any subset μ of positive-dimensional *generators* of Γ is enough. No closure condition is needed.

**Argument.**
- A cone over a marked diagram needs invertible cone cells over every cell of Free(Γ) in the generated marking: generators in μ, identities, coherences, and composites of these.
- Over a generator in μ, the cone cell is invertible by the marked KE rule.
- Over a composite, the cone cell is the derived term `p_t` of Mikhail's Conj 3.6, built by composing the cone cells of the pieces with coherences. That is a composite of invertible cells, hence invertible.
- Over an identity or a coherence, `p_t` is built from coherences alone, hence invertible.

So (M1) is dropped. One residual dependency: the existence of `p_t` for arbitrary Γ is Mikhail's Conj 3.6, which is covered by the Gray-context problem below for the cases needed here.

### 10.3 Free objects as lax colimits of the point (§5.2, R-B): partly proved

The claim splits into three steps.

**Step A (syntactic). The cocone context over const ⊤ is equivalent to `(c:⋆, Σ_{⊤,c}Γ)`.** Sketch:
- Each cocone variable `q_x` differs from the suspended variable only by `id_⊤`-whiskerings in its boundary.
- Unitors are coherences, hence invertible. Define substitutions both ways by transporting along them.
- The composites are unitor-after-inverse, which a coherence connects to the identity.
- **Stall:** confirming that the dual of Mikhail's cone rules produces exactly these whiskered types.

**Step B (all dimensions). Higher cocone morphisms match transfors between Γ-families.**
- Under L, the `ctrf` shapes over const ⊤ are Gray shapes `Σ_{⊤,c}(Γ ⊗ Dⁿ)`, by Step A applied layer-wise.
- Under P, they are the same shapes with connecting cells invertible (§10.1).
- **Stall:** the Gray contexts `Γ ⊗ Dⁿ` exist only for n = 1 (Mikhail Thm 4.4).

**Step C (semantic). `⟦Γ⟧ ≃ Free(Γ)`.**
- Both corepresent Γ-families. For Free(Γ) this is the universal property of free ω-categories on computads, with higher cells via `Free(Γ) ⊗ Dⁿ ≃ Free(Γ ⊗ Dⁿ)`. That equivalence follows from Loubaton's colimit-preserving Gray tensor if `Dᵏ ⊗ Dⁿ` is free on its Steiner basis. That freeness is known: Campion, arXiv 2311.00205, Example 3.2 and Cor. 3.5 (see `conjectures/free-infinity-gray-of-pasting-diagrams.md`, Literature status).

Problems: `conjectures/lax-colimit-of-point.md` (Steps A and C) and `conjectures/gray-contexts-in-catt.md` §6 (Step B).

**What does not depend on any of this.** R-B's universal property is "maps out of ⟦Γ⟧ are cocones", and Step A turns cocones into Γ-families of elements. So *as rules*, R-A's universal property is derivable from R-B once Steps A and B hold, independently of Step C's semantics.

### 10.4 Weighted limits as marked limits (§4.2): reduced to one semantic theorem

- **Syntactic part: done.** With §10.2, marked limits over `Ψ^μ` along `π : Ψ → Γ` are well-defined.
- **Semantic part: open.** Showing they are the weighted limits for the weight π presents is AHM in dimension 2. For ω, it should follow from Loubaton's lax Grothendieck construction with adjustable laxness.
- The condition on π that makes it present a fibration is part of the same problem.

Problem: `conjectures/weighted-as-partially-lax-omega.md`.

### 10.5 The ℕ-like object is discrete (§6.4): solved

**Result** (external researcher, checked here; full proof in `conjectures/free-on-graph.md`). For a graph G:
- The comparison `F_ω(G) → F_1(G)` from the free weak ω-category to the free category is a trivial fibration, hence an ω-weak equivalence.
- The proof combines the cartesian map `L ⇒ T` and its contraction (Leinster), DFMRV's identification of computad-free with Leinster-free, and Benjamin–Markakis homs.
- Each `Hom(a, b)` is equivalent to the discrete ω-category on paths from a to b. For the loop, `Hom_{⟦L⟧}(x, x) ≃ ℕ_disc`, and composition induces addition.

**What this settles:**
- The intended *semantics* of N: in the free weak ω-category on the loop, N is the natural numbers, with all higher structure contractible.
- More generally, any finite **1-dimensional** presentation (a graph) has homs that are the discrete sets of paths. So free categories on graphs behave exactly like their 1-categorical counterparts. This covers objects that act like ℕ, lists, words over an alphabet, and free categories on finite quivers.

**What remains open downstream:**
- **That `⟦L⟧` denotes `F_ω(L)`.** Under R-A this holds by definition. Under R-B it is the lax-colimit-of-the-point problem (§10.3).
- **That the hom-object construction gives the semantic hom.** Under L this follows from the limit rules (§6.2). Under P it needs the Gray thread's lax cotensor (§10.1).
- **Discreteness inside the theory.** The theory can state "every parallel pair of cells in N has a filler", but proving it internally needs a rule or meta-theorem that transfers the semantic fact. The natural candidate is an *admissible* meta-operation: for a 1-dimensional context, fill any parallel pair of cells of dimension ≥ 2 by a coherence over the chain pasting scheme, using the canonical lift of §3 of the response. That is exactly the researcher's syntactic argument (DFMRV Prop. 7.19 and 7.32), so it should be implementable as a tactic with no new rule.
- **Induction on N** still needs a dependent eliminator [→ fibrations].

### 10.6 Smaller claims

- **"CaTT_lim's limits are oriented" (§2.4).** Settled by §10.1: the rules as written are sound for L-style (Gray-enriched) ambients, and under P they need the `⊨` modification.
- **"Cotensors turn colimits into limits" (§6.3).** This is formal once an internal hom exists: `Hom(c, [colim X_i, D]) ≃ Hom(c ⊗ colim X_i, D)`, and ⊗ preserves colimits in each variable (Loubaton). It is therefore a question for the internal-hom thread, not a conjecture here.
- **"Only {lax, oplax} × {lim, colim} are needed" (§4.4).** Under L, `[⟦Γ⟧^{op_S}, C]` gives the other orientations as cotensors, and `⟦Γ⟧^{op_S} = ⟦Γ^{op_S}⟧` because opposites act on contexts (Benjamin–Markakis). Under P, the same holds once the Gray thread supplies lax cotensors.
