# Targets that cannot tell the Gray tensor from the cartesian product

## Setting and notation

- **Cat_ω.** Cat_ω denotes the ∞-category of (∞,ω)-categories. Fix one of its standard models: the inductive one, the colimit of the Cat_(∞,n), or the coinductive one, the limit of the Cat_(∞,n) along the underlying-(∞,n−1)-category functors. If the answer depends on the model, say so.
- **Truncations.** For k ≥ 0, Cat_(∞,k) ⊂ Cat_ω is the full subcategory of (∞,k)-categories. These are the objects all of whose cells of dimension > k are invertible. Write τ_k for the left adjoint of this inclusion.
- **Gray tensor.** ⊗ is the Gray tensor product on Cat_ω (Campion, *The Gray tensor product of (∞,n)-categories*, arXiv:2311.00205). It is closed on both sides.
- **Cartesian product.** × is the cartesian product. Cat_ω is cartesian closed.
- **Comparison map.** κ_{A,W} : A ⊗ W → A × W is the canonical comparison. Its components are the maps A ⊗ W → A ⊗ 1 ≃ A and A ⊗ W → 1 ⊗ W ≃ W, using that the unit of ⊗ is the terminal object.
- **κ-local objects.** Call B **κ-local** if, for all A and W, precomposition
  ```
  κ* : Fun(A × W, B) → Fun(A ⊗ W, B)
  ```
  is an equivalence of mapping spaces. Here Fun is the mapping space of Cat_ω.

## What is known (with proofs, so the question is self-contained)

1. **Discrete targets are κ-local.** A map from X into a set factors through π₀X, the set of objects modulo the equivalence relation generated by 1-cells. Also π₀(A ⊗ W) = π₀A × π₀W = π₀(A × W). The reason is that the objects of A ⊗ W are pairs, and its 1-cells are generated by the cells (f, w) and (a, g).
2. **κ-local implies (∞,1).** Suppose B is κ-local. Then every cell of B of dimension ≥ 2 is invertible.
   - Take k ≥ 1. On Steiner complexes there is a chain map λ(D¹) ⊗ λ(Dᵏ) → λ(Dᵏ⁺¹). It sends:
     - e₁ ⊗ e_k ↦ e_{k+1};
     - 0 ⊗ e_k ↦ e_k⁻ and 1 ⊗ e_k ↦ e_k⁺;
     - e₁ ⊗ e_j^± ↦ 0 for j < k;
     - 0 ⊗ x and 1 ⊗ x ↦ x for the lower generators x.
   - This map sends basis elements to basis elements or to 0, so it defines an ω-functor q : D¹ ⊗ Dᵏ → Dᵏ⁺¹ that hits the top cell.
   - For a (k+1)-cell β : Dᵏ⁺¹ → B, the composite β ∘ q factors through κ. But κ sends the (k+1)-cell e₁ ⊗ e_k to an identity, because D¹ × Dᵏ has dimension k.
   - So β is equivalent to the image of an identity, and is therefore invertible.
3. **A reduction for the converse.** Suppose both of the following hold:
   - (a) the canonical comparison τ₁(A ⊗ W) → τ₁A ⊗₁ τ₁W is an equivalence, and the Gray tensor of (∞,1)-categories is their cartesian product;
   - (b) τ₁ preserves finite products.

   Then τ₁(κ) is an equivalence, so every (∞,1)-category is κ-local.

   Statement (b) is equivalent to Cat_(∞,1) being an exponential ideal: for B an (∞,1)-category, the internal hom [X, B] is again an (∞,1)-category for every X. This holds by Day's reflection theorem.

   For finite n, a plausible route to (b) runs as follows. Cat_(∞,n) is Cat(Cat_(∞,n−1)). τ₁ is the change of enrichment along realization |–| : Cat_(∞,n−1) → S, which preserves finite products. Change of enrichment along a product-preserving functor preserves products (Gepner–Haugseng, arXiv:1312.3178). This argument still needs completion to commute with products, and it needs an extension to ω.

## Questions

1. **(Main.) Is B κ-local exactly when B is an (∞,1)-category?** Give a proof, or a precise citation, of the converse to Fact 2. Do this in the generality of Cat_ω, for whichever model it holds in.
2. **Higher analogue.** For each k ≥ 1, consider the comparison maps between the Gray tensor and the k-truncated tensors, for example τ_k(A ⊗ W) versus τ_kA ⊗_k τ_kW. Is the canonical comparison an equivalence? Does τ_k preserve finite products for every k ≤ n ≤ ω? Equivalently, is Cat_(∞,k) an exponential ideal of Cat_ω?
3. **If Question 1 fails:** describe the class of κ-local objects exactly.
