# Is the lax colimit of the constant point diagram the indexing category?

> **Status (2026-11-18): semantic claim SOLVED (Loubaton, arXiv 2406.05425, Example 4.2.3.12). The syntactic form is FALSE for arbitrary contexts admitted by Mikhail's cone judgment (researcher counterexample, verified below). It is still open for a specified canonical cocone construction.** See "Researcher Response" and "Verification" at the end.

## Background

Let 𝒞 be the (∞,ω)-category of (∞,ω)-categories.
- Loubaton ([2406.05425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05425)) builds it with a lax Grothendieck construction (Thm 4.1.4.2).
- Gepner–Heine ([2608.02592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02592)) define oriented colimits and state that the oriented colimit of a constant functor at A over I is the Gray tensor `A ⊗ I`, while the lax colimit is `A × I`.

For a finite computad Γ, write `Free(Γ)` for the free (∞,ω)-category it generates, i.e. the iterated pushout of globes along boundary inclusions. Write `1` for the terminal (∞,ω)-category.

## Statement

**Main claim.** For every finite computad Γ, the (op)lax colimit of the constant functor `Free(Γ) → 𝒞` at `1` is equivalent to `Free(Γ)`, up to an opposite in some set of dimensions that depends only on the lax/oplax convention.

**Syntactic form.** In a type theory where "lax cocone over a context Γ" is generated by rules, e.g. the dual of Mikhail's cone judgment in [arXiv 2412.13310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13310):
- The cocone context over the constant diagram at a terminal object ⊤ with apex c has one variable `q_x` for each variable `x : A` of Γ.
- Its type is `A` relocated into `Hom(⊤, c)`, with identities `id_⊤` whiskered in.
- **Show that this context is equivalent to the suspension of Γ with its two new objects set to ⊤ and c.** Equivalent means: substitutions in both directions whose composites are connected to identities by invertible cells.

## What is known (partial proof)

- **Semantic side.** For I a 1-category, the lax colimit of the constant functor at 1 is the Grothendieck construction of the terminal functor, which is I. The Gepner–Heine statement gives `1 × I = I` and `1 ⊗ I = I` directly. What is missing is a citation or proof at (∞,ω) level and for the relevant variance.
- **Syntactic side, by induction on Γ.**
  - Each `q_x` differs from the corresponding suspended variable only by whiskerings with `id_⊤` in its source and target.
  - Unitors are coherences, hence invertible.
  - Define the two substitutions by transporting each `q_x` along the unitors on its boundary.
  - Their composites are, componentwise, a unitor composed with its inverse, which is connected to the identity by a coherence.
  - The induction needs a "transport along invertible boundary" operation that is compatible with substitution. Benjamin–Markakis ([2406.12127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12127)) develop the needed invertibility calculus.
  - **The stall** is checking that the cocone rules actually produce the whiskered types claimed. This needs the precise dual of Mikhail's σCond/τCond.

### Literature search (2026-11-18)
- **The (∞,1) case is known.** Gepner–Haugseng–Nikolaus, *Lax colimits and free fibrations in ∞-categories* (Documenta Math.), Cor. 7.5: "Any ∞-category C is the oplax colimit of the constant functor C → Cat∞ with value ∗."
  - Their Thm 1.1 identifies the lax colimit of a functor with its (co)cartesian unstraightening.
  - So with the oplax convention there is no opposite in dimension 1.
- **Open:** the (∞,ω) statement and the syntactic form. Loubaton's lax Grothendieck construction (Thm 4.1.4.2 of [2406.05425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05425)) is the natural tool for extending the GHN argument.

## Why it matters

If it holds, "the free category on a finite presentation" does not need its own rule in a type theory with lax colimits: it is the lax colimit of the point. Its defining universal property (maps out are presentation-shaped families of elements) is then a special case of the colimit's universal property.

## What would count as a solution

A proof of the syntactic form, given Mikhail's rules, together with a reference or proof for the semantic form, or a counterexample (for example, a Γ where an opposite cannot be avoided by convention).

# Researcher Response

**The semantic claim is true, and Loubaton states the required formula explicitly. The raw syntactic cone judgment is too permissive to imply your syntactic form: it admits a three-variable counterexample.**

The distinction is between a specified canonical cocone construction and an arbitrary context admitted by Mikhail’s judgment. The counterexample below concerns the latter, with equivalence by categorical CaTT substitutions and invertible comparison cells.

## 1. The semantic claim has a direct reference

In **Loubaton, Example 4.2.3.12, p. 221**, the constant-diagram calculation is
\[
\operatorname{laxcolim}_{A^\flat}\underline C
\simeq A\times C.
\]
Here \(A^\flat\) marks only identities, as specified in Construction 3.1.1.16. Thus this is the unrestricted lax case. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Taking \(A=\operatorname{Free}(\Gamma)\) and \(C=1\) gives
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{laxcolim}_{\operatorname{Free}(\Gamma)^\flat}
\underline 1
\simeq \operatorname{Free}(\Gamma).
}
\]

This uses Loubaton’s convention in Definition 4.2.3.3. **No opposite is required in that convention, and finiteness of \(\Gamma\) is unnecessary for this calculation.** [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Gepner–Heine’s Theorem 1.8.1 gives the corresponding oriented formula
\[
\operatorname{ocolim}_{I}\underline A\simeq A\boxtimes I,
\]
which also specializes to \(I\) when \(A=1\). [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02592v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

So the semantic part does not require extending the GHN argument yourself.

## 2. What the syntactic rules actually constrain

Suppressing the apex arguments in the notation, write
\[
U_\delta(v,\Pi)=
\bigcup_{\substack{
p\in\Pi,\;
y\in\mathrm{FV}(\delta(v):\partial X)\\
y\propto\tau(p)}}
\mathrm{FV}(p:T_p),
\qquad v:X.
\]

Mikhail’s extension rule appends \(p_v:s\to_A t\) when \(s,t\) are categorical terms satisfying
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{FV}(s:A)&=\{c\}\cup U_\tau(v,\Pi),\\
\mathrm{FV}(t:A)&=\mathrm{FV}(v:X)\cup U_\sigma(v,\Pi),\\
v&\propto t.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
These are the barred source condition and unbarred target condition in §3.1. The linearity condition concerns the newly added diagram variable \(v\); it imposes no corresponding restriction on the number of uses of earlier projections. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13310v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That omission permits the following construction. The counterexample and its verification are derived here from the rules.

## 3. An admitted cone with a repeated projection

Take the finite globular context
\[
\Gamma=
\bigl(
x:\mathrm{Ob},\;
f:x\to x,\;
\alpha:f\Rightarrow f
\bigr).
\]

Use left-to-right composition of 1-cells, \(\cdot\) for vertical composition of 2-cells, and \(\ast\) for horizontal composition.

Begin with the usual first two projections:
\[
q=p_x:c\to x,
\qquad
r=p_f:q\Rightarrow qf.
\]

Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
s&:=r\cdot(r\ast1_f):
q\Rightarrow(qf)f,\\
w&:=1_{qf}\ast\alpha:
(qf)f\Rightarrow(qf)f,\\
t&:=s\cdot w:
q\Rightarrow(qf)f.
\end{aligned}
\]
Now append the 3-cell variable
\[
b=p_\alpha:s\Rrightarrow t.
\tag{2}
\]

### Verification of the side conditions

Both boundaries of \(\alpha\) are \(f\), and
\[
\mathrm{FV}(f:x\to x)=\{x,f\}.
\]

The selected earlier projections are precisely \(q\) and \(r\):

- \(q\) is selected because \(x\propto\tau(q)=x\).
- \(r\) is selected because \(f\propto\tau(r)=qf\).

Consequently,
\[
U_\sigma(\alpha,\Pi)
=
U_\tau(\alpha,\Pi)
=
\{c,x,f,q,r\}
=:U.
\]

Direct calculation gives
\[
\mathrm{FV}\bigl(s:q\Rightarrow(qf)f\bigr)=U
=\{c\}\cup U
\]
and
\[
\mathrm{FV}\bigl(t:q\Rightarrow(qf)f\bigr)
=
U\cup\{\alpha\}
=
\mathrm{FV}(\alpha:f\Rightarrow f)\cup U.
\]

Finally, \(\alpha\propto t\): at each composition constructor, exactly one substitution argument is linear in \(\alpha\). All terms are constructed using CaTT compositions and identities.

Thus (2) satisfies the cone extension rule. **The two uses of \(r\) are permitted.**

## 4. Dualization and constant specialization

Choose the cocone convention obtained by reversing 1-cells only. More precisely, if \(O_1\) denotes that duality, define
\[
K\;\mathrm{cocone}_1\;(\Gamma;c)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
O_1K\;\mathrm{cone}\;(O_1\Gamma;c).
\]

For our loop-shaped \(\Gamma\), the dual of the preceding construction is
\[
\begin{aligned}
q&:x\to c,\\
r&:q\Rightarrow fq,\\
s&:=r\cdot(1_f\ast r):q\Rightarrow f(fq),\\
w&:=\alpha\ast1_{fq}:f(fq)\Rightarrow f(fq),\\
b&:s\Rrightarrow s\cdot w.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

Substitute the constant diagram:
\[
x\mapsto\top,\qquad
f\mapsto1_\top,\qquad
\alpha\mapsto1_{1_\top}.
\]

Under **strict interpretation**, (3) becomes
\[
q:\top\to c,\qquad
r:q\Rightarrow q,\qquad
b:r^2\Rrightarrow r^2,
\tag{4}
\]
where \(r^2=r\cdot r\) is vertical composition of the 2-cell \(r\) with itself.

By comparison, suspension of \(\Gamma\), with endpoints \(\top,c\), is
\[
q:\top\to c,\qquad
r:q\Rightarrow q,\qquad
a:r\Rrightarrow r.
\tag{5}
\]

This calculation assumes no weak normalization theorem: interpreting strictly makes every displayed identity whiskering an actual identity.

The difference between (4) and (5) is therefore an additional factor of the arbitrary projection \(r\), which a unitor cannot remove.

## 5. A finite strict model proves non-equivalence

Different written types alone would not establish non-equivalence. Here is a finite interpretation that does.

We interpret categorical CaTT syntax in strict higher categories, with coherence cells interpreted by identities. This is a special case of its weak-\(\omega\)-categorical semantics; Benjamin–Finster–Mimram establish the model correspondence in Theorem 88. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.04475?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### The test 2-category

Define a strict 2-category \(Q\) with one 0-cell and three 1-cells
\[
u_0,u_1,u_2.
\]

Their composition is
\[
u_nu_m=u_{\min(2,n+m)},
\]
with identity \(u_0\).

There are no 2-cells between distinct 1-cells. The 2-endomorphisms of \(u_n\) are
\[
e_{n,0},e_{n,1},\ldots,e_{n,n}.
\]

Define vertical and horizontal composition by
\[
e_{n,a}\cdot e_{n,b}=e_{n,\max(a,b)}
\]
and
\[
e_{n,a}\ast e_{m,b}
=
e_{\min(2,n+m),\,\max(a,b)}.
\]
The identity 2-cell on \(u_n\) is \(e_{n,0}\).

These operations are well typed because
\[
\max(a,b)\leq\max(n,m)\leq\min(2,n+m).
\]
Associativity follows from associativity of truncated addition and of \(\max\). Interchange reduces to taking the maximum of the same four labels on both sides.

Moreover:

- The only invertible 2-cells are identities: \(\max(a,b)=0\) forces \(a=b=0\).
- The only equivalence 1-cell is \(u_0\): \(\min(2,n+m)=0\) forces \(n=m=0\).

### Interpreting the two contexts

Work in the strict 3-category of strict 2-categories, strict 2-functors, strict 2-natural transformations, and modifications. Set
\[
\top=*,\qquad c=Q,
\]
where \(*\) is the terminal strict 2-category. Thus \(\top\) is genuinely terminal.

There is exactly one functor \(q:*\to Q\). A transformation
\[
r:q\Rightarrow q
\]
selects one of \(u_0,u_1,u_2\), and a modification selects a 2-cell of \(Q\).

The interpretations of (4) and (5) are therefore counted as follows:

| Value of \(r\) | Value of \(r^2\) | Choices for \(a:r\Rrightarrow r\) | Choices for \(b:r^2\Rrightarrow r^2\) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| \(u_0\) | \(u_0\) | 1 | 1 |
| \(u_1\) | \(u_2\) | 2 | 3 |
| \(u_2\) | \(u_2\) | 3 | 3 |
| **Total** | | **6** | **7** |

These counts remain different after passing to equivalence classes. Relative to the fixed endpoints:

1. An invertible comparison at \(q\) must select the equivalence 1-cell \(u_0\).
2. An invertible comparison at \(r\) must then be an identity 2-cell, forcing the two values of \(r\) to agree.
3. A comparison at \(a\) or \(b\) is above the nontrivial dimensions of \(Q\), so it forces equality of the selected labels.

Hence the equivalence relations on both sets of interpretations are equality.

If substitutions in both directions, with invertible inverse comparisons, existed between the categorical contexts (4) and (5), interpretation would give mutually inverse functions between a six-element set and a seven-element set. This is impossible.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
\text{The admitted constant cocone context is not equivalent to the suspension context.}
}
\]

This is a counterexample already for a **globular** \(\Gamma\). Also, every positive-dimensional generator of \(\Gamma\) is an endomorphism. Reversing any fixed set of dimensions preserves its presentation up to renaming, so an opposite convention does not remove the obstruction.

## 6. What this establishes about the proposed proof

The problematic step is the first one:

> Each \(q_x\) differs from the suspended variable only by identity whiskerings.

That statement fails for arbitrary derivations of Mikhail’s cone judgment. In the example, identity whiskerings disappear but two factors of \(r\) remain.

Benjamin–Markakis’s invertibility results apply once suitable comparison cells have been constructed. They do not supply a comparison that removes an additional arbitrary projection. Their closure and support criteria therefore cannot repair this example. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12127?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

A theorem about **a specified canonical construction** is separate. Mikhail’s Theorem 3.3 constructs a particular cone over a globular context; it does not classify every context admitted by the judgment. The extension to arbitrary contexts in Theorem 3.7 is explicitly conditional on Conjecture 3.6. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.13310v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The precise scope of the negative result is equivalence by categorical CaTT substitutions, with terminality available—the setting of the proposed proof using unitors and coherences. I have not established a model of every additional constructor of the full \(\mathrm{CaTT}_{\lim}\) extension, so I am not claiming a nonderivability theorem allowing unrestricted use of those extra constructors.

**Your semantic construction of \(\operatorname{Free}(\Gamma)\) as the lax colimit of \(1\) is valid. To obtain it syntactically, the cone construction needs more specification than the raw \(\sigma\mathrm{Cond}/\tau\mathrm{Cond}\) judgment provides.**

# Verification of the researcher response (2026-11-18)

**Verdict: correct. The semantic claim is solved. The syntactic form, as stated for contexts admitted by Mikhail's raw cone judgment, is false.** The open question that remains is about a *specified* cocone construction (see "What remains" below).

Each step was checked against the primary sources.

1. **Semantic claim.** Loubaton ([arXiv 2406.05425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05425)), Example 4.2.3.12, states laxcolim over A of the constant diagram at C is ≃ A × C for the minimally marked A, and τ₀A × C for the maximally marked A. With C = 1 this gives `laxcolim_{Free(Γ)♭} 1 ≃ Free(Γ)`. Loubaton's lax colimits are defined in his Def. 4.2.3.3, and the convention needs no opposite. This is correct, and it holds for any (∞,ω)-category, not only finite free ones.
2. **The rule as quoted.** Mikhail ([arXiv 2412.13310v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13310)), §3.1, rule (KE): append `p_x : s →_A t` when
   - s satisfies τCond, with `{c}` in place of FV(x : X) and with no linearity requirement;
   - t satisfies σCond, including `x ∝ t`;
   - both terms are "categorical", i.e. built with CaTT rules.

   Mikhail himself writes: "we do not restrict ourselves to any particular way of gluing. All legitimate ways of gluing cells to obtain higher projections are permitted and lead to distinct but valid cones." Nothing restricts how often earlier projections are used. The researcher's summary of the rule is accurate.
3. **The counterexample cone over `x, f : x → x, α : f ⇒ f`.**
   - q = p_x : c → x is admitted: FV(s) = {c}, and FV(t) = {x}.
   - r = p_f : q ⇒ qf is admitted. U_τ(f) = FV(q) = {c, x, q}. FV(qf) = {x, f, c, q}, and f ∝ qf (a single occurrence, dimensions match).
   - For α, both boundaries are f, with FV = {x, f}. Then x ∝ τ(q) = x (dimension 0) and f ∝ τ(r) = qf (dimension 1); x ∝ qf is excluded by dimension. So U = {c, x, f, q, r}.
   - s = r·(r∗1_f) has FV = U, and t = s·(1_{qf}∗α) has FV = U ∪ {α}. Also α ∝ t: α occurs once, through w, and the dimensions agree.
   - The extension b : s ⇛ t is admitted. Confirmed.
4. **Dualising and specialising to the constant diagram.**
   - Reversing 1-cells and substituting x ↦ ⊤, f ↦ 1_⊤, α ↦ 1 gives, after strict interpretation, `q : ⊤ → c, r : q ⇒ q, b : r·r ⇛ r·r`.
   - The suspension is `q, r, a : r ⇛ r`.
   - Confirmed.
5. **The model Q (1 object; 1-cells u₀, u₁, u₂ with truncated addition; 2-cells e_{n,a}, a ≤ n, composed by max).**
   - Well typed: max(a, b) ≤ min(2, n+m), since a ≤ n, b ≤ m and n, m ≤ 2.
   - Units are e_{n,0} and e_{0,0}. Associativity and interchange hold, since both sides are a max of the same labels over the same truncated sum.
   - The only invertible 1-cell is u₀, and the only invertible 2-cells are the e_{n,0}.
6. **Counting.** In the strict 3-category of strict 2-categories, take ⊤ = * and c = Q.
   - A 2-natural transformation q ⇒ q is a choice of u_n, with trivial naturality since * is discrete. A modification is an endo-2-cell of that 1-cell.
   - Counts: r ⇛ r gives 1 + 2 + 3 = 6; r² ⇛ r² gives 1 + 3 + 3 = 7 (u₁² = u₂). Confirmed.
7. **Non-equivalence.**
   - Strict models interpret CaTT, with coherences as identities (Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, [arXiv 2106.04475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04475)). Invertible syntactic comparison cells are therefore invertible in the model.
   - With the endpoints fixed, the comparisons at q and r must be identities, and comparisons in dimension 3 force equality. So mutually inverse substitutions would give a bijection between sets of 6 and 7 elements.
   - One point the response leaves implicit: the endpoints are fixed. In context (4) there is no term of type c → ⊤ and no non-identity loop at either object. So a comparison at an endpoint can only be a coherence on an identity (interpreted as an identity), or the non-invertible q. Hence both composites of the substitutions must fix ⊤ and c, and the counting argument applies.
   - The objects are endomorphisms in every positive dimension, so no choice of opposites removes the mismatch. Confirmed.

**What the counterexample does and does not show.** It refutes the step "each q_x differs from the suspended variable only by identity whiskerings" *for arbitrary derivations of the cone judgment*. It does not touch the canonical cone C(Γ) built in Mikhail's Thm 3.3. For that cone, the types are of the stated form.

**What remains (restated problem).** Let C(Γ) be the *specified* cocone construction: the dual of Mikhail's Thm 3.3 for globular Γ, or of a chosen canonical extension for arbitrary Γ. Is its constant-diagram instance equivalent to the suspension of Γ, with endpoints ⊤ and c? The proof sketch in "What is known" applies to that version. A type theory that wants "free category = lax colimit of the point" syntactically must therefore fix the cocone construction rather than accept every derivation of the judgment. The same permissiveness applies to Mikhail's Gray judgment, which has the same σCond/τCond style of side condition; see `gray-contexts-in-catt.md` §6.
