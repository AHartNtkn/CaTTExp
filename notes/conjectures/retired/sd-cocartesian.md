# Are the cut projections of Campion's decomposition poset cocartesian?

## Background

**Torsion-free complexes.** We use Forest's torsion-free complexes (S. Forest, *Unifying notions of pasting diagrams*, Higher Structures 6 (2022), arXiv 1903.00282), as used by T. Campion, *An (∞,n)-categorical pasting theorem*, arXiv 2311.00200 (v1, Nov 2023; we cite its numbering). Write FP for the free strict ω-category on such a complex P. FP is Θ-regular and hypercancellative (Campion, Prop. 1.10). Torsion-free complexes include all loop-free Steiner complexes (Campion, Remark 1.5; Forest, Thm 3.4.4.22), every object of Θ, the orientals and the lax Gray cubes.

**Decomposition posets** (Campion, Def. 4.1, 4.2 and 4.7). Let µ be an n-cell of FP. Write {µ} for the smallest subcomplex containing µ.
- Sd⊳(µ) is the poset, ordered by inclusion, of subcategories θ ⊆ F({µ}) that contain µ and are isomorphic to an object of Θ_n.
- Its bottom element is µ itself, the trivial decomposition. Sd(µ) = Sd⊳(µ) ∖ {µ}.
- For S ⊆ ℕ, let Θ_{n,S} be the objects of Θ_n assembled from their generating cells using ∘_i only for i ∈ S. Then Sd⊳_S(µ) is the full subposet of θ isomorphic to an object of Θ_{n,S}, and Sd_S(µ) = Sd⊳_S(µ) ∖ {µ}.
- When S is an initial segment of T, the inclusion Θ_{n,S} ⊆ Θ_{n,T} has a right adjoint that composes all ∘_i for i ∈ T ∖ S. It induces a monotone map U^S_T : Sd⊳_T(µ) → Sd⊳_S(µ) (Campion, Obs. 4.8).

**Atom preorders** (Campion, Def. 1.11). At_k(µ) is the preorder on the k-atoms of ∂_k µ defined as follows. Its equivalence is generated by "both lie in ∂_k c for an atom c of {µ} of dimension ≥ k + 1". Its order is generated by a ⊳_{k−1} b. If µ is not an atom, there is a largest k with 1 ≤ k ≤ n such that At_k(µ) is not an equivalence relation (Lemma 1.12).

**Cocartesian maps of posets.** A monotone map U : J → I of posets is a *cocartesian fibration* if the following holds. For every u ∈ J and every v ∈ I with U(u) ≤ v, there is û ∈ J with u ≤ û and U(û) = v, and moreover û ≤ u′ for every u′ ∈ J with u ≤ u′ and v ≤ U(u′). Call û the *least extension* of u over v.

## Statement

Fix a torsion-free complex P and a composite n-cell µ of FP. Let k be the index of Lemma 1.12 (At_i(µ) is an equivalence relation for i > k, and At_k(µ) is not).

**Question 1 (the case used).** Is the composite
  U_{k−2} : Sd(µ) ⊆ Sd⊳(µ) → Sd⊳_{[0,k−2]}(µ), with U_{k−2} = U^{[0,k−2]}_{[0,n−1]} restricted to Sd(µ),
a cocartesian fibration of posets? Give a proof, with an explicit least extension, or a counterexample.

**Question 2 (general form).** For which cuts j and which µ is U^{[0,j]}_{[0,n−1]} : Sd⊳(µ) → Sd⊳_{[0,j]}(µ) cocartesian?
- Is it cocartesian for every j and every µ?
- If not, what is the right general hypothesis? A natural candidate is j ≥ k − 2, with k as above.
- Answer the same for the restriction to Sd(µ) and for the single steps U^{[0,j−1]}_{[0,j]}.

**Question 3 (if cocartesianness fails, or as an alternative).** Let 𝒟 be the smallest class of finite posets closed under the following three rules.
- (a) *Zigzag.* P ∈ 𝒟 if the identity of P is joined to a constant map by a finite zigzag of monotone endomaps of P, each pair adjacent in the zigzag being pointwise comparable. In particular, every poset with a least or greatest element is in 𝒟.
- (b) *Grouping.* For a monotone u : P → Q with Q ∈ 𝒟 and u⁻¹(↓q) ∈ 𝒟 for every q ∈ Q, we have P ∈ 𝒟. If u is cocartesian, it suffices that each fibre u⁻¹(q) is in 𝒟.
- (c) *Bottom variant.* For a cocartesian u : P → Q where Q has a bottom element 0, Q ∖ {0} ∈ 𝒟 and u⁻¹(q) ∈ 𝒟 for every q ≠ 0, we have P ∈ 𝒟.

Is Sd(µ) ∈ 𝒟 for every composite cell µ of every torsion-free complex? Or at least for every composite cell of ν(K), where K is a strongly loop-free Steiner complex with unital basis? Give the derivation, or a composite µ with Sd(µ) ∉ 𝒟.

Every poset in 𝒟 is contractible, but 𝒟 asks for more: explicit maps and an explicit order of gluing. A different explicit derivation of Sd(µ) ∈ 𝒟 that avoids cocartesianness altogether would also answer the need below.

## What is known

1. **Campion's proof of Theorem 4.21** (|Sd(µ)| ≃ ∗ for composite µ).
   - It projects Sd(µ) to Sd⊳_{[0,k−2]}(µ) by U_{k−2}. It asserts that this map is cocartesian "by Lemma 4.12 and Remark 4.20", and applies Quillen's Theorem A.
   - The fibres over θ ≠ µ have initial objects.
   - The fibre over µ is Sd_{[k−1,n−1]}(µ). It maps by U′_{k−1} to Sd⊳_{{k−1}}(µ), again with initial objects in the fibres over θ ≠ µ.
   - Lemma 3.8 then reduces everything to Sd_{{k−1}}(µ). That poset is isomorphic to Lin(At_k(µ)) (Prop. 4.15), and Lin(At_k(µ)) is isomorphic to the barycentric subdivision of the poset of nonempty proper downsets (Lemma 3.2), which is contractible by Arone's coreflection I ↦ I ∩ Min (Lemma 3.3).
2. **The citation does not literally cover U_{k−2}.**
   - Lemma 4.12 states cocartesianness only for forgetting the top cut, [0, n−1] → [0, n−2].
   - Lemma 4.16 gives U^{[0,j−1]}_{[0,j]} for j = k when At_i(µ) is an equivalence relation for i ≥ k + 1, and Lemma 4.17 composes these down to [0, k−1].
   - The last step [0, k−1] → [0, k−2] would be Lemma 4.16 at j = k − 1. That needs At_k(µ) to be an equivalence relation, which fails at the chosen k.
   - Footnote 4 of the paper also names Lemma 4.17, which reaches only [0, k−1].
   - We have neither found a counterexample nor reconstructed the missing step.
3. **Chanavat closes the analogous step for frame-acyclic molecules.** C. Chanavat, *A strengthened (∞,n)-categorical pasting theorem*, arXiv 2603.22242 (v2, Apr 2026), works with Hadzihasanovic's regular directed complexes.
   - Lemma 2.50: for a frame-acyclic molecule U and frdim U ≤ k ≤ dim U − 1, the map Sd◁_{[0,k]}U → Sd◁_{[0,k−1]}U is cocartesian. The proof is an explicit backward induction, and the lifts are pushouts.
   - Corollary 2.51: Sd◁U → Sd◁_{[0,k]}U is cocartesian for frdim U − 1 ≤ k ≤ dim U − 1.
   - Theorem 2.52 runs Campion's proof with k := frdim U + 1 (Comment 2.9 notes the index shift between At_k and Chanavat's M_{k−1}). So Corollary 2.51 covers exactly the projection to [0, k − 2] in Campion's indexing.
   - Comment 2.46 says the lemma is "equivalent to the stronger [Campion] Lemma 4.16", and that it was proved explicitly to be safe.
4. **Relation between the two settings.**
   - For a dimension-wise acyclic regular directed complex P, ν(ℤP) ≅ Mol/P (Hadzihasanovic, *Combinatorics of higher-categorical diagrams*, arXiv 2404.07273, Thm 11.2.18). Acyclic molecules are frame-acyclic (Prop. 8.3.15 there).
   - We do not know whether every torsion-free complex, or every strongly loop-free Steiner complex with unital basis, arises this way with Sd(µ) matching Chanavat's poset. If it does, Corollary 2.51 answers Question 1 for those complexes.
5. **Two small slips in Campion's text**, recorded so they are not mistaken for the issue above. Neither affects the argument.
   - Lemma 3.2 needs nonempty *proper* downsets, although the definition of DC(P) says only "nonempty".
   - Lemma 4.18 writes Sd_{{k}}(µ), where Prop. 4.15 and the proof of Theorem 4.21 use Sd_{{k−1}}(µ).
6. **The single-cut part is explicit.**
   - On nonempty proper downsets, I ↦ I ∩ Min is monotone and lies below both the identity and the constant Min.
   - On nonempty chains bounded by t, C ↦ C ∪ {t} is monotone and lies above both the identity and {t}.

   These give Sd_{{k−1}}(µ) ∈ 𝒟 by rules (a) and (b), with the maximum map as u.

## Why it matters

The contractibility of Sd(µ) is used to build explicit coherence witnesses in weak ω-categories. The aim is a structurally recursive algorithm that compares two weak composites with the same strict value: it descends over Sd(µ), glues local constructions along the poset, and never searches. Contractibility alone gives no such algorithm. The gluing needs the specific order-theoretic witnesses of Question 3: zigzags of monotone maps, and least extensions along the two projections. A positive answer to Question 1, in the generality of strongly loop-free Steiner complexes, completes that algorithm. A negative answer with a replacement derivation in 𝒟 would complete it equally well.

## What would count as a solution

Any one of the following:
- a proof of Question 1, with the least extension described explicitly, for torsion-free complexes or at least for strongly loop-free Steiner complexes with unital basis;
- a counterexample to Question 1, together with an explicit derivation of Sd(µ) ∈ 𝒟 by another route (Question 3);
- a reference that settles Question 1 in the needed generality, including a precise identification of Campion's Sd(µ) with a poset to which Chanavat's Corollary 2.51 applies;
- for Question 2, the exact range of cuts, with proof, and a counterexample just outside it.
