Rounds 1–3 and their verified answers are archived in `answered/gray-closed-scheduling-2026-11-18.md`, `answered/gray-closed-scheduling-r2-2026-11-18.md` and `answered/gray-closed-scheduling-r3-2026-11-18.md`; nothing answered there is re-asked. The smallest open case is Question 1.*

## Background

**Schedules.** Fix a finite poset P on labels {1, …, n}, with numerical order as the left/right order of incomparable labels. Let S(P) be the category with:
- objects: the linear extensions of P;
- generating arrows: swaps ji → ij of adjacent P-incomparable labels with i < j;
- equations: commuting squares for swaps at disjoint positions, and the braid hexagon for three pairwise incomparable labels.

Then S(P)(w, v) is a singleton if Inv_P(v) ⊆ Inv_P(w) and empty otherwise. Here Inv_P(w) is the set of pairs i < j, incomparable, with j before i in w. (Proof: move the first differing label left, then resolve the first swaps by a square or a hexagon and induct.)

**Gray monoids.** In the free strict Gray monoid (a monoid in 2-Cat for Gray's lax tensor) on a set of objects and generating 1-cells, fix the image in the free strict monoidal category, i.e. the string diagram. Then the hom-category between the 1-cells with that diagram is S(P). Here P is the diagram's dependency order, with left/right given by planarity. The 2-cells are whiskered interchangers (f ⊗ B′)∘(A ⊗ g) ⇒ (A′ ⊗ g)∘(f ⊗ B), "right factor first ⇒ left factor first".

**The ω-version.** The hom ω-category of the Steiner complex ν(I^⊗n), from bottom to top, is not thin: two hexagon 2-cells are joined by the 3-cell ***. Gray's 2-tensor is its intelligent 2-truncation (Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches*, A.25 and A.31). For a dependency poset P, the subcomplex K_P ⊆ I^⊗n spanned by the faces (order ideal J, S ⊆ min(P∖J)) has paths equal to the linear extensions of P. Its truncated hom is S(P).

## Setting: the biclosed version

Formulas are built from atoms with ⊗ and the residuals B\A and A/B, with no unit. 𝒢 is the free **Gray-monoidal biclosed strict 2-category** on a set of generating 1-cells fᵢ : Pᵢ → Qᵢ between formulas. Its structure:
- a strict 2-category with a Gray monoidal structure (lax interchangers, as above);
- closed structure: isomorphisms of hom-categories Φ : 𝒢(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ 𝒢(B, A\C), and the mirror one for /.
  - Φ is **strictly natural in the context variable B**.
  - Φ is natural in the **bound variable A only up to the interchanger**.
  - Strict naturality in A would force (f ⊗ B′)(A ⊗ g) = (A′ ⊗ g)(f ⊗ B), collapsing distinct schedules into one 1-cell. That is not wanted.

**Two warnings, both established.**
1. *Nesting is not invariant under naive normalization.* For a generator h : U → U with U = X\Y,

   ```
   h = Λ(ev) ∘ h = Λ(ev ∘ (1 ⊗ h))
   ```

   So h can be "ambient" or "inside a closure". β-reduction ev ∘ (1 ⊗ Λk) = k also changes nesting. Any invariant notion of "event inside a closure" must start from a βη-canonical form.
2. *Unit-free planar coherence.* In the free unit-free biclosed monoidal 1-category, a balanced sequent (each atom twice, opposite polarities) has at most one morphism, and exactly one when it is provable in the Lambek calculus with product. So the purely structural parts of 1-cells are unique.

## What is known from round 1 (proved)

1. **A word invariant.** Sending each generator to its label gives an invariant W of 1-cell equality with values in words (composition order), with W(Λu) = W(u), W(ev) = ε and W(D ⊗ u) = W(u). It is realised by the one-object model whose 1-cells are words, with a unique 2-cell between words with the same multiset. So 1-cells with different generator order are distinct.
2. **Closure trees are not invariants of components.** With generators a : X → A and f : A ⊗ B → C, c = Λ_A(f), e = ev: the interchanger gives θ : s = e∘(a⊗U)∘(X⊗c) ⇒ t = e∘(A⊗c)∘(a⊗B), and t = f∘(a⊗B) by β. Every representative of s has a generator under currying (a Boolean biclosed poset rules out the needed structural prefix), while t is tensor-only. So any βη-normal form fixing tensor-only terms gives s and t different closure trees, though they are connected by a 2-cell. "Cross-level cells are generated by ordinary interchangers" does not imply "the canonical tree is invariant".
3. **Question 1, partial.** For φ : P → C/B and b : X → B, the forward comparison α = e_B ∗ χ_{φ,b} : t₁ ⇒ t₂ exists, W distinguishes t₁, t₂, and Λ_X(t₂) = (C/b)∘φ by strict naturality in the context variable. Whether 𝒢(t₁, t₂) = {α} and 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ is open: the tensor-only schedule theorem does not control 2-cells built by currying, uncurrying and η-expansion.
4. **Mapping-space biclosedness is too weak for Question 4.** Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari (arXiv 2006.14495, Rem. 2.20): Map(X ⊗ Y, Z) ≇ Map(X, Fun^{opgr}(Y, Z)) in general; only cores agree.

## What is known from round 2 (proved)

5. **An integer model.** Let 𝓜 be the strict 2-category with:
   - objects the integers;
   - for q ≥ p, the hom 𝓜(p, q) equal to the poset (ℤ, ≤) of weights; otherwise empty;
   - composition adding weights;
   - w(p ⊗ u) = w(u) + p·δ(u) and w(u ⊗ q) = w(u), where δ(u) = target − source;
   - p\r = r − p and r/q = r − q, with left currying subtracting p·δ and right currying leaving the weight unchanged.

   𝓜 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category. An interchanger raises the weight by δ(f)·δ(g). A matrix-valued version (objects in ℤ^r) records ordered generator pairs.
6. **Question 1, reverse direction.** With distinct atoms, 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ and 𝒢(t, s) = ∅. For t₁ and t₂ the weights are 0 and 1, from |B| = 1, |C| = 2 and |P| = |X| = 0. For s and t they are −1 and 0. Every forward 2-cell between them uses **exactly one** interchanger whose two arguments each contain one generator. Every other interchanger in it has a generator-free argument.
7. **Undirected components.** π₀ 𝒢(A, C) ≅ 𝓑_Σ(A, C), the free ordinary unit-free biclosed monoidal category. Two 1-cells are connected by a zigzag of 2-cells iff their images there agree.
8. **The image and W do not classify 1-cells.** Take generators f : A → B and g : B → C with padding L, R, and let m = L ⊗ (g ∘ f) ⊗ R.
   - Currying g with the right padding gives u ⇒ m.
   - Currying g with the left padding gives m ⇒ v.
   - u, m and v have equal images in 𝓑_Σ, and W(u) = W(v).
   - Their weights (with |L| = |R| = |A| = 0, |B| = 1, |C| = 2) are −1, 0 and 1, so the three 1-cells are distinct.
   - The side of currying is data of the 1-cell, and schedules with only generator events cannot describe this component.

## Further observations (proved)

9. **Subtractive models cannot see interchangers with a structural argument.** Call a model *additive* if 1-cells carry a weight in an ordered abelian group, δ(u) = weight(target) − weight(source) is additive under composition and tensor, and an interchanger χ_{f,g} adds a bilinear term β(δ(f), δ(g)). If moreover the residuals are group subtraction (A\C = C − A, as in the model 𝓜 of item 5 and its matrix version), every structural 1-cell (ev, curried evaluations, units) has δ = 0, so every interchanger with a structural argument has weight 0. Such models cannot decide whether two 2-cells differing in those interchangers are equal. This blindness is specific to subtractive residuals; see item 13.
10. **The axioms do not make evaluation central.** For ev : A ⊗ (A\C) → C and a generator g : Y → Y′, compare (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y), related by χ_{ev,g}. Currying A on the left (using strict naturality in the context variable and the target) turns this into the same question for m_Y := Φ(ev ⊗ Y) and g, so currying only transports it. Likewise for χ_{ev,ev′} at independent positions. Unless centrality is imposed (as in Garner–Hirschowitz–Pardon's premonoidal construction), structural maps are non-central events in 𝒢. In the quotient 𝒢_c of 𝒢 in which every interchanger with a structural argument is an identity, Question 1 follows at once from item 6.

## What is known from round 3 (proved)

11. **Structural loops (round 3).** Take atoms A, B, C, D and U = A\(A⊗B), X = A⊗B, X′ = A⊗U, V = (C⊗D)/D, Y = C⊗D, Y′ = V⊗D. Let u = 1_A ⊗ Λ_A(1) : X → X′, e = ev : X′ → X, v = Λ^R_D(1) ⊗ 1_D : Y → Y′ and d = ev^R : Y′ → Y, so that e u = 1 and d v = 1 by β. Put T = X ⊗ Y and (composition rightmost first)

    ```
    H = (e ⊗ 1_Y)(1_{X′} ⊗ d)(u ⊗ 1_{Y′})(1_X ⊗ v) : T → T.
    ```

    - Whiskered interchangers give ι = (e⊗1) ∗ χ_{u,d} ∗ (1⊗v) : 1_T ⇒ H and π = [(e⊗1)(1⊗d)] ∗ χ_{u,v} : H ⇒ 1_T. The Gray axiom for χ_{u,dv} gives **π ∘ ι = 1**, so E = ι ∘ π is an idempotent on H.
    - **H^n are pairwise distinct.** In the model whose hom-categories are chaotic on the free group ⟨ℓ, r⟩ (objects integers, composition multiplication, left currying k ↦ ℓ^a k, right currying k ↦ r^a k), H ↦ ℓ⁻¹ r⁻¹ ℓ r.
    - All H^n have image 1_T in 𝓑_Σ, empty generator word and zero weight in every model of item 5. Since 2-cells H^m ⇒ 1_T ⇒ H^n exist, the category over 1_T is not isomorphic to any product of schedule categories.
    - **The separation uses that left and right currying do not commute.** The model does not satisfy strict equality of the two mixed curryings of (A\C)/B = A\(C/B).
    - **Forward comparisons.** Item 6's count factors each forward comparison as z₊ ∘ m ∘ z₋, with z_± using only interchangers with a generator-free argument and m a typed instance of the one generator–generator interchanger. Different typed instances of m must still be identified.
12. **Evaluation is not central in 𝒢.** In the same model with a third free generator γ for a generating 1-cell g, (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) ↦ ℓ⁻¹γ and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y) ↦ γℓ⁻¹, which are distinct. So χ_{ev,g} joins distinct 1-cells, and this uses only left closure. A complete invariant of 1-cells must schedule structural events (units, evaluations), not only generator events.

13. **A polarized model sees structural interchangers.** Let 𝓟 have objects (P, N) ∈ ℤ², A ⊗ B = A + B and A\C = C/A = C + swap(A), where swap(P, N) = (N, P). A 1-cell X → Y exists iff (P − N)(X) = (P − N)(Y), and it is an integer weight w. Put d(u) = P(source) − P(target).
    - Composition adds weights, w(A ⊗ u) = w(u) + P(A)·d(u) and w(u ⊗ B) = w(u).
    - Left currying is w ↦ w − P(A)·d(u), and right currying keeps the weight.
    - The homs are codiscrete.
    - 𝓟 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category in the sense above.
    - **ev is not central.** Here d(ev_A) = P(A) + N(A) ≠ 0. With A ↦ (1, 0), C ↦ (0, 0), X ↦ (1, 1), X′ ↦ (0, 0) and g : X → X′, the 1-cells (X′ ⊗ ev) ∘ (g ⊗ A ⊗ A\C) and (g ⊗ C) ∘ (X ⊗ ev) have weights 1 and 2. The same separates the two orders of two independent evaluations, with no generator involved.
    - Together with item 12, the central quotient 𝒢_c of item 10 is a proper quotient of 𝒢.

## Questions

1. **(Is ι ∘ π the identity?)** For H of item 11, is ι ∘ π = 1_H in 𝒢? If yes, ι and π are inverse and the H^n are uniquely isomorphic to 1_T. If no, the hom-category 𝒢(T, T) is not thin. A model with non-chaotic 2-cells (for instance, one built from schedules of structural and generator events) would decide this.
2. **(Strict commutation of left and right currying.)** Suppose the theory also imposes that the two curryings into (A\C)/B = A\(C/B) agree strictly. Is H = 1_T then? Is there a separating example using only left closure (only units and evaluations of \)? Which choice, 𝒢 with or without this equation, matches the (∞,ω) Gray tensor's biclosedness?
3. **(Forward uniqueness, smallest case.)** Prove 𝒢(t₁, t₂) = {α} and 𝒢(s, t) = {θ}, or show that they fail. This includes identifying the forward comparisons built from different typed instances of the crossing interchanger (item 11, last point). If Question 1 fails, the analogous statement may fail too; then describe 𝒢(t₁, t₂).
4. **(1-cells within a component, up to equivalence.)** For a fixed arrow of 𝓑_Σ, is the category of 1-cells over it *equivalent* to a product of schedule posets S(P) whose events include structural events (units, evaluations, curry sides)? Give a complete invariant of 1-cells, or of their isomorphism classes.
5. **(Centrality in the (∞,ω) setting.)** Item 12 shows that ev is not central in 𝒢. For the Gray tensor of (∞,ω)-categories (Campion; biclosed on mapping spaces), is the interchanger of evaluation with a generic cell invertible, or trivial? The answer decides whether 𝒢 or its central quotient 𝒢_c (item 10) is sound for that tensor.
6. **(ω-version.)** With biclosedness on full hom ω-categories, are the hom ω-categories of the free Gray-monoidal biclosed (∞,ω)-category over a fixed arrow of 𝓑_Σ equivalent to products of ν(K_P)-homs for suitable P, now with structural events included?

# Researcher Response

**Strict commutation of left and right currying does not imply \(H=1_T\).** A model satisfying that additional equation still separates the original \(H\) from the identity. The same model separates a version of \(H\) constructed using only left units and evaluations.

For Question 5, the outer comparison between evaluation and another **functor** is invertible, and is an identity in the strict tensor model. Evaluation applied to an interchanger between **internal cells** can be noninvertible. These have different types. I give the distinction and a finite obstruction to the proposed full-hom interpretation below.

I do not decide whether \(\iota\pi=1_H\).

## 1. A model with strictly commuting curryings and \(H\ne1_T\)

Retain the underlying Gray monoid of the free-group model, but change its chosen closed structure.

Let
\[
F=\langle \ell,r\rangle
\]
be the free group. Define \(\mathcal D\) by:

- its objects are integers;
- for every \(m,n\), \(\mathcal D(m,n)\) is the category whose objects are elements of \(F\), with exactly one arrow between every ordered pair;
- composition of 1-cells is multiplication, with \(g\circ f=gf\);
- tensor on objects is addition;
- tensoring by an object preserves the group label of a 1-cell.

The interchanger is the unique 2-cell between its prescribed endpoints. Thus all Gray equations hold.

### 1.1 The new closed structure

Choose an arbitrary family
\[
k_c\in F\qquad(c\in\mathbb Z),
\]
and define
\[
h_{a,c}=k_{c-a}k_c^{-1}. \tag{1}
\]

Use the residual objects
\[
a\backslash c=c-a,\qquad c/a=c-a.
\]
For **both** left and right currying, with bound object \(a\) and codomain \(c\), set
\[
\boxed{\Lambda_{a,c}(f)=h_{a,c}f.} \tag{2}
\]
Uncurrying is multiplication on the left by \(h_{a,c}^{-1}\).

The corresponding fixed-object right adjoint sends
\[
t:c\longrightarrow d
\]
to
\[
R_a(t)=h_{a,d}\,t\,h_{a,c}^{-1}:c-a\longrightarrow d-a. \tag{3}
\]
This is a strict 2-functor. On 1-cells, for example,
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_a(s)R_a(t)
&=h_{a,e}s\,h_{a,d}^{-1}h_{a,d}t\,h_{a,c}^{-1}\\
&=h_{a,e}st\,h_{a,c}^{-1}\\
&=R_a(st).
\end{aligned}
\]

The hom-category maps in (2) are isomorphisms: their maps on objects are inverse bijections, and their maps on arrows are uniquely determined.

Strict context naturality is
\[
\Lambda_{a,c}(fq)
=h_{a,c}fq
=\Lambda_{a,c}(f)q.
\]
Strict codomain naturality is
\[
\begin{aligned}
R_a(t)\Lambda_{a,c}(f)
&=(h_{a,d}t h_{a,c}^{-1})(h_{a,c}f)\\
&=h_{a,d}tf\\
&=\Lambda_{a,d}(tf).
\end{aligned}
\]

The unit at \(b\) and counit at \(c\) have labels
\[
\eta_b^a=h_{a,a+b},
\qquad
\varepsilon_c^a=h_{a,c}^{-1}.
\]
The first triangle is multiplication by
\[
h_{a,a+b}^{-1}h_{a,a+b}=1.
\]
For the second,
\[
R_a(\varepsilon_c^a)=h_{a,c}^{-1},
\qquad
\eta_{c-a}^a=h_{a,c},
\]
so their composite is also \(1\). All equations on 2-cells follow from uniqueness.

Thus this is a model of the stated full hom-category adjunctions.

### 1.2 Mixed currying commutes strictly

Take a 1-cell
\[
f:a+z+b\longrightarrow c.
\]
The two mixed curryings have labels
\[
h_{b,c-a}h_{a,c}f
\quad\text{and}\quad
h_{a,c-b}h_{b,c}f.
\]
But
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_{b,c-a}h_{a,c}
&=k_{c-a-b}k_{c-a}^{-1}k_{c-a}k_c^{-1}\\
&=k_{c-a-b}k_c^{-1}\\
&=k_{c-a-b}k_{c-b}^{-1}k_{c-b}k_c^{-1}\\
&=h_{a,c-b}h_{b,c}.
\end{aligned} \tag{4}
\]
The target objects also agree:
\[
(c-a)-b=(c-b)-a.
\]
Hence the two currying functors are **equal**, including their action on 2-cells.

The model satisfies the additional normalizations
\[
h_{0,c}=1,
\qquad
h_{b,c-a}h_{a,c}=h_{a+b,c},
\qquad
R_bR_a=R_{a+b}.
\]
Thus neither normalization at the tensor unit nor strict normalization of iterated same-side currying removes the example.

### 1.3 The original \(H\) remains nonidentity

Interpret the atoms by
\[
|A|=|B|=1,\qquad |C|=|D|=2,
\]
and choose
\[
k_1=\ell,\qquad k_4=r^{-1},
\qquad k_c=1\text{ for }c\notin\{1,4\}.
\]
In particular, \(k_2=1\).

For the four structural arrows of item 11,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\llbracket u\rrbracket
&=h_{1,2}=k_1k_2^{-1}=\ell,
&
\llbracket e\rrbracket&=\ell^{-1},\\
\llbracket v\rrbracket
&=h_{2,4}=k_2k_4^{-1}=r,
&
\llbracket d\rrbracket&=r^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Tensor sections preserve these labels, so
\[
\boxed{\llbracket H\rrbracket=\ell^{-1}r^{-1}\ell r\ne1.} \tag{5}
\]

Therefore, writing \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{mix}}\) for the theory with the additional strict mixed-currying equation,
\[
\boxed{H\ne1_T\quad\text{in }\mathcal G_{\mathrm{mix}}.} \tag{6}
\]

Moreover, the words
\[
(\ell^{-1}r^{-1}\ell r)^n
\]
are reduced and have lengths \(4n\). Thus the original \(H^n\) remain pairwise distinct even after imposing strict mixed currying.

The distinction from the round-three separator is exact: its currying multiplier depended only on the bound object. Here it depends on both the bound object and the codomain. Equation (4) makes successive multipliers telescope, while the units used in \(H\) have different codomains and can receive noncommuting labels.

## 2. A separating example using only left closure

Keep \(X,X',u,e\) from item 11. Replace the right-closure retraction by
\[
W=C\backslash(C\otimes D),
\qquad
Y=C\otimes D,
\qquad
Y'_L=C\otimes W,
\]
and
\[
v_L
=
1_C\otimes\Lambda_C^L(1_{C\otimes D})
:Y\longrightarrow Y'_L,
\qquad
d_L
=
\operatorname{ev}^L_{C,C\otimes D}
:Y'_L\longrightarrow Y.
\]
Then
\[
d_Lv_L=1_Y.
\]

Define
\[
H_L=
(e\otimes1_Y)
(1_{X'}\otimes d_L)
(u\otimes1_{Y'_L})
(1_X\otimes v_L).
\]
This term uses only tensor, left currying, left evaluation, and composition.

In the same model and under the same atom interpretation,
\[
\llbracket v_L\rrbracket=h_{2,4}=r,
\qquad
\llbracket d_L\rrbracket=r^{-1}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{\llbracket H_L\rrbracket=\ell^{-1}r^{-1}\ell r\ne1.} \tag{7}
\]

Thus:

- the free theory with only left closure already has such nonidentity structural endomorphisms;
- they remain distinct in the biclosed theory with strictly commuting mixed curryings;
- their powers are pairwise distinct;
- the same interchanger construction gives
  \[
  1_T\Rightarrow H_L\Rightarrow1_T
  \]
  with the composite on \(1_T\) equal to its identity.

This answers the algebraic parts of Question 2.

## 3. What mixed currying means for Campion’s tensor

For the monoidal biclosed \(\infty\)-category supplied by Campion, the intrinsic statement is a **canonical equivalence**
\[
\boxed{(A\backslash C)/B\simeq A\backslash(C/B).} \tag{8}
\]
Campion’s Theorem A supplies the monoidal biclosed structure on the external \(\infty\)-category of higher categories. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00205v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Here is the derivation, using only its mapping-space adjunctions. Naturally in \(Z\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Map}\bigl(Z,(A\backslash C)/B\bigr)
&\simeq \operatorname{Map}(Z\otimes B,A\backslash C)\\
&\simeq \operatorname{Map}(A\otimes(Z\otimes B),C)\\
&\simeq \operatorname{Map}((A\otimes Z)\otimes B,C)\\
&\simeq \operatorname{Map}(A\otimes Z,C/B)\\
&\simeq \operatorname{Map}\bigl(Z,A\backslash(C/B)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Yoneda gives (8). No exchange of \(A\) and \(B\) is involved.

The two double-curried maps agree after transport along this comparison. Literal equality of their displayed codomains and currying operations is an additional presentation condition. In Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari’s point-set model, the corresponding comparison is explicitly a natural **isomorphism**, in Remark 2.6. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.14495v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Thus Campion’s biclosedness supplies coherent mixed commutation. It does not, by itself, select a presentation with literal equality or supply the full enriched adjunctions assumed in \(\mathcal G\).

## 4. Question 5: the outer comparison is invertible

Let
\[
e:(C/B)\otimes B\longrightarrow C,
\qquad
g:X\longrightarrow Y
\]
be **functors between higher categories**.

Tensor bifunctoriality gives the comparison
\[
\begin{aligned}
(e\otimes1_Y)
(1_{(C/B)\otimes B}\otimes g)
&\simeq e\otimes g\\
&\simeq
(1_C\otimes g)
(e\otimes1_X).
\end{aligned} \tag{9}
\]
These are equivalences in a mapping space of the external \(\infty\)-category. In the strict Gray tensor on strict higher categories, both composites are literally the functor \(e\otimes g\).

Therefore, for this outer functor-level interpretation,
\[
\boxed{\text{the comparison is invertible; in the strict model it is an identity.}}
\]

This calculation applies to any pair of functors. Evaluation has no special role in it.

### 4.1 Evaluation of an internal interchanger can be noninvertible

A different expression is
\[
e(\chi_{\alpha,u}),
\]
where \(\alpha\) is a 1-cell **of the internal hom** \(C/B\), and \(u\) is a 1-cell **of** \(B\). This is a 2-cell of \(C\), not an outer comparison between two functors.

A finite example shows that it need not be invertible.

Let \(J=[1]\) and
\[
S=J\otimes J.
\]
Write its generating arrows as
\[
\begin{aligned}
h_0 &:00\to10,&h_1 &:01\to11,\\
v_0 &:00\to01,&v_1 &:10\to11.
\end{aligned}
\]
Using the orientation in the question, its generating 2-cell is
\[
\gamma:h_1v_0\Longrightarrow v_1h_0,
\]
with no reverse 2-cell. The directed square and the resulting opposite transformation directions in the two internal homs are described in Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari, Example 2.5 and the following paragraphs. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.14495v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Curry the identity
\[
1_S:J\otimes J\longrightarrow S
\]
in its right argument:
\[
q:J\longrightarrow S/J.
\]
The generating arrow of \(J\) maps to a transformation
\[
\alpha:v_0\longrightarrow v_1
\]
whose two components are \(h_0,h_1\).

For evaluation \(e:(S/J)\otimes J\to S\), β gives
\[
e(q\otimes1_J)=1_S.
\]
Applying this equality to the generating square gives
\[
\boxed{e(\chi_{\alpha,u})=\gamma.} \tag{10}
\]
Thus (10) is noninvertible.

This finite example also applies to Campion’s tensor: his Theorem A preserves the Gray cubes, and the inclusion of gaunt strict higher categories is fully faithful, so passing to that setting does not introduce a reverse cell to \(\gamma\). [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00205v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Equations (9) and (10) therefore answer different questions:
\[
\chi_{e,g}
\qquad\text{versus}\qquad
e(\chi_{\alpha,u}).
\]

## 5. A finite obstruction to the proposed full-hom interpretation

The distinction above does not merely concern notation. The same square prevents the usual Gray residuals from serving as both adjoints for **one common full hom enrichment**.

Consider the two fixed functors
\[
v_0,v_1:J\longrightarrow S.
\]

In the right residual \(S/J\), there is an arrow
\[
v_0\longrightarrow v_1,
\]
namely \(\alpha\) above.

In the left residual \(J\backslash S\), there is **no** arrow from \(v_0\) to \(v_1\). Such an arrow would correspond to a functor
\[
J\otimes J\longrightarrow S
\]
with the argument and parameter axes exchanged. Its components are forced to be \(h_0,h_1\), but the generating square would then require
\[
v_1h_0\Longrightarrow h_1v_0,
\]
the nonexistent reverse of \(\gamma\).

Now suppose there were a common full hom enrichment \(\mathscr H\), using the usual currying maps, with both enriched adjunctions and
\[
\mathscr H(I,Z)\simeq Z
\]
at the tensor unit. The two adjunctions would give
\[
\mathscr H(J,S)
\simeq \mathscr H(I,S/J)
\simeq S/J
\]
and
\[
\mathscr H(J,S)
\simeq \mathscr H(I,J\backslash S)
\simeq J\backslash S.
\]
On objects, both curryings identify a functor \(J\to S\) with that same functor regarded as a point of the residual.

They would therefore identify the homs from \(v_0\) to \(v_1\). One is nonempty and the other empty. This is impossible.

Accordingly, the proposed semantic test in Question 5 needs a qualification:

> Outer functoriality makes the evaluation comparison invertible. It does not supply a common full hom enrichment satisfying both of \(\mathcal G\)’s adjunctions. The finite square already obstructs that interpretation.

This does not preclude a coarser interpretation in an ordinary biclosed category that sends syntactic 2-cells to identities. It does prevent using such an interpretation to decide the intended directed coherence questions.

## 6. A constraint on countermodels for Question 1

The new model does not decide
\[
\iota\pi\stackrel{?}{=}1_H.
\]
Neither can a non-chaotic refinement that keeps the 1-cell algebra of the free-group model or of the polarized integer-weight model unchanged.

The reason is stronger than thinness of their homs: **all their 1-cells have strict inverses**.

In any lax Gray monoid, if \(f\) is strictly invertible, then \(\chi_{f,g}\) is invertible. An inverse is
\[
(f\otimes B')
*
\chi_{f^{-1},g}
*
(f\otimes B).
\]
Its endpoints reduce to those of \(\chi_{f,g}\) in reverse order. The two inverse equations follow from the Gray composition equations for \(ff^{-1}=1\) and \(f^{-1}f=1\).

Hence, in any refinement retaining those 1-cells, the interpretations of \(\iota\) and \(\pi\) are invertible. Since
\[
\pi\iota=1,
\]
their other composite must also be the identity.

A countermodel separating \(\iota\pi\) from \(1_H\) must therefore permit the structural retractions used in \(H\) to fail to be strict 1-cell isomorphisms.

Finally, even a proof of \(\iota\pi=1_H\) would establish that the **specified** \(\iota,\pi\) are inverse isomorphisms. Uniqueness of isomorphisms \(H^n\cong1_T\) would still require an additional coherence argument.
