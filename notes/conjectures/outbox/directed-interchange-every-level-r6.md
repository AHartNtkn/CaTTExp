# Free higher categories with directed interchange in every dimension (sixth round)

*A self-contained problem. No background beyond standard higher category theory is assumed. Rounds 1–5, with their answers, are in `answered/`. Questions are in §4, smallest first.*

## 1. Setting

**Globular sets and composition.** Work with reflexive globular sets with source and target maps s, t, identities, and for k < n a partial composition ∗ₖ of n-cells along k-cells. Composition is written in **diagrammatic order** (u ∗ₖ v means "first u, then v"), and it is strictly associative and unital. The boundary formulas hold: for k < m and (m+1)-cells u, v,

```
s(u ∗ₖ v) = s(u) ∗ₖ s(v),     t(u ∗ₖ v) = t(u) ∗ₖ t(v).        (B)
```

For k < l < n and n-cells in interchange position put `L(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₗ b) ∗ₖ (c ∗ₗ d)` and `R(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₖ c) ∗ₗ (b ∗ₖ d)`. A strict ω-category requires L = R.

**The Gray case (directed interchange along 0-cells only).** In a category enriched in strict ω-categories with the lax Gray tensor product (Ara–Maltsiniotis, arXiv 1607.00668), for 2-cells α : f ⇒ f′ : x → y and β : g ⇒ g′ : y → z there is a 3-cell

```
χ_{α,β} : (f ◁ β) ∗₁ (α ▷ g′)  ⇛  (α ▷ g) ∗₁ (f′ ◁ β)
```

and nothing in the reverse direction. Composition along k-cells with k ≥ 1 is strict. For a finite globular pasting diagram Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ, the free such structure has homs ν(λΘᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘⱼ). Here λ is Steiner's directed complex, ⊗ is Steiner's tensor with `∂(x ⊗ y) = ∂x ⊗ y + (−1)^{|x|} x ⊗ ∂y`, and ν is Steiner's functor to strict ω-categories (Steiner, HHA 2004). Each hom is generated by tuples of cells, and a cell is determined by the set of generators it uses.

**The aim** is the same for directed interchange along k-cells for **every** k, including inside homs.

## 2. What is already known (all proved)

**(K1) Raw higher interchangers are impossible.** If every pair of n-cells in interchange position had an (n+1)-cell L → R, then applying this to identities and using (B) forces L = R one dimension down. That contradicts directedness.

**(K2) A single comparison for each simultaneous variation is impossible.** The obvious repair of (K1) adds, for 3-cells A : a ⇛ a′, …, D : d ⇛ d′ in interchange position, a 4-cell between the parallel 3-cells

```
L(A,B,C,D) ∗₂ χ_{a′}     and     χ_a ∗₂ R(A,B,C,D).        (N)
```

This cannot be required for every instance. Take Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², with two 3-cells A : α ⇛ α′ and B : β ⇛ β′ side by side along a 0-cell, and the instance (A, 1, 1, B). Its two sides map to the hom 2-cells

```
U = (v₀ ∘₀ u₁) ∘₁ c₁₁,     V = c₀₀ ∘₁ (u₀ ∘₀ v₁)
```

of ν(λD² ⊗ λD²), where `u_j = A ⊗ g_j`, `v_i = f_i ⊗ B` and `c_ij = α_i ⊗ β_j`. There is no 4-cell between U and V in either direction: the degree-3 chain would need a positive coefficient at u₀ (or at v₀), but every basis element of degree 3 has a non-positive coefficient there.

**(K3) The two variables have opposite naturality directions.** The degree-3 basis elements are `r_j = A ⊗ β_j` and `t_i = α_i ⊗ B`. Their differentials are

```
∂r_j = c_1j + u₁ − c_0j − u₀,     ∂t_i = c_i0 + v₁ − c_i1 − v₀.
```

So when only A varies, the Gray structure has a naturality 4-cell from the "χ first" side to the "variation first" side (V ⇛ U). When only B varies, it goes the other way (U ⇛ V). The simultaneous variation is the 5-cell A ⊗ B. It goes between two routes of 4-cells, `S → V → T` ⇛ `S → U → T`, where S and T are the composites through c₀₁ and c₁₀. In short, the higher structure is **tensor-shaped**: one Leibniz-signed naturality cell per variable, and higher cells between routes.

**(K4) Supports must use an expanded basis.** If every composite's support were the union of the supports of its parts, L and R would always have equal support. Then "support determines the cell" would force strict interchange.

**(K5) One finite basis forces finitely many cells.** If one finite set B admits injective support maps in every dimension, the structure has finitely many non-identity cells in total.

**(K6) No single directed complex presents the answer, and neither does a hom-wise family of them.** In ν(K), the two composites defining χ have the same chain table, so they are equal. A description must therefore be an iterated tower: objects, then homs presented again by objects and homs, and so on.

**(K7) The first uniform case through hom-dimension 3.** For Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², directing interchange inside the hom 𝒦(x, z) adds exactly two generators of hom-dimension 3 beyond the 25 tensor generators. They are the interchangers χ₁ of (A⊗g, f′⊗B) and χ₂ of (f⊗B, A⊗g′), the only pairs of hom-2-cells composable along a hom-0-cell. Their two sides have different supports. Neither occurs in the boundary of A ⊗ B.

**(K8) The (K7) 3-skeleton cannot be extended by 4-generators alone (round 2, proved).** Write h_i^j = α_i ⊗ g_j, k_j^i = f_i ⊗ β_j, P_ij = k_j^0 h_i^1, Q_ij = h_i^0 k_j^1, so c_ij : P_ij ⇒ Q_ij. The tensor 3-generators are r_j : c_0j ; a_j^+ ⇛ a_j^- ; c_1j and t_i : b_i^- ; c_i1 ⇛ c_i0 ; b_i^+ (a_j^±, b_i^± the whiskered edge cells u_j, v_i), and the interchangers are χ₁ : b_0^+ ; a_1^+ ⇛ a_0^+ ; b_1^+ and χ₂ : a_0^- ; b_1^- ⇛ b_0^- ; a_1^-. The six composite hom-2-cells P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ are S = b_0^- ; c_01 ; a_1^+, V₀ = c_00 ; b_0^+ ; a_1^+, V₁ = c_00 ; a_0^+ ; b_1^+, T = a_0^- ; c_10 ; b_1^+, U₀ = a_0^- ; b_1^- ; c_11 and U₁ = b_0^- ; a_1^- ; c_11. Whiskering gives t̄₀ : S ⇛ V₀, χ̄₁ : V₀ ⇛ V₁, r̄₀ : V₁ ⇛ T, r̄₁ : S ⇛ U₁, χ̄₂ = χ₂ ∗₁ 1_{c₁₁} : U₀ ⇛ U₁ and t̄₁ : U₀ ⇛ T. Since ∂(A⊗B) = t₁ − t₀ + r₁ − r₀, requirement 4 forces a 4-cell between the route t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀ from S to T and a route from S to T using r̄₁ and t̄₁. In the free 3-skeleton with exactly the 25 tensor generators plus χ₁, χ₂ and no 2-equations, the first route exists, but the second would have to go S ⇛ U₁ and then U₁ ⇛ U₀ against χ̄₂: in the directed graph of these composites the only S → T path is t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀. So no list of 4-generators recovers A ⊗ B. The naturality cells of χ₁, χ₂ are trivial here (the edge homs have no 3-generators).

The repair must change dimension-3 data: an extra 3-generator such as h : S ⇛ U₀ whose image under the Gray quotient is r̄₁, or a 2-equation such as U₀ = U₁. Inverting χ₂ would also work but breaks requirement 3.

**(K9) The orientation of χ₁, χ₂ in (K7) is forced by substitution (proved).** One might try to avoid (K8) by orienting every inner interchanger by the factor order of the enclosing tensor (always "Θ₂-cell first ⇛ Θ₁-cell first"), which keeps χ₁ and reverses χ₂. This fails requirement 5. The free object on Σ(D² ∨ D²) has, inside its hom, the interchanger of its two 2-cells A′ (left) and B′ (right), oriented as in the Gray case: "B′ first ⇛ A′ first". The assignment A′ ↦ f₀ ⊗ B and B′ ↦ A ⊗ g₁ (with the hom-0-cells and hom-1-cells sent to f₀⊗g₀, f₀⊗g₁, f₁⊗g₁ and to the matching edges) respects all boundaries, so substitution sends that interchanger to a 3-cell from "A ⊗ g₁ first" to "f₀ ⊗ B first", which is exactly χ₂ in its (K7) orientation. The pair χ₁ is forced the same way via A′ ↦ A ⊗ g₀, B′ ↦ f₁ ⊗ B. So χ₂ cannot be reversed: adding the reversed cell as well breaks requirement 3, which is the "inverting χ₂" option above. Inner interchangers are oriented by position (right cell first ⇛ left cell first), as at the outer level, and some repair of dimension-3 data is needed (see (K10)).

**(K10) The smallest case is repaired by a factorization (round 3, proved).** The six hom-2 composites P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ of (K8) are the maximal paths of an oriented 3-cube with coordinates A (edges a_j^±), B (edges b_i^±) and C (edges c_ij): S = BCA, V₀ = CBA, V₁ = CAB, T = ACB, U₀ = ABC, U₁ = BAC. The six whiskered hom-3 cells t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀, r̄₁, χ̄₂, t̄₁ are its six square faces (adjacent transpositions); every proper interval carries at most one of them. Add one hom-3 generator and one hom-3 equation:

```
h : S ⇛ U₀,        r̄₁ = h ∗₂ χ̄₂.        (F)
```

Then the maximal hom-3 interval is the free category on S → V₀ → V₁ → T, S → U₀ → U₁, U₀ → T, with 11 non-identity cells with pairwise distinct label sets, two routes S → T (t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ and h ; t̄₁), and no reverse of χ̄₁ or χ̄₂. Killing the inner interchangers gives exactly the free Gray diamond with q(h) = q(r̄₁), and A ⊗ B lifts to one 4-generator W : t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ ⇛ h ; t̄₁ with no non-identity contexts. Supports use one label per whiskered context, so r̄₁ is no longer a basis element (its support is that of h χ̄₂). The equation (F) cannot be dropped or replaced by a 4-cell h χ̄₂ ⇛ r̄₁ without a new class of cells killed by the quotient. h is not an interchanger: S and U₀ each contain a c_ij, and every hom-2 path containing a c_ij spans the whole external hom-0 interval.

h is **not forced**. The mirror repair k : U₁ ⇛ T with t̄₁ = χ̄₂ ∗₂ k works equally well, and so does the symmetric repair with both h and k and both equations (12 cells, second route h ; χ̄₂ ; k, same exact Gray quotient). The alternative 2-equation U₀ = U₁ makes χ̄₂ an endomorphism and needs further equations (at least χ̄₂² = χ̄₂ under set supports), so it is not preferred.

**(K12) The source/sink count fails in the ambient graph (round 5, proved).** Reversing one arrow u → v of a finite directed graph changes the number of sources by 1[d⁻(v)=1] − 1[d⁻(u)=0], and the number of sinks by 1[d⁺(u)=1] − 1[d⁺(v)=0].
- Take the interval of λD² ⊗ λD² ⊗ λD² with hom-1 boundary (β₀α₀)γ₀ → (α₁β₁)γ₁. Its hom-2 paths are the 24 orderings of A, B (updates of the first two factors), C (the crossing βα → αβ) and D (the third-factor update).
- Its arrows are BC → CB, CA → AC and DX → XD. The arrow between A and B is positional: AB → BA when C follows the pair, and BA → AB when C precedes it.
- Compare with the graph that uses BA → AB everywhere, which has one source, DBCA, and one sink, ACBD. The positional graph has sources {DBCA, DABC} and sinks {ACBD, BACD}.
- Of the three reversed arrows, BADC → ABDC creates neither a source nor a sink.

So a flipped face does not create one extra source/sink pair in the full comparison graph. It does so only for an isolated polygon.

**(K13) Local factorization does not force alternation (round 5).** For a simple oriented polygon with arms pᵢ : sᵢ → tᵢ and qᵢ : sᵢ₊₁ → tᵢ (indices mod r), add hᵢ : s₀ → sᵢ for 1 ≤ i < r, with p₀ = h₁;q₀ and hᵢ;pᵢ = hᵢ₊₁;qᵢ for 1 ≤ i < r − 1. **(K15) The interchanger-of-a-composite convention.** The substitution in (K14) assumes that crossing against a composite hom-1 path is the composite of the pairwise crossings. That holds in Gray categories, but it does not follow from (B), associativity and units alone.

**Literature.** No construction and no impossibility result is known.
- Closest: n-sesquicategories, which have no interchange at all (Araújo, arXiv 2202.09293 and 2210.07704; Forest–Mimram, arXiv 2211.16099 and *Rewriting in Gray categories*, MSCS 2022).
- Crans's teisi.
- Bourke–Lobbia, arXiv 2610.31448: their §4.2.6 lax 4-categories have non-invertible outer interchangers, but invertible interchange inside homs.
- Bourke–Gurski's cocategorical obstruction needs hypotheses not assumed here.

## 3. Requirements

A **tensor-shaped lax-interchange ω-category** has, for every k < l, elementary interchangers χ : L ⇛ R for (l+1)-cells with no reverse cell in free objects; one naturality cell per varying argument, oriented by the Leibniz sign; higher cells between routes of lower naturality cells; and coherence equations to be determined. The free objects F(Γ) must satisfy:
1. an explicit combinatorial description by generators with explicit boundaries;
2. two cells of the same dimension using the same generators, in the expanded basis, are equal;
3. every elementary interchanger exists and its reverse does not;
4. making all interchangers with k ≥ 1 identities gives the free Gray-enriched category, and making all interchangers identities gives the free strict ω-category;
5. closure under substitution of cells for generators.

## 1. Q4: the isolated polygon has injective support

Assume the polygon’s arms are nonempty, their interiors are pairwise disjoint, and there are no additional graph arrows. Write

\[
p_i:s_i\to t_i,\qquad q_i:s_{i+1}\to t_i
\]

with source indices modulo \(r\). Add

\[
h_i:s_0\to s_i\qquad(1\le i<r)
\]

and impose

\[
p_0=h_1;q_0,
\qquad
h_i;p_i=h_{i+1};q_i
\quad(1\le i<r-1).
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(Q\) be this graph, including the added \(h_i\), and let \(C\) be the presented category.

### 1.1 The entire congruence can be described

Every defining relation in (1) starts at \(s_0\), which has no incoming arrow, and ends at a sink \(t_i\), which has no outgoing arrow. Consequently, **no defining relation admits a nonidentity left or right composition context**.

Moreover, different defining relations have different targets. Thus the nonsingleton congruence classes are exactly

\[
\{p_0,h_1;q_0\},
\qquad
\{h_i;p_i,h_{i+1};q_i\}
\quad(1\le i<r-1).
\tag{2}
\]

Every other path is a singleton class.

To check that this lists all possible parallel paths, observe that a path starting at an arm interior must follow that arm. A path starting at \(s_i\ne s_0\) can follow \(p_i\) or \(q_{i-1}\), whose targets differ. The only ambiguities therefore concern paths from \(s_0\) to a sink:

| Endpoints | The two paths |
|---|---|
| \(s_0,t_0\) | \(p_0,\ h_1;q_0\) |
| \(s_0,t_i\), \(1\le i<r-1\) | \(h_i;p_i,\ h_{i+1};q_i\) |
| \(s_0,t_{r-1}\) | \(h_{r-1};p_{r-1},\ q_{r-1}\) |

It follows that \(C\) is thin except for

\[
C(s_0,t_{r-1})
=
\bigl\{
[h_{r-1};p_{r-1}],\ [q_{r-1}]
\bigr\},
\tag{3}
\]

whose two arrows remain distinct.

### 1.2 A normal form using the actual graph generators

Orient (1) from left to right. Define

\[
N(P)=
\begin{cases}
h_1;q_0,&P=p_0,\\
h_{i+1};q_i,&P=h_i;p_i,\quad 1\le i<r-1,\\
P,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag{4}
\]

Every reduction applies to a complete path. No right-hand side is a left-hand side. Hence reduction terminates in at most one step, and (2) proves that two paths represent the same arrow precisely when they have the same normal form.

Use the finite alphabet consisting of tagged vertices and edges of \(Q\). Set

\[
\operatorname{Supp}_N([P])
=
\operatorname{Vert}(N(P))
\;\sqcup\;
\operatorname{Edge}(N(P)).
\tag{5}
\]

For an identity, the support is its single vertex label.

This support is well-defined and injective. The graph \(Q\) is acyclic, and a nonempty directed path in an acyclic graph is determined by its edge set. Vertex labels distinguish identities.

Composition is computed by

\[
[P];[R]\longmapsto N\bigl(N(P);N(R)\bigr).
\tag{6}
\]

Thus the composite–composite equations cause no local normal-form obstruction. The support in (5) is taken **after normalization**; it is not the union of the raw edge sets of an arbitrary expression.

This construction uses the existing vertex and edge alphabet. It does not assign a separate support atom to each category arrow.

### 1.3 Even a union-preserving finite set invariant exists

There is a stronger algebraic statement, although its labels are incidence predicates rather than constituent generators.

Let \(\le\) be reachability in \(Q\). Choose an edge \(e_\star\) of \(p_{r-1}\); no defining relation uses it. Define

\[
\chi(f)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\text{a path representing \(f\) uses \(e_\star\)},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

This is well-defined by (2).

Take the alphabet

\[
\mathcal A
=
(\{\mathsf{up}\}\times V(Q))
\sqcup
(\{\mathsf{down}\}\times V(Q))
\sqcup\{\star\}.
\]

For \(f:x\to y\), put

\[
\begin{aligned}
S(f)={}&
\{(\mathsf{up},v):x\le v\}\\
&\cup\{(\mathsf{down},v):v\le y\}\\
&\cup
\begin{cases}
\{\star\},&\chi(f)=1,\\
\varnothing,&\chi(f)=0.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{S(f;g)=S(f)\cup S(g)}
\tag{8}
\]

for every defined composition, including identities. Indeed, for \(x\to y\to z\),

\[
\uparrow y\subseteq\uparrow x,\qquad
\downarrow y\subseteq\downarrow z,
\]

and the distinguished-edge predicate combines by disjunction.

The invariant is injective. Its two tagged principal sets recover the source and target. By (3), endpoints distinguish every arrow except the final pair, and \(\star\) distinguishes that pair.

Therefore \(2|V(Q)|+1\) labels suffice for a union-preserving injection.

**The distinction matters:** (5) is a normal-form support on actual graph vertices and generators; (7) is an incidence-based set invariant. The latter is not, by itself, a graded globular generating basis satisfying requirement 1.

### 1.4 What this establishes globally—and what it does not

The local theorem holds for every \(r\) and every positive choice of arm lengths. Its proof is independent of the ambient dimension. Thus the composite–composite equations in K13 do not provide a local reason that sink-joining must eventually replace source-joining.

The proof does not cover additional equations produced by overlapping tensor diagrams or substitutions.

For example, consider the acyclic graph with parallel arrows \(a,b:x\to y\) and \(c,d:y\to z\), and equations

\[
a;c=b;d,\qquad a;d=b;c.
\tag{9}
\]

There are two distinct \(x\to z\) classes:

\[
\{a;c,b;d\},
\qquad
\{a;d,b;c\}.
\]

Nevertheless, every union-preserving support identifies them. Writing \(A=S(a)\), and similarly for the other arrows, (9) implies

\[
A\cup C=B\cup D=A\cup B\cup C\cup D
\]

and

\[
A\cup D=B\cup C=A\cup B\cup C\cup D.
\]

This is a limitation of a prospective global **union-support** theorem, not a counterexample to your requirements: normalized contextual supports remain possible, and I have not realized (9) as forced equations in the tensor construction.

The polygon checker independently computes the congruence and verifies both supports on all 336 choices with \(r\in\{2,3,4\}\) and arm lengths in \(\{1,2\}\), plus an unequal-length example. It checks all 24,291 composable pairs in those examples, including identities.

## 4. What the top lift settles

The construction proves that the K14 hom-3 skeleton has a globular extension carrying the tensor top, provided additional hom-4 lifts are allowed. Therefore the ten sinks, or the incompatible targets of particular prescribed faces, cannot alone prove that new hom-3 data are necessary.

For example, the prescribed context \(bW_xc\) ends at the wrong-class sink \(\texttt{bapqrc}\). It cannot be postcomposed to \(T\). The construction instead uses a different hom-4 lift of its tensor label, namely \(A_{23,22}\), between existing paths with endpoints \(S,T\).

There remains a substantial distinction between:

\[
\text{an extension with a map }q\text{ to }\nu(K)
\]

and

\[
\text{an extension whose prescribed inner-interchange quotient is }\nu(K).
\]

Only the first is proved here. In particular, the new hom-4 lifts may require further factorization equations to identify their quotient images with those of existing lifts. This certificate does not supply those equations or the complete hom-4 variation and disjoint-operation comparisons requested in Q1.

Because no hom-3 data change, the hom-3 support and directedness results of K14 remain intact. A complete hom-4 support theorem is still missing.

## 5. Q3 and Q5: the remaining closure issue

The polygon theorem gives termination and confluence for the **isolated** equations (1): at most one reduction is possible. It does not prove termination or order independence when the rule is applied to overlapping comparison graphs and their substitutions.

Likewise, requirement 4 gives the K15 equation after quotienting. If \(L\) is crossing against a composite and \(R\) is the pairwise-crossing composite, then

\[
q(L)=q(R).
\]

Requirement 5 propagates this to substituted instances:

\[
q(L[\sigma])=q(R[\sigma]).
\]

This does not establish

\[
L[\sigma]=R[\sigma]
\]

before quotienting. Such a derivation needs an additional faithfulness, normal-form, or direct coherence argument. Conversely, this gap is not an independence proof. I have not constructed an all-dimensional model satisfying requirements 4–5 while violating K15.

Thus the proved progress is:

- **Q4 locally:** the source-only polygon equations have explicit normal forms and injective finite supports for arbitrary \(r\) and arm lengths.
- **Q2 at the level of typed extensions:** the fixed K14 hom-3 skeleton supports the displayed top-cell lift using 22 fresh higher generators.
- **Still unresolved:** complete hom-4 coherence and support, the exact Gray quotient of this extension, substitution-compatible saturation, and the uniform theory or impossibility result of Q6.
