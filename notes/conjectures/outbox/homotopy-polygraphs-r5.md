# Which polygraphs are homotopy polygraphs? Recursive criteria, oracle strength and decidable classes in every dimension

*A self-contained problem. It assumes familiarity with polygraphs (computads), strict ω-categories and (∞,ω)-categories, and nothing else. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked, except item 17, which is marked as evidence.*

**What we are asking for.** Homotopicality of finite polygraphs is now known to be undecidable, already on positive bubble-free 3-polygraphs (item 23), so no finite generator-level test exists. An exact criterion from strict cells is known in every dimension (item 19), and an exact finite last-step criterion in dimension 3 (item 20); the naive extension of the last-step criterion to dimension 4 is false (item 24). We now ask for the **correct exact last-step criterion in every dimension**, for the **exact oracle strength** of the problem, and for **large decidable classes** with proofs uniform in the dimension. Isolated low-dimensional examples are of little use to us.

## 1. Setting

- A **polygraph** Γ (computad) is a set of generators of each dimension, each n-generator having a source and a target which are parallel (n−1)-cells of the free strict ω-category on the lower generators. All polygraphs here are finite.
- `F(Γ)` is the free strict ω-category on Γ.
- `Free(Γ)` is the free (∞,ω)-category on Γ: generators are attached one at a time along their boundaries by pushouts of `∂Dⁿ → Dⁿ` in (∞,ω)-categories (Loubaton's Cat_ω, arXiv 2406.05425, or Campion's framework, arXiv 2311.00200). Strict ω-categories are (∞,ω)-categories, so there is a canonical comparison `c_Γ : Free(Γ) → F(Γ)`.
- Γ is a **homotopy polygraph** (or *homotopical*) when c_Γ is an equivalence, i.e. when every strict pushout attaching a generator is also a homotopy pushout.
- A 2-cell `1_x ⇒ 1_x` of F(Γ) other than the identity is a **bubble**.
- Γ is **positive in every dimension** when every generator of dimension n ≥ 2 has a source and a target that are not identity cells. (For n = 2: both boundary paths are nonempty.)
- More generally, a non-identity n-cell `e : 1^{n−1−k}_c ⇒ 1^{n−1−k}_c` (n ≥ 2, source and target both the (n−1)-dimensional iterated identity of a k-cell c, k ≤ n−2) is an **iterated-identity endo-cell**. Γ is **bubble-free in every dimension** when F(Γ) has none. For n = 2, k = 0 these are the bubbles.
- A strict ω-functor p : C → D is a **discrete Conduché functor** when every factorisation of p(x) in D lifts uniquely to a factorisation of x in C.

## 2. Why it matters

Homotopy polygraphs are exactly the computads whose free (∞,ω)-category can be computed strictly. Finite presentations of Grothendieck constructions over a free base, the comparison of weak and strict free objects in type theories for weak ω-categories, and pasting theorems for non-globular shapes all need this hypothesis, and at present each use checks it by hand. Dimension 2 is settled exactly (item 5 below), necessity of bubble-freeness is settled in every dimension (item 14), and bubble-freeness alone is not sufficient from dimension 3 (item 18).

## 3. What is known

1. **Pasting theorems.** For a strongly loop-free Steiner complex K, ν(K) is the free (∞,ω)-category on its atoms (Campion, *Pasting diagrams in higher categories are free*, arXiv 2311.00200). Chanavat (*A strengthened (∞,n)-categorical pasting theorem*, arXiv 2603.22242) extends this to regular directed complexes with frame-acyclic molecules (Thm 3.17), and deduces that positive 2-polygraphs (Cor. 3.39) and regular 3-polygraphs in Henry's sense (Cor. 3.38) are homotopical. Comment 3.40 there describes the extension to arbitrary regular and positive polygraphs as expected, without proof.
2. **Bubbles obstruct.** If F(Γ) has a bubble q, sending every 2-generator to 1 gives r : F(Γ) → B²ℕ with r(q) = k > 0, and q defines i : B²ℕ → F(Γ) by Eckmann–Hilton; ri = [k] acts by kʲ on H_{2j}(K(ℤ,2); ℚ), so the groupoidification of F(Γ) has nonzero rational homology in every even degree, while that of Free(Γ) is a finite CW complex. Example: (x; σ : 1_x ⇒ 1_x), F(Γ) = B²ℕ ≃ K(ℤ,2) after groupoidification, Free(Γ) ≃ S².
3. **Bubble-freeness is undecidable**, already for one-vertex 2-polygraphs with no generating bubble (reduction from reachability in positive semi-Thue systems via an insertion rule 1 ⇒ L w Z and a deletion rule L v Z ⇒ 1).
4. **Dimension 2, combinatorial core.** For a progressive one-colour string diagram G every component of which meets the boundary, the cell complex K_G whose vertices are the *tier encodings* of G (decompositions into layers of simultaneous generators) and whose faces are products of permutohedra (splitting a layer) has contractible components. Proof: right-exchange runs (which terminate for boundary-connected diagrams, Delpeuch–Vicary arXiv 1804.07832, Cor. 2.16) give a unique maximal cell F(v) with source v; deleting vertices in topological order removes a closed star along its antistar, a ball. Two floating scalars give a circle, so the boundary condition is needed.
5. **Dimension 2, exact answer.** A finite 2-polygraph is homotopical **iff it is bubble-free**. The "if" direction uses item 4 and Heidemann's mesh model of (∞,2)-categories (arXiv 2411.04870): a presheaf X_Γ on meshes with L₂ j*X_Γ ≃ Free(Γ), and an objectwise equivalence j*X_Γ → N₂F(Γ) computed as X_Γ(Q) ≃ B(Q↓t) ≃ ⊔_G BP_G over the diagrams G of shape Q, with each BP_G contractible by item 4 (a bubble-free polygraph has no boundary-disjoint diagram component and no diagram automorphisms). Consequences: homotopicality of finite 2-polygraphs is undecidable (item 3); the finite case of Guetta's conjecture (homologically coherent ⟺ bubble-free, *Homology of strict ω-categories*, arXiv 2104.12662, Conj. 6.6.5) holds; every 2-polygraph whose 2-generators all have non-empty target (or all non-empty source) is homotopical, with any number of vertices.
6. **Suspensions.** Σ^k Γ is homotopical iff Γ is (Hom_{ΣC}(0,1) ≃ C, and suspension preserves and detects the comparison). So for 2-polygraphs, Σ^k Γ is homotopical iff Γ is bubble-free.
7. **Graph assembly, every dimension.** For a finite directed graph Q (loops and parallel edges allowed) and finite polygraphs P_e on its edges, the polygraph B_Q(P_•) with vertices Q₀ and (r+1)-generators ⊔_e (P_e)_r (the free category on Q enriched in the F(P_e)) is homotopical iff every P_e is. In particular **adjoining a 1-generator preserves homotopicality in every dimension**.
8. **Discrete Conduché descent (Theorem A).** If p : Γ → Δ is a polygraph map with F(p) discrete Conduché and Δ homotopical, then Γ is homotopical. Surjectivity on generators alone is not enough ([1] → F(loop)).
9. **Typing into positive templates (Theorem B).** A polygraph map Γ → Δ into a homotopical Δ that is source-positive or target-positive in every dimension makes Γ homotopical (Henry's cartesianness for such polygraphs makes F(p) discrete Conduché).
10. **Mapping cylinders (Theorem C).** Strict double mapping cylinders of finite homotopical presentations along arbitrary strict functors have finite homotopical presentations, with generators Δ_n ⊔ ℰ_n ⊔ Γ_{n−1}; Gray cylinders, cones and suspensions are instances (Loubaton arXiv 2406.05425 Thm 2.3.3.22, Campion's pasting theorem for Dⁿ ⊗ [1]). Items 7–10 give a sound finite-certificate system, but typing into templates from a good class cannot leave that class, so it does not reach all positive polygraphs.
11. **Positivity is not necessary.** (∗; t; η : 1 ⇒ t) is homotopical (Blans–Linskens, arXiv 2605.03150), and so is its suspension, a non-positive 3-polygraph. Regularity is not necessary either: the positive non-regular 3-polygraph x, y, z; p, q : x → y, r : y → z; α, β : p ⇒ q; ζ : α ▷ r ⇛ β ▷ r is homotopical.
12. **Positivity in the top dimension alone is not enough.** One vertex, b : 1 ⇒ 1, Ω : b ⇛ b: Ω has non-identity boundaries, but b is a bubble.
13. **Strict versus "stricter".** Chanavat (*Homotopy theory of stricter n-categories*, arXiv 2509.26563, Comment 2.67) gives a 4-dimensional regular directed complex whose molecules do not form a polygraph under strict composition. This is **not** a counterexample to the positive case: the extra equations appear in the passage from the free strict category to molecules, not in the free strict category itself.

14. **Higher bubbles obstruct in every dimension.** If a nonidentity n-cell is an endomorphism of an iterated identity on any k-cell, k ≤ n − 2, then the strict n-skeleton has nonzero mod-p cohomology in arbitrarily high degrees for some prime p. The free cellular n-skeleton has cohomological dimension at most n, and homotopicality passes to skeleta. This covers composite bubbles and positive-dimensional bases. (Proof: the inclusion of the n-skeleton is discrete Conduché, so homotopicality passes to it; counting n-generators gives r : F(Γ_{≤n}) → Bⁿℤ, the endo-cell q gives i : Σ^{n−2}B²ℕ → F(Γ_{≤n}) with r i of degree m = r(q) > 0 on the bottom class; for an odd prime p ∤ m the iterated reduced powers P^{p^{j−1}}⋯P^1 of the pulled-back fundamental class restrict to m σ^{n−2}(x^{p^j}) ≠ 0.) So bubble-freeness in every dimension is necessary.

15. **Groupoidification does not recognise homotopicality from dimension 3.** Π = (x; a : 1_x ⇒ 1_x; ε : a ⇛ 1_{1_x}) is not homotopical (its 2-skeleton has the bubble a, and homotopicality passes to skeleta), yet both groupoidifications are contractible. Free(Π) groupoidifies to S² ∪_{deg 1} D³. In F(Π) the hom on 1_{1_x} is the free commutative monoid in Cat on (a, ε : a → 0), whose morphisms reduce to ε^{⊗k} ⊗ 1_{a^n}, so it is the poset (ℕ, ≥) with terminal object 0. Π is also homologically coherent. Suspensions give such examples in every dimension ≥ 3.
16. **Positive-to-one polygraphs are homotopical.** If every d-generator (d ≥ 2) has a generating (d−1)-cell as target and a non-identity source, Γ is homotopical. The proof: Zawadowski (arXiv 0708.2658, Cor. 13.5) identifies such polygraphs with presheaves on positive opetopes, which are acyclic atoms forming a full sieve (Leclerc; Hadzihasanovic); then Chanavat's Cor. 3.27 applies (directed complexes with acyclic atoms generate homotopy polygraphs). The same holds after choosing, independently in each dimension, source or target as the generator side (globular opposites). This gives a decidable sufficient test.
17. **The combinatorial core in dimension 3 (evidence, not proved).** Following item 4 one dimension up, fix a 3-dimensional diagram G (a strict 3-cell of F(Γ) together with a representing composite). Its *3-tier sequences* (decompositions into layers of simultaneous 3-steps) are ordered by refinement; call the poset Π_G. When no 3-step has an idle boundary wire (every wire in the source region of a step is touched by it), Π_G appears to be the poset of braid-fan cells inside an open convex cone cut out by a partial order on the 3-steps, hence contractible: checked by computer on about 550 random diagrams of that kind. The step "two 3-steps that can each fire first can fire together" fails in general (item 18). The true fibre also contains *interchange slices* (2-dimensional exchanges between 3-steps), which make it an infinite category. Truncating at K interchange slices is not homotopy-correct (K = 1 already gives a wedge of circles).

18. **Bubble-free does not suffice in dimension 3.** Objects 0 → 1 → 2 → 3, edges f₀, f₁ : 0 → 1, g : 1 → 2, h₀, h₁ : 2 → 3, 2-generators α₀, α₁ : f₀ ⇒ f₁, β₀, β₁ : h₀ ⇒ h₁, 3-generators A : α₀g ⇛ α₁g and B : gβ₀ ⇛ gβ₁. Γ is positive in every dimension, has an acyclic 1-graph and is bubble-free in every dimension (occurrence counts), but the fibre of Free(Γ) over the unique strict 3-cell α₀gβ₀ ⇛ α₁gβ₁ is S¹: the hom from 0 to 3 is (D × Q) ⊔_{Q×Q} (Q × D), with Q the walking parallel pair and D the walking 2-cell. In item 17's terms, A and B overlap on the idle wire g, so each can fire first but they cannot fire together, and the two serial orders are joined by two different exchanges. Further known facts for positive 3-polygraphs (all generating faces non-identity):
    - If no idle right suffix of a generating 3-face equals an idle left prefix of a generating 3-face, every fibre is contractible, so Γ is homotopical. For width-preserving generators this matching test is exact.
    - Every weight-two fibre is a point, an interval or a circle, and a circle occurs exactly when some path is an idle suffix of **both** faces of one generator and an idle prefix of **both** faces of another.
    - For every k ≥ 2 there is a positive 3-polygraph with a full-support fibre ≃ S^{k−1} ("seam rings") whose weight-two fibres are all contractible, so no bounded-weight test is exact.

19. **An exact criterion from strict cells, every dimension.** For n ≥ 3 let P be the set-valued rectangular n-fold array NF(Γ_{<n}) ⊔_{∐_z N∂Dⁿ} ∐_z NDⁿ (z ranging over the n-generators). Iterating the necklace formula of Barkan–Steinebrunner (arXiv 2301.08650, Thm A and Prop. 2.16) one direction at a time, with Thomason's theorem, gives ordinary categories A(c) over each new strict n-cell c whose classifying spaces are the fibres of Free(Γ) → F(Γ). So Γ is homotopical iff it is bubble-free in every dimension and every A(c) has connected, simply connected and acyclic nerve (a finite edge-word and integral-cycle test per challenge). This puts homotopicality in Π⁰₂. It is decidable with the jump of a bubble-freeness oracle; whether the bubble-freeness oracle alone suffices is open.

20. **A finite last-step criterion in dimension 3.** For a bubble-free 3-polygraph, introduce a marker h_z parallel to each 3-generator z. A chart is a strict 2-cell Q over the 2-skeleton and the markers; [Q] performs all marked changes. For a strict 3-cell c of positive weight, L(c) has one simplex with vertex set I for each strict factorization c = [Q] ∘ p with slot set I ≠ ∅, and its face on J ⊆ I performs I∖J first (appended to p) and keeps J. Then **a finite 3-polygraph is homotopical iff it is bubble-free in every dimension and every L(c) is contractible** (connected, trivial edge-path group, zero reduced integral homology). Each L(c) is finite and effectively computable, non-positive generators are allowed, and the proof is Quillen's Theorem A applied to "last chart and prefix" from the category of chart strings, by induction on weight. Examples: the middle wire of item 18 has L(c) a circle; the two- and three-seam rings give a square boundary and an octahedron boundary; a repeated deletion a a → 1 has two distinct prefixes joined by an edge (so the prefix must be kept).

21. **A sufficient condition for positive polygraphs, every dimension.** For a positive presentation of dimension n = d + 1 ≥ 3, form the d-skeleton with one parallel d-marker per top generator; its slots are canonical by Henry's representation theorem (arXiv 1711.00744). If the marked lower presentation passes recursively and, for every positive-weight strict c, the one-slot final charts with their prefixes are the vertices of a single simplex of final choices (every nonempty subset occurs for exactly one (p, Q)), then Γ is homotopical; more generally it suffices that every L(c) is contractible. This recovers item 16 (positive-to-one) and the no-matching case of item 18.

22. **No bounded test.** A uniform weight bound on the strict cells to test would contradict the seam rings of item 18, so any finite criterion must be at the level of generators (or of some finite structure other than weight-bounded cells).

23. **Undecidability, even with positivity promised (2026-11-20).** Homotopicality is undecidable (co-r.e.-complete) on finite positive 3-polygraphs with five 3-generators, each occurring at most once in any strict 3-cell, and on finite positive n-polygraphs for each n ≥ 3 (suspension, item 6). Reduction: take the two-seam ring S of item 18 (lanes s₀, p₁, s₁, p₂, s₂; changes a₁, b₁, a₂, b₂, z; one full-support label with circle fibre, all proper-support labels contractible) and insert a *passive strip* between p₁ and s₁ with edges Q, U and a tape on a private vertex m, with ordinary 2-gates Q ⇒ l w r, the rules x ⇒ y of a positive semi-Thue system T, and l v r ⇒ U; insert 1_U on both faces of b₁ and 1_Q on both faces of z. An *erasure lemma* identifies every chart-string complex of the result G(T,w,v) with that of its image in S, so **G(T,w,v) is homotopical iff w ↛_T* v**. Every strict 3-cell has top weight ≤ 5, so the unbounded information sits in the lower diagram. Consequences: no finite generator-level test (Question 1 of the previous round is answered negatively); no sound and complete r.e. system of finite certificates of homotopicality, even on positive inputs; the general problem is 0′-hard and (item 19) at most 0″. On this family one query to a bubble-freeness oracle on another presentation suffices, so the oracle question is still open in general.
24. **The naive last-step criterion fails in dimension 4 (2026-11-20).** The positive 4-polygraph with the 2-skeleton of item 18, 3-generators A₀, A₁ : α₀ ⇛ α₁ and B : gβ₀ ⇛ gβ₁, and one 4-generator Z : A₀g ⇛ A₁g has homotopical lower skeleta (no-matching test of item 18) and every L(c) a single vertex (every nonidentity strict 4-cell has weight one), yet one fibre is S¹. The circle lives *inside one marked step*: the marked 3-presentation M₁ (old generators plus a marker C₁ : α₀g ⇛ α₁g parallel to the boundary of Z) contains the middle wire of item 18 (C₁ and B overlap on the idle wire g). So the recursive hypothesis of item 21 (the marked lower presentations must themselves pass) has content, and Question 3 of the previous round is false as stated. Checked: `tools/homotopy_polygraph_guarded_ring.py` (96 chart strings, Betti (1,1), for both tape orders; 97 proper strings, Betti (1)) and `tools/homotopy_polygraph_unary_chart.py` (17 declarations, a full-support 4-term, Agda certificate type-checked).

## 4. Questions

**Question 1 (main: the exact recursive criterion in every dimension).** Formulate and prove an exact criterion of last-step type in every dimension, for all finite bubble-free polygraphs, positive or not. Item 24 shows that it must also test the *marked lower presentations* M_k (the d-skeleton with k parallel markers per top generator, for every k), and item 21 does this for positive presentations as a sufficient condition. The natural candidate: Γ is homotopical iff every marked lower presentation M_k is homotopical (recursively, with markers in every dimension) and every L(c) is contractible. Prove it, or find the correct statement. The obstacle in the non-positive case is canonicity of slots without Henry's representation theorem.

**Question 2 (oracle strength).** Item 23 gives 0′-hardness and item 19 gives a 0″ upper bound. Is homotopicality of finite polygraphs decidable relative to the halting problem (equivalently, is it Π⁰₁ or Δ⁰₂, or Σ⁰₂/Π⁰₂-complete)? A natural route: show that bubble-freeness of the input together with a bubble-freeness or reachability oracle on finitely many auxiliary presentations (for example the marked lower presentations) decides homotopicality, or encode a Π⁰₂-complete problem (for example totality) into homotopicality.

**Question 3 (decidable classes, every dimension).** Find large classes of finite polygraphs, defined at the level of generators and closed under suspension, on which homotopicality is decidable, ideally with an exact generator-level test on the class: for example polygraphs whose lower skeleton has a terminating and confluent (or finite-derivation-type) rewriting system, polygraphs with a finite strict category of lower cells, regular or loop-free polygraphs, or polygraphs whose top generators do not overlap on idle subdiagrams in any dimension (a "no seam ring" condition). Item 23 shows the class must control the lower diagrams, not only the top weight.

## 5. What counts as a solution

A proof of Question 1 (or a counterexample to the candidate with a proof), an exact placement in the arithmetic hierarchy, a decidable class with a uniform proof in every dimension, or a published reference.

# Researcher Response 1

## A last-step criterion with marked comparison fibres

A last step has two pieces of data. Its **strict chart** says which changes occur at each time. Its **coefficient** records the ways the lower-dimensional diagram realizes that chart. The face maps between coefficients matter. Keeping them gives an exact criterion, including when a change has an identity face. The time indices belong to a simplex and are ordered even when the occurrences of a generator admit permutations.

Fix a rank $n=d+1$. Suppose first that the ordinary $d$-skeleton is homotopical and put $B=F(Γ_{≤d})$. Write $M_a$ for its presentation extended by $h_{z,i}$ for each $n$-generator $z$ and $1≤i≤a$. The marker is a $d$-generator parallel to the two $d$-faces of $z$. In particular $F(M_0)=B$. Fix the lower poles of the $d$-cells under consideration. A **time simplex** $σ$ of length $a$ is a strict $d$-cell of $F(M_a)$ at these poles. A marker with index $i$ changes on the edge from $i-1$ to $i$ of $[a]$. On restriction along an ordinal map, pull back the cut at $i$. If the pullback is constant, substitute the corresponding face of $z$. Otherwise give the marker its new change index. This defines actual strict substitutions, including when a substituted face is composite. Write $[σ]$ for the strict $n$-cell from vertex zero to vertex $a$: substitute the $n$-cell $z$ at each change and identities on the ordinary generators, and compose. Interchange makes this evaluation independent of the expression for $σ$ and compatible with subdivision in time.

Call $σ$ **normal** if every edge of $[a]$ has at least one change. The weight of $[σ]$ is the total number of marker occurrences, counted with multiplicity. Deleting a vertex merges the two adjacent lists of changes, or discards an exterior list at an exterior vertex. Thus every positive-dimensional injective face of a normal simplex is normal.

For a positive-weight strict $n$-cell $c$, define the ordinary category $D(c)$ as follows.

* An object is $(p,σ)$ with $σ$ nonempty and normal and $c=[σ]∘p$. The prefix $p$ is a **strict cell**, with its source fixed by $c$.
* Out of $(p,σ)$ of length $a$, take every list $j=i_0<i_1<⋯<i_b=a$, with $b≥1$. The new last simplex is the restriction to the list. The new prefix is $[σ|_{[0,j]}]∘p$. The arrow remembers the list. Composition composes the injections.

To specify the coefficient $E(σ)$ from strict data, take the rectangular array
$$
 P_a=NB\amalg_{\coprod_{z,i}N\partial D^d}\coprod_{z,i}ND^d
 \longrightarrow R_a=NF(M_a).
$$
Use the horizontal $d$-direction necklace recursion of item 19 on this map and take the category over $σ$ at $(1,\ldots,1)$. Its classifying space is $E(σ)$. Old labels use the terminal category. This construction also gives the restriction maps $E(σ)→E(σ|_{i_0,\ldots,i_b})$: apply the indicated substitution on the arrays, using $NB$ for a substituted face. In particular one need not decide that $M_a$ is homotopical to construct a coefficient. A finite version of this recursion is given below. Define
$$
             Λ(c)=\operatorname {hocolim}_{(p,σ)\in D(c)} E(σ).
$$

**Theorem.** For a finite bubble-free polygraph, the exact condition is that $Λ(c)$ be contractible for every positive-weight strict cell in every rank $n≥3$. The coefficient recursion uses only ranks below $n$. Both the incidence category and a model of the space tested at any *specified* cell are finite and effective. There is no bound here on the cells of a presentation that must be tested. On arbitrary finite input include bubble-freeness as in items 5 and 14.

**Identification of the coefficients.** Use the rectangular attachment of item 19. Its slice at final index $a$ is precisely $P_a$. Indeed a map $[a]→[1]$ is either constant at one of the two $d$-faces or changes at a single cut. For each cut the horizontal globe has just its boundary identified with the old part. Boundary inclusions are entrywise monomorphisms, so the set pushout also computes the presheaf homotopy pushout. The first $d$ Segal reflections act horizontally at each $a$. Since the base skeleton is homotopical, this is the comparison for adjoining the $d$-markers to that base. At fixed poles its value is a disjoint union over the strict labels $σ$ of the spaces $E(σ)$. At time zero the vertices are the discrete strict cells of $B$. This uses the same globularity and completeness check as item 19: zero-index rectangles are old and the strict free target has only identity equivalences, whose old fibres are points. The successive-reflection and necklace formulas used here are [Barkan–Steinebrunner, Theorem A and Proposition 2.16](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.08650).

Omitting a time colour unused by a label preserves its whole horizontal fibre. In a strict composite the number of occurrences of that colour is a sum of nonnegative counts. Thus all rectangles in any horizontal necklace over that label are already over the smaller set of markers. On raw lifts this is an identification, and taking decorations and necklace restrictions preserves it inductively. In particular inserting an idle time edge acts by an equivalence on coefficients. A wholly idle bead has the unique old coefficient.

**From necklaces to last steps.** Let $H(c)$ consist of strings of nonempty normal time simplices with matching vertices and composite $c$. Use the empty string for an identity. In the direction of restriction an arrow deletes some interior vertices of beads and promotes some of the retained interior vertices to joints. It keeps all existing joints and records all incidences. Put $U(x)=\prod_{σ\in x}E(σ)$; a split uses restriction to both new beads. The actual comparison fibre $X(c)$ is
$$
                X(c)\simeq\operatorname {hocolim}_{H(c)}U .
$$
Here are details of the normalization in this formula. Apply the final-direction necklace formula, decomposing each bead value by its strict label. Matching at joints is a product over *discrete* vertex labels. Taking the homotopy colimit first over the labels and then over necklaces is the homotopy colimit over labelled necklaces. Erase every idle edge, quotienting its two vertices together, and omit point beads. If a necklace map crosses an interval of weight zero in its target, it crosses only zero-weight edges there. More explicitly, the weight of each pulled-back edge is the sum of the weights of the target edges it spans. The quotient map therefore descends on both sides and defines a functor to normal necklaces. The quotient maps give a natural transformation from the included normalization to the identity in the restriction direction. Its maps on coefficients are equivalences by the unused-colour argument. Normalization fixes the normal subcategory, so the two homotopy colimits agree. Between normal necklaces a map cannot collapse an edge. Its ordered vertex injection must visit every joint of the target (no simplex straddles a joint), and those joints must be visited at joints of the source. This is exactly the face description of $H(c)$ in the restriction direction.

Send a string to its last simplex and the strict composite of the preceding beads. This gives $f:H(c)→D(c)$ and projection to the last coefficient gives $U→f^*E$. Compute its left Kan extension in spaces. At $l=(p,σ)$ the indexing category is $(f↓l)$. An object specifies a string and a final injection with first vertex $j$. In its last bead retain the **entire** initial segment up to $j$ as one bead if $j>0$, split there, and in the suffix keep exactly the selected vertices. Leave all earlier beads in place. This is the least face ending with the prescribed incidence: any further face realizing that incidence must retain the split at $j$ and has just the indicated final bead. Its prefix refinements therefore factor uniquely through this face. This is uniqueness of ordered vertex maps and holds even when two faces have equal strict labels. The operation is left adjoint to the inclusion of strings over $l$ with identity incidence. That inclusion is final. Its category is $H(p)$, by appending $σ$, and on it the coefficient is $U(\text{prefix})×E(σ)$. Products with a fixed space preserve colimits. The pointwise comparison from the left Kan extension to $E$ is thus the projection
$$
                   X(p)×E(σ)\longrightarrow E(σ).
$$
The prefix has strictly smaller weight. Consequently $X(c)\simeq Λ(c)$ whenever all smaller-weight fibres are contractible. The weight-zero fibre is a point. If the comparison is an equivalence, every prefix fibre is contractible and all the tests follow. Conversely the tests give all the fibre equivalences by induction on weight. Induction on rank now proves the theorem: the necessity for the base skeleton is item 14's skeletal descent, dimension two is item 5, and item 19 identifies the remaining all-ones fibres as the precise comparisons to check. This argument explains why replacing each marked coefficient by a point requires a separate hypothesis.

## Finite computation at a specified cell

Two finiteness details are needed. Finiteness of the generating sets alone does not bound the length of a factorization containing identity factors.

**Enumeration in the strict category.** Given a strict $(s-1)$-source $u$ and $r$, the strict $s$-cells on $u$ with at most $r$ occurrences of $s$-generators form an effective finite list. For $s=1$ enumerate paths of bounded length. Induct on $s$. Serialize a composite as a sequence of elementary changes, each a single generator in a lower context. For a change $z$ on $u$, enumerate $(s-1)$-cells with one new marker parallel to the faces of $z$. Their $(s-2)$-source is that of $u$, and they have at most $|u|_{s-1}+1$ top occurrences: every ordinary occurrence survives substitution of the source of $z$. Keep the one-marker cells whose source substitution actually gives $u$. Substitute the target to continue at the next source, to depth at most $r$. This enumerates all possibilities because one-hole diagrams give precisely the elementary changes (serialization follows by interchange and whiskering). Deduplicate by the word problem for free strict categories on computads. Likewise, all factorizations of a specified $s$-cell into a prescribed number of factors along cut $k<s$ are effective and finite. For $k=s-1$ enumerate consecutive factors on successive sources with top count bounded by that of the given cell. For $k<s-1$, first factor its $(s-1)$-source at cut $k$ by induction, and enumerate the $s$-factors on those prescribed sources with the same count bound. Test composability and equality of the composite. These arguments permit empty generating boundaries. For the top-extension presentation and decidability of strict equality see [Ara et al., Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00429) and [Makkai, *The word problem for computads*](https://math.mcgill.ca/makkai/WordProblem/WordProblemCombined.pdf).

For $D(c)$ enumerate the strict two-factor splittings of $c$ in top composition. For each suffix of weight $r'>0$, enumerate $1≤a≤r'$ and charts over $M_a$ from its specified starting vertex. The number of ordinary $d$-generators is bounded by the count of that starting vertex, since source substitution preserves them. There are exactly $r'$ markers. The preceding enumeration in rank $d$, followed by evaluation and equality tests, gives all the objects. The arrows are finite lists of injections.

**Finite horizontal fibres and their face maps.** The following algorithm applies to each $P_a→R_a$ above, treating the time direction as a parameter. It constructs finite categories $K_h(γ)$ over strict rectangular labels of shape $(1^h,\mathbf q)$ after reflection in the first $h$ directions. Their nerves realize the corresponding fibres. In this construction *old* means no marker in the rectangular label. Give old labels a singleton. This computes their fibre because all factorizations of old rectangles have zero marker count, and the entire old array is the strict nerve $NB$. At stage zero take the discrete set of raw lifts. At stage $h≥1$ use the following recursion.

1. Regard the strict row in direction $h$ as the nerve of an ordinary category. Enumerate factorizations of its arrow $γ$ into nonidentity arrows. For each factorization take all partitions of this edge list into consecutive nonempty beads. This gives the normal necklace category of the row, with the face incidences described above. Include the point necklace for an identity.
2. A bead of length $m$ has a single strict rectangle $δ$ of shape $(1^{h-1},m,\mathbf q)$ by strict Segal composition. Decorate it by $K_{h-1}(δ)$. Form the ordinary Grothendieck construction of the product of the bead categories over this incidence category. This is $K_h(γ)$. Bead ends are over old zero-index rectangles, whose fibres are singletons.

Here is a bound and the degeneracy argument which justify these instructions. A row edge is a rectangular strict diagram with unit length in the $h$-direction. Its globular slots can be read recursively by the enriched nerve: a diagram of outer length $m$ is a list of $m$ diagrams in hom categories with their endpoint objects. Iterate this description, retaining also the endpoint objects at each level. For each globular slot of rank $s≥h$ add the number of $s$-generators in that slot. In a row composite the slots of these ranks, at each fixed choice of the other indices, compose along cut $h-1$. The sum is therefore additive. Zero means an identity row edge: every $h$-slot is an identity on its source, and inductively each higher slot is an identity on the already specified identity face. Conversely the row degeneracy gives just these slots. This bounds the number of nonidentity factors. For each permitted length, enumerate the factorizations of the individual slots along this cut by the preceding strict enumeration, matching common faces. Slots below $h$ are fixed at the cut. This gives precisely the finite list in step 1.

Maintain the following stronger invariant: duplicating a vertex in an unprocessed direction induces an **isomorphism of categories** on the fibres over the original and the resulting degenerate strict label. At stage zero an entry outside $NB$ in an attached globe is specified by one active cut in each direction (iterate the suspension description of the globe). A lift over a duplicated vertex cannot have its active cut on the resulting identity edge. It therefore descends uniquely. For the induction step let $j>h$ be the direction of the duplication. In any $h$-factorization of a $j$-degenerate label restrict to the duplicated $j$-edge. For every slot of rank $s≥j$, its composite has zero $s$-count there. Its $h$-factors have zero count there too, by additivity along cut $h-1$. Induction up the slots as above says that the restricted edge is a $j$-identity in *every* factor. Deleting the duplicated vertex and then reinserting it recovers the entire factorization by the strict Segal lists in direction $j$. This bijects the normal necklaces, preserves their incidences, and gives the coefficient isomorphisms by induction.

For clarity, this invariant supplies actual functors on finite models. On any restriction in a remaining direction apply the strict restriction to the bead and the previous-stage restriction functor to its decoration. Erase row edges that have become identities. The bead decoration descends along their quotient by the inverse of the preceding degeneracy isomorphism. A wholly idle bead descends to a vertex with singleton fibre. For a necklace map the induced map on quotient necklaces is the unique one commuting with the vertex quotients. Erasing in two stages produces the same quotient, and the descended decoration is unique, so compositions and identities are preserved. The same prescription applies to maps between panels in the time parameter, since they preserve old cells and directional identities. An unused time colour is handled by the support argument above. These rules start with literal maps of finite sets at stage zero.

The labelled necklace proof of normalization now applies in direction $h$ with this invariant in place of the unused-colour argument: collapsing an idle edge induces the stated isomorphism on its decoration. Hence the normalized homotopy colimit computes the directional reflection. Thomason's theorem identifies the result with the classifying space of the ordinary category in step 2. Induction proves the assertion about $K_d(σ)$ and its restriction maps. All categories in this calculation are finite and have no nonidentity oriented cycles. In the base incidence category a nonidentity arrow either deletes an interior bead vertex or promotes it to a joint, strictly decreasing the number of interior nonjoint vertices. A cycle in a Grothendieck construction over such a base would lie within one coefficient, where induction excludes it. The same reasoning applies over $D(c)$ using length of the last simplex. Thus the nerve of $\int_{D(c)} K_d(σ)$ has finitely many nondegenerate simplices and realizes $Λ(c)$. [Thomason, *Homotopy colimits in the category of small categories*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100055535)

In particular a halting oracle suffices for each specified test: compute reduced integral homology by integer matrices and connectedness by the edge graph. For a connected complex its finite edge-and-triangle presentation has trivial group exactly if proofs killing every generator eventually appear. Simple connectivity and acyclicity imply contractibility by Hurewicz and Whitehead. The quantifier over *all* strict cells remains. This gives no reduction of the general problem to a single halting oracle; the general bounds in items 19 and 23 remain $0'≤_T\mathrm{Homotopicality}≤_T0''$.

## A generator-recognizable class with a two-sided decision

First, finiteness of the strict cells through any given finite rank is decidable from the presentation. Having enumerated the finite set of $(s-1)$-cells, make the finite directed graph on them whose edges are the elementary $s$-changes. The one-marker enumeration above constructs it. A directed cycle can be iterated with strictly increasing $s$-count. In its absence every serial history is a path in a finite acyclic graph; enumerate all and quotient by strict equality. Start with the vertices in rank zero. This decides the finiteness condition and when it holds lists the actual cells. Such a presentation is bubble-free, since iterating a bubble would violate finiteness.

Here is one class for which an ordinary algorithm returns both yes and no. For each presentation with finite strict cells use the following finite preliminary computations at every rank $n≥3$. At rank three compute the complexes $L(c)$ of item 20. In higher rank compute the finite nerves for $Λ(c)$ above, using the strict arrays even when testing a presentation whose lower comparison has yet to pass. Accept a graph for testing immediately: connectedness and zero first Betti number decide contractibility, including parallel edges. Record a disconnected or empty complex as a negative test. For a connected complex of higher dimension form the usual finite presentation of the fundamental group, contracting a spanning tree. Use the following explicit, decidable restriction on the resulting presentations. Repeatedly eliminate a generator which occurs exactly once with exponent $±1$ in some cyclically reduced relator, solving that relation and substituting in the others. Freely and cyclically reduce, dropping empty relators. Allow every elimination choice (strictly fewer generators at each step). Require that some resulting presentation has explicit commutator relators, up to cyclic conjugation and inverse, for every pair of distinct remaining generators. Zero or one generator automatically qualifies. Spanning trees may likewise be tried exhaustively. This is a sufficient syntactic test for abelianness, defined by finite computations on the input; it requires no group oracle.

On presentations passing the restriction, the graph tests are decided directly, and the higher-dimensional tests have abelian fundamental group by the displayed presentation operations. Their abelianizations are their first integral homology groups. Hence connectedness and vanishing of reduced integral homology decide every test for contractibility. At the first failing rank report no; if every rank passes report yes. Computations made at a higher rank before a failing lower comparison have no bearing on this induction. This is a total recognition and decision algorithm on the stated class.

A further rank-recursive rule gives a structural test for diagrams with at most two top changes, in any dimension. Enumerate the strict cells if they are finite and check that their maximum top weight is at most two. For rank at least three, if the proper skeleton and the two presentations $M_1,M_2$ receive positive decisions by the class algorithm in lower rank, all the coefficients that can occur are contractible. Every last simplex has length at most two, and each proper incidence strictly shortens it. Hence every $D(c)$ is a finite graph, and the tree test on these graphs is necessary and sufficient. This rule admits the input regardless of how large its marked nerves are. Failure of an auxiliary membership or affirmative decision requirement only makes this particular rule inapplicable. The alternative tests remain available. All recursive calls have smaller rank.

There is a useful extension to graphs of presentations, including directed cycles. Take the closure of the preceding class under item 7's graph assembly. Membership still has a finite generator test. At a nonzero rank try every partition of the finite set of 1-generators into nonempty edge blocks having the same ordered endpoints within a block. Vertices of the proposed graph are the actual 0-generators. Assign each higher generator to a block, strip its outer suspension in that block, and require its attaching faces to be the resulting forward-hom faces. More explicitly, the vertices of a factor are the arrows in its block. After constructing its lower ranks, enumerate the preimages of each attaching face as follows. Write the face as an expression and use the free enriched path-category normal form: a strict cell is a path of graph edges decorated by one factor cell per edge, with identities on a vertex given by the empty path. Outer composition concatenates paths, and higher compositions act coordinatewise on equal paths. This computes the single factor cell when the path is the required singleton and rejects the assignment otherwise. For equal outer endpoints a singleton loop is still distinct from an empty path. Reassemble the factors; the induced generator bijection and the checked faces identify the original presentation. Empty factors contribute no generators and may be omitted. Test the factors recursively, with rank decreased by one. A presentation of rank zero belongs to the finite base class. This gives decidable membership, and item 7 makes the decision on an assembly the conjunction of the factor decisions. A suspension is the one-edge assembly, so the class is suspension-closed. The algorithm also permits cyclic assemblies with infinitely many strict lower cells.

For concrete scope, the finite base class contains all finite-strict 3-polygraphs of top weight at most two: their $L(c)$ are graphs. The two-sided decision detects the circle in item 18. On the same lower skeleton, take the changes acting just on the left and right gates, $α_0⇛α_1$ and $β_0⇛β_1$. The no-matching criterion of item 18 gives a positive answer there. Both have finite strict categories (the object graph is a finite line and each gate and change can occur at most once per lane). Arbitrarily many copies, arbitrary graph assemblies of these factors and repeated suspensions are allowed. More generally the finite presentation test has no bound on top weight or dimension. For the larger class of **all** finite-strict presentations, the same enumeration and per-cell oracle test give a uniform $0'$ upper bound without the abelianness or graph restriction.

## The halting oracle suffices at bounded top weight, with unbounded lower diagrams

Let $G$ be a finite positive 3-polygraph. Given $R$, a halting oracle decides **simultaneously** whether every strict 3-cell of weight at most $R$ has contractible comparison fibre. There is no bound on the size of its source 2-diagram or on the number of strict labels tested by this statement. Consequently homotopicality on positive 3-polygraphs promised to have bounded top weight has Turing degree $0'$, even if the bound is not supplied. The same degree holds on their iterated suspensions. Item 23 supplies the lower bound. The upper bound requires uniform control of the topology as the lower diagram varies.

**Generic occurrence diagrams.** Write $P(v)$ for the positive polyplex of a strict cell $v$: a finite polygraph carrying a universal cell and a generator-preserving map to $G$. Work in $P(c)$ over a weight-$r$ cell $c$. Composition of generic diagrams glues them by pushout on their common face. Equal strict composites produce the same universal diagram up to unique isomorphism preserving its universal cell. In particular the $r$ occurrences of top generators in $P(c)$ give names independent of the serialization. Each carries a specified map of its generating plex, including the maps of its two planar faces. This uses positivity in the sense of the problem. [Henry, §§2.2.5–2.2.9, Corollary 2.3.12 and §2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.00744)

Let $S≥1$ bound the total number of generators of ranks zero through two in the generic planar diagram of either face of **one generating** 3-cell. It is computed from the finitely many generating faces. An intermediate 2-state $u$ of any serialization has a map $P(u)→P(c)$. Even when this map identifies wires, every generator of $P(c)$ has at most $1+rS$ preimages under it.

Here is a proof of the multiplicity bound. Look at one step, given by a one-hole planar context $Q$ and a generator $z:s→t$. Its generic diagram is obtained from the generic diagram of the incoming state $Q[s]$ by adjoining the generating plex at the indicated copy of $P(s)$, i.e. by pushout along $P(s)→P(z)$. Indeed, express the context by whiskerings and compositions with identity 3-cells on its ordinary pieces. In that expression the state and step are the same gluing diagram except at the marked piece, where $P(s)$ is replaced by $P(z)$. The gluing interfaces at that piece have dimension at most one and their maps into $P(z)$ factor through the specified face. Associativity of pushouts gives the asserted pushout, including for a composite context. Starting at the outgoing state gives the same description with $P(t)$. This argument permits noninjective attaching maps.

Starting at $P(u)$ adjoin each subsequent plex successively along its source, and then each preceding plex in reverse order along its target. Composition along the whole intervening state is a pushout too, so the resulting diagram is $P(c)$. On sets of generators these are set pushouts. Adjoining along a set of at most $S$ elements imposes at most $S$ identifications among previously distinct classes of old elements: the kernel of the map from the old set is generated by pairs of images of attaching elements with the same image on the new side. Thus the total decrease in the number of distinct classes of the elements of $P(u)$ is at most $rS$. Equivalently one can count all old-class merges at each step, including merges involving classes with no representative in $P(u)$. The stated bound on each preimage follows.

**Counting charts over one label.** Fix a prefix with current planar diagram $u$ and its map into $P(c)$. Specifying this map does not restrict the strings being counted. Initially it is the canonical source map. Given a chosen next occurrence and its incoming face map into $P(u)$, the pushout describing the step carries its map to $P(c)$ by the universal property. Restrict it to the outgoing state to continue. For a tier one may use any one fixed serialization of its named events. Thus every actual chart string has a record starting from the very same initial map.

To record a tier of $b$ changes choose the $b$ names, choose the maps of their planar **source** diagrams into $P(u)$ over their specified maps to $P(c)$, and record a total order of the $b$ markers in a spatial serialization of the joint planar chart. There are at most $(1+rS)^{bS}$ possible tuples of face maps, by the preimage estimate on each generator. The total order gives at most $b!$ choices. The next observation is why a long passive diagram contributes no further factor to this bound.

In a positive planar 2-diagram the generic 2-generators are its concrete gates and its generic 1-generators are its concrete wire segments between ports or the exterior boundary. This description follows by composing the lists of ordered ports and identifying the matching wires at a cut. A through wire of a generator-free part of an expanded face maps to a particular segment of the ambient diagram. Thus a specified tuple of source maps records every gate to be compressed and every wire port at its actual position, including the segment of every idle read. Reconstruct a candidate tier by contracting the indicated groups of gates to their respective markers. Along a segment on which markers merely read, insert their ports in the recorded common spatial order, between the two ends of that segment. Where a segment meets an active gate in a specified group, its end is already specified by that port. Internal wires of a contracted group disappear, exterior gates and their ports stay in place, and the left-to-right orders on the exterior paths are inherited from $u$. For data coming from a chart these operations recover its entire directed planar circuit with ordered ports. Any two serializations of such a circuit differ by exchanges of incomparable, disjoint gates, hence give the same strict chart. Positivity excludes a gate component floating away from the ordered boundary. This proves uniqueness for *realized records*; counting all the other records as well only increases the bound. In particular one may not just bound the number of embeddings of a face by the number of times a generator name occurs in $G$.

A string uses each of the $r$ names once. One permutation and a choice of cuts among its consecutive names specify its successive tiers and their internal spatial orders. The preceding record therefore gives at most
$$
                    r!\,2^{r-1}(1+rS)^{rS}
$$
chart strings for $c$ when $r>0$. Allowing multiple spatial serializations of a chart gives an overcount. Conversely every realization of an initial segment of a record determines the next incoming labelled diagram: expand the marked tier at its target, using the indicated occurrences to map the step into $P(c)$ as above. Thus the counting argument applies to whole strings, rather than merely to the number of choices at a single designated state. Face incidences are bounded too: each refinement partitions the at most $r$ distinct markers into ordered blocks in their own tiers. For example $r!2^r$ bounds the number of refinements out of any string. Items 18–20 identify the classifying space of this finite incidence category with the comparison fibre. They also give an effective construction at each specified cell. Consequently only **finitely many isomorphism types of incidence categories** occur at weights through $R$, with a size bound depending just on $R$ and $S$. This last uniformity is stronger than finiteness for each individual cell.

**Oracle algorithm.** Enumerate strict labels through weight $R$ by enumerating finite typed composites, compute their chart-string categories by item 20's finite enumeration, and canonicalize the finite incidence tables by exhaustive renaming. To decide whether another isomorphism type occurs outside an already collected finite list, ask the halting oracle whether that enumeration ever outputs a new type. On an affirmative answer run it until the new type appears. Finiteness proves termination of this loop, regardless of how many ordinary gates precede the first realization of a type. For each collected category the oracle decides contractibility by the finite integral-homology and edge-group test of Response 1. The 2-skeleton is homotopical by positivity. Under the bounded-weight promise, to obtain $R$ first ask for each successive $k$ whether enumeration ever finds a strict cell of weight greater than $k$. Stop on the first negative answer. All these queries are ordinary halting queries. Item 23 reduces the complement of reachability to this very promise with $R=5$. Suspension preserves and detects the comparison by item 6.

For fixed numerical $R,S$ there is even a nonuniform co-r.e. upper bound on the promised inputs: the finite universe of table types of the indicated sizes has a finite subset of noncontractible types. Hardcoding this subset and looking for one of its members semidecides failure. This does not compute that finite subset uniformly as $R,S$ vary. Since the five generating changes of the guarded ring have fixed-size faces independent of the contents and rules of the passive tape, a fixed pair of bounds already gives a co-r.e.-complete promise class.

## What an unrestricted lower bound would have to realize

There is an unconditional semidecision procedure for the following failures on **all** finite inputs. Dovetail the bubble search and the finite computations of Response 1 for every rank and strict label. Report failure if there is a bubble, a disconnected or empty test complex, nonzero reduced integral homology, or a nontrivial finite quotient of the edge group of a connected test complex. For the last test enumerate finite permutation representations of its finite presentation, requiring that at least one generator act nontrivially. Every finite quotient occurs this way by its regular action. All four conditions contradict the exact criterion. The single halting query asking whether this procedure ever reports failure decides their union.

If this search misses a bad input, that input is bubble-free and all of its test complexes are connected and integrally acyclic. At least one has **nontrivial superperfect fundamental group with no nontrivial finite quotient**. Indeed a simply connected acyclic finite complex is contractible. For a connected acyclic complex $K$, $H_1(π_1K)=H_1(K)=0$ and $H_2(K)→H_2(π_1K)$ is surjective: attach cells of dimensions at least three to form a classifying space for the group. Thus both group homology groups vanish. In particular, if each bad input has at least one detectable failure as above, the unrestricted problem is co-r.e.-complete by item 23. A reduction showing that $0'$ fails to suffice must escape this search on some negative instances. In the positive three-dimensional case it must also escape the bounded-weight promise on its range, by the preceding theorem. Neither the counting proof nor the finite-quotient search settles whether such an unrestricted reduction exists.

## The least failing weight can encode an entire halting time

There is no computable bound, even as a function of the **whole presentation**, on the least weight of a noncontractible fibre of a bad positive 3-polygraph. This is stronger than the absence of a constant bound in item 22. The passive-tape family of item 23 has constant witness weight. The following construction makes each machine instruction cost one unit of witness weight.

Start with the two-seam ring $S$ of item 23. On its internal signal lane $s_1$ keep the edges $q,u,v$, the gate $t:q⇒u$ and the gates $L,R:u⇒v$. Use two private parallel 1-edges $P,T$ on this lane. Add the following unary 2-gates:
$$
       l:u⇒P,\quad x:P⇒P,\quad h_j:P⇒T\ (j\text{ a control state}),
       \quad y:T⇒T,\quad e:T⇒v .
$$
The word $l x^a h_j y^b e$ represents two counters and a program counter. Give $b_1$ the signal change $L⇒l h_{\rm start} e$, and $a_2$ the signal change $l h_{\rm accept} e⇒R$. Keep their old seam actions. The other three ring changes are unchanged, including $z$'s read of $q$ on this lane. All machine changes are 3-generators acting on the internal signal lane alone. For increment on the left use $h_j⇒x h_k$; for increment on the right use $h_j⇒h_k y$. For a left zero-test/decrement use $l h_j⇒l h_k$ on the zero branch and $x h_j⇒h_i$ on the decrement branch. On the right use $h_j e⇒h_k e$ and $h_j y⇒h_i$. These are parallel 2-words. An unconditional jump consumes and replaces just the head. Initialize any fixed input by a program prefix and on halt clear both counters before entering a fresh accept state. The deterministic accepting run from the sealed start to the sealed accept word has length $t$ precisely when this compiled program accepts in $t$ transitions. Halting of such machines is r.e.-complete. [Dudenhefner, *Certified Decision Procedures for Two-Counter Machines*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSCD.2022.16) · [explicit many-one reduction from Turing machine halting](https://www.ps.uni-saarland.de/Publications/documents/Dudenhefner_2022_SemiUnification.pdf)

**All-label calculation.** On an arbitrary path the outer line visits this lane at most once. The gate types permit at most one head in its word: after reaching $T$ there is no way back to $P$. Every machine change consumes that head and produces the next. Thus a chosen serial representative fixes a chain of head instructions, including their order and their matches on the word. Its order is invariant under exchange, since two such matches cannot coexist in a single tier. A match for a given instruction, if present, is unique at the head, even when counters have repeated letters. If present, $b_1$ precedes all the instructions, and $a_2$ follows them. This also covers a representative that starts inside the tape or stops before accept. If **both** occur, the complete intervening word was supplied by $b_1$. Neither exterior context nor the other ring changes can modify its counters. In particular an arbitrary initial tape cannot fake a shorter computation between the two markers.

For each fixed representative use the sign sheets of the [two-seam calculation, including its all-label argument](../answered/relative-coherence-all-dimensions-r6-2026-11-20.md#full-support-spheres-with-arbitrarily-late-first-homotopy-group). On the internal signal substitute the actual chain consisting of the present start change, head instructions and finish change for the former precedence $b_1<a_2$. Each tier contains at most one member of this chain. Its marked subword and its surrounding ordinary tape are determined by the prefix of the chain executed so far. In a tier also containing $z$, its read of $q$ is before $t$, and the tape marker is after $t$. If $q$ is absent a representative involving $z$ is impossible there in the first place. If there is a $q$-read it is unique, since no gate outputs $q$. All machine-only markers have no ports on the other lanes. Hence these lane words always glue for an ordered partition satisfying the chain order and the original two outer signal orders: place $z$ first when present, respect the specified seam comparisons, then insert the ordinary unary gates between their consecutive markers on each lane. Conversely every chart restricts to precisely these words. Splitting a tier restricts its marked subwords on every lane. ## Cutting a strict history at a named occurrence

In a positive 3-polygraph choose a strict cell $c$ and one of its named top occurrences $e$. An occurrence is a top generator of the representing polyplex of $c$, so its name survives strict exchanges, as in Response 2. Consider all strict splittings
$$
                     c=s\circ[Q]\circ p
$$
in which the simultaneous chart $Q$ contains $e$. Give each such splitting a cube with one axis for each marker of $Q$ other than $e$. A face specifies disjoint sets of those markers to do before and after the middle chart. Append the first set to $p$, prepend the last set to $s$, and substitute the corresponding faces in the middle chart. Keep the incidence even when two faces have identical labels. Call the result $W_e(c)$.

If all smaller-weight comparison fibres are contractible, then the fibre over $c$ is homotopy equivalent to $W_e(c)$. Indeed, send a chart string to its unique bead containing $e$ and its strict prefix and suffix. This is a functor to the cubical face category in the refinement direction. An object of the comma category at a fixed face specifies the markers of its middle bead to put before, keep with $e$, and put after. Split into those three chronological blocks, omitting empty outside blocks. This is the least refinement with the prescribed face incidence. Every further refinement over that incidence factors through it uniquely, by restricting the ordered partitions of the original bead. Thus strings with the prescribed middle bead and identity incidence form a final subcategory. This subcategory is the product of the string categories for $p$ and $s$. Both have contractible classifying space by hypothesis, including the empty history at an identity. Quillen's Theorem A and item 20 give the assertion. The same proof works for directed subcomplexes of a cube with its coordinates as event names: use all strings between the endpoints if the strict component has yet to be identified.

This calculation isolates a difficulty with finding the unrestricted oracle in Question 2. In finite directed cubical diagrams the section at one event can be an arbitrary finite homotopy type, **with contractible comparison fibres at every proper endpoint pair**. The following construction proves this and gives a sharp complexity result for diagrams specified by lists of cubes.

## A cubical diagram with one prescribed fibre

Let $K$ be a nonempty finite connected simplicial complex with vertex set $V$, facet set $F$, and incidence relation $v\in f$. A state has coordinates $(I,J,b)$ where $I\subseteq V$ increases, $J\subseteq F$ decreases, and the switch $b$ increases from 0 to 1. All cubes are ordinary faces of this one Boolean cube. Use the following three cubical subcomplexes in the $(I,J)$ coordinates. In each take every face all of whose corners satisfy the indicated conditions.

* In $A$, $I$ must be a face of $K$ (the empty face is allowed), and $I=\varnothing$ requires $J=F$.
* In $B$, the facets in $J$ must have a common vertex (the empty family permits every vertex), and $J=\varnothing$ requires $I=V$.
* In $W$, both sets are nonempty and every vertex of $I$ lies in every facet of $J$.

Use $X(K)=(A\times\{0\})\cup(W\times[0,1])\cup(B\times\{1\})$. In particular a cube crossing the switch needs all its transverse corners in $W$. The extreme endpoints are $(\varnothing,F,0)$ and $(V,\varnothing,1)$.

**The two levels.** For comparable vertices within $A$, the full complex of cube chains is contractible. To see this, use the directed path model of cube chains. All vertex coordinates of any path lie in the simplex given by its final $I$. Reparameterize its increasing row coordinates at time $t$ by $\min((1+h)t,1)$, and its decreasing column coordinates by $\max(0,(1+h)t-h)$, for $0≤h≤1$. This stays in $A$: once any column coordinate has fallen below one, at least one row coordinate was already one and speeding up the rows preserves that condition. This is precisely the corner condition on the smallest face containing the point. At $h=1$ all rows finish in the first half and all columns move in the second. This subspace of paths is convex by coordinatewise interpolation and contains a path for each comparable pair. When both row sets are empty, the endpoints coincide. Time reversal and interchange of rows and columns prove the corresponding assertion for $B$ using the simplicial complex of facet families with a common vertex. The cube-chain theorem applies because these are finite Euclidean cubical complexes with the sum of completed coordinates as height, and each cube is determined by its extreme vertices. [Ziemiański, *Spaces of directed paths on pre-cubical sets*, Theorems 1.7–1.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08305)

**Across the switch.** Fix comparable endpoints $x=(I_0,J_0,0)$ and $y=(I_1,J_1,1)$. The possible middle cubes are exactly $q\times[0,1]$ for cubes $q$ of
$$
             W_{xy}=W\cap\{I_0\subseteq I\subseteq I_1,\quad
                                  J_1\subseteq J\subseteq J_0\}.
$$
Every such cube has a prefix from $x$ to its lower endpoint and a suffix to $y$, by the two level calculations. The comma category argument for the distinguished switch now gives an equivalence from the whole endpoint cube-chain complex to $|W_{xy}|$. In this application the prefix and suffix have unique strict labels whenever reachable, since their path spaces are contractible. If the section is empty there is no endpoint history.

If $I_0$ is nonempty and $W_{xy}$ is nonempty, shrink the row coordinates to $I_0$, then grow the facet coordinates to all members of $J_0$ containing $I_0$. These are straight-line deformations in the section. Throughout the first one the fixed vertices in $I_0$ stay present, and throughout the second at least one facet stays present. Every positive row coordinate is incident to every positive facet coordinate at every time, as required by the corner rule. This contracts the section. If $J_1$ is nonempty there is a dual contraction fixing $J_1$. The only remaining pair has $I_0=J_1=\varnothing$, which forces the two extreme endpoints.

For this pair the section is $W$. Cover it by the subcomplexes where a specified facet coordinate is one. They cover: every cube of $W$ keeps at least one facet coordinate equal to one even at its smallest facet set. For a nonempty set $D$ of prescribed facets, their intersection is nonempty precisely when the facets in $D$ share a vertex. In that case shrink the facet coordinates to $D$ and then grow the rows to all of its common vertices. This contracts the intersection. The nerve of the cover is also the nerve of the facet cover of $K$, whose nonempty intersections are simplices. The nerve lemma gives $|W|\simeq |K|$. It is connected, so the extreme histories have exactly one strict path label (serializations modulo square exchanges). Every other reachable pair has exactly one label with contractible fibre. In particular the conclusion concerns all labels, even those starting or stopping in the middle of a run.

## The exact complexity for cube lists and the finite-polygraph realization problem

For an explicit finite Euclidean cube list, the question whether all strict path labels have contractible cube-chain fibres is $\Sigma^0_1$-complete. For a program producing one finite list per natural input, the all-inputs question is $\Pi^0_2$-complete. The lower bound still holds when every program in the reduction is total, every fibre is integrally acyclic, and every fibre group has trivial profinite completion. Here is an effective construction enforcing the last two restrictions.

From a machine and input take a finite group presentation $G$ which presents the trivial group exactly if the machine halts. This is the machine-to-word and Adian–Rabin construction. For each generator of $G$ write $a_i,b_i,c_i$ for its images in the three factors of $G*G*G$. The words $a_i b_i$, $b_i c_i$, $a_i b_i c_i$ together normally generate the free product: imposing all three equal to one kills each of $a_i,b_i,c_i$. Each listed word is either one or has infinite order by cyclically reduced free-product normal form. This holds even if the original generator has finite order. [Nabutovsky–Weinberger, *Algorithmic unsolvability of the triviality problem for multidimensional knots*, p. 429](https://math.uchicago.edu/~shmuel/NW-knots.pdf)

Attach one copy of Higman's four-generator group per listed word, equating that word to the first standard generator. This generator has infinite order, normally generates its group, and the group has no nontrivial finite quotient. Thus a trivial attaching word collapses that copy, while an infinite-order attaching word makes an amalgamation along an infinite cyclic subgroup, embedding the preceding group. These cases justify the construction without having to decide which words vanish. The resulting finitely presented group $D$ is trivial exactly when $G$ is, and every finite quotient kills all Higman copies and then all the normal generators. In particular $D$ is perfect. Compute a finite presentation for its universal central extension $U$. It is superperfect and is trivial exactly when $D$ is. In any finite quotient of $U$, dividing by the central image of the kernel gives a finite quotient of $D$. The original quotient is therefore abelian and perfect, hence trivial. [Higman, *A finitely generated infinite simple group*](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-26.1.61) · [Bridson, *Decision problems and profinite completions of groups*, §§1–3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.0390)

Let $L$ be the finite presentation 2-complex of $U$. Compute a basis of the free abelian group $H_2(L;\mathbb Z)$. The Hurewicz map $π_2(L)→H_2(L)$ surjects by the Hopf exact sequence and $H_2(U)=0$. For each basis element, enumerate simplicial sphere maps into a finite triangulated model of $L$ until the induced class is that element. Simplicial approximation guarantees termination. Attach one 3-cell along each such sphere. The new boundary map onto $H_2(L)$ is an isomorphism on the new relative chains, and $H_1(L)=0$. Thus the result is connected and acyclic, with fundamental group $U$. The mapping cylinders and cones of these finite simplicial attaching maps give an effective finite polyhedral model, which can be subdivided to obtain $K$. By the simply connected Whitehead theorem $K$ is contractible exactly if the machine halts. This construction uses spheres in the ordinary 2-complex; no word-problem test in $U$ is needed.

Now output $X(K)$, which always finishes. The preceding all-endpoints calculation gives the one-input many-one reduction from halting. Letting the input vary gives the many-one reduction from totality. For the upper bounds enumerate the finitely many cube chains, their refinements and their connected components in each list, and use the integral-homology and finite edge-group certificate of Response 1. The affirmative test for one list is r.e. For programs, require for each input a finite computation producing a valid list and certificates for all its components. This is a $\Pi^0_2$ predicate.

**Why this does not yet transfer to Question 2.** The usual face computad of $X(K)$ gives a demonstrably different answer. Each of its generating cubes maps to the corresponding generating face of the full directed Gray cube. Its attaching faces are composites of subfaces, so this is a generator-preserving map of polygraphs. The full Gray cube is positive and homotopical by the strongly loop-free pasting theorem (item 1). Item 9 therefore makes the face computad homotopical for *every* $K$, including the Higman examples. The hard test above requires the cubes to describe simultaneous occurrences of top changes in a marked time row. Turning a cube into a globular generator imposes a genuinely different comparison. Even a realization for each single finite cube list would yield the one-input hardness result only. For the totality reduction one finite presentation for each machine must enforce all its input diagrams and must account for all other strict labels of that same presentation.

**Validation.** The [switch checker](../../../tools/homotopy_polygraph_cubical_switch.py) enumerates the full cube chains and all ordered-split boundaries directly from the state predicates. For a triangle boundary it checks all 951 reachable endpoint pairs: only the extreme pair has first Betti number one. The path and filled-triangle controls have respectively 334 and 142 reachable pairs with point homology throughout. A separate signed cubical boundary computation on the 1,561 cells of the switch section for a triangulated projective plane gives Betti numbers $(1,1,1)$ modulo two and $(1)$ modulo three. The arbitrary-complex fibre identification, the group restrictions, and the failure of the face-computad conversion are proved above. The oracle strength for finite polygraphs themselves remains unresolved.

# Researcher Response 4

## Which cubical corners can occur in positive dimension three?

The realization problem at the end of Response 3 has a local obstruction. Fix an ordinary planar 2-diagram $D$ in a positive 3-polygraph. A **signed germ** is a one-marker chart with a choice of face whose expansion gives $D$. A joint corner is a single marked chart with those particular signed unary faces. Two different joint charts count separately, even if their entire boundaries agree.

**Corner theorem.** Every finite pairwise joint family of distinct signed germs at $D$ has a joint corner. The set of its joint corners is described exactly by a satisfiable Boolean formula in conjunctive normal form with clauses of at most two literals. Its variables are the spatial orders of pairs sharing only identical idle gaps. Two corners are adjacent when they differ on just one variable. This graph is a median graph, isometrically embedded in the sign cube. Thus both the existence and the multiplicity of spatial charts have restrictions that do not hold for arbitrary cubical data.

Use the local lanes of [the positive three-dimensional link calculation](../answered/relative-coherence-all-dimensions-r6-2026-11-20.md#positive-3-computads-with-no-matching-idle-boundary-paths). Briefly, the width of a concrete wire is the number of boundary-to-boundary paths through it. Positivity makes this number positive and equates the two total widths at a gate. Gates repartition a consecutive interval of lanes. Exchanges of disjoint gates leave these intervals invariant. In the expansion of a signed germ, its box occupies one lane interval. On each lane of that interval it covers a consecutive block of gates, possibly just a gap. At least one block contains a gate, since the chosen generating face is nonidentity. The concrete gate and port positions are fixed by the planar diagram with its ordered boundary. Pairwise jointness implies that the different boxes use disjoint sets of gates. Retain those boxes and also every exterior gate as a separate box.

On each lane number the cuts in its gate list. Associate to a box on the lane the midpoint of its first and last cuts, equal to the cut itself for a gap. For any two intersecting boxes, their midpoints are weakly ordered in one direction throughout their overlap. For two germs this follows from a joint two-marker chart, for a germ and an exterior gate from the one-marker chart, and for two exterior gates from $D$. Blocks are disjoint in gate interiors. Consequently equal midpoints on a shared lane mean equal empty gaps. Orient a pair if its midpoints differ somewhere, in the direction of increase. This forces its order in every serialization.

Here is the point that allows arbitrary idle matches. **The forced comparisons are acyclic.** Suppose there is a shortest directed cycle. On every edge choose a shared lane witnessing strict increase, and choose the leftmost of these witnesses, say on $A\to B$. Write the adjacent edges as $P\to A$ and $B\to Q$. Their witness lanes both lie weakly to its right. If the witness for $P\to A$ comes first, then $B$ still occupies that lane, since its interval reaches the witness for $B\to Q$. There $P<A\le B$, giving the shortcut $P\to B$. If the witness for $B\to Q$ comes first, the same reasoning gives $A\to Q$. A shortcut contradicts minimality or, in a triangle, contradicts the opposite comparison. This also handles equal witness lanes. Thus there is a linear extension of all forced comparisons.

Any such linear extension gives a chart. Replace each germ box, in order, by its prescribed face and retain the exterior gates. Within each face use any spatial serialization. This reproduces the precedence of the original gates on every lane, hence is a serialization of $D$. When a box is reached its exact input wires are at the specified cuts. All exterior gates and all nonempty blocks on each of its idle lanes are on the prescribed sides of its gap. Equal-gap boxes leave the wire through that gap unchanged on expansion. Contracting each block therefore gives precisely the specified unary germs. Two orders giving the same comparisons on all intersecting intervals give the same chart, since their linear extensions differ by interchanges of disjoint intervals. Conversely a chart gives these comparisons from its serialization, and the order of boxes sharing a wire is recorded on that wire. This proves joint existence and the asserted parametrization by acyclic orientations of the full interval intersection graph.

Only a pair tied on its whole overlap still needs a sign. Exterior gates have positive block length throughout their intervals, so all such pairs consist of germs. Orient every tied pair. A directed cycle in the resulting graph always contains a directed triangle: in a shortest cycle of length at least four the interval with smallest right endpoint has intersecting neighbours, and their oriented chord shortens the cycle. No triangle has three tied pairs. In such a triangle there could be no interval containment, since the contained box has an active lane. Thus the three starts and the three ends are in the same strict order. The two outer intervals intersect and cover the middle interval between them. A middle box tied to both would have no active lane. Each forbidden cyclic orientation of a triangle consequently forbids one assignment on at most two variables. Taking all these clauses gives the formula. Its solutions are connected by shortest sign paths: take a spatial serialization of each of two corners and sort the first into the second by swapping inverted adjacent entries. Forced comparisons never swap. A disjoint swap leaves the corner fixed and every differing sign flips exactly once. Finally the coordinatewise majority of three solutions satisfies each clause, since a majority falsifying each of two literals would include a solution falsifying both. The majority is the unique common point of the three pairwise graph intervals by isometry into the cube. This proves the median assertion.

**Consequence for Question 2.** In an entire connected component of the positive three-dimensional transition cubical set, suppose no pair of signed germs has two square corners. Its links are then simplicial flag complexes by the corner theorem, including uniqueness in higher dimensions. Each cube corner has distinct germs because it expands disjoint nonempty sets of gates. The cubical universal cover is CAT(0). The oriented edges dual to a lifted hyperplane are oriented consistently by the time coordinates of squares. Directed paths cross each such hyperplane at most once and the directed interval between their lifted endpoints is the cubing of order ideals in the separating hyperplanes. Its endpoint chart space is contractible. Lifting a serial path and all its square exchanges identifies its strict fibre with this interval calculation, as in the cited link proof. In particular a whole such transition component which is a subcomplex of a Boolean cube can only give contractible strict fibres. An arbitrary cubical subdiagram selected inside a larger component need not enjoy this conclusion. This rules out realizing a hard cube list from Response 3 as the entire transition component of a positive 3-polygraph, even when its cubes are intended as time charts. A realization preserving only homotopy types may use multiple square corners.

## What independent choices at seams can encode

There is also a global restriction on the use of the seam constructions for Question 2. Suppose the chart histories at fixed endpoints and fixed occurrence data are covered by subcomplexes indexed by all binary sign lists on a finite set $I$. Suppose that an intersection for a nonempty family of lists is the ordered-partition complex of a fixed temporal order $H$ with the comparisons $a_i<b_i$ added precisely at the coordinates where the family varies. For the following argument it suffices that this intersection be empty when the comparisons are cyclic and contractible otherwise. Define
$$
 K=\{J\subseteq I\mid H\cup\{a_i<b_i:i\in J\}\text{ is acyclic}\}.
$$
Then the union has the homotopy type of the cubical subcomplex $Z_K$ of $[-1,1]^I$ consisting of all faces whose varying coordinates belong to $K$ (the real moment-angle complex). Indeed the nerve of the given cover has one vertex per sign list. For each maximal cubical face of $Z_K$, all of its vertices span a full simplex in that nerve. These simplices cover the nerve by the intersection hypothesis. A collection of them intersects in the simplex on the vertices of the common cube face, or is empty. The maximal faces themselves cover $Z_K$ with exactly the same nonempty, contractible intersections. Two applications of the nerve lemma give the claim, component by component.

A coordinate whose singleton is absent from $K$ is constant on each component. After fixing those signs, all remaining unit sign flips are available, so the resulting subcomplex is connected. That component is contractible exactly when the remaining coordinate set belongs to $K$. Otherwise choose a minimal missing subset $J$ among those coordinates. Its size is at least two. Coordinate projection gives a retraction onto $\partial[-1,1]^J$, with section obtained by fixing every other coordinate at a vertex. Thus the component has a sphere as a retract in the homotopy category and has nonzero reduced integral homology. This explains a limitation even for constructions with arbitrarily complicated temporal orders and overlapping cycles: a presentation whose strict fibres all have these covers cannot supply the acyclic noncontractible obstruction required by Response 2. On any promise of such presentations with homotopical lower skeleton the search for nonzero fibre homology detects every failure; no effective choice of the covers is required.

The same obstruction holds with **constrained sign choices**. Let $M$ be an isometric median subgraph of the sign cube, closed under coordinatewise majority, as in the corner theorem. Fill its coordinate cubes. Suppose the cover uses its vertices as sheets, and a family has contractible intersection precisely when it lies in a cube of $M$ whose varying coordinates belong to a fixed downward-closed family $K$, with empty intersection otherwise. Assume $\varnothing\in K$. The same pair of nerve comparisons gives the subcomplex of those allowed cubes. Coordinates forbidden even as singletons are constant on each component. Conversely a fixed set of values there cuts out a connected slice if nonempty: any shortest sign path between two of its vertices keeps all their common coordinates fixed. The slice is again an isometric median graph, so its full cubing is CAT(0) and contractible. [Chepoi, *Graphs of Some CAT(0) Complexes*, Theorem 6.1](https://pageperso.lis-lab.fr/~victor.chepoi/cat0.pdf)

If the allowed subcomplex in this slice omits any cube, take an inclusion-minimal omitted cube $Q$. All its boundary faces are present and its dimension is at least two. Project by freezing coordinates outside the directions of $Q$ to their values on $Q$. On vertices this is majority with opposite vertices of $Q$; on a cube it is ordinary coordinate projection. No allowed cube maps onto $Q$, since its direction set would contain the forbidden direction set of $Q$. Projection is therefore a retraction onto $\partial Q$. Thus imposing a common set of permitted direction sets on median choices also cannot produce an acyclic noncontractible fibre. This statement requires the same direction rule at different sign values. The local corner theorem alone gives no such rule for a whole history.

**An actual two-bridge family.** Take a nonempty list of directed edges between two vertices $U,V$, in either direction, with repetitions allowed and no loops. There is a finite positive 3-polygraph for which the canonical full fibre has the homotopy type of the complete bipartite graph
$$
                              K_{2^p,2^q},
$$
where $p$ edges point from $U$ to $V$ and $q$ point back. Its first Betti number is $(2^p-1)(2^q-1)$. The presentation is homotopical exactly when all edges have the same direction. Here the same two top generators participate in every cycle.

Use three consecutive lanes per edge $i\to j$, laid out along a directed line of objects, with private parallel 1-edges on each lane. Two long 3-generators, also named $U,V$, span the line. The short generators $a,b$ of this edge span respectively its first two and last two lanes. The following words specify their parallel generating faces. All gates are unary between 1-edges, and every omitted change on a traversed lane in the description is an indicated identity read.

* Each outer lane is a signal with three parallel gates $L,M,R:x\Rightarrow y$, with $x\ne y$. At the first signal $i$ changes $L$ to $M$ and $a$ changes $M$ to $R$. At the last signal $b$ changes $L$ to $M$ and $j$ changes $M$ to $R$. If the acting bridge is $U$, add a gate $h:y\Rightarrow v$ and let $V$ read $v$ by identity on both faces. If it is $V$, add $t:u\Rightarrow x$ and let $U$ read $u$ on both faces. Use fresh edges for $u,v$.
* On the middle lane take $t:u\Rightarrow w$, $h:w\Rightarrow v$ and $d,e:w\Rightarrow w$. The short changes are $d\Rightarrow 1_w$ for $a$ and $1_w\Rightarrow e$ for $b$. The bridges read $u$ and $v$ respectively, on both faces. Each top generator has an active signal and therefore two nonidentity faces.

The canonical run changes $L$ to $R$ on each signal with the indicated prefix or suffix, and $tdh$ to $teh$ on each seam. Execute all the $b$'s first, then the bridges, then all the $a$'s, inserting beside the chosen $d$ on each seam. For arbitrary strict labels the outer path visits a lane at most once. Each signal carries at most one active gate among $L,M,R$, so each event occurs at most once. Its two changes obey the indicated precedence whenever both occur. The reads at $u$ and $v$ are unique whenever available: no gate enters $u$ or leaves $v$. They lie respectively before and after the middle activities. This includes histories starting at an interior lane or with only part of its ordinary word present.

At a seam with both short events the chosen initial $d$ and final $e$ have definite positions preserved by exchange. Omitting these occurrences gives a common persistent word. Different gaps in that word give a unique spatial placement and allow either time order. For a common gap, the sign specifies on which side the insertion lies when it occurs first or simultaneously with deletion. When deletion occurs strictly first, the insertion goes into one empty gap and both signs describe the same history. At a seam with one event its position is fixed. These statements follow directly by inserting and expanding the two markers in the word, including when other $d$'s or $e$'s persist.

For fixed signs, every ordered temporal partition respecting the surviving signal precedences gives exactly one whole chart string. In a tier place $U$ first if present, then the short markers in the specified orders within disjoint seam pairs, then $V$ if present. On an active signal its two successive changes cannot be in one tier. On every other shared wire the prefix and suffix place precisely the indicated bridge before or after the shorts. Thus this spatial order works on every lane. The intervening unary gates are inserted in their specified positions and have independent orders on different lanes. Conversely the marked words determine all these data, and restriction to a temporal face just expands the appropriate markers. This proves the required sign-cover hypothesis with $H$ consisting of $i<a$ and $b<j$ when the respective events occur. A sheet or an intersection with acyclic precedence is contractible by the order-ideal cubing for linear extensions. The only added edge for a varying common gap is $a<b$.

A directed cycle must traverse both bridges, going out along a selected edge in each direction. Indeed its only route from one bridge to the other is $i<a<b<j$. Thus with either bridge absent every sign subset is feasible. With both present, $K$ consists of the subsets of common gaps selected entirely in one direction or entirely in the other. Every singleton is feasible. The union is therefore connected, so the fixed data give a single strict label by exchanges; the preceding invariance of positions and events makes this its entire fibre. Write $p,q$ now for the two counts of common gaps in the given label. The cubical model consists of the $2^q$ copies of the $p$-cube with the second coordinates fixed at vertices, and the $2^p$ copies of the $q$-cube with the first coordinates fixed. Each cube of the first family meets each of the second in one vertex; within either family the cubes are disjoint. Their nerve is $K_{2^p,2^q}$ when $p,q>0$. If either count vanishes the whole model is a cube. The canonical run realizes all the given edges as common gaps. This proves both the full-fibre formula and the assertion on all labels. The lower skeleton is homotopical by positivity, and item 6 transports the calculation through suspension.

## The group before imposing three-change exchanges

Fix one positive label $c$ of weight $r$ in any of these tests. Let $B(c)\subseteq T_j(c)$ contain the strings in which each simultaneous chart has one or two slots. Its vertices are serial histories of $r$ events. An edge replaces two consecutive events by their opposite serialization in a specified two-slot chart. Both choices of a chart are retained when they have the same endpoints. The group of each component of $B(c)$ embeds in an explicit finitely generated right-angled Artin group. The group of $T_j(c)$ is its quotient by just the hexagons of the three-slot charts in serial contexts. In particular, if the integral first homology of the binary complex is cyclic (including zero), **every** quotient of its fundamental group is abelian.

Here are the embedding and its proof. A two-slot chart $Q$ at positions $i,i+1$ gives an edge label $(i,Q)$, independent of the other beads. Use one generator per such label. Choose an order on the two concrete slots of each $Q$ and orient the edge toward that serialization. Two generators commute in the Artin presentation precisely when their position pairs are disjoint. Map a binary string with several two-slot beads to the corresponding commuting cube in the Salvetti complex. The link map at a serial history is injective: the position, chart and sign specify exactly which two unary beads to replace, including which facet gives the present serialization. Its image is full. Any set of incident signed exchanges with disjoint pairs can be performed by replacing all the indicated pairs in that very string. The resulting cube is unique and has precisely the prescribed corner. This proves local isometry and injectivity on fundamental groups. The argument also applies to the uncoloured chart model of any bubble-free 3-polygraph in item 20. There the marked planar diagrams have concrete slots: each component meets the boundary, and ordered planar ports propagate from that boundary, fixing every gate under any automorphism. No names for the events of the *entire* 3-cell are needed for the embedding. [Haglund–Wise, *Special cube complexes*, §2](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~frederic.haglund/special%28corrected%29.pdf)

The only two-dimensional string polytopes are a square from two disjoint pairs and a hexagon from one triple. Higher polytopes leave the fundamental group unchanged. This proves the quotient description. Right-angled Artin groups are residually torsion-free nilpotent, so each binary group is residually nilpotent. If a group $H$ has cyclic abelianization then $\gamma_2H/\gamma_3H=0$, since commutators there factor alternately and bilinearly through the abelianization. Its lower central series is therefore constant from $\gamma_2H$ onward. Residual nilpotence forces $\gamma_2H=1$. More generally, even without cyclic abelianization, a nontrivial binary group cannot itself be perfect. Thus at a connected acyclic shadow with nontrivial group there must be a ternary hexagon and the abelianization of the binary group must be noncyclic. The hexagon classes generate that abelianization, so at least two hexagons are needed. All of these necessary conditions are computable at the specified label. [Wade, *The lower central series of a right-angled Artin group*](https://ems.press/journals/lem/articles/13987)

For positive inputs there is a further integral check on the relators. For any three named occurrences, project a serial vertex to their order, one of the six vertices of a hexagonal **circle**. An exchange of two of the three traverses the corresponding edge, and any other exchange is constant. A disjoint-exchange square has at most one of its axes moving that order and hence extends over the square. This defines a winding homomorphism on $H_1(B(c);\mathbb Z)$. The boundary of a ternary chart winds once, up to sign, for its own triple and zero for every other triple. In the latter case at most two retained events are moving and the image lies in one edge. Consequently the product of the winding homomorphisms over the distinct triples that occur in hexagons splits onto a free abelian group with that many generators, by choosing one oriented hexagon per triple. Several hexagons for the same triple can still impose different group relations.
