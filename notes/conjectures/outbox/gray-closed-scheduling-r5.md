# Schedules in free Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-categories, round 5

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Rounds 1–4 and their verified answers are archived in `answered/gray-closed-scheduling-2026-11-18.md` and `answered/gray-closed-scheduling-r2-2026-11-18.md` through `-r4-2026-11-18.md`; nothing answered there is re-asked. The smallest open case is Question 1.*

## Background

**Schedules.** Fix a finite poset P on labels {1, …, n}, with numerical order as the left/right order of incomparable labels. Let S(P) be the category with:
- objects: the linear extensions of P;
- generating arrows: swaps ji → ij of adjacent P-incomparable labels with i < j;
- equations: commuting squares for swaps at disjoint positions, and the braid hexagon for three pairwise incomparable labels.

Then S(P)(w, v) is a singleton if Inv_P(v) ⊆ Inv_P(w) and empty otherwise. Here Inv_P(w) is the set of pairs i < j, incomparable, with j before i in w. (Proof: move the first differing label left, then resolve the first swaps by a square or a hexagon and induct.)

**Gray monoids.** In the free strict Gray monoid (a monoid in 2-Cat for Gray's lax tensor) on a set of objects and generating 1-cells, fix the image in the free strict monoidal category, i.e. the string diagram. Then the hom-category between the 1-cells with that diagram is S(P). Here P is the diagram's dependency order, with left/right given by planarity. The 2-cells are whiskered interchangers (f ⊗ B′)∘(A ⊗ g) ⇒ (A′ ⊗ g)∘(f ⊗ B), "right factor first ⇒ left factor first".

**The ω-version.** The hom ω-category of the Steiner complex ν(I^⊗n), from bottom to top, is not thin: two hexagon 2-cells are joined by the 3-cell ***. Gray's 2-tensor is its intelligent 2-truncation (Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches*, A.25 and A.31). For a dependency poset P, the subcomplex K_P ⊆ I^⊗n spanned by the faces (order ideal J, S ⊆ min(P∖J)) has paths equal to the linear extensions of P. Its truncated hom is S(P).

## Setting: the biclosed version

Formulas are built from atoms with ⊗ and the residuals B\A and A/B, with no unit. 𝒢 is the free **Gray-monoidal biclosed strict 2-category** on a set of generating 1-cells fᵢ : Pᵢ → Qᵢ between formulas. Its structure:
- a strict 2-category with a Gray monoidal structure (lax interchangers, as above);
- closed structure: isomorphisms of hom-categories Φ : 𝒢(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ 𝒢(B, A\C), and the mirror one for /.
  - Φ is **strictly natural in the context variable B**.
  - Φ is natural in the **bound variable A only up to the interchanger**.
  - Strict naturality in A would force (f ⊗ B′)(A ⊗ g) = (A′ ⊗ g)(f ⊗ B), collapsing distinct schedules into one 1-cell. That is not wanted.

**Two warnings, both established.**
1. *Nesting is not invariant under naive normalization.* For a generator h : U → U with U = X\Y,

   ```
   h = Λ(ev) ∘ h = Λ(ev ∘ (1 ⊗ h))
   ```

   So h can be "ambient" or "inside a closure". β-reduction ev ∘ (1 ⊗ Λk) = k also changes nesting. Any invariant notion of "event inside a closure" must start from a βη-canonical form.
2. *Unit-free planar coherence.* In the free unit-free biclosed monoidal 1-category, a balanced sequent (each atom twice, opposite polarities) has at most one morphism, and exactly one when it is provable in the Lambek calculus with product. So the purely structural parts of 1-cells are unique.

## What is known from round 1 (proved)

1. **A word invariant.** Sending each generator to its label gives an invariant W of 1-cell equality with values in words (composition order), with W(Λu) = W(u), W(ev) = ε and W(D ⊗ u) = W(u). It is realised by the one-object model whose 1-cells are words, with a unique 2-cell between words with the same multiset. So 1-cells with different generator order are distinct.
2. **Closure trees are not invariants of components.** With generators a : X → A and f : A ⊗ B → C, c = Λ_A(f), e = ev: the interchanger gives θ : s = e∘(a⊗U)∘(X⊗c) ⇒ t = e∘(A⊗c)∘(a⊗B), and t = f∘(a⊗B) by β. Every representative of s has a generator under currying (a Boolean biclosed poset rules out the needed structural prefix), while t is tensor-only. So any βη-normal form fixing tensor-only terms gives s and t different closure trees, though they are connected by a 2-cell. "Cross-level cells are generated by ordinary interchangers" does not imply "the canonical tree is invariant".
3. **Question 1, partial.** For φ : P → C/B and b : X → B, the forward comparison α = e_B ∗ χ_{φ,b} : t₁ ⇒ t₂ exists, W distinguishes t₁, t₂, and Λ_X(t₂) = (C/b)∘φ by strict naturality in the context variable. Whether 𝒢(t₁, t₂) = {α} and 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ is open: the tensor-only schedule theorem does not control 2-cells built by currying, uncurrying and η-expansion.
4. **Mapping-space biclosedness is too weak for Question 4.** Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari (arXiv 2006.14495, Rem. 2.20): Map(X ⊗ Y, Z) ≇ Map(X, Fun^{opgr}(Y, Z)) in general; only cores agree.

## What is known from round 2 (proved)

5. **An integer model.** Let 𝓜 be the strict 2-category with:
   - objects the integers;
   - for q ≥ p, the hom 𝓜(p, q) equal to the poset (ℤ, ≤) of weights; otherwise empty;
   - composition adding weights;
   - w(p ⊗ u) = w(u) + p·δ(u) and w(u ⊗ q) = w(u), where δ(u) = target − source;
   - p\r = r − p and r/q = r − q, with left currying subtracting p·δ and right currying leaving the weight unchanged.

   𝓜 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category. An interchanger raises the weight by δ(f)·δ(g). A matrix-valued version (objects in ℤ^r) records ordered generator pairs.
6. **Question 1, reverse direction.** With distinct atoms, 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ and 𝒢(t, s) = ∅. For t₁ and t₂ the weights are 0 and 1, from |B| = 1, |C| = 2 and |P| = |X| = 0. For s and t they are −1 and 0. Every forward 2-cell between them uses **exactly one** interchanger whose two arguments each contain one generator. Every other interchanger in it has a generator-free argument.
7. **Undirected components.** π₀ 𝒢(A, C) ≅ 𝓑_Σ(A, C), the free ordinary unit-free biclosed monoidal category. Two 1-cells are connected by a zigzag of 2-cells iff their images there agree.
8. **The image and W do not classify 1-cells.** Take generators f : A → B and g : B → C with padding L, R, and let m = L ⊗ (g ∘ f) ⊗ R.
   - Currying g with the right padding gives u ⇒ m.
   - Currying g with the left padding gives m ⇒ v.
   - u, m and v have equal images in 𝓑_Σ, and W(u) = W(v).
   - Their weights (with |L| = |R| = |A| = 0, |B| = 1, |C| = 2) are −1, 0 and 1, so the three 1-cells are distinct.
   - The side of currying is data of the 1-cell, and schedules with only generator events cannot describe this component.

## Further observations (proved)

9. **Subtractive models cannot see interchangers with a structural argument.** Call a model *additive* if 1-cells carry a weight in an ordered abelian group, δ(u) = weight(target) − weight(source) is additive under composition and tensor, and an interchanger χ_{f,g} adds a bilinear term β(δ(f), δ(g)). If moreover the residuals are group subtraction (A\C = C − A, as in the model 𝓜 of item 5 and its matrix version), every structural 1-cell (ev, curried evaluations, units) has δ = 0, so every interchanger with a structural argument has weight 0. Such models cannot decide whether two 2-cells differing in those interchangers are equal. This blindness is specific to subtractive residuals; see item 13.
10. **The axioms do not make evaluation central.** For ev : A ⊗ (A\C) → C and a generator g : Y → Y′, compare (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y), related by χ_{ev,g}. Currying A on the left (using strict naturality in the context variable and the target) turns this into the same question for m_Y := Φ(ev ⊗ Y) and g, so currying only transports it. Likewise for χ_{ev,ev′} at independent positions. Unless centrality is imposed (as in Garner–Hirschowitz–Pardon's premonoidal construction), structural maps are non-central events in 𝒢. In the quotient 𝒢_c of 𝒢 in which every interchanger with a structural argument is an identity, Question 1 follows at once from item 6.

## What is known from round 3 (proved)

11. **Structural loops (round 3).** Take atoms A, B, C, D and U = A\(A⊗B), X = A⊗B, X′ = A⊗U, V = (C⊗D)/D, Y = C⊗D, Y′ = V⊗D. Let u = 1_A ⊗ Λ_A(1) : X → X′, e = ev : X′ → X, v = Λ^R_D(1) ⊗ 1_D : Y → Y′ and d = ev^R : Y′ → Y, so that e u = 1 and d v = 1 by β. Put T = X ⊗ Y and (composition rightmost first)

    ```
    H = (e ⊗ 1_Y)(1_{X′} ⊗ d)(u ⊗ 1_{Y′})(1_X ⊗ v) : T → T.
    ```

    - Whiskered interchangers give ι = (e⊗1) ∗ χ_{u,d} ∗ (1⊗v) : 1_T ⇒ H and π = [(e⊗1)(1⊗d)] ∗ χ_{u,v} : H ⇒ 1_T. The Gray axiom for χ_{u,dv} gives **π ∘ ι = 1**, so E = ι ∘ π is an idempotent on H.
    - **H^n are pairwise distinct.** In the model whose hom-categories are chaotic on the free group ⟨ℓ, r⟩ (objects integers, composition multiplication, left currying k ↦ ℓ^a k, right currying k ↦ r^a k), H ↦ ℓ⁻¹ r⁻¹ ℓ r.
    - All H^n have image 1_T in 𝓑_Σ, empty generator word and zero weight in every model of item 5. Since 2-cells H^m ⇒ 1_T ⇒ H^n exist, the category over 1_T is not isomorphic to any product of schedule categories.
    - This particular model does not satisfy strict equality of the two mixed curryings of (A\C)/B = A\(C/B). Item 14 removes that dependence.
    - **Forward comparisons.** Item 6's count factors each forward comparison as z₊ ∘ m ∘ z₋, with z_± using only interchangers with a generator-free argument and m a typed instance of the one generator–generator interchanger. Different typed instances of m must still be identified.
12. **Evaluation is not central in 𝒢.** In the same model with a third free generator γ for a generating 1-cell g, (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) ↦ ℓ⁻¹γ and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y) ↦ γℓ⁻¹, which are distinct. So χ_{ev,g} joins distinct 1-cells, and this uses only left closure. A complete invariant of 1-cells must schedule structural events (units, evaluations), not only generator events.

13. **A polarized model sees structural interchangers.** Let 𝓟 have objects (P, N) ∈ ℤ², A ⊗ B = A + B and A\C = C/A = C + swap(A), where swap(P, N) = (N, P). A 1-cell X → Y exists iff (P − N)(X) = (P − N)(Y), and it is an integer weight w. Put d(u) = P(source) − P(target).
    - Composition adds weights, w(A ⊗ u) = w(u) + P(A)·d(u) and w(u ⊗ B) = w(u).
    - Left currying is w ↦ w − P(A)·d(u), and right currying keeps the weight.
    - The homs are codiscrete.
    - 𝓟 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category in the sense above.
    - **ev is not central.** Here d(ev_A) = P(A) + N(A) ≠ 0. With A ↦ (1, 0), C ↦ (0, 0), X ↦ (1, 1), X′ ↦ (0, 0) and g : X → X′, the 1-cells (X′ ⊗ ev) ∘ (g ⊗ A ⊗ A\C) and (g ⊗ C) ∘ (X ⊗ ev) have weights 1 and 2. The same separates the two orders of two independent evaluations, with no generator involved.
    - Together with item 12, the central quotient 𝒢_c of item 10 is a proper quotient of 𝒢.

## What is known from round 4 (proved)

14. **Strict mixed currying does not help, and left closure alone suffices.**
    - *The model 𝒟.* Keep the chaotic free-group Gray monoid of item 11. Choose any k_c ∈ ⟨ℓ, r⟩ for c ∈ ℤ, put h_{a,c} = k_{c−a} k_c⁻¹, and let both curryings with bound object a and codomain c be f ↦ h_{a,c} f.
    - *Properties.* The multipliers telescope, so the two mixed curryings are equal, R_b R_a = R_{a+b}, and h_{0,c} = 1.
    - *Separation.* With |A| = |B| = 1, |C| = |D| = 2, k₁ = ℓ, k₄ = r⁻¹ and every other k_c = 1, H ↦ ℓ⁻¹r⁻¹ℓr. So H ≠ 1_T and the H^n are pairwise distinct even in the theory 𝒢_mix that imposes strict mixed currying.
    - *Left closure only.* Replace v and d by v_L = 1_C ⊗ Λ_C(1_{C⊗D}) and d_L = ev, through W = C\(C⊗D). The resulting H_L also maps to ℓ⁻¹r⁻¹ℓr. So the free left-closed theory already has non-identity structural loops 1_T ⇒ H_L ⇒ 1_T with π ∘ ι = 1.
15. **Countermodels for ι ∘ π = 1_H need non-invertible retractions.** In a Gray monoid, if f is strictly invertible, then χ_{f,g} is invertible, with inverse (f ⊗ B′) ∗ χ_{f⁻¹,g} ∗ (f ⊗ B). Hence in any model where u, e, v and d are strict isomorphisms, ι ∘ π = 1. That covers every refinement of the free-group, 𝒟 and 𝓟 models. A countermodel must have u ∘ e ≠ 1.
16. **Two different "centrality" questions.**
    - *Outer comparison.* For functors e, g between strict ω-categories, (e ⊗ 1)(1 ⊗ g) and (1 ⊗ g)(e ⊗ 1) are both e ⊗ g. So the interpretation of 𝒢's 1-cells as functors and its 2-cells as transformations sends every interchanger, generator or structural, to an identity, and cannot test directedness.
    - *Internal interchanger.* Evaluation applied to an interchanger of internal cells can be non-invertible. For S = J ⊗ J (J = [1]) with square γ : h₁v₀ ⇒ v₁h₀, let q : J → S/J curry 1_S and let α : v₀ → v₁ be the image of the arrow of J. Then e(χ_{α,u}) = γ.
17. **𝒢's two hom-category isomorphisms do not hold in the intended model.**
    - In S/J there is an arrow α : v₀ → v₁.
    - In J\S there is none: such an arrow would need the reverse of γ.
    - A single 2-category of strict ω-categories, with one orientation of transformation as 2-cells and ℋ(I, Z) ≅ Z, would identify both S/J and J\S with ℋ(J, S), identically on objects. That is impossible.

    So the Gray tensor's left and right closures are matched by 2-cells of opposite orientation (lax for one side, oplax for the other; Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari, Ex. 2.5). Campion's tensor gives (A\C)/B ≃ A\(C/B) canonically, but not literally.

# Research Response

**Question 1 admits a precise correction using two-sided Gray enrichment.** For the interpretation in which formulas denote strict \(2\)-categories and the generating \(1\)-cells denote strict \(2\)-functors, this correction has the following consequences:

- Left and right currying preserve different hom \(2\)-categories.
- Each pair of formulas also has a double hom, with the two transformation orientations as its horizontal and vertical arrows.
- \(H=H_L=1_T\) as maps between formulas.
- Noninvertible Gray interchange remains, but it compares schedules of **transformations by a modification**. It is not the original interchanger between schedules of functors.

I give a free unit-free construction below, with an explicit universal property and a model check. Its freeness is relative to the stated axioms. This does **not** decide whether \(\iota\pi=1_H\) in the original \(\mathcal G\).

The relevant existing framework is **bienrichment**, or **bioriented category theory**. Gepner–Heine explicitly use the two-sided family
\[
(P,Q)\longmapsto \operatorname{Map}(P\otimes A\otimes Q,B)
\]
and identify the Gray-closed category of higher categories as an example. Their §1.5 and §3.2, rather than only Corollary 4.1.11, address this distinction. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10504v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 1. The two homs and the correct currying equations

Write \(I\) for the point, \(J=[1]\), and \(D_2\) for the walking \(2\)-cell. To avoid convention-dependent uses of “lax” and “oplax”, label transformations by the position of their parameter.

For strict \(2\)-categories \(A,C\), define:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 & \mathbb H_\ell(A,C)&\mathbb H_r(A,C)\\ \hline
0\text{-cells}&A\to C&A\to C\\
1\text{-cells}&J\otimes A\to C&A\otimes J\to C\\
2\text{-cells}&D_2\otimes A\to C&A\otimes D_2\to C .
\end{array}
\]

All maps in this table are strict \(2\)-functors. Boundaries are obtained by restricting along the boundary inclusions of the probes. Thus the table specifies cells with their boundaries, not unrelated sets.

These are the two functor \(2\)-categories: strict functors, transformations of the respective orientation, and modifications. In residual notation,
\[
\mathbb H_\ell(A,C)=C/A,
\qquad
\mathbb H_r(A,C)=A\backslash C.
\]

The enriched currying equations are

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb H_r(A\otimes B,C)
\cong
\mathbb H_r(B,A\backslash C)
}
\tag{1}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb H_\ell(A\otimes B,C)
\cong
\mathbb H_\ell(A,C/B).
}
\tag{2}
\]

Here is the reason for the orientation in (1). For a probe \(Q\), associativity and ordinary currying give
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\mathrm{Cat}((A\otimes B)\otimes Q,C)
&\cong 2\mathrm{Cat}(A\otimes(B\otimes Q),C)\\
&\cong 2\mathrm{Cat}(B\otimes Q,A\backslash C).
\end{aligned}
\]
Naturality in \(Q\) identifies boundaries, identities, compositions, and modifications. Equation (2) follows from
\[
2\mathrm{Cat}(P\otimes A\otimes B,C)
\cong
2\mathrm{Cat}(P\otimes A,C/B).
\]

These are isomorphisms of full hom \(2\)-categories, not merely bijections of functors or equivalences of cores. A useful strict reference is Nikolić, *Strictification tensor product of 2-categories*, §4.1, equation (143); its Corollary 2.5, equation (32), gives the corresponding mixed-orientation identity. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.12213?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### What happens with parameters on both sides

Define
\[
\mathbb H(A,C)(P,Q)
=
2\mathrm{Cat}(P\otimes A\otimes Q,C).
\]

For arbitrary \(P,Q\), the valid equations are
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\mathrm{Cat}(P\otimes A\otimes B\otimes Q,C)
&\cong
2\mathrm{Cat}(B\otimes Q,(P\otimes A)\backslash C),\\
2\mathrm{Cat}(P\otimes A\otimes B\otimes Q,C)
&\cong
2\mathrm{Cat}(P\otimes A,C/(B\otimes Q)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
\]

In the first line, the bound argument is \(P\otimes A\), not \(A\). In the second, it is \(B\otimes Q\), not \(B\).

Consequently, merely assigning each transformation a lax/oplax label is insufficient. The positions of its parameters matter.

## 2. A precise category of models

The following definition gives a free theory without presupposing an unspecified notion of “Gray-monoidal double category”.

### Fixed probes

Fix a small monoidal category \(\mathcal P\) of strict \(2\)-category probes. Require that it include:

- \(I,J,D_2\);
- finite globular pasting shapes;
- their Gray products;
- the strict functors selecting boundaries, identities, and composites.

Choose finite **nonempty connected** colimit diagrams in \(\mathcal P\), computed in \(2\mathrm{Cat}\), sufficient for these pasting operations. One can take a small full subcategory generated by \(I,J,D_2\) under Gray tensor and finite nonempty connected colimits.

Associators can either be retained or handled in a fixed strict monoidal presentation. I suppress them below.

### Data types, probes, and tensor

A model consists of an ordinary monoidal category
\[
\mathcal E=\mathcal P\amalg\mathcal D
\]
with no arrows between the two displayed sorts, satisfying the following conditions.

1. **Probe tensor.** Tensor on \(\mathcal P\) is its specified Gray tensor.

2. **Data tensor and probe actions.** A tensor product with at least one factor in \(\mathcal D\) belongs to \(\mathcal D\). Thus there are operations
   \[
   \mathcal D\times\mathcal D\to\mathcal D,\qquad
   \mathcal P\times\mathcal D\to\mathcal D,\qquad
   \mathcal D\times\mathcal P\to\mathcal D.
   \]

3. **Unit-free data syntax.** The unit \(I\) belongs to the probe sort. No data-sort tensor unit is designated.

4. **Data adjunctions.** For every data type \(A\), there are chosen ordinary adjunctions on \(\mathcal D\):
   \[
   A\otimes-\dashv A\backslash-,
   \qquad
   -\otimes A\dashv -/A.
   \tag{4}
   \]

5. **Probe gluing.** Every one-hole tensor-word context \(W(P)\), with at least one fixed data factor, preserves the designated probe colimits.

The last condition means that, for a designated cocone \(j_i:P_i\to P\),
\[
P=\operatorname{colim}_iP_i
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
W(P)=\operatorname{colim}_iW(P_i)
\quad\text{in }\mathcal D.
\tag{5}
\]

The varying probe \(P\) must appear **exactly once** in \(W(P)\). For example,
\[
P\longmapsto R\otimes A\otimes P\otimes B
\]
is allowed. No diagonal preservation requirement is imposed on \(P\mapsto P\otimes A\otimes P\).

The adjunctions in (4) quantify over data types. We do not assert that the entire disjoint union \(\mathcal E\) is biclosed. That stronger assertion would be inconsistent: testing a proposed data residual against the probe unit would give
\[
\mathcal E(I,A\backslash C)
\cong
\mathcal E(A,C),
\]
whose left side is empty and whose right side need not be.

Morphisms of models fix \(\mathcal P\) and preserve tensor, the chosen data adjunctions, and the specified structure.

## 3. The free unit-free theory \(\mathcal G'_\Sigma\)

Let \(\Sigma\) be the original signature of atoms and generating maps
\[
f_i:P_i\longrightarrow Q_i.
\]

Define data-type expressions by
\[
A::=
a
\mid A\otimes A
\mid A\backslash A
\mid A/A
\mid P\triangleright A
\mid A\triangleleft P,
\qquad P\in\mathcal P.
\tag{6}
\]

The last two constructors denote the probe actions. The original formula grammar is the subgrammar without them. There is no data-unit constructor.

Generate arrows by:

- the \(f_i\);
- identities and composition;
- tensor of arrows, including the actions of probe arrows;
- both evaluations and both currying operations;
- the following finite gluing operations.

For a designated probe colimit \(j_i:P_i\to P\), an allowed context \(W\), and a compatible family
\[
t_i:W(P_i)\longrightarrow B,
\]
introduce
\[
[t_i]:W(P)\longrightarrow B.
\]

Compatibility means
\[
t_j\,W(r)=t_i
\]
for every arrow \(r:P_i\to P_j\) of the indexing diagram. Impose
\[
[t_i]\,W(j_i)=t_i
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
[\,h\,W(j_i)\,]_i=h
\qquad
\text{for every }h:W(P)\to B.
\tag{8}
\]

Also impose the category and monoidal equations, the fixed probe equations, and the two adjunctions’ \(\beta\eta\) and naturality equations.

This defines a typed term theory with finite-arity gluing operations whose applicability is specified by finite equations. Equivalently, take the least typed congruence closed under those operations and equations.

### Universal property

For every model \(M\) defined in §2,
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{models}}(\mathcal G'_\Sigma,M)
\cong
\operatorname{Interpretations}(\Sigma,M).
}
\tag{9}
\]

**Proof.** Interpret type and arrow expressions recursively. Interpret a gluing expression by the unique map supplied by the colimit condition (5). Equations (7) and (8) hold by the colimit universal property; all other equations hold by the model axioms. Hence the interpretation descends to the quotient. Conversely, a structure-preserving interpretation must have these values on every constructor, so it is unique. ∎

This is the promised free construction. Its universal property is explicitly relative to §2; no equivalence with every possible definition of a biclosed double category is being asserted.

## 4. Genuine hom \(2\)-categories and double homs

In any model, put

\mathbb H(A,B)(P,Q)

\qquad
g:R\otimes B\otimes S\to C,

g\star f

g(1_R\otimes f\otimes1_S):

Associativity follows from ordinary composition and tensor bifunctoriality. The identity has parameter pair \((I,I)\).

Boundaries are restrictions. Horizontal and vertical composition of squares glue the corresponding interval parameter. The two orders of composing a grid agree because both produce the unique amalgamation of the same compatible family.

## 5. Model check and an erasure check

### The intended strict Gray model

Take \(\mathcal D\) to be a tagged copy of the ordinary category of strict \(2\)-categories and strict \(2\)-functors. Interpret all tensor operations using the lax Gray tensor and both data residuals using its two internal homs.

The probe sort is the chosen \(\mathcal P\), disjoint from the tagged data sort. A particular model may have a data type whose underlying \(2\)-category is \(I\); that does not introduce a unit constant into the free data syntax.

Every required one-hole tensor context preserves the designated colimits: it is a composite of tensor sections, and each section has a right adjoint. Thus all axioms of §2 hold.

The resulting homs (12) are the full functor \(2\)-categories of the appropriate orientations. In particular, this model retains the distinction detected by the walking Gray square in item 17.

### Ordinary biclosed arrows are not further identified

There is a second useful model. Start with the ordinary free unit-free biclosed category \(\mathcal B_\Sigma\) and give every probe the constant action
\[
P\triangleright A=A=A\triangleleft P.
\]
Every probe arrow acts by an identity.

A nonempty connected probe diagram then becomes a constant identity diagram. Its colimit is its constant value: compatibility forces every cocone leg to be the same arrow. Hence this is a model of §2.

It follows that there are functors

\mathcal G'_\Sigma

\mathcal B_\Sigma

Equation (14) supplies an erasure map. It is not a classification of its fibers.

## 6. What happens to \(H\), \(H_L\), and interchange

### The structural loops become equalities of outer maps

Tensor is a bifunctor on the data category. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
H
&=(e\otimes1_Y)
  (1_{X'}\otimes d)
  (u\otimes1_{Y'})
  (1_X\otimes v)\\
&=(e\otimes d)(u\otimes v)\\
&=(eu)\otimes(dv)\\
&=1_X\otimes1_Y\\
&=1_T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
\]
The same computation gives
\[
H_L=1_T.
\tag{16}
\]

There is no independent operation in this corrected theory assigning a nonidentity transformation to the two tensor schedules of data maps. Both schedules denote their tensor product.

Accordingly, if the displayed \(\iota,\pi\) are interpreted using tensor functoriality, their interpretations are identity transformations. Their composite is an identity. This does not establish an equation between the original terms in \(\mathcal G\), whose interchanger is a different operation.

Nor does (15) imply that every transformation \(1_T\Rightarrow1_T\), or every automorphism of \(1_T\), is unique.

\qquad
\beta:J\otimes B\to C

Let

\chi:D_2\to J\otimes J

select the generating directed \(2\)-cell of the walking Gray square. Then

F(\chi\otimes1_A):
D_2\otimes A\to C
\tag{17}
\]
is the interchange **modification** between the two schedules of \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\).

This cell need not be invertible, even in the free construction. For any atom \(A\), consider

\chi\otimes1_A:
D_2\otimes A\to(J\otimes J)\otimes A.

**A correction is available using two-sided Gray enrichment.** It has separate hom \(2\)-categories for the two transformation orientations, together with double homs recording their interaction.

There is a necessary change of dimension. When the generating \(1\)-cells denote strict functors, tensoring those functors is bifunctorial, so

Noninvertible Gray interchange then relates two composites of **transformations** by a **modification**. It is not the original \(2\)-cell between two tensor schedules of functors.

Below I give a free unit-free construction \(\mathcal G'_\Sigma\) with an explicit universal property and a model check. This answers the construction part of Question 1. It does **not** decide \(\iota\pi=1_H\) in the original \(\mathcal G\).

## 1. The structure to retain: parameters on both sides

Let \(\mathcal V\) be the ordinary monoidal category of strict \(2\)-categories and strict \(2\)-functors, with the lax Gray tensor. Write
\[
I=D_0,\qquad J=D_1=[1],\qquad D_2
\]
for the point, walking arrow, and walking \(2\)-cell.

For strict \(2\)-categories \(A,C\), retain the entire two-sided family
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb H(A,C)(P,Q)
=
2\mathrm{Cat}(P\otimes A\otimes Q,C).
}
\tag{1}
\]

This is the parameter description of *bienrichment*. Gepner–Heine explicitly use the corresponding family
\[
(P,Q)\longmapsto \operatorname{Map}(P\boxtimes A\boxtimes Q,C)
\]
in §1.5, and describe the Gray self-bienrichment of higher categories in Example 3.2.6. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10504v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The two one-sided restrictions of (1) yield different hom \(2\)-categories:

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
&\text{objects}&\text{arrows}&\text{\(2\)-cells}\\ \hline
\mathbb H_\ell(A,C)
&2\mathrm{Cat}(A,C)
&2\mathrm{Cat}(J\otimes A,C)
&2\mathrm{Cat}(D_2\otimes A,C)\\[2pt]
\mathbb H_r(A,C)
&2\mathrm{Cat}(A,C)
&2\mathrm{Cat}(A\otimes J,C)
&2\mathrm{Cat}(A\otimes D_2,C).
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]

Sources and targets are restrictions along the boundary maps of the probes. Thus, for specified endpoints, one takes the corresponding fibers of the displayed sets.

These are the two functor \(2\)-categories with opposite transformation orientations and with modifications as their \(2\)-cells. The subscripts \(\ell,r\) specify the **position of the parameter**, avoiding a convention-dependent choice of the names “lax” and “oplax.”

### The correct enriched currying laws

The laws are
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb H_r(A\otimes B,C)
\cong
\mathbb H_r(B,A\backslash C)
}
\tag{3}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb H_\ell(A\otimes B,C)
\cong
\mathbb H_\ell(A,C/B).
}
\tag{4}
\]

For example, at a right-hand probe \(Q\), equation (3) is
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\mathrm{Cat}((A\otimes B)\otimes Q,C)
&\cong 2\mathrm{Cat}(A\otimes(B\otimes Q),C)\\
&\cong 2\mathrm{Cat}(B\otimes Q,A\backslash C).
\end{aligned}
\]
The bijections commute with every probe map, including the maps defining boundaries, identities, and composition. Consequently they give isomorphisms of the full hom \(2\)-categories.

For explicit strict \(2\)-categorical formulas, Nikolić gives the enriched lax Gray closure in §4.1, equation (143), and a mixed lax/oplax currying isomorphism in Corollary 2.5, equation (32). [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.12213?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For general two-sided parameters, the formulas instead read
\[
\begin{aligned}
2\mathrm{Cat}(P\otimes A\otimes B\otimes Q,C)
&\cong
2\mathrm{Cat}(B\otimes Q,(P\otimes A)\backslash C),\\
2\mathrm{Cat}(P\otimes A\otimes B\otimes Q,C)
&\cong
2\mathrm{Cat}(P\otimes A,C/(B\otimes Q)).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
\]

Equation (5) explains why attaching two labels to the old hom-category is insufficient: currying must respect the **positions** of the parameters.

## 2. A precise category of unit-free models

To specify “free,” fix the following category of models. This choice is part of the definition of \(\mathcal G'_\Sigma\).

### Probe category

Fix a small monoidal category \(\mathcal P\) of strict \(2\)-category probes which:

- includes \(I,J,D_2\);
- includes their finite globular pasting shapes and Gray products;
- includes the boundary, identity, and composite-selecting maps;
- is closed under chosen finite, nonempty, connected colimits computed in \(2\mathrm{Cat}\).

One can obtain such a small category by repeatedly closing the initial set of probes under these operations. Associators can either be retained or suppressed using a fixed strict monoidal presentation.

The required pasting colimits include
\[
J\amalg_I J,\qquad
D_2\amalg_J D_2,\qquad
D_2\amalg_I D_2,
\tag{6}
\]
for composition of arrows, vertical composition of \(2\)-cells, and horizontal composition of \(2\)-cells, respectively.

### Data types and probe actions

A model has an ordinary monoidal category
\[
\mathcal E=\mathcal P\amalg\mathcal D
\]
with no arrows between the two displayed sorts, subject to these conditions:

1. The tensor on \(\mathcal P\) is the specified probe tensor.
2. A tensor expression is data-sort whenever at least one factor is data-sort.
3. The tensor unit \(I\) is probe-sort. There is no designated data-sort unit.
4. For each data type \(A\), there are chosen adjunctions on \(\mathcal D\):
   \[
   A\otimes-\dashv A\backslash-,
   \qquad
   -\otimes A\dashv -/A.
   \tag{7}
   \]
5. Every tensor context \(W(P)\) with **exactly one varying probe position** and at least one fixed data factor preserves the designated probe colimits.

The last condition means that a designated cocone
\[
P_i\xrightarrow{j_i}P=\operatorname{colim}_i P_i
\]
induces a colimit cocone
\[
W(P_i)\xrightarrow{W(j_i)}W(P)
\]
in \(\mathcal D\).

The restriction to one varying position matters. For example, preservation by \(P\mapsto P\otimes A\otimes Q\), with \(Q\) fixed, is required. Preservation by the diagonal expression \(P\mapsto P\otimes A\otimes P\) is not required.

Also, (7) quantifies over **data types**, not all of \(\mathcal E\). Requiring biclosure throughout \(\mathcal E\) would incorrectly demand
\[
\mathcal E(I,A\backslash C)
\cong
\mathcal E(A,C),
\]
whose left side is empty because the sorts differ.

Morphisms of models fix \(\mathcal P\) and preserve the specified tensor, adjunctions, and probe actions.

## 3. The free construction \(\mathcal G'_\Sigma\)

Let \(\Sigma\) be your signature of atoms and generating arrows between unit-free formulas.

Construct a typed term category as follows.

### Type terms

Data types are generated by
\[
A::=
a
\mid A\otimes A
\mid A\backslash A
\mid A/A
\mid P\triangleright A
\mid A\triangleleft P,
\qquad P\in\mathcal P.
\tag{8}
\]

The last two constructors denote the left and right probe actions. The original formula language is the sublanguage omitting those constructors. There is no data-unit constructor.

### Arrow terms

Generate arrows by:

- the arrows of \(\Sigma\);
- identities and composition;
- all well-typed tensor operations, including probe actions;
- evaluations and currying for the two data adjunctions;
- the following finite gluing operations.

For a designated probe colimit \(P=\operatorname{colim}_iP_i\), an allowed tensor context \(W\), and a compatible family
\[
t_i:W(P_i)\longrightarrow B,
\]
introduce
\[
[t_i]:W(P)\longrightarrow B.
\tag{9}
\]

Compatibility means that for each arrow \(r:P_i\to P_j\) of the indexing diagram,
\[
t_j\,W(r)=t_i.
\]

Impose
\[
[t_i]\,W(j_i)=t_i,
\qquad
[\,hW(j_i)\,]_i=h.
\tag{10}
\]

The remaining equations are the category equations, ordinary monoidal equations, equations of the fixed probes and their actions, and the two adjunctions’ \(\beta\eta\) and naturality equations.

More formally, gluing is a partial algebraic operation whose domain is specified by the finite compatibility equations. Take the least typed congruence closed under these operations and equations. In the quotient, (10) states precisely the required colimit universal property.

### Universal property

The resulting structure satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{models}}(\mathcal G'_\Sigma,M)
\cong
\operatorname{Interpretations}(\Sigma,M).
}
\tag{11}
\]

To prove existence, interpret type and arrow terms recursively. Interpret (9) by the unique arrow supplied by the corresponding colimit in \(M\). Every defining equation holds in \(M\).

To prove uniqueness, a structure-preserving functor is already determined on the signature, probe arrows, tensor, evaluations, and curry operations. Equation (10) then determines its value on each gluing term.

Thus (11) is an explicit free construction **in the category of models specified above**. It does not assert that all possible notions of “biclosed double category” define the same free structure.

## 4. The resulting hom \(2\)-categories and double homs

For any such model, define
\[
\mathbb H(A,B)(P,Q)
=
\mathcal D(P\triangleright A\triangleleft Q,B).
\tag{12}
\]

Composition is fully positional. Given
\[
f:P\otimes A\otimes Q\to B,
\qquad
g:R\otimes B\otimes S\to C,
\]
put
\[
g\star f
=
g(1_R\otimes f\otimes1_S):
(R\otimes P)\otimes A\otimes(Q\otimes S)\to C.
\tag{13}
\]
Associativity follows directly from tensor bifunctoriality and associativity of composition. The identity has parameter pair \((I,I)\).

### Genuine hom \(2\)-categories

Define
\[
\mathbb H_\ell(A,B)_k
=
\mathcal D(D_k\triangleright A,B),
\qquad
\mathbb H_r(A,B)_k
=
\mathcal D(A\triangleleft D_k,B),
\quad k=0,1,2.
\tag{14}
\]

Here is why these are strict \(2\)-categories, rather than just collections of cells.

Given two composable left-parameter transformations, glue their maps along
\[
(J\amalg_I J)\triangleright A.
\]
Then restrict along the map
\[
J\longrightarrow J\amalg_I J
\]
which selects the composite arrow. This defines composition.

The other two pasting colimits in (6) similarly define the two compositions of local \(2\)-cells. Identities come from the probe maps \(J\to I\) and \(D_2\to J\). Associativity, units, and interchange hold because the corresponding composite-selecting maps between strict \(2\)-category probes are equal, and the amalgamations are unique.

The proof of (3)–(4) now works verbatim, with \(\mathcal D\) in place of \(2\mathrm{Cat}\). Therefore \(\mathcal G'_\Sigma\) has the required full hom \(2\)-category currying isomorphisms, with opposite parameter positions.

### A strict double hom

For each \(A,B\), there is also a strict double category with:

| Double-category data | Parameterized maps |
|---|---|
| Vertices | \(\mathcal D(A,B)\) |
| Horizontal arrows | \(\mathcal D(J\triangleright A,B)\) |
| Vertical arrows | \(\mathcal D(A\triangleleft J,B)\) |
| Squares | \(\mathcal D(J\triangleright A\triangleleft J,B)\) |

Boundaries are probe restrictions. Compose squares by gluing in one interval position at a time. Both orders of composing a rectangular grid give the unique amalgamation of the same grid; hence the double-category interchange equation holds.

In the strict \(2\)-category model, these squares are mixed lax/oplax modifications. Fairbanks–Shulman describe this double hom explicitly; see the strict case in their introduction and the general construction in Appendix A, Definition A.7 and Proposition A.8. This establishes **double homs**. It does not assert that both curryings are adjunctions for one global double-category structure with unchanged parameter positions; equation (5) specifies their actual behavior.

## 5. Model check

### The standard Gray model

Take \(\mathcal D\) to be a data-sort copy of strict \(2\)-categories and strict \(2\)-functors. Interpret all tensors and probe actions by the lax Gray tensor, and interpret the residuals by its two internal homs.

Then:

- the two data adjunctions are the ordinary Gray biclosed adjunctions;
- the probe actions have the prescribed associativity;
- tensoring in either variable preserves colimits because it has a right adjoint;
- consequently every allowed one-position tensor context preserves the designated probe colimits.

This proves that the specified model category has the intended Gray model.

Its two hom \(2\)-categories in (14) are exactly the functor \(2\)-categories in (2), so the construction preserves the distinction exposed by your walking-square test.

### The ordinary biclosed syntax remains faithful

There is a second useful model. Let \(\mathcal B_\Sigma\) be the ordinary free unit-free biclosed category and give it constant probe actions:
\[
P\triangleright A=A=A\triangleleft P,
\]
with every probe arrow acting as an identity.

A nonempty connected constant diagram with identity transition maps has its constant value as colimit: compatibility forces all cocone legs to be equal. Hence this model satisfies the gluing axioms.

We obtain functors
\[
\mathcal B_\Sigma
\xrightarrow{j}
\mathcal G'_\Sigma
\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}
\mathcal B_\Sigma,
\qquad
\varepsilon j=1.
\tag{15}
\]

Thus adjoining the probe structure introduces no extra equalities between ordinary biclosed arrow terms. The map \(\varepsilon\) also supplies a precise meaning for “over a fixed arrow of \(\mathcal B_\Sigma\)” in this particular corrected theory.

## 6. What happens to \(H\), \(H_L\), \(\iota\), and \(\pi\)?

The outer arrows of \(\mathcal G'_\Sigma\) tensor bifunctorially. Therefore, with your notation,
\[
\begin{aligned}

&=(e\otimes1_Y)(1_{X'}\otimes d)
  (u\otimes1_{Y'})(1_X\otimes v)\\

\]

\boxed{H_L=1_T.}

This uses neither strict mixed currying nor invertibility of \(u\) or \(v\).

The old interchanger between two tensor schedules of **data arrows** is not an independent constructor of \(\mathcal G'_\Sigma\). Those schedules already denote the same data arrow. Their tensor-functoriality comparison is the degenerate transformation.

Equation (18) is a statement about this correction. It supplies no equality between the corresponding terms of the original \(\mathcal G\).

## 7. Where directed interchange survives

Let
\[
\alpha:J\triangleright A\to B,
\qquad
\beta:J\triangleright B\to C
\]
be two left-parameter transformations. They determine
\[
F=
\beta(1_J\triangleright\alpha):
(J\otimes J)\triangleright A\to C.
\tag{19}
\]

Let
\[
\chi:D_2\to J\otimes J
\]
select the directed Gray square. Restriction gives
\[
F(\chi\triangleright1_A):
D_2\triangleright A\to C.
\tag{20}
\]

This is the interchange **modification** between the two transformation schedules.

It need not be invertible, even in the free construction. Indeed,
\[
\chi\triangleright1_A:
D_2\triangleright A
\longrightarrow
(J\otimes J)\triangleright A
\tag{21}
\]
is a local \(2\)-cell in
\[
\mathbb H_\ell\bigl(A,(J\otimes J)\triangleright A\bigr).
\]
Interpret the atom \(A\) as the terminal \(2\)-category in the standard model. Equation (21) becomes the generating \(2\)-cell of the walking Gray square, which has no inverse. Therefore (21) has no inverse in \(\mathcal G'_\Sigma\).

The dimensions are consequently:

| Syntax in the corrected structure | Standard interpretation |
|---|---|
| Data type | Strict \(2\)-category |
| Data arrow | Strict \(2\)-functor |
| Left- or right-\(J\)-parameter map | Transformation of the corresponding orientation |
| Left- or right-\(D_2\)-parameter map | Modification |
| Interchange of two data-arrow tensor schedules | Equality of functors |
| Interchange of two transformation schedules | A potentially noninvertible modification |

This also explains why a strict \(2\)-category with two classes of transformations is insufficient for the full intended semantics: composition of transformations introduces the directed square (20), requiring the next dimension. Gepner–Heine identify precisely this failure of strict interchange in Remark 3.2.3. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10504v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Thus **\(\mathcal G'_\Sigma\) gives a free, unit-free correction for the external-functor interpretation**, with both full oriented hom \(2\)-categories and their mixed double homs. In it, \(H\) and \(H_L\) are identities, and noninvertible interchange survives between transformations. Preserving the original directed schedules of the \(f_i\) requires assigning those generators a different semantic degree or choosing a different doctrine; changing only the labels on the old \(2\)-cells does not accomplish that.

The idempotent and forward-uniqueness questions in the original \(\mathcal G\), and the proposed product classification, remain undecided here.
