# Finite certificates for invertible cells in free Gray-enriched ω-categories

## Setting

**Gray-enriched ω-categories.** Let ω-Cat be the category of strict ω-categories, with the (lax) Gray tensor product ⊗ of Ara–Maltsiniotis (*Joint et tranches pour les ∞-catégories strictes*, Mém. SMF 165, 2020; arXiv:1607.00668). A *Gray ω-category* is a category enriched in (ω-Cat, ⊗). In such a K:
- inside each hom K(a, b), cells compose as in a strict ω-category;
- composition across objects, K(a, b) ⊗ K(b, c) → K(a, c), sends the top cell of x ⊗ y (for cells x, y of positive dimension) to a *directed interchanger*, which in general is not invertible.

The weak analogue is an oriented (∞,ω)-category in the sense of Gepner–Heine (arXiv:2510.10504), enriched in Cat_ω for the Gray tensor.

**Invertibility (coinductive).** A cell f : x → y of dimension n ≥ 1 is *invertible* if there are cells g, g′ : y → x and invertible (n+1)-cells l : g ; f → 1_y and r : f ; g′ → 1_x. Here f ; g is composition along the (n−1)-boundary in *diagrammatic* order (first f, then g), used throughout. The definition is coinductive: the invertibility of l and r is again of this kind (Cheng; Rice; Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara; Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv:2406.12127).

**Marked computads.** A *marked computad* is a finite polygraph (computad) C together with a set M of its generators of positive dimension, called *marked*. Let F(C, M) be the Gray ω-category freely generated by C in which every marked generator is invertible. Concretely: adjoin, for each marked generator m, generators m⁻ˡ, m⁻ʳ, λ_m : m⁻ˡ ; m → 1, ρ_m : m ; m⁻ʳ → 1, then mark λ_m and ρ_m and repeat. This is the Gray analogue of the computads with invertible generators of Benjamin–Champin–Markakis (arXiv:2606.30254). The weak version of F(C, M) is defined the same way in oriented (∞,ω)-categories.

**Certified cells.** Cert(C, M) is the *smallest* set of cells of F(C, M) that is closed under the following rules:
1. **Marked generators.** Every marked generator m is in Cert, and so are m⁻ˡ, m⁻ʳ, λ_m and ρ_m.
2. **Identities and coherences.** Every identity is in Cert. In the weak setting, every coherence cell whose source and target have the same image in the strict quotient is in Cert.
3. **Composites.** A composite (pasting) of cells inside a hom is in Cert if each of its top-dimensional constituents is in Cert.
4. **Gray cells.** Take an elementary Gray cell: the image of the top cell of x₁ ⊗ … ⊗ x_k under iterated composition across objects. It is in Cert if some xᵢ of positive dimension (as a cell of its hom) is in Cert, or if every xᵢ has dimension 0 in its hom (is a 1-cell of K) and all of them are in Cert.
5. **Inverses.** If f is in Cert, so is any chosen inverse g of f together with the cells witnessing it.
6. **inv-intro.** If f : x → y and there are cells g, g′ : y → x and l : g ; f → 1_y, r : f ; g′ → 1_x with l and r in Cert, then f is in Cert.

Every certified cell is invertible.

**What is known.**
- *Ordinary (non-Gray) case, no marks.* A cell of the free weak ω-category on a computad is invertible iff its top-dimensional support consists of invertible cells, and coherence cells of dimension above the computad's dimension are invertible (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv:2406.12127, Thm 33 and Prop 37).
- *Ordinary case, with marks: finite certificates suffice.* Every cell of dimension greater than dim C is in Cert: by induction on the term, its top constituents have the same dimension, and identities, coherences and inverses are certified. So if f is invertible, the coinductive tower (l, r, then their witnesses, …) reaches certified cells after at most dim C − dim f + 1 levels, and rule 6 applied finitely often certifies f.
- *Gray case.* That argument fails. Directed interchangers of every dimension above dim C exist and are not invertible: in the free Gray ω-category on two composable non-invertible 2-cells across an object, the 3-cell interchanger has no inverse.
- *Decidability.* Membership in Cert is not decidable uniformly in (C, M). It contains the unit problem of finitely presented monoids: one object, the generators as 1-cells, each relation u = v as a marked 2-cell u ⇒ v. That problem is undecidable (Narendran–Ó'Dúnlaing–Otto, Discrete Math. 98 (1991)).

## Questions

1. **(Main question.)** For every finite marked computad (C, M), is every invertible cell of F(C, M), the free Gray ω-category, in Cert(C, M)? Answer for the strict Gray ω-categories above, and for the weak (oriented (∞,ω)) version. If the answer differs between the two, say where.

2. **(If not.)** Give a finite marked computad and an invertible cell of F(C, M) outside Cert(C, M). Then give the smallest extension of rules 1–6 that is complete, for example a rule that takes a single term built over a generic "walking invertible cell" context as a finite description of an infinite tower, in the style of a recursor.

3. **(Characterization.)** Characterize the invertible cells of F(C, M) in terms of supports and marks, in the spirit of the Benjamin–Markakis criterion: for example, by a condition on the top-dimensional constituents of a normal form, relative to the congruence generated by M. Is there a characterization that separates the decidable part (rules 1–5) from the undecidable part (rule 6)?

4. **(Pseudo cells.)** Call a cell t of a hom K(a, b), of dimension ≥ 1 as a cell of that hom, *pseudo* if, for every cell y of positive dimension in a hom K(c, a), the Gray cell of y ⊗ t is invertible. Is t pseudo as soon as the Gray cells y ⊗ t are invertible for the *generic* such y, that is, for y the top cell of a disk Dᵏ attached at a, for every k ≥ 1? Is k = 1 enough when t has dimension 2 (a transformation)? Answer for the free Gray ω-categories F(C, M) and in general.
