# Which polygraphs are homotopy polygraphs? The exact degree, and one class containing the decidable ones

*A self-contained problem. It assumes familiarity with polygraphs (computads), strict ω-categories and (∞,ω)-categories, and nothing else. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked, except item 17, which is marked as evidence.*

**What we are asking for.** The exact last-step criterion is known in every dimension (item 25). Homotopicality of finite polygraphs is truth-table hard with a Π⁰₂ upper bound (items 23, 27, 28). The new results give Π⁰₂-completeness only on a supplied family of poles (item 30). They also give four generator-level classes with terminating tests: single-transfer, one-destination, resource-monotone, and binary with bounded milestones (items 31–35). We now ask for the **exact degree for finite polygraphs**, and for **one natural class at the level of generators that contains all four**, with its exact complexity and uniform proofs in every dimension. Isolated examples or one more special family are not what we want.

## 1. Setting

- A **polygraph** Γ (computad) is a set of generators of each dimension, each n-generator having a source and a target which are parallel (n−1)-cells of the free strict ω-category on the lower generators. All polygraphs here are finite.
- `F(Γ)` is the free strict ω-category on Γ.
- `Free(Γ)` is the free (∞,ω)-category on Γ: generators are attached one at a time along their boundaries by pushouts of `∂Dⁿ → Dⁿ` in (∞,ω)-categories (Loubaton's Cat_ω, arXiv 2406.05425, or Campion's framework, arXiv 2311.00200). Strict ω-categories are (∞,ω)-categories, so there is a canonical comparison `c_Γ : Free(Γ) → F(Γ)`.
- Γ is a **homotopy polygraph** (or *homotopical*) when c_Γ is an equivalence, i.e. when every strict pushout attaching a generator is also a homotopy pushout.
- A 2-cell `1_x ⇒ 1_x` of F(Γ) other than the identity is a **bubble**.
- Γ is **positive in every dimension** when every generator of dimension n ≥ 2 has a source and a target that are not identity cells. (For n = 2: both boundary paths are nonempty.)
- More generally, a non-identity n-cell `e : 1^{n−1−k}_c ⇒ 1^{n−1−k}_c` (n ≥ 2, source and target both the (n−1)-dimensional iterated identity of a k-cell c, k ≤ n−2) is an **iterated-identity endo-cell**. Γ is **bubble-free in every dimension** when F(Γ) has none. For n = 2, k = 0 these are the bubbles.
- A strict ω-functor p : C → D is a **discrete Conduché functor** when every factorisation of p(x) in D lifts uniquely to a factorisation of x in C.

## 2. Why it matters

Homotopy polygraphs are exactly the computads whose free (∞,ω)-category can be computed strictly. Finite presentations of Grothendieck constructions over a free base, the comparison of weak and strict free objects in type theories for weak ω-categories, and pasting theorems for non-globular shapes all need this hypothesis, and at present each use checks it by hand. Dimension 2 is settled exactly (item 5 below), necessity of bubble-freeness is settled in every dimension (item 14), and bubble-freeness alone is not sufficient from dimension 3 (item 18).

## 3. What is known

1. **Pasting theorems.** For a strongly loop-free Steiner complex K, ν(K) is the free (∞,ω)-category on its atoms (Campion, *Pasting diagrams in higher categories are free*, arXiv 2311.00200). Chanavat (*A strengthened (∞,n)-categorical pasting theorem*, arXiv 2603.22242) extends this to regular directed complexes with frame-acyclic molecules (Thm 3.17), and deduces that positive 2-polygraphs (Cor. 3.39) and regular 3-polygraphs in Henry's sense (Cor. 3.38) are homotopical. Comment 3.40 there describes the extension to arbitrary regular and positive polygraphs as expected, without proof.
2. **Bubbles obstruct.** If F(Γ) has a bubble q, sending every 2-generator to 1 gives r : F(Γ) → B²ℕ with r(q) = k > 0, and q defines i : B²ℕ → F(Γ) by Eckmann–Hilton; ri = [k] acts by kʲ on H_{2j}(K(ℤ,2); ℚ), so the groupoidification of F(Γ) has nonzero rational homology in every even degree, while that of Free(Γ) is a finite CW complex. Example: (x; σ : 1_x ⇒ 1_x), F(Γ) = B²ℕ ≃ K(ℤ,2) after groupoidification, Free(Γ) ≃ S².
3. **Bubble-freeness is undecidable**, already for one-vertex 2-polygraphs with no generating bubble (reduction from reachability in positive semi-Thue systems via an insertion rule 1 ⇒ L w Z and a deletion rule L v Z ⇒ 1).
4. **Dimension 2, combinatorial core.** For a progressive one-colour string diagram G every component of which meets the boundary, the cell complex K_G whose vertices are the *tier encodings* of G (decompositions into layers of simultaneous generators) and whose faces are products of permutohedra (splitting a layer) has contractible components. Proof: right-exchange runs (which terminate for boundary-connected diagrams, Delpeuch–Vicary arXiv 1804.07832, Cor. 2.16) give a unique maximal cell F(v) with source v; deleting vertices in topological order removes a closed star along its antistar, a ball. Two floating scalars give a circle, so the boundary condition is needed.
5. **Dimension 2, exact answer.** A finite 2-polygraph is homotopical **iff it is bubble-free**. The "if" direction uses item 4 and Heidemann's mesh model of (∞,2)-categories (arXiv 2411.04870): a presheaf X_Γ on meshes with L₂ j*X_Γ ≃ Free(Γ), and an objectwise equivalence j*X_Γ → N₂F(Γ) computed as X_Γ(Q) ≃ B(Q↓t) ≃ ⊔_G BP_G over the diagrams G of shape Q, with each BP_G contractible by item 4 (a bubble-free polygraph has no boundary-disjoint diagram component and no diagram automorphisms). Consequences: homotopicality of finite 2-polygraphs is undecidable (item 3); the finite case of Guetta's conjecture (homologically coherent ⟺ bubble-free, *Homology of strict ω-categories*, arXiv 2104.12662, Conj. 6.6.5) holds; every 2-polygraph whose 2-generators all have non-empty target (or all non-empty source) is homotopical, with any number of vertices.
6. **Suspensions.** Σ^k Γ is homotopical iff Γ is (Hom_{ΣC}(0,1) ≃ C, and suspension preserves and detects the comparison). So for 2-polygraphs, Σ^k Γ is homotopical iff Γ is bubble-free.
7. **Graph assembly, every dimension.** For a finite directed graph Q (loops and parallel edges allowed) and finite polygraphs P_e on its edges, the polygraph B_Q(P_•) with vertices Q₀ and (r+1)-generators ⊔_e (P_e)_r (the free category on Q enriched in the F(P_e)) is homotopical iff every P_e is. In particular **adjoining a 1-generator preserves homotopicality in every dimension**.
8. **Discrete Conduché descent (Theorem A).** If p : Γ → Δ is a polygraph map with F(p) discrete Conduché and Δ homotopical, then Γ is homotopical. Surjectivity on generators alone is not enough ([1] → F(loop)).
9. **Typing into positive templates (Theorem B).** A polygraph map Γ → Δ into a homotopical Δ that is source-positive or target-positive in every dimension makes Γ homotopical (Henry's cartesianness for such polygraphs makes F(p) discrete Conduché).
10. **Mapping cylinders (Theorem C).** Strict double mapping cylinders of finite homotopical presentations along arbitrary strict functors have finite homotopical presentations, with generators Δ_n ⊔ ℰ_n ⊔ Γ_{n−1}; Gray cylinders, cones and suspensions are instances (Loubaton arXiv 2406.05425 Thm 2.3.3.22, Campion's pasting theorem for Dⁿ ⊗ [1]). Items 7–10 give a sound finite-certificate system, but typing into templates from a good class cannot leave that class, so it does not reach all positive polygraphs.
11. **Positivity is not necessary.** (∗; t; η : 1 ⇒ t) is homotopical (Blans–Linskens, arXiv 2605.03150), and so is its suspension, a non-positive 3-polygraph. Regularity is not necessary either: the positive non-regular 3-polygraph x, y, z; p, q : x → y, r : y → z; α, β : p ⇒ q; ζ : α ▷ r ⇛ β ▷ r is homotopical.
12. **Positivity in the top dimension alone is not enough.** One vertex, b : 1 ⇒ 1, Ω : b ⇛ b: Ω has non-identity boundaries, but b is a bubble.
13. **Strict versus "stricter".** Chanavat (*Homotopy theory of stricter n-categories*, arXiv 2509.26563, Comment 2.67) gives a 4-dimensional regular directed complex whose molecules do not form a polygraph under strict composition. This is **not** a counterexample to the positive case: the extra equations appear in the passage from the free strict category to molecules, not in the free strict category itself.

14. **Higher bubbles obstruct in every dimension.** If a nonidentity n-cell is an endomorphism of an iterated identity on any k-cell, k ≤ n − 2, then the strict n-skeleton has nonzero mod-p cohomology in arbitrarily high degrees for some prime p. The free cellular n-skeleton has cohomological dimension at most n, and homotopicality passes to skeleta. This covers composite bubbles and positive-dimensional bases. (Proof: the inclusion of the n-skeleton is discrete Conduché, so homotopicality passes to it; counting n-generators gives r : F(Γ_{≤n}) → Bⁿℤ, the endo-cell q gives i : Σ^{n−2}B²ℕ → F(Γ_{≤n}) with r i of degree m = r(q) > 0 on the bottom class; for an odd prime p ∤ m the iterated reduced powers P^{p^{j−1}}⋯P^1 of the pulled-back fundamental class restrict to m σ^{n−2}(x^{p^j}) ≠ 0.) So bubble-freeness in every dimension is necessary.

15. **Groupoidification does not recognise homotopicality from dimension 3.** Π = (x; a : 1_x ⇒ 1_x; ε : a ⇛ 1_{1_x}) is not homotopical (its 2-skeleton has the bubble a, and homotopicality passes to skeleta), yet both groupoidifications are contractible. Free(Π) groupoidifies to S² ∪_{deg 1} D³. In F(Π) the hom on 1_{1_x} is the free commutative monoid in Cat on (a, ε : a → 0), whose morphisms reduce to ε^{⊗k} ⊗ 1_{a^n}, so it is the poset (ℕ, ≥) with terminal object 0. Π is also homologically coherent. Suspensions give such examples in every dimension ≥ 3.
16. **Positive-to-one polygraphs are homotopical.** If every d-generator (d ≥ 2) has a generating (d−1)-cell as target and a non-identity source, Γ is homotopical. The proof: Zawadowski (arXiv 0708.2658, Cor. 13.5) identifies such polygraphs with presheaves on positive opetopes, which are acyclic atoms forming a full sieve (Leclerc; Hadzihasanovic); then Chanavat's Cor. 3.27 applies (directed complexes with acyclic atoms generate homotopy polygraphs). The same holds after choosing, independently in each dimension, source or target as the generator side (globular opposites). This gives a decidable sufficient test.
17. **The combinatorial core in dimension 3 (evidence, not proved).** Following item 4 one dimension up, fix a 3-dimensional diagram G (a strict 3-cell of F(Γ) together with a representing composite). Its *3-tier sequences* (decompositions into layers of simultaneous 3-steps) are ordered by refinement; call the poset Π_G. When no 3-step has an idle boundary wire (every wire in the source region of a step is touched by it), Π_G appears to be the poset of braid-fan cells inside an open convex cone cut out by a partial order on the 3-steps, hence contractible: checked by computer on about 550 random diagrams of that kind. The step "two 3-steps that can each fire first can fire together" fails in general (item 18). The true fibre also contains *interchange slices* (2-dimensional exchanges between 3-steps), which make it an infinite category. Truncating at K interchange slices is not homotopy-correct (K = 1 already gives a wedge of circles).

18. **Bubble-free does not suffice in dimension 3.** Objects 0 → 1 → 2 → 3, edges f₀, f₁ : 0 → 1, g : 1 → 2, h₀, h₁ : 2 → 3, 2-generators α₀, α₁ : f₀ ⇒ f₁, β₀, β₁ : h₀ ⇒ h₁, 3-generators A : α₀g ⇛ α₁g and B : gβ₀ ⇛ gβ₁. Γ is positive in every dimension, has an acyclic 1-graph and is bubble-free in every dimension (occurrence counts), but the fibre of Free(Γ) over the unique strict 3-cell α₀gβ₀ ⇛ α₁gβ₁ is S¹: the hom from 0 to 3 is (D × Q) ⊔_{Q×Q} (Q × D), with Q the walking parallel pair and D the walking 2-cell. In item 17's terms, A and B overlap on the idle wire g, so each can fire first but they cannot fire together, and the two serial orders are joined by two different exchanges. Further known facts for positive 3-polygraphs (all generating faces non-identity):
    - If no idle right suffix of a generating 3-face equals an idle left prefix of a generating 3-face, every fibre is contractible, so Γ is homotopical. For width-preserving generators this matching test is exact.
    - Every weight-two fibre is a point, an interval or a circle, and a circle occurs exactly when some path is an idle suffix of **both** faces of one generator and an idle prefix of **both** faces of another.
    - For every k ≥ 2 there is a positive 3-polygraph with a full-support fibre ≃ S^{k−1} ("seam rings") whose weight-two fibres are all contractible, so no bounded-weight test is exact.

19. **An exact criterion from strict cells, every dimension.** For n ≥ 3 let P be the set-valued rectangular n-fold array NF(Γ_{<n}) ⊔_{∐_z N∂Dⁿ} ∐_z NDⁿ (z ranging over the n-generators). Iterating the necklace formula of Barkan–Steinebrunner (arXiv 2301.08650, Thm A and Prop. 2.16) one direction at a time, with Thomason's theorem, gives ordinary categories A(c) over each new strict n-cell c whose classifying spaces are the fibres of Free(Γ) → F(Γ). So Γ is homotopical iff it is bubble-free in every dimension and every A(c) has connected, simply connected and acyclic nerve (a finite edge-word and integral-cycle test per challenge). This puts homotopicality in Π⁰₂. It is decidable with the jump of a bubble-freeness oracle; whether the bubble-freeness oracle alone suffices is open.

20. **A finite last-step criterion in dimension 3.** For a bubble-free 3-polygraph, introduce a marker h_z parallel to each 3-generator z. A chart is a strict 2-cell Q over the 2-skeleton and the markers; [Q] performs all marked changes. For a strict 3-cell c of positive weight, L(c) has one simplex with vertex set I for each strict factorization c = [Q] ∘ p with slot set I ≠ ∅, and its face on J ⊆ I performs I∖J first (appended to p) and keeps J. Then **a finite 3-polygraph is homotopical iff it is bubble-free in every dimension and every L(c) is contractible** (connected, trivial edge-path group, zero reduced integral homology). Each L(c) is finite and effectively computable, non-positive generators are allowed, and the proof is Quillen's Theorem A applied to "last chart and prefix" from the category of chart strings, by induction on weight. Examples: the middle wire of item 18 has L(c) a circle; the two- and three-seam rings give a square boundary and an octahedron boundary; a repeated deletion a a → 1 has two distinct prefixes joined by an edge (so the prefix must be kept).

21. **A sufficient condition for positive polygraphs, every dimension.** For a positive presentation of dimension n = d + 1 ≥ 3, form the d-skeleton with one parallel d-marker per top generator; its slots are canonical by Henry's representation theorem (arXiv 1711.00744). If the marked lower presentation passes recursively and, for every positive-weight strict c, the one-slot final charts with their prefixes are the vertices of a single simplex of final choices (every nonempty subset occurs for exactly one (p, Q)), then Γ is homotopical; more generally it suffices that every L(c) is contractible. This recovers item 16 (positive-to-one) and the no-matching case of item 18.

22. **No bounded test.** A uniform weight bound on the strict cells to test would contradict the seam rings of item 18, so any finite criterion must be at the level of generators (or of some finite structure other than weight-bounded cells).

23. **Undecidability, even with positivity promised (2026-11-20).** Homotopicality is undecidable (co-r.e.-complete) on finite positive 3-polygraphs with five 3-generators, each occurring at most once in any strict 3-cell, and on finite positive n-polygraphs for each n ≥ 3 (suspension, item 6). Reduction: take the two-seam ring S of item 18 (lanes s₀, p₁, s₁, p₂, s₂; changes a₁, b₁, a₂, b₂, z; one full-support label with circle fibre, all proper-support labels contractible) and insert a *passive strip* between p₁ and s₁ with edges Q, U and a tape on a private vertex m, with ordinary 2-gates Q ⇒ l w r, the rules x ⇒ y of a positive semi-Thue system T, and l v r ⇒ U; insert 1_U on both faces of b₁ and 1_Q on both faces of z. An *erasure lemma* identifies every chart-string complex of the result G(T,w,v) with that of its image in S, so **G(T,w,v) is homotopical iff w ↛_T* v**. Every strict 3-cell has top weight ≤ 5, so the unbounded information sits in the lower diagram. Consequences: no finite generator-level test (Question 1 of the previous round is answered negatively); no sound and complete r.e. system of finite certificates of homotopicality, even on positive inputs; the general problem is 0′-hard and (item 19) at most 0″. On this family one query to a bubble-freeness oracle on another presentation suffices, so the oracle question is still open in general.
24. **The naive last-step criterion fails in dimension 4 (2026-11-20).** The positive 4-polygraph with the 2-skeleton of item 18, 3-generators A₀, A₁ : α₀ ⇛ α₁ and B : gβ₀ ⇛ gβ₁, and one 4-generator Z : A₀g ⇛ A₁g has homotopical lower skeleta (no-matching test of item 18) and every L(c) a single vertex (every nonidentity strict 4-cell has weight one), yet one fibre is S¹. The circle lives *inside one marked step*: the marked 3-presentation M₁ (old generators plus a marker C₁ : α₀g ⇛ α₁g parallel to the boundary of Z) contains the middle wire of item 18 (C₁ and B overlap on the idle wire g). So the recursive hypothesis of item 21 (the marked lower presentations must themselves pass) has content, and Question 3 of the previous round is false as stated. Checked: `tools/homotopy_polygraph_guarded_ring.py` (96 chart strings, Betti (1,1), for both tape orders; 97 proper strings, Betti (1)) and `tools/homotopy_polygraph_unary_chart.py` (17 declarations, a full-support 4-term, Agda certificate type-checked).

25. **The exact last-step criterion, every dimension (2026-11-20).** Fix a rank n = d + 1 with homotopical ordinary d-skeleton B = F(Γ_{≤d}). Let M_a extend Γ_{≤d} by markers h_{z,i} (1 ≤ i ≤ a) parallel to each n-generator z. A *time simplex* σ of length a is a strict d-cell of F(M_a); a marker with index i changes on the edge i−1 → i, and restriction along an ordinal map substitutes the corresponding face of z where the pulled-back cut is constant. σ is *normal* if every edge has a change. For a positive-weight strict n-cell c, D(c) has objects (p, σ) with σ nonempty normal and c = [σ] ∘ p (p a strict prefix), and arrows given by lists 0 ≤ i₀ < ⋯ < i_b = a (restrict the last simplex, absorb the initial segment into the prefix). The coefficient E(σ) is the classifying space of the category over σ given by the horizontal necklace recursion of item 19 applied to NB ⊔ ∐ N∂D^d → ∐ ND^d → NF(M_a), **with its restriction maps**. Then the comparison fibre is X(c) ≃ hocolim over strings of beads, and X(c) ≃ Λ(c) = hocolim_{(p,σ) ∈ D(c)} E(σ) when all smaller-weight fibres are contractible. **A finite bubble-free polygraph is homotopical iff every Λ(c) is contractible, for every positive-weight strict cell in every rank n ≥ 3.** Each Λ(c) at a specified cell is finite and effective; no positivity is needed. Omitting the face maps between coefficients is what makes item 24's naive criterion fail.
26. **Decidable classes so far (2026-11-20).** Finiteness of the strict cells through any rank is decidable. On presentations with finitely many strict cells whose tested complexes are graphs or have abelian fundamental group by the displayed presentation, the criterion is a two-sided algorithm; this includes every finite-strict-cell polygraph of top weight ≤ 2 in any dimension (each D(c) is then a finite graph and the test is "is a tree"), and the class is closed under graph assembly (item 7) and suspension. For one-resource semaphore rings with arbitrary finite control graphs the presentation is homotopical iff at most two processes admit an excursion (acquire then later release), a reachability test. For two-resource rings without excursions it fails iff four processes can be assigned transfer directions with both represented (fibre S^{N−3}). For reversal-bounded controls (no strongly connected component both acquires and releases one resource) there is an effective finite list of complexes whose contractibility is equivalent to homotopicality, so the property is r.e. there; at fixed resource count and reversal bound a nonuniform decision procedure exists by Dickson's lemma. At bounded top weight, a single halting query decides homotopicality of positive 3-polygraphs (degree exactly 0′ there).
27. **Σ⁰₁-hardness (2026-11-20).** For every nonempty finite connected simplicial complex K there is an effective positive rank-10 polygraph with finitely many strict cells, homotopical lower comparisons and a single top fibre type of homotopy type |K|; it is homotopical iff K is contractible. So homotopicality is Σ⁰₁-complete on finite-strict-cell inputs. One fixed positive 4-polygraph U with 15 generators has Σ⁰₁-complete local problem "is the fibre of this strict 4-cell contractible?" and realises every finite connected homotopy type as a fibre.
28. **Truth-table hardness (2026-11-20).** For K as above and a machine M there is an effective positive rank-10 presentation G(K, M) with G(K, M) homotopical ⟺ (M never halts) ∨ (|K| contractible). Hence every set truth-table reducible to 0′ many-one reduces to homotopicality, and no computable approximation with a fixed finite number of mind changes decides it. Failures (a bubble, a disconnected test complex, nonzero reduced homology, a nontrivial finite quotient of an edge group) are semidecidable on all inputs, and there is no computable bound, as a function of the presentation, on the least weight of a failing fibre.
29. **Group tests (2026-11-20).** Three time colours suffice to present the fundamental groupoid of each fibre; the group of the binary-chart complex embeds in a finitely generated right-angled Artin group, and the full group is its quotient by the ternary hexagons; if the binary complex has cyclic H₁, every quotient is abelian. Homology of the marked lower presentation is necessary in positive ranks. For the abstract model of programs producing finite cube lists, "all fibres contractible" is Π⁰₂-complete, but this has not been realised by finite polygraphs.

*Items 30–35 use the typed semaphore rings (positive presentations whose processes run finite control graphs of acquire and release instructions on counted resources) and the bounded-box construction defined in full in Responses 9 and 16 of `notes/conjectures/answered/homotopy-polygraphs-r5-2026-11-20.md`; the proofs are in `notes/conjectures/answered/homotopy-polygraphs-r6-2026-11-20.md`.*

30. **Π⁰₂ on supplied poles, and why positive typing cannot select them (2026-11-20).**
    - In one fixed positive rank-six presentation V there is, for each machine d, a decidable set of pairs of rank-four poles such that every selected hom passes iff d is total. So the comparison test on that uniformly given family is Π⁰₂-complete.
    - Every positive generator-preserving lift of a complete routed extreme label already has a weight-three circle at other poles, even when the selected complex is a point. So positive typing cannot enforce that pole selection.
    - A positive rank-eleven request tree with bounded-support relays forces every incidence comparison of a board. Its whole-presentation test is contractibility of the prescribed complex K, which makes the test Σ⁰₁-complete on a supplied family of boards, one per K. If instead the incidence comparisons are independently optional, the whole presentation passes iff the non-incidence relation D has an isolated vertex or maximum matching at most 2, a linear-time test.
31. **Single-transfer controls (2026-11-20).** Consider typed semaphore rings in which every control walk acquires at most one resource and releases at most one other. Record an edge r → s for a process when a walk acquires r before releasing s.
    - The presentation fails iff some choice of one possible edge per process has a strong component with at least two more edges than vertices.
    - Each fibre is a product of spheres S^{n−m−1} over fully guarded strong components, and every finite product of positive-dimensional spheres occurs.
    - Recognition is coNP-complete (3-SAT on two-branch controls). Failure has a certificate with at most R + 2 processes, a bound attained for every R ≥ 3.
    - Recognition is fixed-parameter tractable in the resource count R.
32. **One destination per process (2026-11-20).** On every walk all acquisitions precede all releases, and all releases use one resource distinct from the acquired ones. The time reversal of this class is covered too.
    - Every fibre is a product of spheres, given by a sink formula: S^{n_C−|C|−1} for a guarded sink strong component with no external-only arrival.
    - Recognition is coNP-complete, with circle witnesses on at most R + 2 moving processes.
    - If each process may choose its class individually, specified-fibre contractibility is r.e.-complete.
33. **Resource-monotone presentations (2026-11-20).** No walk of one process both acquires and releases one resource.
    - Whole-presentation homotopicality is decidable by integral homology alone, by a two-subcomplex induction on attainable records with a free-fundamental-group argument.
    - On the same class, contractibility of a specified fibre is r.e.-complete and every finite connected homotopy type occurs.
    - The algorithm uses at most 2^R Σ_k R!/(R−k)! encodings per axis, and the problem is coNP-hard.
34. **Binary resources with bounded milestones (2026-11-20).** Some resources are used by at most two processes; on each of the others, no control strong component both acquires and releases it.
    - Homotopicality is truth-table reducible to 0′, with the query list computable in advance.
    - Erasing every instruction except the zero-crossings ("milestones") of the non-binary resources preserves homotopy type, after taking transitive closure.
    - On a second class, failure is r.e. In that class mixed roles and unbounded reversals are allowed, with at most one initial-only and one final-only role per resource and assignable counts at most two.
    - The oracle is needed for the table queries. With ten binary resources, occurrence of one fixed 100-milestone table is r.e.-complete, by a two-counter machine with request and acknowledgement locks, although every such input is homotopical.
35. **Closure.** Each class in items 31–34 is recognized by finite reachability on the control graphs and is closed under suspension and graph assembly (item 7).

## 4. Questions

**Question 1 (main: the exact degree).** Decide whether homotopicality of finite polygraphs is decidable relative to the halting problem (Δ⁰₂), or prove it Π⁰₂-complete.
- A Π⁰₂ lower bound needs one finite presentation per machine that realises the universal input quantifier. Item 30 does this on supplied poles, and shows that positive typing cannot select them. Say whether a non-positive presentation, or a different routing, can enforce the selection.
- A Δ⁰₂ upper bound needs a bound, computable from a halting oracle, on the records to examine. Item 34 gives one when only the non-binary resources have bounded milestones. Say whether a milestone-type reduction extends to all resources.
- Item 34's r.e.-complete table attainability is a candidate source of hardness. Say whether the topology can be made to detect one fixed table in the presence of all other records.
- If neither is reachable, settle the question on positive presentations.

**Question 2 (one class containing the decidable ones).** Items 31–34 give four generator-level classes.
- Find one natural class, defined by finite checks on the generators and closed under suspension and graph assembly, that contains all four and on which item 25's criterion becomes a terminating test. Give the algorithm and its exact complexity.
- On the monotone class (item 33), is recognition coNP-complete, or harder?
- Does homology alone decide homotopicality on every class where the two-subcomplex induction of item 33 applies?
- Give the exact boundary between classes where whole-presentation homotopicality is decidable and classes where it is undecidable.

**Question 3 (the fibres).** On the single-transfer and one-destination classes every fibre is a product of spheres.
- Characterise the homotopy types of fibres on the monotone and binary classes, and on the smallest class where non-sphere-product fibres occur.
- Is there a natural class whose fibres are exactly the wedges or products of spheres, or exactly the finite complexes with free fundamental group?

## 5. What counts as a solution

An exact placement in the arithmetic hierarchy; one decidable class at the level of generators containing the four of items 31–34, with its exact complexity and a uniform proof in every dimension; a characterisation of the fibres that occur on a natural class; or a published reference.
