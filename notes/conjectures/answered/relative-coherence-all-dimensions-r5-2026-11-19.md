# Relative coherence in CaTT in every dimension: free weak ω-categories are strict exactly on homotopical computads

*A self-contained problem. It assumes familiarity with globular weak ω-categories, strict ω-categories, polygraphs (computads) and Steiner complexes, and nothing else. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked.*

**What we are asking for.** One theorem valid in every dimension: the exact class of finite computads on which the free weak ω-category is strict (in the sense of §1.2), ideally "exactly the homotopical ones". Individual new cases (one more context, one more dimension) are not useful to us. Bubbles obstruct strictness in every dimension (item 20), so the question is the comparison on bubble-free contexts. It has two halves. The first is the contraction, homotopical ⇒ strict. The second is necessity, which now reduces to one statement: the *coinductive* reconstruction map at free strict targets is an equivalence (items 22–23). The inductive reconstruction map is known to fail. Several exact reductions are known (items 9–14, 22–25).

## 1. Setting

### 1.1 CaTT

CaTT (Finster–Mimram, LICS 2017, arXiv 1706.02866) is a dependent type theory whose models are weak ω-categories (Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, arXiv 2106.04475: models are exactly Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis weak ω-categories).

```
Types          A ::= ⋆ | u →_A v
Terms          t ::= x | coh_{P, A}[γ]
Contexts       Γ ::= ∅ | Γ, x : A
Substitutions  γ ::= ⟨⟩ | ⟨γ, x ↦ t⟩
```

- `dim ⋆ = −1`, `dim(u →_A v) = dim A + 1`, and a term of type A has dimension `dim A + 1`.
- A **pasting context** `P ⊢ps` presents a globular pasting diagram. It has boundaries `∂⁻P`, `∂⁺P`.
- **The only term former.** If `P ⊢ps`, `P ⊢ u →_A v` and `Δ ⊢ γ : P`, then `Δ ⊢ coh_{P, u→_A v}[γ] : (u →_A v)[γ]`, provided either
  - (coherence) `Var(u : A) = Var(P) = Var(v : A)`, or
  - (composition) `Var(u : A) = Var(∂⁻P)` and `Var(v : A) = Var(∂⁺P)`.
- Definitional equality is syntactic equality. There are no other equations.
- Binary composites `u ∗_k v` and whiskerings are composition coherences over two discs glued along a k-disc.
- Contexts are exactly finite computads, and the terms of Γ are the cells of the free weak ω-category on Γ (Benjamin–Markakis–Sarti, arXiv 2405.00398). Coherence terms are invertible in the coinductive sense (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2406.12127).

### 1.2 Strict image and relative coherence

Every context Γ is a polygraph. Write `F(Γ)` for the free strict ω-category on it. Strict ω-categories are CaTT models, so every term t has a **strict image** `π(t) ∈ F(Γ)`: a coherence is sent to the unique strict composite of its arity.

> **RC(n) for Γ.** Any two parallel n-terms `T, T′` of Γ with `π(T) = π(T′)` are connected by an (n+1)-term `T → T′`.

Because coherence terms are invertible, RC(n) is symmetric. Write **RC(∞)(Γ)** for "RC(n) holds for Γ for every n ≥ 1".

> **RC_id(n) for Γ.** As RC(n), with the connecting term required to have identity image: `π(T → T′) = 1_{π T}`. Write RC_id(∞) as above.

RC_id implies RC. They agree when every endo-(n+1)-cell on an n-cell of F(Γ) is an identity, which holds in the loop-free class of Question 1. With loops, plain RC is too weak: in (x; f : x → x; α : f ⇒ f), the pair (f, f) is joined by α itself, whose image is not an identity. Wherever loops can occur, RC_id is the statement that matters.

*Equivalent form (proved for every finite context).* Every strict cell of F(Γ) is the image of some term. Given that, RC_id(∞)(Γ) is equivalent to: **π_Γ : Free(Γ) → F(Γ) is a trivial fibration of CaTT models**, and also to π_Γ being a weak equivalence (F(Γ) is gaunt: its only invertible cells are identities, since the multiset of top generators is additive). The trivial-fibration form says: for every pair of parallel (n−1)-terms u, v and every strict n-cell c : π(u) → π(v) there is an n-term t : u → v with π(t) = c. (Lift c with some boundary, then conjugate by RC_id connections, which have identity image.) 

### 1.3 Contexts we need

All of the following are finite. The full cylinders (Y = Var(Q)), the joins, the Gray products of globes and the orientals present strongly loop-free Steiner complexes (Steiner, *Omega-categories and chain complexes*, HHA 2004; Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches pour les ∞-catégories strictes*), so F(Γ) = ν(K) has cells determined by their supports. The partial cylinders (Y ≠ Var(Q)) also present strongly loop-free unital Steiner complexes (§3 item 9), and they are positive in every dimension (the source of x↑ has x⁻ as a factor and its target has x⁺).

- **Cylinders.** For a pasting context Q and an up-closed set `Y ⊆ Var(Q)` (if y ∈ Y occurs in the type of x then x ∈ Y), the context `Q↑Y` keeps each `x ∉ Y` and replaces each `x ∈ Y` by two copies `x⁻, x⁺` and a cell `x↑ : a_x → b_x`, where for x of dimension n

  ```
  a_x := ( … ((x⁻ ∗₀ (τⁿx)↑) ∗₁ (τⁿ⁻¹x)↑) … ) ∗_{n−1} (τx)↑
  b_x := (σx)↑ ∗_{n−1} ( … ((σⁿ⁻¹x)↑ ∗₁ ((σⁿx)↑ ∗₀ x⁺)) … )
  ```

  with every factor `w↑` for `w ∉ Y` omitted. For Y = Var(Q) the strict ω-category F(Q↑Y) is the Gray cylinder `D¹ ⊗ Q`. The smallest example is the **lax square** (x⁻, x⁺, y⁻, y⁺; x↑, y↑, f⁻, f⁺; F : f⁻ ∗₀ y↑ ⇒ x↑ ∗₀ f⁺).
- **Joins.** For a pasting context Q, `Cone(Q; c)` adds an object c and, for each variable t of Q of dimension m, a variable `p_t : p_{τt} → p_{σt} ∗_{m−1} (p_{σ²t} ∗_{m−2} ( … (p_{σᵐt} ∗₀ t)))` (with `p_x : c → x` for objects). F(Cone(Q; c)) is the Ara–Maltsiniotis join `c ⋆ Q`.
- **Gray molecules.** Gray products `D^{n₁} ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ D^{n_k}` of globes, and more generally pasting diagrams in Steiner's or Hadzihasanovic's sense, presented in CaTT by giving each cell the type `⌈∂⁻x⌉ → ⌈∂⁺x⌉` with chosen composite terms for its boundary molecules.
- **Orientals.** For example the **triangle** (x, y, z; f : x → y, g : y → z, h : x → z; α : f ∗₀ g ⇒ h).


## 2. Why it matters

RC is the single missing ingredient in several constructions in CaTT: lax naturality of every coherence with respect to every up-closed set of its variables (equivalently a lax cylinder Γ ⊗ D¹ for every context Γ); composites, unique up to higher cells, for non-globular pasting diagrams (Gray products of globes, joins); cones over every coherence; and the comparison of the free CaTT model on a computad with its strict free ω-category, which is the unit condition in comparing CaTT models with (∞,ω)-categories.

## 3. What is known

1. **RC(1) and RC(2) hold in every context.** For 2-terms: normalise to vertical composites of whiskered 2-variables; two composites with the same strict image are related by exchange moves (Ara–Burroni–Guiraud–Lafont–Métayer, *Polygraphs*, arXiv 2312.00429, §4.1), and each move is one coherence.
2. **Support lemma.** supp(π t) = supp(t) in every context, and if t has no variable of dimension dim t in its support then supp(t) = supp(σt) = supp(τt). Parallel terms whose image is an identity on a cell w are joined by one coherence over supp(w) whenever supp(w) is a pasting context. For a pasting context P, π_P is a trivial fibration (this is the coherence and composition rule).
3. **RC(3) is known** for the lax square, the triangle, every cylinder Q↑Y, the 2 × 2 and 2 × 3 grids, the Gray cube D¹ ⊗ D¹ ⊗ D¹, and joins over vertical chains of 2-discs. Each proof is a hand-built normalisation for one context; in each, RC(n+1) needed a separately constructed coherence of the normalisation one dimension up.
4. **Adjoining top generators in dimension 3.** If Λ has dimension ≤ 2 and an acyclic generating 1-graph, and Γ adds 3-generators between parallel 2-terms of Λ, then RC(3)(Γ) ⟺ RC(3)(Λ) (using depth-1 naturality, Benjamin–Markakis–Offord–Sarti–Vicary, arXiv 2501.11620, Thm 3.1). In dimension 4 only a conditional version (assuming RC(3) and RC(4) for Λ) is known.
5. **Exchange combinatorics in dimension 2 are settled.** For a 2-dimensional context with an acyclic generating 1-graph and non-empty boundary paths, the 2-paths representing a strict 2-cell c are the linear extensions of an event poset, and the graph of exchange moves with squares and hexagons as 2-cells is simply connected; squares and hexagons are single coherences.
6. **Loops obstruct.** In Γ = (x; a : 1_x ⇒ 1_x) the Eckmann–Hilton 3-cell a ∗₀ a ⇛ a ∗₀ a and the identity are parallel with equal strict image, and no 4-term joins them (the weak free object groupoidifies to S², the strict one B²ℕ to K(ℤ, 2), and π₃(S²) ≠ 0).
7. **Gaunt targets.** F(Γ) is gaunt for every finite Γ (generator counts), so an FHM ω-weak equivalence into F(Γ) is a trivial fibration. RC_id(∞)(Γ) ⟺ π_Γ is a trivial fibration ⟺ π_Γ is a weak equivalence.
8. **Homotopical computads.** Γ is *homotopical* when the comparison from the free (∞,ω)-category on Γ (generators attached by homotopy pushouts) to F(Γ) is an equivalence. Strongly loop-free Steiner complexes with unital basis, and regular directed complexes with frame-acyclic molecules, are homotopical (Campion, arXiv 2311.00200; Chanavat, arXiv 2603.22242, Thm 3.17). **A finite 2-polygraph is homotopical if and only if F(Γ) has no bubble** (no non-identity 2-cell 1_x ⇒ 1_x); in particular every 2-polygraph whose 2-generators all have non-empty target (or all non-empty source) is homotopical, loops and cycles in the 1-graph allowed. Bubble-freeness is undecidable. For a homotopical context, RC_id(∞)(Γ) is equivalent to the unit of the realization adjunction between CaTT models and (∞,ω)-categories being a weak equivalence at the free CaTT model on Γ.
9. **Partial cylinders are strong Steiner.** For Y ⊆ Var(Q) up-closed, the complex K_Y obtained from the Steiner tensor I ⊗ λQ by identifying c⁻ = c⁺ and killing Ic for c ∉ Y is strongly loop-free and unital, with ∂⁻-iterates (∂⁻)^j Ix = (σ^{j−1}x)⁻ + I(τ^j x), (∂⁺)^j Ix = (τ^{j−1}x)⁺ + I(σ^j x) (omitted terms zero); so F(Q↑Y) ≅ ν(K_Y).
10. **Cartesian strictification.** Call Γ *positive* if every generator of dimension ≥ 2 of its polygraph has non-identity source and target. For every generator-preserving map f : Δ → Γ of positive CaTT contexts (variables to variables, attaching terms preserved), the square Free(Δ) → Free(Γ) over F(Δ) → F(Γ) is a pullback in every dimension: a term t of Γ and a strict cell d of F(Δ) over π(t) have exactly one lift. (Proof: Henry's theorem that composition is cartesian on positive polygraphs, *Non-unital polygraphs form a presheaf category*, Cor. 2.3.13, 2.4.9, plus induction on terms.)
11. **Reduction to polyplexes.** For a strict cell c of F(Γ), Γ positive, let P_c → Γ be its generic (Henry) polyplex, with the uniquely lifted weak attaching terms. Then π_Γ is a trivial fibration iff every π_{P_c} is; the same holds for RC(n) and RC_id(n) for each n. Positive 2-polyplexes are strong Steiner, so **RC(n) for every finite positive context of dimension ≤ 2 is equivalent to RC(n) for every finite strong-Steiner context of dimension ≤ 2** (no hypothesis on the 1-graph).
12. **The fibre over one generator.** If Γ = (Λ, z : a → b) is a strong Steiner atom polygraph with z of top dimension m, then Free(Γ) ×_{F(Γ)} 𝔻^m is the free extension, by one m-cell, of Free(Λ) ×_{F(Λ)} ∂𝔻^m (𝔻^m the strict m-globe mapped onto π(z)). So the trivial-fibration property over the cell π(z) would follow from: *adjoining an m-cell to a trivial fibration B → ∂𝔻^m, along an attaching sphere over the two faces, gives a trivial fibration B[ẑ] → 𝔻^m.* That statement is unproved, and it does not cover strict composites of z with other generators.
13. **Arity lemma.** If a coherence coh_{P,A}[γ] has a substituted strict boundary using a top generator z exactly once, the variables of P labelled by terms involving z form one suspended subtree Σ^m Q of P's Batanin tree.
14. **Maximal variables.** If Y ⊆ Var(P) consists of variables occurring in no other variable's boundary, P↑Y is a pasting context, and every term over P (any dimension) has a naturality term over P↑Y with its full cylinder boundary. This does not give compatibility under substitutions that change a selected variable's boundary.
15. **Not counterexamples.** Chanavat's 4-dimensional regular directed complex (arXiv 2509.26563, Comment 2.67) has a polygraph Q whose strict free category differs from the "stricter" one, but the extra equations appear only in Q → r^>Q, so they do not give equal strict images in F(Q). Benjamin–Markakis's hom-computads (hom weak ω-categories of free computads are free) do not transport π: for (x), the hom computad has distinct 0-cells id_x and id_x ∗₀ id_x, while Hom_{F(x)}(x, x) has one.
16. **A related open conjecture.** Dean (*Globular multicategories with homomorphism types*, thesis, Thm 5.4.0.1) proves an all-dimensional acyclic-fibration strictification for fibrational homomorphism types with a strict computation law; the weak analogue is his Conj. 5.4.0.9. No equivalence with RC is known.

17. **Two-dimensional bubbles obstruct RC.** Let Λ be a finite context of dimension ≤ 2 such that F(Λ) has a non-identity 2-cell c : 1_x ⇒ 1_x. Then there are parallel 3-terms T, T′ with π(T) = π(T′) and no 4-term T → T′ at all, so RC(3)(Λ) fails. Proof:
    - Choose a 2-term U with π(U) = c, and correct its boundaries by coherences over the context (x) to get a : 1_x ⇒ 1_x literally, with π(a) = c.
    - Take T = eh(a, a), the Eckmann–Hilton 3-term a ∗₁ a → a ∗₁ a (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2402.01611, §6.1), and T′ = 1_{a∗₁a}.
    - Interpret Λ in the double delooping 𝒢 of the strict braided monoidal groupoid with objects ℕ, End(p) = ℤ, ⊗ = + and braiding β_{p,q} = pq. Every 1-cell goes to the unique 1-cell, and every 2-generator to 1.
    - 𝒢 is a Gray category, so it is an algebra of the Gray-category 3-operad. That operad is contractible with top-dimensional uniqueness (Batanin–Cisinski–Weber, arXiv 1209.2776, Ex. 3.6, §4.5, Ex. 5.7), and extended by identities it is a CaTT model with only identity 4-cells.
    - There, [[T]] = ±N² and [[T′]] = 0, where N > 0 is the number of 2-generators in c.
18. **Heredity.** If Δ ⊆ Γ is dependency-closed, RC_id(n)(Γ) ⇒ RC_id(n)(Δ): an identity-image filler H has supp(H) = supp(πH) ⊆ Var(Δ), by item 2. With item 17: if F(Γ_{≤2}) has a bubble, then RC_id(3)(Γ) fails, whatever the higher generators of Γ. For finite contexts of dimension ≤ 2: RC_id(∞) ⇒ RC(3) ⇒ bubble-free ⇔ homotopical.
19. **Higher endo-cells obstruct homotopicality.** If F(Γ) has a non-identity n-cell q : 1^{n−1−k}_c ⇒ 1^{n−1−k}_c (0 ≤ k ≤ n − 2, c a k-cell), then Γ is not homotopical. Proof: the n-skeleton inclusion is discrete Conduché, so homotopicality passes to Γ_{≤n}. Counting n-generators gives F(Γ_{≤n}) → Bⁿℤ, and q gives Σ^{n−2}B²ℕ → F(Γ_{≤n}). Steenrod powers then detect nonzero mod-p cohomology of the groupoidification in unboundedly many degrees, while the free cellular n-skeleton has dimension n. The converse ("no such endo-cell ⇒ homotopical") is proved in dimension 2 (item 8) and for positive-to-one polygraphs in every dimension, and is open in general.
20. **Higher bubbles obstruct RC_id(∞) in every dimension.** Suppose F(Γ) has an n-cell q : 1_b ⇒ 1_b, q ≠ 1, n ≥ 2, where b is an (n−2)-cell (possibly itself an iterated identity). Then π_Γ is not a trivial fibration, so RC_id(∞)(Γ) fails. Composite q of even weight and b of positive dimension are included. Proof:
    - *A list model.* Fix n and a set C of colours. Take one cell in each dimension below n, finite lists in C as n-cells, colour-preserving bijections of positions as (n+1)-cells, and identities above. This is a CaTT model, natural in recolourings. Every n-head concatenates its input lists, one block per n-generator occurrence of its generic strict cell, in a fixed order. Every (n+1)-head matches positions: give the positions in each *slot* distinct auxiliary colours, transport them along the labelled bijections, and match equal colours. A slot is a class of n-variables of the pasting context joined by (n+1)-variables. Two facts about generic pasting contexts make this well defined: each strict n-cell uses each slot at most once, and the two n-faces of a higher cell use the same slots. Both are proved by the decomposition of a Batanin tree into suspended subtrees, and checked by machine on all trees with at most 8 edges. (n+2)-heads are well defined because colour-preserving bijections between lists without repeated auxiliary colours are unique.
    - *The separation.* With distinct colours, the suspended Eckmann–Hilton term eh_{n−2}(a, b) : a ∗_{n−1} b → b ∗_{n−1} a (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2402.01611, §§6.1, 6.3) must be the block exchange, and by naturality it stays the block exchange after recolouring to one colour. For a = b of length m > 0 this is not the identity.
    - *The reduction.* By heredity (item 18), pass to the n-skeleton Λ. Boundary lifting gives a with literal type 1_d → 1_d and π(a) = q. Send each n-generator to a one-element list, so a has length m = (number of n-generators in q) > 0. Then eh(a, a) and 1_{a∗a} are parallel, have the same identity image, and are separated in the model.
21. **The unrestricted extension theorem is false.** Adjoin α : 1_f ⇒ 1_f (a 3-generator on the weak identity of f) to the 2-disc (x, y; f, g : x → y; β : f ⇒ g). The 2-disc satisfies RC_id(∞) (item 2), but the extended context fails RC(4) by item 20 with n = 3. The same works in each attachment dimension m ≥ 3. So adjoining generators can preserve RC_id(∞) at best for attachments that keep the context homotopical.
22. **Split criterion and the reduction of necessity to reconstruction.** Let A = Free(Γ), let C = F(Γ) as a CaTT model, and let q : KC → C be the computadic cofibrant replacement. KC has one generator for each parallel pair (u, v) of cells of KC and each strict cell c : q(u) → q(v) (Dean–Finster–Markakis–Reutter–Vicary, arXiv 2208.08719, §5; Markakis, arXiv 2303.11978, §5). Sending each generator x of Γ to the generator labelled by π(x) and its already-embedded boundary gives a generator inclusion i : A → KC over C. KC is A with the remaining generators freely attached.
    - **RC_id(∞)(Γ) holds iff there is a strict CaTT map r : KC → A with r i = id_A and π r = q.** (⇒) extends id_A across the remaining generators by boundary lifting. (⇐) lifts through q and applies r.
    - Let L be realization from CaTT models to (∞,ω)-categories, and N the nerve. Let e : LA → NC and d : LKC → NC be induced by π and q. Then e is the comparison defining homotopicality, and d is the *reconstruction map* of the strict target C. We have d ∘ Li = e. Under the split criterion we also have e ∘ Lr = d and Lr ∘ Li = id, so **e is a retract of d over NC**. Hence RC_id(∞)(Γ) together with "d is an equivalence for C = F(Γ)" implies that Γ is homotopical. More precisely, under RC_id(∞)(Γ), Γ is homotopical iff there is s : NC → LKC with d ∘ s ≃ id and s ∘ e ≃ Li.

23. **Inductive reconstruction fails; coinductive reconstruction suffices.** With the notation of item 22, the inductive reconstruction map d_C : L(KC) → NC is **not** an equivalence for any nonempty strict C. Proof:
    - Let D be the free strict ω-category with one object p, a loop ℓ, and in each dimension k ≥ 2 one generator for each ordered parallel pair of (k−1)-cells. D is gaunt, because top-generator counts are additive, and D → 1 has boundary lifting in every dimension.
    - K1 has one object v and a loop generator u. Sending u to ℓ and to 1_p gives two maps L(K1) → ND. They restrict to different points of the discrete space Map(D¹, ND), so they lie in different components, while Map(N1, ND) is a single point. So d₁ is not an equivalence.
    - For nonempty C, 1 → C → 1 exhibits d₁ as a retract of d_C.
    
    However, D → 1 is an FHM weak equivalence, so Q(ND) ≃ 1, where Q is coinductive completion. For Γ of generator dimension ≤ m, both ends of e lie in (∞,m)-categories. That subcategory is coreflective, with the m-core as right adjoint, so it is closed under the colimits building LA, and its objects are coinductively complete (Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde, arXiv 2603.11005, Lemmas 4.4, 5.22–5.23). Hence **e is an equivalence iff Qe is**, and by the retract of item 22, **RC_id(∞)(Γ) together with Q d_{F(Γ)} being an equivalence implies that Γ is homotopical**. Equivalently, with η the unit at A and W the FHM weak equivalences: given RC_id(∞), Γ is homotopical iff η ∈ W; given homotopicality, RC_id(∞) holds iff η ∈ W.
24. **Simultaneous algebraic lifts of strict simplices.** Suppose π : A → C has globular boundary lifting. Call a strict CaTT map h : K Oₙ → A with π h = σ q an *algebraic lift* of a strict simplex σ : Oₙ → C (Oₙ is Street's oriental). The pairs (σ, h) form a simplicial set, and forgetting h is a trivial fibration onto the Street nerve of C. Proof: the face suborientals satisfy O(F) ∩ O(G) = O(F ∩ G) (Steiner's tableaux), and the subcomputads K O(F) ⊆ K Oₙ satisfy the same formula. So compatible face lifts glue, and the remaining generators lift one at a time. This does not give the geometric section s of item 22, which needs a comparison from geometric simplices to the realizations of K Oₙ.
25. **The dimension-3 exchange step reduces to a wedge lemma.** Assume (H): Γ is finite, of dimension ≤ 2, has an acyclic generating 1-graph, and every 2-generator has nonempty source and target paths. Then the 2-paths representing a strict 2-cell are exactly the linear extensions of an event poset, and exchange moves are swaps of adjacent incomparable events (item 5).
    - Under (H), a contiguous antichain of events in a 2-path spans, in its generic polyplex (item 11), a wedge A₁ ∨₀ ⋯ ∨₀ A_k of single-generator contexts glued at cut vertices. So, by the polyplex reduction, the part of RC(3) that concerns permuting such a block is exactly **the wedge lemma**: RC(3)(A₁ ∨₀ ⋯ ∨₀ A_k) follows from RC(3)(A_i) for each i.
    - For k = 2 this is known: two independent letters with a cut vertex are handled by horizontal normal forms and one interchange coherence.
    - The rest of RC(3) under (H) consists of forced-order statements: letters joined by connectors that cross their boundary blocks, whose fibres have a single linear extension. This includes a fusion statement comparing normalisers along substitution. These are of the kind already proved for cylinders and grids.
    - The smallest wedge with k = 3 is the polyplex of the hexagon triple in the 3 × 3 grid: three lax squares glued at two cut vertices. Its key ingredient is the three-factor interchange (α ∗₀ β ∗₀ γ) · (α′ ∗₀ β′ ∗₀ γ′) ⇛ (α·α′) ∗₀ (β·β′) ∗₀ (γ·γ′).

## 4. Questions

**Question 1 (main: the exact class).** Prove that for every finite CaTT context Γ,

> RC_id(∞)(Γ)  ⟺  Γ is homotopical,

equivalently (item 7) that π_Γ : Free(Γ) → F(Γ) is a trivial fibration of CaTT models exactly when the polygraph of Γ is homotopical. Or give a counterexample to either direction with a separating invariant.

- **(⇐, the contraction).** This is the main content. By item 11 the positive case reduces to generic positive polyplexes, and by item 12 the strong-Steiner case reduces to one attachment statement plus composites. Routes that would do:
  - (a) prove the attachment statement of item 12 for arbitrary trivial fibrations B → ∂𝔻^m of CaTT models (a homotopy-pushout property of free cell extensions of CaTT models), and extend it from a single generator to strict composites;
  - (b) describe the fibre of π_Γ over each strict cell as a CaTT-definable object and show it is contractible;
  - (c) a relative Guiraud–Malbos normalisation strategy towards the strict cells of F(Γ), producing all higher comparisons at once;
  - (d) an equivalence with Dean's Conj. 5.4.0.9 or another named open statement;
  - (e) by item 22, construct for every finite homotopical Γ a retraction r : KC → A over C with r i = id, for example by contracting the fibres of π over the cells of C, or by a homotopy-pushout property of attaching the generators of KC that are not in A. Item 24 gives compatible algebraic lifts over all strict simplices, which may help organise the choices;
  - (f) a **wedge theorem in every dimension**: RC_id(∞)(A₁ ∨₀ ⋯ ∨₀ A_k) from RC_id(∞)(A_i), for contexts glued at cut vertices (and its analogue for gluing along higher cells where the gluing is a cut), together with a forced-order normalisation for the remaining fibres, which have a single linear extension. In dimension 3 under (H) this is exactly item 25, and with the polyplex reduction (item 11) it would give the positive 2-dimensional case of Question 3.
- **(⇒, necessity on bubble-free contexts).** By item 20, RC_id(∞) already forces bubble-freeness in every dimension. Prove that a finite context with RC_id(∞) is homotopical, or give a bubble-free, non-homotopical context with RC_id(∞), together with a separating invariant. By items 22–23, it suffices to prove that the **coinductive** reconstruction map Q d_C : Q L(KC) → Q NC is an equivalence for every free strict ω-category C = F(Γ) on a finite polygraph. The inductive d_C never is (item 23). A proof for all gaunt C, or for all C, is preferred. Equivalently, prove that the unit η at Free(Γ) is an FHM weak equivalence whenever RC_id(∞)(Γ) holds, or produce the section s of item 22. $$

Use the CaTT presentation of the globe cylinder $I\otimes\mathbb D^n$ with its two endpoint globes. The endpoint variables have their globular types, and the cylinder is obtained from their disjoint union by freely adjoining the mixed generators in increasing dimension. Below, map the strict cylinder to $C$ by collapsing $I$ and selecting $c$. Above, prescribe $u$ and $v$ on the two endpoint globes. For each mixed generator its two attaching terms now evaluate to parallel cells in $X$ with the prescribed images in $C$. Boundary lifting for $p$ extends the assignment across that generator. Both composites admit cancellation witnesses over identities by another application of boundary lifting, so the class satisfies the coinductive inverse condition. The strict map $h$ preserves this inverse data. Consequently the composite cylinder labelling in $D$ sends every mixed generator to an identity.

Since $D$ is strict, this labelling evaluates the strict Gray cylinder. For a face generator $x$ of the globe, its cylinder generator has source and target obtained by whiskering $x^-$ and $x^+$ with the mixed generators on their lower faces, as in §1.3. The two constants give its boundary, and the $k$ changes give the fresh 2-globes above the common 1-boundary. Thus horizontal complete Segal localisation gives precisely $NF(B_k)$, naturally in $k$. [Haugseng on the rectangular and $\Theta$ models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.08480)

Only horizontal **Segal** localisation at fixed $k$ is needed. Here is a flag check using the uncompleted $\Theta_2$ presentation of the horizontal pushout. Its input strict nerves correspond under Haugseng's comparison. Restriction to $\Theta_1$ sends every horizontal Segal spine to a 1-categorical Segal equivalence. Indeed a tree $[p]([q_1],\ldots,[q_p])$ restricts to the nerve of the free 1-category on a line with $q_i+1$ parallel arrows in slot $i$. Restriction preserves colimits and the restriction of a Segal-local object is 1-Segal, since the linear trees have their own spine tests. It therefore computes the lower path diagram of the horizontal Segal localisation by 1-Segalising the restricted pushout. At this restriction the attached 2-globes have precisely their boundary, so the result is the original discrete free path nerve. Thus the horizontal Segal object has the flags of objects and 1-arrows of $F(B_k)$. Completion is fully faithful on homs between existing flags, successively in the two directions. Its completion here is the gaunt nerve $NF(B_k)$, and on both flag spaces the map is the identity on the indicated paths and objects. It is therefore already an objectwise equivalence.

Now localise in the last direction at each horizontal rectangle. Horizontal Segal products are products over discrete spaces: their intersections are lower-dimensional rectangles, constant in the last direction by globularity. Categorification preserves products and coproducts and hence products over a discrete base. The result still satisfies horizontal Segal and globularity. Its last-direction core is the discrete set of old vertices up to equivalence. Indeed every arrow is represented by a necklace, and the number of changes in its long edge is nonnegative and additive. The only weight-zero beads, in every simplicial degree, are constant simplices. The rigidified mapping spaces over weight zero are consequently identity points or empty. Every equivalence has weight zero. The same argument applies at a rectangle with several slots, by summing the weights. The bottom layer for testing the remaining completeness conditions is therefore $NF(B)$, already complete. The two localisations impose conditions satisfied by every threefold complete Segal object, and their output is local, which proves the universal property. In particular the lower globe evaluations are discrete up to equivalence and the specified top mapping-space fibre computes the globe fibre with its boundaries allowed to vary. ### Cells of the mapping space

A single $d$-cube attaches one $(d-1)$-ball of morphisms from its initial to its final vertex. More precisely, in coherent rigidification the extreme hom of the cube is the nerve of the poset of vertex chains from $\varnothing$ to $[d]$ in its Boolean lattice, ordered by inclusion. The least chain is the diagonal. In the boundary cube the allowable flags of chains are those whose smallest chain has an interior cut: bead joints must divide the largest chain into beads contained in proper faces. Thus the boundary hom is the subdivision of the order complex of the nonempty proper subsets. The full hom is its cone. At every other endpoint pair the boundary already has the full hom. A facet with a specified cut $S$ is the product of the two smaller balls: chains containing $S$ are exactly pairs of chains below and above it, and the identification is the enriched composition. This describes the permutohedron with its subdivided faces, including the point for $d=1$. [Dugger–Spivak, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.0814)

Rigidification preserves the pushouts attaching cubes. Consequently a mapping space has cells indexed by composable strings of these balls, with dimension $\sum_i(d_i-1)$. This remains true when a cube has repeated vertices. Explicitly, freely compose the new morphism balls with arrows at their endpoints and impose their boundary maps; a word of interior points of balls has a unique such expression, and on a boundary facet its factor splits into the two factors at that cut. This is the free enriched-category pushout since in the individual cube no nonidentity arrow precedes its initial or follows its final vertex. Iteration gives the asserted CW description. Fibrantly replacing these homs preserves their weak homotopy types. Over weight $r>0$ every string has $\sum_i d_i=r$, so its dimension is at most $r-1$.

A vertex is a serial computation of generating 3-cells in one-hole lower contexts. An edge exchanges two consecutive occurrences in a specified two-hole context. Equality of strict labels of serial computations is generated by just these exchanges and equality of individual lower contexts, so every $E(c)$ is connected. Weight zero gives an identity point and every unary label gives a point. The only 2-cells are a square for two disjoint two-event blocks in a string, or a hexagon for a single three-event block, in each case possibly preceded, followed or separated by fixed steps. This description uses no positivity or width hypothesis on individual lower generators. [Ara et al., Appendix D.3, Propositions 6, 9 and 12](https://www.normalesup.org/~ara/files/polybook.pdf)

### Representing edges and faces by terms

Choose parallel weak lifts of the outer 1-boundaries, a weak 2-term for each intermediate strict 2-cell between them, and a 3-term for each individual rewriting step with those chosen boundaries. Use relative boundary lifting. Represent a serial computation by a fixed bracketing of its 3-terms. Its semantic identification with the corresponding vertex is made by composing the unary identifications. These choices are contractible within the unary factors.

For a simultaneous $d$-event diagram there is a globular pasting context that realises its cube. Serialise the **lower marked 2-diagram** into rows, each a whiskered ordinary lower generator or a whiskered marker. Use one 2-disc for each ordinary row and one 3-disc for each marker row, composing along 1-discs. Choose the weak 1-term at each cut, then the two faces and the whiskered 3-term of a marker row by boundary lifting. This labels the globular paste in $Γ$. Its generic extreme top hom category is the ordinary $d$-cube. Its map to $X$ realises the specified characteristic cube in $T$. In fact the canonical map in the transition presentation substitutes the whiskered marker at each time index and the original strict labels for the ordinary rows. The map from the chosen weak labelling agrees up to homotopy over the strict labels: maps out of the row paste are the pullback of the individual row maps at the cuts, all of whose label fibres are contractible by the unary and lower calculations. This also identifies the restrictions to fixed faces of the cube.

In a two-event paste the two serial paths are joined by a 4-term by absolute coherence on the paste. Its image represents the exchange edge, since the extreme hom in the generic cube is an interval with its indicated ends. To get the chosen global endpoints, connect the four corner 2-terms to their chosen representatives by identity-image 3-cells $k_p$. Along a side $p\to q$ compare $k_q l$ with $l' k_p$ by a 4-term, where $l$ is the paste side and $l'$ the chosen step. These comparisons have unary labels. Paste them to the generic exchange, cancelling the corner equivalences at the ends. This gives a 4-term between the two chosen serial paths, representing the edge with the specified vertex identifications. The corrections have unique relative path classes in the unary fibres. Choose this term once per two-hole diagram. To place it within a longer computation, whisker by the remaining factors and rebracket. Choose the rebracketing comparisons on the generic string of factors. Absolute coherence there makes their composites compatible with further regrouping.

Every edge loop in $E(c)$ is now represented by a 4-term loop at a fixed 3-term. For a backwards edge use a weak inverse, available over the identity label by boundary lifting. RC in degree four joins the resulting term to its identity by a 5-term, which realises a nullhomotopy. Thus $\pi_1E(c)=0$ for every $c$. In particular every weight-two fibre is a connected simply connected graph, hence contractible. This last conclusion controls the relative homotopy classes of the corrections needed one dimension higher.

For the square 2-cell, regard the two chosen local 4-terms as labels of two 4-discs composable along a 2-disc. The two boundary paths are the two ways to apply them consecutively. In this globular paste the interchange between those paths is a 5-term. Semantically it is the product square of the two paths under composition. This follows by evaluating in the paste: after passage to $(∞,3)$-categories the two independent 4-arrows give the two interval parameters, and in their product the two routes have a unique homotopy class of fillings relative to the sides. Constant intermediate or exterior factors are inserted by composition. Regrouping changes the boundary by the associativity comparisons on the generic string.

For the hexagon, use the row paste for the three marked occurrences. Inside it, use the two-event construction on each boundary facet with the third step fixed before or after it. The two three-edge routes from one serialization to its reverse give parallel 4-terms. Absolute coherence in the whole paste supplies a 5-term between them. In the three-cube its filling, with those facet identifications, represents the hexagon: the extreme hom is a disk, and the chosen facet paths are its six boundary edges up to homotopy relative to their endpoints.

The following boundary check is essential. Induced facets of the row paste may use different 4-terms from the ones chosen for the edges of the global complex. Choose a corner comparison $k_p$ at each of the eight cube corners, and a side comparison $k_q l\Rightarrow l' k_p$ at each of its twelve sides, just as for the two-event case. For each two-event facet from $p$ to $q$, there are now two parallel 4-terms going from $k_q$ composed with the first chart route to the second chosen route composed with $k_p$. One uses the chart exchange followed by the side comparisons. The other uses the side comparisons followed by the chosen exchange. RC supplies a 5-term between them. Its label has **two** occurrences. Its semantic homotopy class relative to these paths is therefore unique, by contractibility of the weight-two fibre. This is precisely the comparison of paths in the facet before composing with the fixed third step.

To see that these six corrections match, at each serial vertex slide $k$ along its three successive steps using the three chosen side comparisons in order. This gives one vertex comparison used by both incident hexagon edges. Whisker the facet's 5-term with the fixed third step. When its side comparison passes the facet exchange, use the interchange square for these two 4-terms, as in the preceding square construction. This gives the edge homotopy between the two vertex comparisons. All endpoint $k$'s can be cancelled by using the same inverse and cancellation at the common endpoint. Reassociation of a string or a whiskered cell here is performed in the globular paste of that string with the varying cells as inputs. Its structural comparisons and comparisons between their composites exist in that paste and realise the structural homotopies there. This describes the usual pasting of a 3-cell with invertible face comparisons in the twice-iterated hom: its 1-, 2- and 3-morphisms are respectively the 3-, 4- and 5-cells, the last taken modulo 6-cells. Equivalently one may do the pasting in its biased tricategory, choosing representatives of the resulting 3-morphisms. The generic-paste check ensures that it respects composition on realisation. Thus the transported 5-term represents the actual hexagon relative to the previously chosen edges. No choice of a filling in the unknown full fibre is used to identify this relative class. Finally contract the map of the 2-skeleton to $E(c)$. Choose a base vertex and a 4-term from its representative to that at each vertex. For each edge choose a 5-term comparing its composite with the incoming base path to the outgoing base path. These choices contract the graph in $E(c)$. ### A contraction with no bound on the number of occurrences

There is also a family for which the preceding geometric presentation decides **all** homotopy groups. Give each generating 1-edge a positive integer width, additive on paths, and suppose every generating 2-cell preserves width and has positive width. In a path of width $w$ use the ordered unit positions $1,\ldots,w$. Each 2-generator in a diagram occupies a consecutive interval of these positions. For each 3-generator suppose the positions meeting a 2-generator are the same on both its faces, and this set is nonempty. The other positions pass unchanged through both faces.

For such a 3-computad the following criterion is exact. Check whether a generating 3-cell has a nonempty right whiskering path $g$ in common on its two faces and a generating 3-cell has exactly this $g$ as a left whiskering path on its two faces. Either whiskering may be part of a longer one, and the generators may coincide. If such a match exists, the computad fails homotopicality and relative coherence. If no match exists, **every geometric fibre is contractible**. In particular relative coherence implies contractibility throughout this family. This includes diagrams with different numbers of lower gates on the two faces, repeated top generators, partial overlaps and unchanged wires inside a replacement.

For the geometric calculation above, all the $B_k$ now have positive width on every 2-generator, so they are bubble-free without assuming RC. When a match exists, place the two rules so that just the path $g$ overlaps. Their order on this common path gives two different two-hole diagrams. They are distinct because the two marked lower boxes meet on an ordered wire. The boxes have different left endpoints since each has an active position outside the overlap. On substituting either face for one hole, the two diagrams coincide: the substituted face is the identity along the overlap. Thus the two characteristic squares have the same perimeter routes and different diagonals. They give a cycle in the weight-two graph, where there are no 2-cells to kill it. The fundamental-group result above excludes RC.

Suppose henceforth there is no match. Record a lower diagram by the types and intervals of its gate occurrences and their total orders on each position. These data determine the strict 2-cell. All serial listings of the gates are linear extensions of the orders, and adjacent incomparable gates occupy disjoint intervals. Swapping them preserves typing and is strict interchange. For the typing calculation one can spell an edge of width $d$ as the string of letters $(e,1),\ldots,(e,d)$. A gate replaces its designated subword by another of the same length. Labelling the occurrences is unambiguous up to unique renaming because every gate is fixed by an ordered position it meets.

Choose a serial computation of a strict 3-cell $c$. At each position follow the word of lower gate occurrences there. Distinguish its gaps as well as its letters, including the exterior gaps. In a top step an active position consumes a nonempty block of letters with its interior gaps and produces a new nonempty block with fresh interior gaps. Its two exterior gaps survive with the same edge letters since the two lower diagrams are parallel. At an unchanged position the step reads a particular gap and preserves it. Keep occurrences distinct, naming produced tokens by their producer, position and output port.

At each position order a token's producer before every reader or consumer and each reader before its eventual consumer. Any linear extension of this local partial order is a valid local execution. For two adjacent incomparable steps, the second uses none of the first's outputs and cannot consume a gap just read by the first. Since an active block and its replacement are both nonempty, the second block pulls back to a disjoint block or gap on the same side. Two reads may also coincide at one unchanged gap. Thus the two steps swap and yield the same named word. This proves the assertion by adjacent swaps. Two locally incomparable steps have a fixed spatial comparison whenever both are enabled. Execute the ideal consisting of their strict predecessors first. Both input positions are then exposed. Every other prefix exposing both can be reached from that ideal by steps that preserve their tokens, hence also their relative order or their common gap.

Take the union of the local partial orders. For two steps incomparable on each shared position, add their chronological comparison if two shared positions force opposite spatial orders. Take the transitive closure and call the resulting poset $H$. All these comparisons agree with the original serial computation. They are invariant under a genuine two-hole exchange, since lower boxes have the same relative order on every shared position. The occurrence identifications made under exchanges are canonical: each event consumes a letter on at least one active position, each such letter has only one consumer, and the ordered initial letters and output ports fix these identifications successively. The gap names then follow as well.

If two overlapping incomparable steps had only ties on their common positions, both would read identical unchanged gaps there. Neither interval could contain the other because each has an active position. Their intersection is therefore a suffix of one and prefix of the other, passing unchanged through both rules. The typed letters at those gaps agree. The ends of the intersection are boundaries of whole edges, since the letters include their edge offsets. This gives the excluded whiskering match. Thus every intersecting pair of incomparable steps has a uniquely forced spatial order on its intervals, including across any tied positions.

To prove that $H$ describes exactly the strict computation, take consecutive incomparable events in a valid execution. Revert the second event's matches past the first as in the local argument and collapse both source regions to marked boxes. Each outside gate keeps its comparisons with the first box from the state before the first step and with the second box from the state after the first step. Reverting a disjoint letter replacement preserves the latter comparisons. A gate of the second match is entirely unchanged: if the first had produced any of its letters there would be a producer dependence. On a lane the boxes inherit the order of the disjoint blocks and read gaps; only multiple reads of one gap can tie. Break those ties by the forced orders on intersecting intervals.

Here is why this simultaneous collapse is legal for any number of boxes with these pairwise comparisons. Regard outside gates as boxes too. All pair orders on intersecting intervals respect the weak spatial orders on their lanes. A shortest directed cycle of length at least four would have a chord, since the two neighbours of an interval with the smallest right endpoint both contain that endpoint. Either direction of the chord shortens the cycle. Three pairwise intersecting intervals have a common lane. A directed triangle would require all three positions to be tied on *every* lane shared by all three, by transitivity of the weak lane order. Each pair would then need an exclusive lane on which its strict comparison is witnessed. To the left of the triple intersection only the pair omitting the interval that starts last can overlap, and to the right only the pair omitting the interval that ends first can overlap. This supplies at most two of the necessary three pairs. Hence all precedence is acyclic.

A topological ordering gives a typed marked circuit. Indeed expanding the holes by their source diagrams reproduces exactly the original orders of all gates on their lanes, hence a valid serialization of the source. The input letters at an active lane agree with those at its first expanded gate. At a read gap the prescribed typed letter survives, including on gaps bordering the other replacements. Output types follow at the other end, so collapsing really gives the specified lower boxes. Their diagram is unique since all its lane orders are forced. For two events this proves the required exchange and hence shows that every linear extension of $H$ is in the strict class. For any ideal $I$, the named state after $I$ is independent of its listing by the swap property. Each minimal remaining event can execute there by choosing it first in an extension. A set of such events therefore has individually enabled boxes with the pairwise comparisons used above and admits a unique joint context. Conversely a joint context allows every ordering of its events, so they form an antichain.

Let $K$ be the subcomplex of the ideal nerve $N\mathcal I(H)$ consisting of chains whose total difference is an antichain. Send a chain to the simplex of $T$ given by the joint context with changes indexed by the successive differences. Uniqueness makes this compatible with faces and degeneracies. Every endpoint necklace in $T$ with label $c$ lifts uniquely to $K$ from the empty to the full ideal: serialise each bead, read off its successive cuts in $H$, and use its occurrences as the changes. The occurrence identifications and the cuts are unchanged by reordering simultaneous changes. Conversely a bead is recovered from its cuts by uniqueness of the joint context. This also verifies naturality for maps of necklaces, which compose steps or restrict their times. Thus $E(c)$ is the extreme mapping space of $K$.

The inclusion $K\subseteq N\mathcal I(H)$ is a categorical equivalence. Add the missing intervals $[I,J]$ in increasing order of the cardinality of $D=J\setminus I$. For a non-antichain $D$ the old part of this interval nerve is exactly the union of the faces omitting either endpoint. In rigidification all homs except the extreme one are unchanged. The old extreme hom is the nerve of nonempty chains of the open interval by the bead-joint calculation above. The open interval consists of the proper nonempty ideals $U$ of $D$ and is contractible: $U\cap\min D$ is nonempty and lies below both $U$ and the proper ideal $\min D$. The new extreme hom is also contractible, as its chain poset contains the least chain of just the endpoints. Hence each attachment is a pushout of a monomorphic categorical equivalence. The extreme hom of the full ideal nerve is a point. The lower-dimensional fibres are already contractible by the lower-skeleton calculation, proving the criterion.

The same criterion gives the implication under RC for every iterated suspension of a context in this family. The strict and geometric free comparisons of a suspension are suspensions of the corresponding comparisons: with the two poles fixed, prescribing the suspended generators and their attaching maps is precisely prescribing the original cellular diagram in the hom between the poles. Thus the positive case preserves all the contractions. In the overlap case the original four-cycle is still present in that iterated hom. If the suspended context satisfied RC, apply the two-event construction above using the suspended row paste, lifting corners and unary sides there by suspended boundary lifting. The unary fibres and the facet paths are the original ones in the iterated hom. The resulting loop would both traverse the four-cycle and admit an RC nullhomotopy. This proves failure in the suspended context without assuming that arbitrary terms can be desuspended.

## Positive 3-computads with no matching idle boundary paths

Let every generating 2-cell have nonempty input and output paths and every generating 3-cell have nonidentity 2-faces. A face has an *idle right suffix* $w$ if it factors horizontally as $a*₀1_w$ with $w$ a nonempty 1-path. Define an idle left prefix dually. In the following test choose the faces independently, including faces of the same generator.

**Theorem.** If no idle right suffix of a generating 3-face is an idle left prefix of a generating 3-face, every geometric fibre of the 3-computad is contractible. In particular this contracts full-support fibres at arbitrary weight. It needs neither a positive width grading on the generators nor equality of the active wires on opposite faces. For example on one loop $x$ take $r:xx⇒x$, $p:xxx⇒xx$ and $A:r*₀1_x⇒p$. The rule $r$ precludes every positive width grading. The two faces of $A$ act on different sets of wires, and the test applies. Horizontal powers of $A$ have full support and unbounded weight.

**Local widths.** Use the cubical presentation of the transition simplicial set $T$ of Response 4. That construction applies here before assuming relative coherence. Each lower marked polygraph still has nonempty inputs and outputs at every gate, hence is bubble-free and homotopical by item 8. Positivity also makes every marked circuit rigid. Fix a vertex of $T$, an ordinary planar 2-diagram $D$. Give each concrete wire segment the number of directed boundary-to-boundary graph paths containing it, allowing any incident input to continue to any output at a gate. Every wire has positive width, and the sum of input widths equals the sum of output widths at every gate, by counting the paths through it. At a serial cut partition a fixed interval of unit lanes in wire order with these widths. Executing a gate repartitions its interval. Swapping independent gates leaves their intervals fixed, so each gate and each wire of $D$ acquires a definite interval. This construction only uses one vertex $D$ at a time.

A signed edge germ is a one-marker circuit together with its endpoint choice. Expanding the marker by that face gives $D$. The isomorphism to $D$ fixes every gate and ordered port, since every component is boundary-anchored. In a serialization of the frame the wires entering the marker consequently give a definite consecutive interval of lanes, equal to the interval of its output wires after expansion. A different serialization exchanges disjoint gates and gives the same data. On each of these lanes the expanded face occupies a consecutive block of gates, or the gap on a through wire if that lane is idle. A germ includes that gap, even if its face has no gates on the wire. An ordinary gate of $D$ occupies one gate on each lane of its own interval.

**Links.** Take a finite family of pairwise adjacent germs at $D$. In a joint two-marker circuit, the expansions of the two markers have disjoint sets of gates of $D$. Their blocks on any common lane can be executed in the frame order. Regard a block as the interval between its first and last cut in that lane's gate list. One lies weakly before the other, and both orders are possible on that lane only for equal empty gaps. If two marker intervals overlap but *all* common lanes tie, neither interval can contain the other, since each face has a gate. Thus the common lanes occupy an end of each interval. On every one the same concrete wire passes through both faces untouched. The overlap consists of whole wires: its two endpoints are port boundaries of the two boxes, and a wire passing unchanged through a box cannot be cut by its port boundary. The ordered word of the common wires is an idle suffix of the left box's face and an idle prefix of the right box's face. The hypothesis excludes this. Consequently an intersecting pair has a unique order, forced on some common lane and valid on all of them.

Keep the proposed markers and the individual ordinary gates exterior to all of them. For a marker and an exterior gate the one-marker frame gives their comparisons, strict on an intersecting lane since the exterior gate occupies a gate there. Two ordinary gates retain their order in $D$. Orient the intersection graph of all these intervals by the resulting comparisons. This graph is acyclic. In a shortest directed cycle of length at least four the interval with smallest right endpoint gives a chord between its neighbours, shortening the directed cycle whichever way the chord points. Three pairwise intersecting intervals share a lane. A directed triangle forces three equal gaps on *every* triple-covered lane by transitivity of the lane order. Each pair needs a strictly witnessing lane outside the triple intersection. Only the pair omitting a last-starting interval can overlap to its left, and only the pair omitting a first-ending interval can overlap to its right. These can witness at most two distinct pairs.

Serialize the resulting acyclic graph. Expanding the marker blocks in this order preserves every original gate order on every lane. In particular every wire predecessor in $D$ precedes its successor. This is a serialization of $D$: linear extensions of the gate order differ by interchanging disjoint, enabled gates. When a block starts, its input port wires are the concrete wires at the prescribed lane cuts. On an idle gap this follows because every exterior gate at that lane has been placed on the prescribed side of the gap. Its whole input word, including the idle ports, is therefore present with the right types. The same holds at its output. Replacing each such block by its marker gives the joint circuit. Any two of the resulting orders differ by interchanging gates on disjoint intervals, so the joint circuit is unique. Conversely a cube corner gives pairwise adjacent germs. Its germs are distinct because their expanded faces contain disjoint nonempty sets of gates. The cubical links are consequently simplicial flag complexes, including the signed links at vertices with directed loops.

**From links to the actual fibre.** Take the universal cover of the cubical component of $T$ containing a serial representative of $c$, using the ordinary cubical realization before triangulation. Its links are flag. It is thus a simply connected nonpositively curved cube complex, with median 1-skeleton. Opposite edges of every square have the same transverse orientation, by the time coordinates of the marked circuit. Upstairs a hyperplane is two-sided, with its dual edge directions related by going across squares. The lifted directions select one side as positive on the entire hyperplane. A directed path can therefore cross each hyperplane at most once and is an edge geodesic. These are combinatorial facts and require no upper bound on the dimension of cubes over the whole component. [Genevois, §2 and Theorem 2.1](https://aif.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/aif.3430.pdf) · [Ardila–Owen–Sullivant, §2](https://fardila.com/Articles/cat0.pdf)

Lift the chosen serial representative from $v$ to $w$. In the interval between them the vertices are the order ideals of the finite poset of separating hyperplanes, ordered by the halfspaces containing $v$. More precisely use the order in which nested halfspaces must be exited when leaving $v$. Rooting at $v$, the ideal for $w$ is consistent, so all its subideals are consistent. The cubes in the interval are precisely independent additions of minimal remaining elements. Every edge geodesic between these endpoints is directed, and any two are related by swapping consecutive incomparable elements. Thus its projection has label $c$. Conversely every serial representative of $c$ lifts between those same endpoints, since strict equality is generated by exchanges in squares. This correspondence also lifts every cube necklace and its subdivisions uniquely: lift from the first vertex across the successive beads, each lying in a cube, and compose the lifts at their common vertices. Every bead lies in the interval since each of its vertex chains can be extended to a directed endpoint path. Projection is inverse and respects the face and composition operations. The mapping space over $c$ is therefore the extreme mapping space of the triangulated interval cubing. The order-ideal attachment argument at the end of Response 4 proves this space contractible. The lower fibres follow from the lower-skeleton calculation, and iterated identities give the same globe fibres. [Ardila–Owen–Sullivant, Theorem 2.5 and §3](https://fardila.com/Articles/cat0.pdf)

## Exactly what a two-event overlap can detect

For *any* positive 3-computad as above, without the no-overlap hypothesis, every weight-two fibre is a point, an interval with one or two exchange edges, or a circle with two exchange edges (using the unsubdivided permutohedra of Response 4). A circular component occurs somewhere if and only if there are two top generators, possibly the same one, and a nonempty path $w$ that is an idle suffix of **both** faces of one and an idle prefix of **both** faces of the other.

Indeed a vertex of a weight-two fibre is an ordered pair of serial steps. Its incident exchanges are exactly the joint two-marker frames at the intermediate state for the incoming and outgoing germs. The preceding lane argument gives at most two: the orders with every exterior gate are forced, and only the relative order of the two markers can vary. Each choice fixes all comparisons of intersecting intervals, hence at most one frame. If both orders occur, the shared lanes are equal idle gaps as above. In the order with those two markers left incomparable (retain only their forced orders with exterior gates), neither can reach the other by a directed path: both linear orders have occurred. They can consequently be placed consecutively with identical exterior prefix and suffix, in either order. Their common ports are a nonempty consecutive word of wires running directly between the two boxes. Reordering the two lower boxes across that word gives the second frame.

Suppose both serial endpoints of a given exchange have degree two. Work in its two-marker frame, with the consecutive lower serialization just described. At the other serial endpoint the markers are expanded by the opposite choices of face. Every wire port directly connecting the two boxes still gives a shared lane interval of positive width, using the path count at this new state. For ambiguity at that endpoint, *all* those wires must pass unchanged through the two newly chosen faces. Thus all four faces are idle on the same connecting word. Reordering the two lower boxes gives the same second frame at both endpoints: expanding either marker by either face preserves its through wires and hence gives identical one-marker circuits before and after the reordering. The two exchanges are parallel edges, and they exhaust the degrees of both vertices. An exchange cannot be a loop, since at its initial state in $T$ the two possible first steps use disjoint nonempty sets of ordinary gates. A path of three edges in any other component would have an edge with two degree-two endpoints. This proves the list, since the strict label indexes a connected component.

Conversely write the four faces as $a_i*₀1_w$ and $1_w*₀b_j$. Whisker and vertically compose their markers in either lower order across $w$, from the diagram $a_0*₀1_w*₀b_0$ to $a_1*₀1_w*₀b_1$ in transition time. The two frames are distinct by the order of the markers along a connecting wire. Their four one-marker sides agree in pairs by the displayed factorizations. Hence they give the circular component, which rules out relative coherence by the edge-realization argument of Response 4. If only one intermediate serial state is ambiguous, the two alternatives form an interval. Such an ambiguity alone cannot certify a violation of relative coherence. The all-weight case with these one-sided ambiguities, as well as the general higher-dimensional case, remains open here.

**Finite checks.** The [circuit enumerator](../../../tools/relative_coherence_local_links.py) compares cliques of signed one-marker germs with all simultaneous marked circuits independently enumerated from the lower generators. Faces in this check each expand to one gate, so bounding the number of gates introduces no missing corner at an included state. It also builds the actual exchange multigraph by expanding each two-marker circuit in its two serial orders. With four initial wires and at most four gates, the no-overlap example with additional splitting and unary gates has 25,007 states and 3,504 four-marker corners; every clique has exactly one corner. On five initial wires and at most three gates, allowing opposite one-sided idle faces gives 33 two-edge interval components. Adding two rules idle on both faces gives 33 circular components as well. These checks exercise the port and multiplicity distinctions in the proof.

# Researcher Response 6

## Four occurrences, including one-sided overlaps

Let $Γ$ be positive through dimension three and satisfy $\mathrm{RC}_{\mathrm{id}}(∞)$. **Every 3-cell fibre of weight at most four is contractible.** More generally, for every strict 3-cell $c$ of weight $r\ge2$,
$$
H_{r-1}(E(c);\mathbb Z)=0.
$$
The proof of this homology vanishing only needs the exclusion of the two-sided overlaps classified in Response 5. One-sided overlaps are allowed, and gates can change arity. By Response 2 it suffices that these hypotheses hold on the support of $c$.

**Occurrence names.** Fix the initial ordinary 2-diagram $D_0$ of a serial representative of $c$. Name its concrete gates by their occurrences. Name a rewriting event by its rule and the names of the gates it consumes, indexed by the gate occurrences in its source face. Give its output gates fresh names consisting of the event name and their positions in its target face. Every event consumes at least one gate. Inducting through a run, gate names are fresh: repeating the name of an earlier event would require consuming an already consumed gate again, and the initial names and the output names are disjoint. Rigidity of positive planar diagrams makes the identifications in a particular frame unique. A square swap changes neither event's inputs nor the names of the outputs. It follows that occurrence names are invariant throughout the strict class. In particular all full simultaneous $r$-marker frames over $c$ have the same named events, with the same pairwise disjoint, nonempty sets of source gates in $D_0$.

The only cells of dimension $r-1$ in Response 4 are the single permutohedra of full frames. If there are none the claim follows at once. Otherwise use the path-count widths of Response 5 on $D_0$, and choose an event $x$ whose leftmost active source lane is as far left as possible among all the events. This uses the gates in $D_0$ and hence the *same* choice works in all the full frames. For any such frame $C$, consider its two facets splitting off $x$ alone, first or last in rewriting time. These are distinct open cells, distinguishable by their ordered blocks of named events. Inside a full permutohedron each of these facets occurs once, also by the occurrence names.

**Changes across a facet.** Suppose $C'$ shares one of these two facets with $C$. At the intermediate ordinary vertex $M$ of the split, the facet supplies the signed germ of $x$ and the germs of all the others simultaneously. Compute widths afresh on $M$. The two frames compress the same expanded blocks and idle gaps there. Every comparison between two markers different from $x$ is fixed by the common factor in which they are still markers: expanding $x$ to its face retains their order on every shared lane. Every comparison with a gate outside the blocks is fixed by the corresponding germ. Therefore the only comparisons that can change are between $x$ and a marker $y$ tied with it at idle gaps on *all* common lanes.

More explicitly take a serialization of each frame on these blocks and exterior gates and pass between the two orders by adjacent inversions, crossing only pairs whose orders in these serializations differ. Disjoint intervals commute. For intersecting intervals an inversion can only cross $x$ with such a $y$. Both new blocks still expand to $M$, by the lane and port reconstruction in Response 5. The two factors at the split are preserved: replacing either of the consecutive boxes by its chosen face leaves the other free to pass its idle wires. Thus the successive orders give full frames on this very facet. A nontrivial crossing involves a nonempty word of directly connecting ports, an idle suffix of the spatially left box's chosen face and an idle prefix of the right box's chosen face.

At **every** such crossing $x$ is the spatially left box. To see this, expand the consecutive boxes and all other markers by their *source* faces, giving $D_0$. At least one of the two crossing faces used at $M$ was a source: at the first split it was $y$'s source, and at the last split it was $x$'s source. Thus in $D_0$ at least one of the two expanded boxes acts only outside the common port word. In the fixed widths of $D_0$, their intervals meet exactly along the suffix and prefix of the consecutive boxes at the cut between them. If the left box is idle there, all its source gates are left of the right box's interval. If the right box is idle there, all its source gates are right of the left box's interval. In either case *all* active source lanes of the spatially left box lie strictly to the left of those of the other. The choice of $x$ excludes $y$ being on the left. This argument never identifies the numerical widths at $D_0$ with those at $M$.

It follows that all the markers crossed during a change of frame at this facet meet the **rightmost lane of $x$** at $M$. On that lane their order amongst themselves is fixed. The slot of $x$ in this ordered list can only move in one direction from $C$ to $C'$ when crossing only inverted pairs. If the slot does not move, only disjoint intervals were interchanged and $C'=C$. Measure the direction intrinsically on the full marked circuit by the number of gates that are strict graph ancestors of $x$, counting other markers as gates. Each move of $x$ upward in the lower circuit across a consecutive $y$ strictly increases this number. Every former input ancestor still feeds $x$, either directly on the unchanged ports or through $y$ on the shared ports; $y$ itself becomes an ancestor. The reverse move strictly decreases the number. This count compares the two singleton facets using the same circuit $C$, even though their intermediate diagrams differ.

**A facet with nonzero boundary coefficient.** Suppose a nonzero integral top cycle exists. Among the full frames with nonzero coefficient choose one minimizing this ancestor count. Cancellation at each of its two singleton facets would require a different frame in the cycle sharing that facet. By minimality, at either facet the first nontrivial move toward such a frame must move $x$ upward. In both instances the first marker $y$ crossed is *the same gate of $C$*: it is the next gate on the rightmost output wire of $x$. Indeed immediately before that crossing the two boxes can be serialized consecutively and share that output port. Any preceding interchanges of disjoint intervals preserve the marked circuit and its wire incidences.

The word of direct connections from $x$ to $y$ in $C$ is likewise intrinsic. Crossing at the $x$-first facet requires the target face of $x$ and the source face of $y$ to pass this word untouched. Crossing at the $x$-last facet requires the source of $x$ and the target of $y$ to do so. The same nonempty word is now an idle suffix and prefix on both sides of the respective rules. Response 5 excludes this under relative coherence. At least one of the two facets consequently retains the nonzero coefficient of $C$, up to orientation. This contradicts the cycle condition.

For $r=4$ the CW model has dimension at most three. Response 4 gives connectedness and vanishing of $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$, while the top-cycle argument gives $H_3=0$. The space is simply connected and has trivial reduced integral homology in all degrees, hence is contractible by Hurewicz and Whitehead. Smaller weights were settled in Response 4. A positive 3-dimensional counterexample under relative coherence would therefore require weight at least five and a nonzero group in an intermediate degree: at its *first* nonzero positive homotopy group, Hurewicz and the top-degree vanishing force $3\le j\le r-2$.

The [circuit checker](../../../tools/relative_coherence_local_links.py) builds the facet incidence relation by independently expanding the two factors at every cut. On 5,970 four-marker frames with arity-changing rules it eliminates every full frame using facets that split off one event first or last. A separate four-event example has cyclic temporal preferences for the ambiguous pairs; the same test eliminates all 18 frames. For a chain with three two-sided overlaps the eight distinct full frames instead have even total incidence at **every** facet, giving a nonzero mod-two top cycle. This last calculation tests the use of the two-sided exclusion in the proof.

# Researcher Response 7

## Gluing temporal orders across cuts

There is an all-weight contraction allowing one-sided overlaps, insertions, deletions and several successive simultaneous blocks. Suppose the support of a strict 3-cell $c$ is positive and each generating 2-cell in it has one input edge and one output edge. These edges can have different labels. The positions in either outer 1-path are then a fixed line of **lanes**. A 2-diagram is a word of unary gates on each lane. Each top occurrence $v$ has a footprint $I_v$, the consecutive lanes on which its marker is placed, including any idle lanes in the marker.

Partition the line into consecutive strips so that each $I_v$ is a union of strips. On a strip $b$ keep just the events $V_b$ that cover it. Project one serial computation for $c$ there, keeping distinct occurrence names. In this projection an event replaces one subword on each lane of the strip; a subword on either face may be empty, even on every lane. An exchange specifies in which spatial order the two markers occur across the strip. Let $K_b$ be the component of the projected computation in the local complex of simultaneous rewrites, defined below.

**Cut hypothesis.** For every cut between successive strips, the events crossing it occur in one fixed, strict temporal order throughout each of its two local components, with the same order on both sides. In particular they cannot be simultaneous there. This holds automatically for cuts crossed by at most one occurrence. The hypothesis only concerns the events *crossing* the cut. Other events in either strip can exchange, or occur simultaneously with a crossing event.

Under this hypothesis the actual geometric fibre satisfies
$$
E(c)\simeq\prod_b K_b.
$$
Moreover **if $Γ$ has relative coherence, $E(c)$ is contractible**. No bound on the number of occurrences or on the number of lanes in a strip is needed. The assertion applies equally to full-support labels. In particular one can string together arbitrarily many overlapping events, with one event crossing each cut and arbitrarily many additional events inside the strips. The proof treats intermediate-dimensional cells as well as the single full frames of Response 6.

**Local complexes and occurrence names.** A serial step on a strip specifies the cut position of its source subword on each lane. A simultaneous tier specifies a marked word on every lane: expanding each marker by the corresponding source subword gives the incoming state, and expanding by the target gives the outgoing state. The marker names must occur in the same spatial order on every lane. Strict equality of lower diagrams imposes precisely this condition: the markers span the strip, and ordinary gates on distinct lanes commute. Thus these words determine the tier uniquely. For a sequence of tiers take the product of their temporal permutohedra as in Response 4. A face splits one or more tiers in time, expanding each marker before its turn by its source and afterwards by its target. Work with each prescribed event once. Distinct ordered refinements of a tier have distinct lists of names, so each closed cell is regular. With specified initial state and finitely many named events the complex is finite, since the word lengths and the number of temporal steps are bounded. $K_b$ consists of the component containing the given projection. An empty event list contributes a point.

Globally the names can be recovered in every serial computation by the input-gate and output-port construction of Response 6. Every event consumes a gate on at least one lane. Its name and the lane of that gate fix the whole footprint by its offset in the source. Initial gates have prescribed lanes, and gates produced by a named event retain their prescribed output lanes. Consequently these footprints and names agree across exchanges. Naming the events in this way does not change the fibre. In a strip we keep the global names even when the entire projected face is empty, so a particular projected component need not be the fibre of an *unlabelled* local computad.

**Proof of the product.** First take all global cube chains on these named events whose projections belong to the specified $K_b$, with the given initial and final states. Project a tier onto a strip by restricting its lane words; omit tiers with no marker on that strip. This commutes with taking faces. It gives a map of face posets, ordered by inclusion, to the product of the face posets of the $K_b$.

Fix a tuple $p$ of local cells. To compute the inverse image of its lower ideal, impose $v<w$ whenever $v,w$ belong to successively ordered (distinct) tiers in any one of these cells. These inequalities generate a partial order $H$. Indeed, adding strips from left to right, the names in the new strip that have already appeared are exactly those crossing the preceding cut, since their footprints are intervals. They form the same strict chain on both sides. Two acyclic orders glued along a common chain have acyclic union: any cycle visiting both sides breaks up into paths between common vertices, and each nonconstant such path moves strictly forward in the chain.

An ordered partition of all names respecting every strict inequality of $H$ prescribes, on each strip, a unique refinement of its cell in $p$. Conversely these are exactly the temporal partitions of the global chains over the lower ideal. For the existence assertion, reconstruct one global tier at a time from the prescribed refined words, keeping the current word where a strip has no step. On either side of a cut these diagrams have at most one marker of that tier in common by the cut hypothesis. Their gate orders can therefore be glued: the union of the orders along at most one common node is acyclic. Repeating along the line gives a serialization. It is well typed because on each lane it executes exactly the prescribed word with the specified ports. Two such serializations differ by interchanges on disjoint lanes; all pairs meeting on a lane already have their comparison fixed by that word. This proves uniqueness of the marked circuit. Its expansions at either end reproduce the corresponding strip states, so the constructed tiers compose. It also proves compatibility with further refinement.

The inverse image of the lower ideal is therefore the face poset of ordered partitions strictly respecting $H$. Its classifying space is contractible. Indeed it is the subdivision of the extreme mapping complex of the cubing of order ideals of $H$: a block adds an antichain of currently minimal remaining elements. Response 4 proves the contraction for this cubing by attaching the missing non-antichain intervals in its ideal nerve. Quillen's Theorem A applied to the projection now gives an equivalence with $\prod_b K_b$. This proves connectedness of the global complex just constructed as well. Its vertices are related by genuine two-event exchanges, so they all have the original strict label $c$. Conversely any computation of $c$ is reached by such exchanges and hence projects into the selected local components. All cube chains of that label were included. Response 4 thus identifies the domain of the equivalence with $E(c)$ itself.

**Why simple connectedness suffices on a strip.** This step allows local events idle on their whole strip, even when several read the same gap. Orient a local exchange toward the serial order that agrees with the spatial order of its two boxes. Count $+1$ on each oriented edge and $-1$ on its reverse. This integral cellular cochain is closed. On a square the two exchanges commute, and on a hexagon the spatial order of the three markers is a total order. In either case the edge counts in that cell are the differences of the number of correctly ordered pairs at its vertices. Expanding a third marker preserves the order of the two remaining markers because they span the entire strip. This last fact is essential for the cochain to agree on common edges.

Suppose $K_b$ is simply connected. The cochain is then a vertex-potential difference, and the finite oriented graph has no directed cycle. At a serial vertex let $a_i$ and $p_i$ on a given lane denote the length of the prefix before step $i$ and the length of its source subword. Positions are measured immediately before the respective step. The exchange of $i,i+1$ is outgoing exactly when $a_{i+1}+p_{i+1}\le a_i$ on every lane. Indeed this puts the later input wholly in the unchanged prefix of the earlier step and determines the simultaneous chart with the later marker on the left. If both markers occupy the same empty gap, this still selects just one of their two spatial orders.

On a consecutive run of outgoing exchanges those inequalities imply $a_j+p_j\le a_i$ for each earlier $i$ and later $j$ in the run, lane by lane. All input blocks can therefore be cut in the original words before the run, with coincident empty cuts taken in reverse temporal order. This produces one simultaneous chart, in that reverse order on every lane. Merge each maximal such run. The resulting product of permutohedra $F(v)$ has $v$ as unique source and contains every cell with source $v$ as a face: within each tier of such a cell the temporal listing at its source must be the reverse of its spatial listing. Every vertex of $F(v)$ is reachable from $v$ by oriented edges. In particular two outgoing moves have a common descendant. Finite termination and this local confluence give a unique sink in the connected component.

Remove vertices other than the sink in a topological order. At the removal of $v$ its remaining closed star is $F(v)$: a remaining cell containing $v$ whose source were different would have an earlier source, and all vertices of $F(v)$ survive up to this step. This polytope deformation retracts onto its union of faces missing $v$, the antistar of a vertex. This gives a deformation retraction at each step and contracts the component. This argument uses the [tier-collapse proof for planar diagrams](homotopy-polygraphs-2026-11-19.md) with the inequalities imposed on *every* lane; here termination follows from the closed cochain on the simply connected component.

Under relative coherence Response 4 gives $\pi_1 E(c)=0$. By the product equivalence every $K_b$ has trivial fundamental group, so each is contractible by the preceding argument. This proves the contraction.

**Checks and limit of the gluing argument.** The [strip checker](../../../tools/relative_coherence_strip_fibres.py) enumerates whole marked circuits independently of their strip projections, including all ordered cuts of each simultaneous tier. For five overlapping events whose faces delete and insert letters on their shared lanes, it finds 2,961 global cells. Every one of the 625 local cell tuples has an inverse lower ideal with the mod-two homology of a point. For four events with both faces idle on each shared lane the global Betti numbers are $(1,3,3,1)$, as predicted by the three circle factors. A separate test has two shared events forced into the same order by a consumed output on both sides. All 25 lower ideals pass there as well. A two-lane strip with unequal input lengths on its lanes passes all nine lower-ideal checks. If two shared events can exchange on both sides but require opposite spatial orders, the checker instead finds two isolated global serial paths. The two local exchanges cannot be glued. Thus a cut with unconstrained shared events cannot in general be treated as a product by this proof.
