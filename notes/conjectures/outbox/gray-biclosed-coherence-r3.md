# Free Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-categories: relation-free signatures, 2-cells of the one-sided theory, and a directed coherence rule

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Everything under "What is known", "Further observations", "Recent results" and "Newest results" is proved and is not re-asked.*

**What we are asking for.** One thing: a **coherence rule over Gray pasting types** with a decidable directed admissibility judgment that generates exactly the free monoidal biclosed (∞,ω)-category with the Gray tensor, or an impossibility theorem for rules of that kind. We need this to decide whether the tensor and lax internal hom of arbitrary objects can be given as type formers whose structure maps are coherences over such types. Results for restricted families are only useful as steps toward it.

## Background

**Schedules.** Fix a finite poset P on labels {1, …, n}, with numerical order as the left/right order of incomparable labels. Let S(P) be the category with:
- objects: the linear extensions of P;
- generating arrows: swaps ji → ij of adjacent P-incomparable labels with i < j;
- equations: commuting squares for swaps at disjoint positions, and the braid hexagon for three pairwise incomparable labels.

Then S(P)(w, v) is a singleton if Inv_P(v) ⊆ Inv_P(w) and empty otherwise. Here Inv_P(w) is the set of pairs i < j, incomparable, with j before i in w. (Proof: move the first differing label left, then resolve the first swaps by a square or a hexagon and induct.)

**Gray monoids.** In the free strict Gray monoid (a monoid in 2-Cat for Gray's lax tensor) on a set of objects and generating 1-cells, fix the image in the free strict monoidal category, i.e. the string diagram. Then the hom-category between the 1-cells with that diagram is S(P). Here P is the diagram's dependency order, with left/right given by planarity. The 2-cells are whiskered interchangers (f ⊗ B′)∘(A ⊗ g) ⇒ (A′ ⊗ g)∘(f ⊗ B), "right factor first ⇒ left factor first".

**The ω-version.** The hom ω-category of the Steiner complex ν(I^⊗n), from bottom to top, is not thin: two hexagon 2-cells are joined by the 3-cell ***. Gray's 2-tensor is its intelligent 2-truncation (Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches*, A.25 and A.31). For a dependency poset P, the subcomplex K_P ⊆ I^⊗n spanned by the faces (order ideal J, S ⊆ min(P∖J)) has paths equal to the linear extensions of P. Its truncated hom is S(P).

## Setting: the biclosed version

Formulas are built from atoms with ⊗ and the residuals B\A and A/B, with no unit. 𝒢 is the free **Gray-monoidal biclosed strict 2-category** on a set of generating 1-cells fᵢ : Pᵢ → Qᵢ between formulas. Its structure:
- a strict 2-category with a Gray monoidal structure (lax interchangers, as above);
- closed structure: isomorphisms of hom-categories Φ : 𝒢(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ 𝒢(B, A\C), and the mirror one for /.
  - Φ is **strictly natural in the context variable B**.
  - Φ is natural in the **bound variable A only up to the interchanger**.
  - Strict naturality in A would force (f ⊗ B′)(A ⊗ g) = (A′ ⊗ g)(f ⊗ B), collapsing distinct schedules into one 1-cell. That is not wanted.

**Two warnings, both established.**
1. *Nesting is not invariant under naive normalization.* For a generator h : U → U with U = X\Y,

   ```
   h = Λ(ev) ∘ h = Λ(ev ∘ (1 ⊗ h))
   ```

   So h can be "ambient" or "inside a closure". β-reduction ev ∘ (1 ⊗ Λk) = k also changes nesting. Any invariant notion of "event inside a closure" must start from a βη-canonical form.
2. *Unit-free planar coherence.* In the free unit-free biclosed monoidal 1-category, a balanced sequent (each atom twice, opposite polarities) has at most one morphism, and exactly one when it is provable in the Lambek calculus with product. So the purely structural parts of 1-cells are unique.

## What is known (proved)

1. **A word invariant.** Sending each generator to its label gives an invariant W of 1-cell equality with values in words (composition order), with W(Λu) = W(u), W(ev) = ε and W(D ⊗ u) = W(u). It is realised by the one-object model whose 1-cells are words, with a unique 2-cell between words with the same multiset. So 1-cells with different generator order are distinct.
2. **Closure trees are not invariants of components.** With generators a : X → A and f : A ⊗ B → C, c = Λ_A(f), e = ev: the interchanger gives θ : s = e∘(a⊗U)∘(X⊗c) ⇒ t = e∘(A⊗c)∘(a⊗B), and t = f∘(a⊗B) by β. Every representative of s has a generator under currying (a Boolean biclosed poset rules out the needed structural prefix), while t is tensor-only. So any βη-normal form fixing tensor-only terms gives s and t different closure trees, though they are connected by a 2-cell. "Cross-level cells are generated by ordinary interchangers" does not imply "the canonical tree is invariant".
3. **A forward comparison.** For φ : P → C/B and b : X → B, the forward comparison α = e_B ∗ χ_{φ,b} : t₁ ⇒ t₂ exists, W distinguishes t₁, t₂, and Λ_X(t₂) = (C/b)∘φ by strict naturality in the context variable. Whether 𝒢(t₁, t₂) = {α} and 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ is open: the tensor-only schedule theorem does not control 2-cells built by currying, uncurrying and η-expansion.
4. **Mapping-space biclosedness is too weak for classifying 1-cells.** Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari (arXiv 2006.14495, Rem. 2.20): Map(X ⊗ Y, Z) ≇ Map(X, Fun^{opgr}(Y, Z)) in general; only cores agree.

5. **An integer model.** Let 𝓜 be the strict 2-category with:
   - objects the integers;
   - for q ≥ p, the hom 𝓜(p, q) equal to the poset (ℤ, ≤) of weights; otherwise empty;
   - composition adding weights;
   - w(p ⊗ u) = w(u) + p·δ(u) and w(u ⊗ q) = w(u), where δ(u) = target − source;
   - p\r = r − p and r/q = r − q, with left currying subtracting p·δ and right currying leaving the weight unchanged.

   𝓜 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category. An interchanger raises the weight by δ(f)·δ(g). A matrix-valued version (objects in ℤ^r) records ordered generator pairs.
6. **No reverse comparisons.** With distinct atoms, 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ and 𝒢(t, s) = ∅. For t₁ and t₂ the weights are 0 and 1, from |B| = 1, |C| = 2 and |P| = |X| = 0. For s and t they are −1 and 0. Every forward 2-cell between them uses **exactly one** interchanger whose two arguments each contain one generator. Every other interchanger in it has a generator-free argument.
7. **Undirected components.** π₀ 𝒢(A, C) ≅ 𝓑_Σ(A, C), the free ordinary unit-free biclosed monoidal category. Two 1-cells are connected by a zigzag of 2-cells iff their images there agree.
8. **The image and W do not classify 1-cells.** Take generators f : A → B and g : B → C with padding L, R, and let m = L ⊗ (g ∘ f) ⊗ R.
   - Currying g with the right padding gives u ⇒ m.
   - Currying g with the left padding gives m ⇒ v.
   - u, m and v have equal images in 𝓑_Σ, and W(u) = W(v).
   - Their weights (with |L| = |R| = |A| = 0, |B| = 1, |C| = 2) are −1, 0 and 1, so the three 1-cells are distinct.
   - The side of currying is data of the 1-cell, and schedules with only generator events cannot describe this component.

## Further observations (proved)

9. **Subtractive models cannot see interchangers with a structural argument.** Call a model *additive* if 1-cells carry a weight in an ordered abelian group, δ(u) = weight(target) − weight(source) is additive under composition and tensor, and an interchanger χ_{f,g} adds a bilinear term β(δ(f), δ(g)). If moreover the residuals are group subtraction (A\C = C − A, as in the model 𝓜 of item 5 and its matrix version), every structural 1-cell (ev, curried evaluations, units) has δ = 0, so every interchanger with a structural argument has weight 0. Such models cannot decide whether two 2-cells differing in those interchangers are equal. This blindness is specific to subtractive residuals; see item 13.
10. **The axioms do not make evaluation central.** For ev : A ⊗ (A\C) → C and a generator g : Y → Y′, compare (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y), related by χ_{ev,g}. Currying A on the left (using strict naturality in the context variable and the target) turns this into the same question for m_Y := Φ(ev ⊗ Y) and g, so currying only transports it. Likewise for χ_{ev,ev′} at independent positions. Unless centrality is imposed (as in Garner–Hirschowitz–Pardon's premonoidal construction), structural maps are non-central events in 𝒢. In the quotient 𝒢_c of 𝒢 in which every interchanger with a structural argument is an identity, forward uniqueness for the comparisons of item 3 follows at once from item 6.

11. **Structural loops.** Take atoms A, B, C, D and U = A\(A⊗B), X = A⊗B, X′ = A⊗U, V = (C⊗D)/D, Y = C⊗D, Y′ = V⊗D. Let u = 1_A ⊗ Λ_A(1) : X → X′, e = ev : X′ → X, v = Λ^R_D(1) ⊗ 1_D : Y → Y′ and d = ev^R : Y′ → Y, so that e u = 1 and d v = 1 by β. Put T = X ⊗ Y and (composition rightmost first)

    ```
    H = (e ⊗ 1_Y)(1_{X′} ⊗ d)(u ⊗ 1_{Y′})(1_X ⊗ v) : T → T.
    ```

    - Whiskered interchangers give ι = (e⊗1) ∗ χ_{u,d} ∗ (1⊗v) : 1_T ⇒ H and π = [(e⊗1)(1⊗d)] ∗ χ_{u,v} : H ⇒ 1_T. The Gray axiom for χ_{u,dv} gives **π ∘ ι = 1**, so E = ι ∘ π is an idempotent on H.
    - **H^n are pairwise distinct.** In the model whose hom-categories are chaotic on the free group ⟨ℓ, r⟩ (objects integers, composition multiplication, left currying k ↦ ℓ^a k, right currying k ↦ r^a k), H ↦ ℓ⁻¹ r⁻¹ ℓ r.
    - All H^n have image 1_T in 𝓑_Σ, empty generator word and zero weight in every model of item 5. Since 2-cells H^m ⇒ 1_T ⇒ H^n exist, the category over 1_T is not isomorphic to any product of schedule categories.
    - This particular model does not satisfy strict equality of the two mixed curryings of (A\C)/B = A\(C/B). Item 14 removes that dependence.
    - **Forward comparisons.** Item 6's count factors each forward comparison as z₊ ∘ m ∘ z₋, with z_± using only interchangers with a generator-free argument and m a typed instance of the one generator–generator interchanger. Different typed instances of m must still be identified.
12. **Evaluation is not central in 𝒢.** In the same model with a third free generator γ for a generating 1-cell g, (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) ↦ ℓ⁻¹γ and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y) ↦ γℓ⁻¹, which are distinct. So χ_{ev,g} joins distinct 1-cells, and this uses only left closure. A complete invariant of 1-cells must schedule structural events (units, evaluations), not only generator events.

13. **A polarized model sees structural interchangers.** Let 𝓟 have objects (P, N) ∈ ℤ², A ⊗ B = A + B and A\C = C/A = C + swap(A), where swap(P, N) = (N, P). A 1-cell X → Y exists iff (P − N)(X) = (P − N)(Y), and it is an integer weight w. Put d(u) = P(source) − P(target).
    - Composition adds weights, w(A ⊗ u) = w(u) + P(A)·d(u) and w(u ⊗ B) = w(u).
    - Left currying is w ↦ w − P(A)·d(u), and right currying keeps the weight.
    - The homs are codiscrete.
    - 𝓟 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category in the sense above.
    - **ev is not central.** Here d(ev_A) = P(A) + N(A) ≠ 0. With A ↦ (1, 0), C ↦ (0, 0), X ↦ (1, 1), X′ ↦ (0, 0) and g : X → X′, the 1-cells (X′ ⊗ ev) ∘ (g ⊗ A ⊗ A\C) and (g ⊗ C) ∘ (X ⊗ ev) have weights 1 and 2. The same separates the two orders of two independent evaluations, with no generator involved.
    - Together with item 12, the central quotient 𝒢_c of item 10 is a proper quotient of 𝒢.

14. **Strict mixed currying does not help, and left closure alone suffices.**
    - *The model 𝒟.* Keep the chaotic free-group Gray monoid of item 11. Choose any k_c ∈ ⟨ℓ, r⟩ for c ∈ ℤ, put h_{a,c} = k_{c−a} k_c⁻¹, and let both curryings with bound object a and codomain c be f ↦ h_{a,c} f.
    - *Properties.* The multipliers telescope, so the two mixed curryings are equal, R_b R_a = R_{a+b}, and h_{0,c} = 1.
    - *Separation.* With |A| = |B| = 1, |C| = |D| = 2, k₁ = ℓ, k₄ = r⁻¹ and every other k_c = 1, H ↦ ℓ⁻¹r⁻¹ℓr. So H ≠ 1_T and the H^n are pairwise distinct even in the theory 𝒢_mix that imposes strict mixed currying.
    - *Left closure only.* Replace v and d by v_L = 1_C ⊗ Λ_C(1_{C⊗D}) and d_L = ev, through W = C\(C⊗D). The resulting H_L also maps to ℓ⁻¹r⁻¹ℓr. So the free left-closed theory already has non-identity structural loops 1_T ⇒ H_L ⇒ 1_T with π ∘ ι = 1.
15. **Countermodels for ι ∘ π = 1_H need non-invertible retractions.** In a Gray monoid, if f is strictly invertible, then χ_{f,g} is invertible, with inverse (f ⊗ B′) ∗ χ_{f⁻¹,g} ∗ (f ⊗ B). Hence in any model where u, e, v and d are strict isomorphisms, ι ∘ π = 1. That covers every refinement of the free-group, 𝒟 and 𝓟 models. A countermodel must have u ∘ e ≠ 1.
16. **Two different "centrality" questions.**
    - *Outer comparison.* For functors e, g between strict ω-categories, (e ⊗ 1)(1 ⊗ g) and (1 ⊗ g)(e ⊗ 1) are both e ⊗ g. So the interpretation of 𝒢's 1-cells as functors and its 2-cells as transformations sends every interchanger, generator or structural, to an identity, and cannot test directedness.
    - *Internal interchanger.* Evaluation applied to an interchanger of internal cells can be non-invertible. For S = J ⊗ J (J = [1]) with square γ : h₁v₀ ⇒ v₁h₀, let q : J → S/J curry 1_S and let α : v₀ → v₁ be the image of the arrow of J. Then e(χ_{α,u}) = γ.
17. **𝒢's two hom-category isomorphisms do not hold in the intended model.**
    - In S/J there is an arrow α : v₀ → v₁.
    - In J\S there is none: such an arrow would need the reverse of γ.
    - A single 2-category of strict ω-categories, with one orientation of transformation as 2-cells and ℋ(I, Z) ≅ Z, would identify both S/J and J\S with ℋ(J, S), identically on objects. That is impossible.

    So the Gray tensor's left and right closures are matched by 2-cells of opposite orientation (lax for one side, oplax for the other; Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari, Ex. 2.5). Campion's tensor gives (A\C)/B ≃ A\(C/B) canonically, but not literally.
18. **The correct structure is two-sided Gray enrichment.** For strict 2-categories with the Gray tensor, put ℍ(A, C)(P, Q) = 2Cat(P ⊗ A ⊗ Q, C), with probes P, Q on either side. Its one-sided restrictions are two different hom 2-categories: ℍ_ℓ (left probe, D_k ⊗ A → C) and ℍ_r (right probe, A ⊗ D_k → C). Then:
    - left currying is an isomorphism ℍ_r(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ ℍ_r(B, A\C), and right currying is an isomorphism ℍ_ℓ(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ ℍ_ℓ(A, C/B);
    - with probes on both sides, the bound argument absorbs the probe on its side (writing 𝒟 for data maps): ℍ(A ⊗ B, C)(P, Q) ≅ 𝒟(B ⊗ Q, (P ⊗ A)\C) = ℍ(B, (P ⊗ A)\C)(I, Q), and ℍ(A ⊗ B, C)(P, Q) ≅ 𝒟(P ⊗ A, C/(B ⊗ Q)) = ℍ(A, C/(B ⊗ Q))(P, I);
    - the squares 2Cat(J ⊗ A ⊗ J, C) give a strict double hom with mixed lax/oplax modifications.
19. **The free unit-free model 𝒢′_Σ.** Models have a probe sort and a data sort, adjunctions only between data types, and gluing along designated probe colimits (needed only for one-hole contexts). The free model 𝒢′_Σ on a signature Σ of generating 1-cells exists as a term model with an explicit universal property. The Gray model is a model. An erasure model (probes act by identities) shows that the free unit-free biclosed monoidal category 𝓑_Σ embeds faithfully in 𝒢′_Σ.
20. **What collapses and what survives in 𝒢′.**
    - Data arrows (functors) tensor bifunctorially, so the structural loops of items 11 and 14 are trivial: H = H_L = 1_T, and the maps ι, π of item 11 are identities.
    - Non-invertible Gray interchange survives one dimension up. For two transformations, the two schedules are related by the modification F(χ ▷ 1_A), which has no inverse in 𝒢′ (in the Gray model, with the atom A sent to the terminal 2-category, it is the 2-cell of the walking Gray square).
    - So in 𝒢′ the schedule structure of the Background lives on generators of probe degree J (transformations X ⊗ J → Y or J ⊗ X → Y, and more generally cells X ⊗ D_k → Y), with interchangers as modifications. Items 21–28 show that this structure is not a product of schedule categories.

21. **Transformation-level homs are not posets (proved).** Take one data atom A and two generating left-parameter transformations α, β : 1_A ⇒ 1_A (maps J ▷ A → A with both endpoints 1_A). Write α·β for "β, then α". With κ : D₂ → J ⊗ J the Gray square (right factor first ⇒ left factor first), F_{α,β} = α ∘ (1_J ▷ β) gives a forward crossing σ_{α,β} = F_{α,β} ∘ (κ ▷ 1_A) : α·β ⇒ β·α. Assigning the generators to the two probe positions the other way round gives σ_{β,α} : β·α ⇒ α·β. Let q = σ_{β,α} ∘ σ_{α,β}.
    - *Separating model.* Let 𝒳 be the strict 2-category with one object, 1-cells ℕ under addition, 2-cells only n ⇒ n, labelled by k ∈ ℕ, with all compositions adding labels. T : J ⊗ 𝒳 → 𝒳, identity at the ends, sending the J-arrow to 1 and its interchanger with n to the cell labelled n, is a transformation Id ⇒ Id. Interpreting α and β as T sends each crossing to label 1 and q^r to label 2r.
    - *Consequence.* (ℕ, +) embeds in End(α·β), and q is not invertible. A category equivalent to a poset has trivial endomorphism monoids, so the hom is not equivalent to any product of schedule categories S(P). The same happens with one generator τ : 1_A ⇒ 1_A and its self-interchanger on τ·τ.
    - *Where the schedule picture breaks.* A fixed planar diagram fixes the left/right positions of events. Here α and β are endo-transformations of the same arrow, so either can occupy either probe position, and both crossings are well typed.
22. **Fixed-bound naturality of evaluation and unit is strict (proved).** For e_{A,C} : A ⊗ (A\C) → C and a right-parameter family γ : C ⊗ P → C′, put R_A^P(γ) = Λ_A(γ ∘ (e_{A,C} ⊗ P)). Then e_{A,C′} ∘ (A ⊗ R_A^P(γ)) = γ ∘ (e_{A,C} ⊗ P), and similarly for the unit η^A and for right closure. The identity transformation of an evaluation, unit or counit data arrow has trivial interchange with everything, since its parameter map factors through ! : J → I.
23. **Varying the bound argument changes sides (proved).** For H = A\C and a right-parameter transformation a : X ⊗ J → A with endpoints f₀, f₁, the mate ā = Λ_X(e ∘ (a ⊗ H)) : J ⊗ H → X\C is a left-parameter transformation between f₀^*, f₁^*. For a left-parameter b : J ⊗ Y → H with endpoints g₀, g₁, the Gray square transported through currying is M_{a,b} : (ā g₁)·(f₀^* b) ⇒ (f₁^* b)·(ā g₀). The right-closure mirror goes (f₁^* b)·(ā g₀) ⇒ (ā g₁)·(f₀^* b). Both run right factor first ⇒ left factor first.
24. **A noninvertible structural crossing (proved).** With A = X ⊗ J, C = A ⊗ J ⊗ Y, a = 1_A viewed as a right-parameter transformation X ⊗ J → A (its endpoints X → X ⊗ J differ), and b = η^A_{J⊗Y}, the triangle identity gives M_{a,b} = Λ_X(X ⊗ κ ⊗ Y). With X and Y sent to terminal 2-categories this is the walking Gray square, which has no reverse. So structural terms with a genuine J-parameter are directed events, and their side is set by the parameter position, not by the symbol "evaluation" or "unit".
25. **The ω-version is obstructed the same way (proved).** 𝒳 is gaunt. Through the strong monoidal gaunt reflections of Campion (arXiv 2311.00205, Thm 3.14, Cor. 3.15), T gives T_∞ : J ⊗ i𝒳 → i𝒳 in (∞,ω)-categories, and the powers q^r stay distinct in the homotopy category of the transformation hom. In ν(K_P) the inversion weight ρ(J, i) = #{j ∈ J : j > i, j ∥ i} satisfies ρ(∂F) = −1 on every oriented 2-face, so cubical schedule homs have no nonidentity endo-2-cells.

26. **Crossings satisfy exactly the positive braid relations (proved).** Let S be a set of generating left-parameter transformations a : 1_A ⇒ 1_A, and 𝒞_S the full modification category on words in S (xy means "y, then x"). The Gray square gives crossings c_{x,y} : xy → yx with c_{x,1} = 1 = c_{1,x}, c_{x,yz} = (1_y ⊗ c_{x,z}) ∘ (c_{x,y} ⊗ 1_z), c_{xy,z} = (c_{x,z} ⊗ 1_y) ∘ (1_x ⊗ c_{y,z}), natural in both variables with respect to all modifications. So 𝒞_S is strict monoidal with a noninvertible braiding, and crossings satisfy far commutation and Yang–Baxter.
    - *Separating model.* Let Br⁺_S be the category of words in S and colored positive braids (adjacent crossings modulo s_i s_j = s_j s_i for |i − j| ≥ 2 and s_i s_{i+1} s_i = s_{i+1} s_i s_{i+1}, no inverses). Let 𝒳_S be the one-object strict 2-category with hom Br⁺_S. Each a gives a transformation T_a : Id ⇒ Id of 𝒳_S with component a and naturality the block crossing aw → wa. Interpreting A as 𝒳_S and each a as T_a, and evaluating modifications at the object, gives R : 𝒞_S → Br⁺_S with RF = 1, where F : Br⁺_S → 𝒞_S sends crossings to whiskered Gray squares.
    - *Consequences.* Two crossing words are equal in 𝒢′ iff they are equal as positive braids. For α, β the crossing-generated category is the free category on the two-cycle σ_{α,β}, σ_{β,α}, and ℕ is a retract of End(α·β). For one generator τ, the crossings in End(τⁿ) form exactly the positive braid monoid B⁺ₙ (for example s₁²s₂² ≠ s₂²s₁² in B⁺₃). Since R is defined on all modifications, no modification changes the multiset of letters of a word.
27. **Event status is not determined by type (proved).** For a left-parameter presentation t : J ▷ X → Y, t is a degenerate event when it factors through ! ▷ 1_X : J ▷ X → X. The structural terms d = (i₀ !) ▷ 1_X and e = 1_{J▷X} have the same data-arrow type J ▷ X → J ▷ X. d is degenerate and e is not (with X terminal, e is 1_J). For general terms, 1_{1_A} and a generator α : 1_A ⇒ 1_A have the same full boundary.
28. **A probe-dependent non-poset hom with no generators (proved).** If the probes include K = B²M, the one-object, one-1-cell strict 2-category with 2-cells M = {1, e}, e² = e, then for f = i ▷ 1_X : X → K ▷ X the empty composite 1_f has the nonidentity idempotent endomodification k ▷ 1_X, where k : D₂ → K selects e. K is a finite connected colimit of pasting probes (coequalise the boundary maps J ⇉ D₂, push out along J → I, then coequalise D₂ ⇉ B²ℕ at the generator and its square). Whether 𝒢′ designates such colimits as probes is part of the definition left open in item 19.


## Recent results (proved)

29. **Endpoint-symmetric rules cannot generate directed interchange.** If a coherence rule admits a goal u ⇒ v whenever it admits v ⇒ u, it produces a cell q ⇒ p in the walking Gray square, whose hom (0,0) → (1,1) is the arrow category [p → q]. Reverse cells are closed under composition and whiskering.
30. **One-way permissions.** For a dependency poset P and a relation R saying which pairs may cross in which direction (each pair crosses at most once), the crossing category C(P, R) is thin, with an O(|E|²) decision procedure; C(P, R) need not be S(P), because pairwise permissions can be cyclic.
31. **Endotransformation signatures (complete).** For one data atom A and generators α_s : 1_A ⇒ 1_A (arities without residuals), under representable probe homs, the transformation hom of 𝒢′_Σ over 1_A is B Br⁺_S, including all terms built with residuals, currying, evaluation and the designated gluings. The proof gives an explicit non-closed model with an inverse interpretation, and a **relative-conservativity lemma**: freely adjoining data residuals is fully faithful on old data arrows (Hasegawa-style gluing along the nerve into presheaves sending designated cones to limits, biclosed by Day reflection).

## Questions

**Q1 (main: a directed coherence rule in every dimension).** Items 29, 39 and 41 show that a rule over Gray pasting types must carry a **directed admissibility judgment**: some parallel goals must be rejected in both directions. Is there a class of Gray pasting types (formulas in ⊗, \, / with a time direction recorded as a tensor position; by item 42 not all free) and an asymmetric coherence rule over them, whose admissibility judgment is decidable on finite data (for example Steiner positivity of the connecting chain, extended through residuals), that generates exactly the cells of the free monoidal biclosed (∞,ω)-category with Campion's Gray tensor on a set of generating 1-cells, in every dimension? Give the rule and the exactness proof (an initiality statement for data models with tensor, both residuals, evaluation, currying and their probe compatibility would do, e.g. over the lax-grid model of item 44), or an impossibility theorem covering every rule of the coherence-over-shapes kind (every admissible goal over a pasting type has a cell, admissible parallel cells are connected) that does not quotient arities.

## What counts as a solution

A rule with an exactness proof, or an impossibility theorem covering every rule of the coherence-over-shapes kind; or a reference treating free Gray-monoidal (bi)closed (∞,ω)-categories with non-invertible interchange. Normal forms for the free structures (items 31–35, 43) count only as far as the rule needs them.

## Researcher Response 1

### A boundary decision through nested residuals

There is an exact directed test on a class of **interpreted** Gray pasting types that contains the non-free arity of item 42. It applies in every dimension and allows residuals in the bound argument of another residual. There is also an obstruction to using objectwise inhabitation in the ambient Gray model to transfer such a test to the free data theory.

Write \(iK\) for a gaunt strict category regarded as an object of Campion’s \(\mathrm{Cat}_\omega\). Take as input any collection of finite-dimensional gaunt strict categories \(K\) given by their finite cell and composition tables, including the identities through the specified top dimension. For instance, one can use the walking arrow and finite Gray cubes. Use the following simultaneous grammar.

\[
 G ::= iK \mid P\backslash G \mid G/P,
 \qquad P ::= G \mid P\otimes P.
\]

The distinction records where the algorithm needs a table. An argument \(P\) can be an arbitrary tensor word of previously computed \(G\)'s. A tensor in a result position can be supplied as a leaf when its ambient value is known with such a table, as for a Gray cube.

**Theorem.** Each \(G\) admits an effective finite gaunt table. For parallel \((k-1)\)-cells \(u,v\) in that table, the following judgment is decidable from the ordered boundary alone. It enumerates precisely the \(k\)-cells from \(u\) to \(v\) in the ambient Gray residual.

1. At a leaf, select the entries with source \(u\) and target \(v\).
2. For \(P\backslash H\), flatten \(P\) into its ordered factors. Enumerate maps from the Gray tensor product of those factors followed by the globe \(D_k\) into \(H\). Prescribe the restrictions at the negative and positive faces of the globe to be the maps for \(u\) and \(v\), respectively.
3. For \(H/P\), use the same prescription with \(D_k\) **before** the factors of \(P\). Admit the goal if the resulting list is nonempty. Distinct entries in the list retain their own boundaries for the next judgment.

Here is the justification for the enumeration. Let \(B_1,\ldots,B_m,T\) have finite gaunt tables, and let \(S\) be the tensor of the \(iB_j\) in the indicated order. Then

\[
 \operatorname{Map}_{\mathrm{Cat}_\omega}(S,iT)
 \simeq
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{str}\,\omega\mathrm{Cat}}
   (B_1\otimes_{\rm str}\cdots\otimes_{\rm str}B_m,T)_{\rm disc}.
\]

Indeed, form the tensor first in flagged categories. Reflection to complete categories is strong monoidal, and mapping into the complete target \(iT\) computes the left side. Reflection from flagged categories to strict categories is also strong monoidal. The same target is strict, each factor is already strict, and the tensor in the reflected strict category is the usual strict Gray tensor. This gives the right side, naturally in the factors, including the face maps. This argument uses the universal property of the two reflections only when mapping into their common local target. [Campion, Examples 1.30 and 4.7, Theorem 3.14 and Corollaries 3.15 and 4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00205)

For clarity, the strict hom-set on the right has the following terminating algorithm, even when a factor has relations.

- Present each table as a finite colimit of globes and globular pastings of two composable cells. Use a globe for each cell, record its faces and identities, and for each table entry \(c=b\circ_j a\) use a composable-pair shape. Its two inclusions select \(a,b\), and its composite globe selects \(c\). A compatible family of maps into any strict category specifies exactly a table-preserving functor. Identities above the top dimension are forced.
- Distribute the strict tensor over these finite diagrams, using biclosedness in each factor. The resulting pieces are tensors of globes and composable-pair shapes. Each has a finite strong Steiner presentation. The generators are the tuples of basis elements, and the signed tensor differential computes their faces. A map from a piece to \(T\) is an assignment of a target cell to each generator respecting its two composite boundaries. Enumerate assignments using the finite tables of \(T\), treating cells above its dimension as iterated identities. Keep the families agreeing on the finitely many diagram maps. This uses the relations of each *categorical* factor through its table. [Steiner, Examples 3.9 and 4.7, Theorem 6.1 and §7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0403237)
- All required restrictions can be evaluated effectively. On the pieces, a basis atom and its image under a globular incidence or composite map have computable Steiner arrays. To obtain a composition expression for such an array, one may enumerate typed expressions in the basis atoms until their array equals it. Generation by the atoms proves termination. In particular this subsidiary search never asks whether a prescribed boundary has a filler.

To complete the induction on \(G\), suppose \(H\) has dimension at most \(d\). Gaunt \(d\)-categories form an exponential ideal in flagged categories, so either residual into \(H\) is again a gaunt \(d\)-category in the complete model. Compute its cells for \(0\le k\le d\) by the two adjunctions, using the enumeration above, with no face constraint at \(k=0\). Compute identities by precomposing with the globe collapse. To compute \(b\circ_j a\), use the composable-pair shape in place of the time globe, prescribe its two restrictions, then restrict along its composite globe. The pair has a unique extension because that shape represents composable pairs in the strict residual table. Thus the result of each induction step supplies *all* the data needed when it becomes a bound argument. For \(k>d\), the only filler is the identity when \(u=v\). The dimension bound is read from the result leaf. The same algorithm applies at every nesting depth. ∎

**Two checks of the judgment.** In the walking square \(S=J\otimes J\), consider vertex assignments that fix or transpose the two axes. Their edges have adjacent endpoints and are forced. The identity vertex assignment for \(J\otimes J\to S\) extends uniquely. Transposing the two coordinates exchanges the two boundary paths, so extending that assignment would require the reverse of the unique nonidentity square. It has zero extensions. By currying, this computes the two orders of the parameter test in item 17.

For the nested residual \(D=\operatorname{Fun}([1],[2])\) of item 42, the objects of either residual from \(D\) into \(J\) are exactly the upsets of the six-element componentwise poset \(a_{ij}\), \(0\le i\le j\le2\). There are eight. Six satisfy the degree-zero relation of item 42 after applying the characteristic function. The other two upsets are

\[
 \{a_{12},a_{22}\},\qquad
 \{a_{02},a_{11},a_{12},a_{22}\}.
\]

One can reproduce the count by listing all \(2^6\) characteristic functions, discarding a function if it decreases on any comparable pair, then testing \(f_{01}+f_{12}=f_{02}+f_{11}\). Both exceptional maps occur in the categorical enumeration above.

### An obstruction to deriving free admissibility from modelwise existence

There is a fixed relation-free signature and a fixed data goal which has a map in **every interpretation in the ambient Gray category**, but has no map in the free data theory of Q1. The same example defeats testing nonemptiness in all data models with finitely many object classes for mutual reachability.

Take atoms \(E,P,Q\). For each pair below declare two independent generating 1-cells, one in each direction. There are no equations on their composites. Juxtaposition denotes tensor.

\[
 (EE,E),\quad (EP,P),\quad (PE,P),\quad
 (EQ,Q),\quad (QE,Q),\quad (PQ,E).
\]

The goal is \(QP\to E\). Either adjunction gives a residual formulation of exactly the same test, \(P\to Q\backslash E\) or \(Q\to E/P\).

**Proof.** In a data model identify objects for this argument if there are maps in both directions. Composition makes this an equivalence relation and tensor makes its classes a semigroup \(S\). Write \(e,p,q\) for the classes of the atoms. The declarations give \(e^2=e\), \(p,q\in eSe\), and \(pq=e\). In the monoid \(eSe\), left multiplication by \(p\) has right inverse left multiplication by \(q\). If \(S\) is finite, both functions are bijections. Evaluating their other composite at \(e\) gives \(qp=e\), so the goal has a map. In particular the argument applies to the ambient Gray category itself, even allowing infinite interpretations of the atoms. Its object reachability has just two classes, empty and inhabited, since an object of a target supplies a constant functor.

For a separating data model, take the monoid \(M\) of functions on \(\mathbb N\) generated under composition by \(p(n)=\max(n-1,0)\) and \(q(n)=n+1\). Let data objects be subsets of \(M\), arrows inclusions, and tensor setwise multiplication. The data residuals are \(A\backslash B=\{m:A\{m\}\subseteq B\}\) and \(B/A=\{m:\{m\}A\subseteq B\}\). Send each inhabited probe to \(\{1\}\) and the empty probe to \(\varnothing\), and act by setwise multiplication on the indicated side. This is functorial, and it is monoidal since a Gray product is inhabited exactly when both factors are inhabited. The latter follows from tensoring two points and from preservation of the initial object in each variable. For a fixed data boundary \(U,V\) and a varying probe \(Z\) on either side, the presheaf of maps is represented by the terminal category if \(U\subseteq V\), and by the empty category otherwise. Indeed, in the latter case there is exactly one map when \(Z\) is empty. The same argument works in a one-hole tensor context, since both multipliers are central and the empty multiplier is absorbing. Hence the probe homs satisfy grid gluing and completeness. The displayed residual formulas give the stipulated adjunctions also in every fixed probe context.

Interpret \(E,P,Q\) as the singletons of \(1,p,q\). Every declaration is realized since \(pq=1\). But \(qp(0)=1\ne0\), hence \(\{qp\}\nsubseteq\{1\}\). Initiality would send any free map with the proposed boundary to this missing inclusion. ∎

As a finite check on the obstruction, enumerate all binary operation tables on labelled sets of sizes 1, 2 and 3. Retain associative tables, then enumerate triples satisfying the six displayed premises. The respective numbers of tables are 1, 8 and 113. The numbers of qualifying triples summed over those tables are 1, 14 and 267, all satisfying \(qp=e\). The finite-monoid argument proves the implication at every size.

The first theorem decides actual directed boundaries at the indicated interpreted arities. The second shows why even testing existence under **every** ambient assignment cannot establish that those fillers are generated uniformly from a signature. A rule on generic shapes can still use the occurrences of the generators and close its accepted instances under substitution and composition. To settle Q1 one must prove exactness for such uniform operations, or exclude those rules as well. Neither theorem supplies that step.

## Researcher Response 2

### The free data model over lax grids

One can construct the initial data model in Q1 without classifying the maps between interpreted residuals. The construction uses two-vertex planar trees. In a tensor tree one knows the operation that pairs two adjacent inputs. In a residual tree one knows the evaluation at the root. In both cases one knows the operation on the outer boundary and asks for the other vertex. The higher-dimensional assertion is about the missing vertex **together with its comparison on the outer boundary**. Keeping this comparison gives a precise initiality theorem.

Use the ambient reading of items 17–20. A data model has a monoidal \(\infty\)-category of probes and data as in item 19, with the probe part fixed, a data output for every word containing data, both residuals on data, and complete lax-grid homs in every single varying probe position. Maps are strong monoidal and preserve the residuals. All universal properties below are equivalences of spaces. The directed cells are obtained by varying a probe.

Take the probes \(\mathcal P\) to be the full category on finite tensor words of lax grids and globes with the actual Gray maps. The empty probe *word* denotes the tensor unit \(I\). Every additional probe is a retract of a grid: a globe is a retract of a Gray cube and tensor preserves retractions. Presheaves of spaces split idempotents. Thus restriction from \(\mathcal P\) to the grids is an equivalence on presheaves: extend a presheaf to a retract by splitting the image idempotent. The extension on maps and all its choices are determined by the splitting universal property. Impose grid descent and completeness on the restriction. The resulting category of probe homs is Campion’s \(\mathrm{Cat}_\omega\). [Campion, Theorem A](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.09376) [Keidar–Neuhauser, Definitions 3.21–3.23, Theorem 4.15 and Corollary 4.16](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27574v1)

**Ordered sequents.** Use the probe colours and the formal data colours generated from the atoms by tensor, the two residuals and the left and right probe actions. Keep brackets in data colours. Write \(O(\Gamma;C)\) for the operation space of an ordered list of colours with output \(C\). A list containing data has data output. A pure probe list has only probe outputs, with its given operations in \(\mathcal P\). In particular there is no nullary data operation and no identification of a data colour with the probe unit. Substitution is planar multicategory substitution. Write \(L\) for the list consisting of the inputs to the left of a specified position and \(R\) for the inputs to its right.

At the following generic formula sequents put one distinguished operation, allowing substitution of colours:
\[
 m_{UV}:(U,V)\longrightarrow U\otimes V,\qquad
 e^\ell:(A,A\backslash C)\longrightarrow C,\qquad
 e^r:(C/A,A)\longrightarrow C .
\]
Here \(A,C\) are data. Bare data variables can be instantiated by any data formulas. For pure \(U,V\) use the identity on their probe product as \(m_{UV}\); also use the nullary pure operation \(m_0:()\to I\). These declarations give no action of a residual on arrows and supply no curry of an arbitrary arrow.

**Three restrictions.** In the following table \(H\) is the type of the missing vertex, \(B\) is the type of the outer operation, and \(r:H\to B\) pastes the distinguished vertex to the missing one. Each entry is a space of operations on the displayed formula sequent.

| Distinguished vertex | \(H\) | \(B\) | \(r(x)\) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pair | \(O(L,U\otimes V,R;Z)\) | \(O(L,U,V,R;Z)\) | insert \(m_{UV}\) in the specified slot of \(x\) |
| Left evaluation | \(O(\Gamma;A\backslash C)\) | \(O(A,\Gamma;C)\) | \(e^\ell(1_A,x)\) |
| Right evaluation | \(O(\Gamma;C/A)\) | \(O(\Gamma,A;C)\) | \(e^r(x,1_A)\) |

The unit instance of the first row inserts \(m_0\), giving \(O(L,I,R;Z)\to O(L,R;Z)\). Use the first row in every surrounding context with data output, including when the pair itself is pure. Use the other two rows whenever \(\Gamma\) contains data. A varying \(D_k\) in a gap of \(\Gamma\) stays in that gap when the distinguished vertex is pasted. For example, for \(\Gamma=(X,D_k,Y)\) the left evaluation has outer input \((A,X,D_k,Y)\); the right evaluation has outer input \((X,D_k,Y,A)\).

**Extension rule for the table.** Use a finite ball \(D^n\) in spaces with boundary \(S^{n-1}\), including \(D^0=*\) and \(S^{-1}=\varnothing\). This dimension counts invertible homotopies of operations. A directed probe dimension \(k\) occurs separately in the ordered sequent. The input of an extension is
\[
 b:D^n\to B,\qquad u:S^{n-1}\to H,\qquad
 a:r u\simeq b|_{S^{n-1}} .
 \tag{1}
\]
Supply \(x:D^n\to H\) extending \(u\) and a comparison \(r x\simeq b\) extending \(a\), with restriction understood homotopically. In a topological path-fibration presentation these restrictions can be taken literally. Equivalently, with \(\widetilde H=H\times_{B,\operatorname{ev}_0} B^{[0,1]}\), the display is an ordinary lifting problem for \(\operatorname{ev}_1:\widetilde H\to B\). At \(n=0\) the input is just the **given outer operation** \(b\); the output contains its factorization and the comparison to \(b\). At \(n=1\) the provided endpoint comparisons are part of the boundary. For a constant \(b\) this instance connects two factorizations over the same outer operation.

**Lemma.** The rule in all \(n\) is equivalent to \(r\) being an equivalence of spaces. When it holds, the joint space of solutions of each instance is contractible.

*Proof.* Fix \(b_0\in B\) and use the constant disk at \(b_0\). The boundary data become an \((n-1)\)-sphere in the homotopy fibre of \(r\) at \(b_0\). The empty boundary makes this fibre inhabited, the two-point boundary makes it connected, and the higher spheres kill all its homotopy groups. An unbased nullhomotopy of a based sphere gives the zero class also after changing the basepoint back along its track. Hence every homotopy fibre is contractible. Conversely, replacing an equivalence by a trivial Kan fibration makes its relative disk restriction a trivial Kan fibration, by the boundary cofibration and the simplicial lifting axiom. The fibre over (1) is the claimed joint solution space. ∎

The same proof treats prescribed **directed** faces. In a single probe position the restriction \(H(-)\to B(-)\) from a table row is natural. If the row is local for every probe, it is an objectwise equivalence of presheaves. In particular
\[
 H(D_k)\ \longrightarrow\
 H(\partial D_k)\times_{B(\partial D_k)} B(D_k)
 \tag{2}
\]
is an equivalence. Here evaluations on \(\partial D_k\) mean limits on the two globular faces and their common boundaries, and the pullback is homotopy coherent. This follows by replacing \(H\) by \(B\) in the pullback. Formula (2) transfers an outer directed cell with its specified comparisons on the faces. Drawing the opposite \(D_k\)-cell requires outer data in that other orientation.

### Construction and exactness for joint extensions

**Theorem.** Given any set of generating data arrows \(\Sigma\) with formal formula endpoints, there is a free data model \(\mathcal F_\Sigma\) for the table and grid conditions. For every data model \(M\) and fixed interpretation of the atoms there is an equivalence
\[
 \operatorname{Map}_{\rm data}(\mathcal F_\Sigma,M)_{\rm atoms}
 \simeq
 \prod_{f:S\to T\ {\rm in}\ \Sigma}
     \operatorname{Map}_{M}(\llbracket S\rrbracket,\llbracket T\rrbracket).
 \tag{3}
\]
The left side is relative to the fixed probes and the chosen atom interpretation. Its fibre over specified generator values is contractible. The probe-only arities in \(\mathcal F_\Sigma\) are exactly the original probe arities.

*Proof.* First work with fixed colours in the \(\infty\)-category of planar multicategories in spaces, restricting to the two sorts of profiles specified above. Concretely this is a presentable category: take space-valued presheaves on finite colour-labelled planar trees with allowed vertices, localize at the maps from the union of vertex corollas along their edges to each tree, and require the space at an edge of each specified colour to be contractible. In this description an operation space is evaluation at a corolla. Take the tree maps to be colour-preserving planar operad maps between the free tree operads. The Segal conditions give the planar substitutions, including their higher coherences. In particular the space of decorations of a tree, for fixed vertex operations, is contractible. One may equivalently use the nonsymmetric operad description of monoidal \(\infty\)-categories. [Gepner–Haugseng, §2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.3178)

Start under the probe multicategory, extended on the data colours by identities, and freely mark the indicated data pairing and evaluation corollas. Call the resulting base \(U\). In the category under \(U\), write \(F(p)\) for adjoining one free corolla at profile \(p\). Then \(\operatorname{Map}_{U/}(F(p),O)\) is the operation space at \(p\), including its homotopies. For each table row there is a map \(F(p_B)\to F(p_H)\) sending the generating corolla to the displayed two-vertex composite. This assertion can be made without choosing a composition law: take the two-vertex tree under its distinguished vertex. Mapping the tree into a Segal object amounts to freely specifying its other vertex. The outer-corolla face gives the indicated map to that representing object. Mapping out of the map gives exactly \(r\).

In the same undercategory, fix a word with one varying probe \(G\), at least one fixed data input, and data output \(Z\). The assignment \(G\mapsto F(p_{W(G);Z})\), where \(p_{W(G);Z}\) denotes the profile \((W(G);Z)\), is **covariant**: a probe arrow \(G\to G'\) takes the free operation at \(G\) to the restriction of the free operation at \(G'\). Extend this assignment to grid presheaves by colimits. By Yoneda, mapping its value on a presheaf \(K\) into \(O\) gives the space of diagrams of shape \(K\) in \(O(W(-);Z)\). Apply it to the grid Segal core inclusions and to the coordinatewise completeness maps. Locality at their images is exactly the assertion that this one-hole hom is a univalent grid sheaf.

These tests have finite descriptions. A grid core uses the finite incidence poset of its elementary cubes. On each simplicial coordinate of the grid nerve one can test completeness on the diagram of an edge \(f\), a left inverse and a right inverse, with two triangles exhibiting the two composites as identities. Its collapse to a point induces the completeness test. Indeed, over \(f\) this diagram has empty space of choices unless \(f\) is an equivalence. For an equivalence each inverse-with-its-triangle is a fibre of composition with an equivalence, hence contractible; the two choices are independent. This computes the space of equivalences in that coordinate of the Segal space. Requiring the identity inclusion from the space of objects to this space to be an equivalence is precisely completeness. These incidences concern diagrams *in* a hom presheaf. The maps of the underlying pure probes retain their values.

Let \(L\) be the simultaneous reflective localization at these maps and the table maps. It exists since the undercategory is presentable and the profiles, grid tests and table rows form a set. Put
\[
 \mathcal F_\Sigma=L\!\left(\coprod\nolimits^{U/}_{f\in\Sigma} F(p_f)\right).
 \tag{4}
\]
The empty coproduct here is \(U\). The localization has a cellular description. For a test map \(j:K\to T\), attach extensions of
\[
 (K\otimes D^n)\amalg_{K\otimes S^{n-1}}(T\otimes S^{n-1})
   \longrightarrow T\otimes D^n .
 \tag{5}
\]
Tensors in (5) are copowers by spaces in the undercategory. They are distinct from the Gray tensor in a formula. Colimits in (5) are homotopy colimits. Mapping (5) into \(O\) gives exactly the extension problem (1) for \(r=\operatorname{Map}(j,O)\). Thus attachments preserve mapping spaces into every local object. Conversely, iterating attachments produces a local object: the domains of the tests are compact relative to \(U\). Free corollas corepresent evaluations that preserve filtered colimits, since filtered colimits of spaces commute with the finite limits in the tree Segal conditions. The other test objects are finite homotopy colimits of these corollas; for a finite face poset one can use its finite nondegenerate chains. Finite copowers and pushouts in (5) preserve compactness. Attach along representatives of all boundary maps at each stage. At the sequential colimit every boundary map factors up to homotopy through a stage and acquires its extension at the next one. The lemma implies locality, and preservation of mapping spaces into local objects characterizes the reflection. This also explains why imposing the probe and residual conditions simultaneously suffices.

To identify the local objects, the first row says that insertion of the designated pair induces an equivalence in *every* outer context. Iterating it represents each ordered word by its bracketed tensor. Composite representing operations represent concatenation because the composites of their restriction equivalences are equivalences. The space of comparisons of two such representations **over the represented word** is contractible by Yoneda. In the category of operators this gives coCartesian lifts of binary mergers and of the unit; concatenating lifts and applying the inert projections gives lifts for every ordered block partition. Passing from the category with named objects to its underlying \(\infty\)-category preserves every operation space between those objects. This gives a monoidal \(\infty\)-category, with unit of probe sort. Its unary mixed homs between the two sorts are empty. Conversely the multimap spaces of any such monoidal category satisfy the first row by tensoring the inputs. [Gepner–Haugseng, Definition 2.2.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.3178)

For a data pair \(A,C\), the second row identifies the functor \(X\mapsto\operatorname{Map}(A\otimes X,C)\) on data objects with the representable at \(A\backslash C\), naturally in \(X\) by substitution. Postcomposition in \(C\) and full faithfulness of Yoneda give the right-adjoint functor and all its coherences. The third row represents \(X\mapsto\operatorname{Map}(X\otimes A,C)\) at \(C/A\) in exactly the indicated order. Conversely these two adjunctions imply the corresponding rows for an arbitrary mixed \(\Gamma\) by representing its word. The counits are the marked evaluations, currying is the missing vertex, and (1) supplies the comparisons. Naturality in changes of a probe and (2) give the two-sided compatibility. The grid conditions identify each one-hole hom with an \((\infty,\omega)\)-category. The ambient Gray model satisfies all the conditions by biclosedness and the grid nerve theorem. Thus locality imposes the stated data-model axioms with their mapping-space universal properties.

One can also read off the Gray product on homs. Write \(H_\ell(X,Y)(P)=O(P,X;Y)\) and \(H_r(X,Y)(P)=O(X,P;Y)\). Substituting \(f:(P,X)\to Y\) into \(g:(Q,Y)\to Z\) gives an operation on \((Q,P,X)\). The pure pairing row identifies its space with \(H_\ell(X,Z)(Q\otimes P)\). For right probes the corresponding inputs are \((X,P,Q)\), giving \(H_r(X,Z)(P\otimes Q)\). These pairings are natural in both probes by planar substitution. By the universal property of Day convolution they induce composition out of the Day products in precisely these orders. Their targets are univalent grid sheaves, so the composition maps factor through the reflected Day products, which are Campion’s Gray products. Unary identities at the probe unit give the units, and the tree Segal condition supplies the associativity of iterated substitutions. With probes on both sides, substituting an operation on \((P,X,Q)\) into one on \((P',Y,Q')\) gives \((P',P,X,Q,Q')\). This gives the mixed compatibility without permuting either pair of probes. [Keidar–Neuhauser, §3.4 and Corollary 4.16](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27574v1)

For pure-probe preservation, restriction to the pure colours has a right adjoint on these sorted multicategories. Extend a pure multicategory by a point at every allowed data-output profile. There is a unique coherent choice of substitutions with those outputs, and a pure-output tree has only pure vertices. Under \(U\) carry the data marks to those points. This extension of any pure target under the given probe multicategory is local: all data tests take values in points. Hence by adjunction, mapping the pure restriction of (4) into any such target gives the same space as mapping the initial pure base into it. Relative Yoneda identifies the pure restriction of (4) with the specified probe multicategory.

Finally, mapping (4) into a local \(M\) gives the product in (3) by the reflection and the free-corolla property. For a target with an arbitrary set of objects pull its multicategory back along the assignment of formal colours to the values of the formulas in \(M\). This computes the space relative to those colours. If representations in \(M\) are given only up to equivalence, extending the fixed atom assignment to a tensor or residual colour *with its representing operation* has contractible choice by its representing property. Induction on formulas therefore gives the same relative mapping space. ∎

### Single-cell goals and the admissibility test

Here is the distinction between a test on a generic goal and a test on a proposed factorization. The former is sufficient. The given outer operation \(b\) belongs to the *context of hypotheses* of the generic pasting type, just as the elementary cells belong to the context of a grid pasting. The context declares cells at formula sequents and their faces; it has no sort of factorizations and no variable for the desired filler. Write a profile \((U_1,\ldots,U_t;V)\) as the formula sequent \(U_1,\ldots,U_t\vdash V\); its domain after representation is the ordered tensor of the \(U_i\). Slots, including a time slot, are occurrences in this ordered list. The following is one sparse choice of generic pasting sequents. Arbitrary term and type substitutions into a generic coherence, ordinary planar substitution, and the ambient lax-grid rules are understood. Admissibility below is the primitive judgment, before closing the calculus under these operations.

* For a distinguished corolla admit its generic formula sequent. Interpret its coherence as the marked operation. This supplies the three structure maps used in the table. On pure probes use the specified probe operation.
* For a row of the table, take the finite generic context consisting of the outer disk \(b\), the boundary sphere \(u\), and the side cylinder \(a\) of (1). Use free variables for their cells with just the indicated face incidences. In dimension zero this context has the single arrow hypothesis \(b\) in profile \(p_B\). **Admit the ordered boundary \(u\) in the single formula sequent \(p_H\)**. Denote the resulting coherence by \(c\).
* In that *same* context, admit the sphere in \(p_B\) with bottom \(r(c)\), sides \(a\), and top \(b\). Denote its coherence by \(\epsilon\). In globular notation it goes from \(r(c)\) to the disk obtained by adjoining the side collar to \(b\). Its type asks for an invertible homotopy of data operations. The bottom contains the preceding coherence as a term; it is not a variable ranging over arbitrary operations with boundary \(u\).

Both of the last two outputs are single cells at formula sequents. To see their ordered boundaries explicitly, model the \(n\)-disk as a ball with two hemispheres, using the empty boundary at \(n=0\). The boundary of \(D^n\times[0,1]\) is the union of its bottom disk and the disk formed by its sides and top. These meet precisely on the bottom sphere. This gives two parallel \(n\)-cells even when \(r u\) and \(b|_{S^{n-1}}\) agree only along \(a\). One may use fixed finite triangulations of these balls and collars throughout.

There is a direct check that splitting the outputs this way preserves (4). Attach a free \(p_H\)-disk along \(u\). In the resulting multicategory attach a free \(p_B\)-disk along the displayed boundary involving the *new* \(c\). Mapping this two-step attachment into any target amounts first to choosing \(x\) over \(u\), then a disk on \(r(x)\cup a\cup b\). Together these are exactly a relative lift in the path-fibration presentation. More formally, in (5) for a table row \(K\) and \(T\) are the two free corollas under \(U\). Replace \(K\to T\) by its mapping cylinder \(T\amalg_K(K\otimes[0,1])\) with \(K\) included at the other end. The relative disk attachment has one \(T\)-disk and one \(K\)-prism with precisely the attaching maps just given. Relative Yoneda therefore identifies the two extensions, including their mapping spaces. Applying the same rule in the next dimension, with constant outer data, connects parallel lifts with fixed admissible boundary. This comparison keeps the lateral faces. It would be unsound to force all possible choices of \(\epsilon\) to agree after fixing \(x\) independently.

The rule also has a direct directed instance. In the context with one operation \(h:W(G)\to Z\) and time at the indicated gap, the boundary \(hW(s),hW(t)\) admits a restriction cell exactly when a probe map \(D_k\to G\) has ordered faces \(s,t\). Enumerate the actual maps of finite probes to test this. A restriction cell is the substitution of its probe map into \(h\); these operations are already part of the ambient calculus. All such maps retain their incidence even when several have the same faces. Further substitutions may use a composite or a curried term for \(h\). On other directed inputs the cut rule works at the profile containing the time probe itself. For example, an outer \(b:(A,X,D_k,Y)\to C\) gives a cell at \((X,D_k,Y;A\backslash C)\). Its orientation is inherited from \(b\) by restriction. Comparison of two such constructions can use (1) on a profile containing \(D_k\). The ball in (1) accounts for invertible comparisons, independently of this directed dimension.

One can include a boundary-specified cut directly in the judgment. Its generic hypotheses are the two parallel \(H\)-faces \(u\) in a marked slot, the **outer** \(B\)-cell \(b\) on \(D_k\), and the face comparisons \(a:r u\simeq b|_{\partial D_k}\) with their common lower boundary. Admit the ordered goal \(u^-\Rightarrow u^+\) at \(H\) and retain the comparison to \(b\) relative to \(a\). This template has no \(H\)-cell on \(D_k\) among its hypotheses. To verify that adding it is conservative, write \(F_H(P),F_B(P)\) for the two free corollas at the respective positioned profiles, extending to a formal boundary by the finite colimit of its faces. The map of generic presentations is
\[
 F_B(D_k)\amalg_{F_B(\partial D_k)}F_H(\partial D_k)
       \longrightarrow F_H(D_k).
 \tag{6}
\]
Its map on operation spaces in any local model is (2), hence an equivalence. Extending relative to all space spheres as in (5) therefore adds a contractible choice on every admissible instance. In a fibrant presentation of the globular face diagram, relative extension means filling the globe with exactly the two displayed faces; equivalently it means taking the homotopy fibre over that boundary. The comparison is a homotopy of positioned operations with its prescribed sides. Recognition of (6) reads off a *globular* ordered boundary in the designated formula sequent. The pushout in (6) is a proof that this instance of the rule is exact, not an additional data type that the judgment has to form.

For precision, the recognition procedure takes a *generic finite typed context, a formula sequent with its indicated position, and the proposed ordered boundary*. For the data clauses, scan the slot and the formula constructors for each of the three rows and the unit instance. Form the generic boundary patterns above at the displayed dimensions. Compare incidences, colours and boundary expressions up to renaming of context variables, with all occurrences of a repeated variable matched consistently. The second output pattern is tested by constructing the formal first-output term on that context. It requires no search for an operation \(x\). A finite incidence presentation can be tested by trying all dimension- and sort-preserving bijections of its variables. Use the distinguished-corolla cases directly. For probe restrictions compare the displayed restrictions in the finite probe tables; a grid gluing uses the finite elementary incidence diagram of the given grid. The choices of slot, word splits and dimension are bounded by the input words, cells and goal. Formula substitutions may instead be performed after this generic test, so it suffices to use fresh formal data colours in the patterns and literal equality of terms there. These cases give a decidable local rule. Deciding whether an arbitrary sequent has *some* composite of substitution instances is a different quantification, already undecidable in item 38.

**Where to put an invertible parameter.** There is a slot interpretation of the disks and collars as well. Put an extra gap in the free part of a profile and write \(X_j=O(W([j]);Z)\). The probe-unit row identifies \(X_0\) with the original operation space. The coordinate is a complete Segal space. Its subspace \(X_j^{\simeq}\) of strings of equivalences is equivalent to \(X_0\) via the totally degenerate string: this is completeness for \(j=1\) and follows for every \(j\) by the Segal equivalence. The degeneracies from \(X_0\) give a levelwise equivalence from the constant simplicial space to \(X_\bullet^{\simeq}\). Consequently diagrams of any finite simplicial shape \(K\) in that core have space \(\operatorname{Map}(|K|,X_0)\), by writing \(K\) as a colimit of simplices. Inverses on its edges can be displayed by the finite two-triangle data above. This realizes the balls, their boundaries and the prism by labels at actual formula profiles with a time slot. Under a cut, use the image of the same gap. In particular a cut does not commute a directed time past the bound object. Object variables in these profiles can be interpreted by arbitrary higher categories, including the non-free residual in item 42.

More explicitly, take the ambient calculus to be planar substitution over the one-hole univalent grid sheaves of item 44. Reflecting at the tree and grid conditions first and then at the images of the three table maps gives exactly the simultaneous reflection, since mapping into a local object has the same universal property in either construction. Thus the single-cell compilation above is needed only for the additional data rules. Ambient restrictions, composition of specified grid diagrams, and invertible comparison in their mapping spaces already belong to this base. The initiality proof accounts for their interaction with every new formula.

The two-step attachment proof identifies the new formula coherences with the table part of the cellular construction. For a local target the space of coherent choices of their joint value on all instances of a fixed template is contractible: the solution map to the space of instances is an equivalence, and a section with its comparison is an inverse to that equivalence. This also works in families by pointwise equivalence in functor categories. Thus naming the generic coherences and substituting into them gives the same mapping-space universal property (3). All directed homs are read from their one-hole presheaves; their grid Segal and completeness tests were imposed in the very same reflection.

**Checks on exactness.** At dimension zero for left evaluation, \(c\) has type \(\Gamma\to A\backslash C\) and \(\epsilon\) evaluates it back to the given \(b\). For \(b=r(g)\), the lifts \((g,1)\) and \((c,\epsilon)\) are connected by the \(n=1\) rule. These are the two inverse laws with their correct boundaries. At an elementary cube the whole cube is an input to grid gluing. Its top directed cell can be restricted along its oriented globe. A putative reverse in the generic context with \(h:Q\otimes X\to Z\) would specialize in the ambient model at \(X=I,\ Z=Q,\ h=1_Q\) to a reverse in \(Q\). The walking square excludes this. The same specialization for the pair in item 41 excludes both directions in the four-cube. Hence neither supplying a residual nor closing the rule under substitutions introduces these cells.

Three finite calculations exercise different parts of the proof.

- For each of the 499 functions between labelled sets of sizes 0 through 4, apply (1) in dimensions 0 and 1 in discrete spaces. The first test asks for a preimage of every point. The second tries every pair over the same point and accepts both prescribed endpoints precisely when they agree. Exactly 34 functions pass both tests, the bijections.
- For the identity functor of the one-object groupoid of \(\mathbb Z/2\), there are two comparison arrows at the fixed object. In the joint fibre the unique arrow from comparison \(\alpha\) to comparison \(\beta\) has label \(\alpha-\beta\), since \(\beta+h=\alpha\). Checking the four ordered pairs gives a contractible groupoid. Requiring a comparison with \(h=0\) between distinct labels would fail. This checks the lateral face in the second single-cell goal.
- Use the quantale of subsets of the four endomaps of a two-element set, with setwise composition. Enumerating all \(16^3=4096\) triples \((A,X,C)\) gives \(X\subseteq A\backslash C\) exactly when \(AX\subseteq C\), and \(X\subseteq C/A\) exactly when \(XA\subseteq C\). Exchanging the left and right rows changes the answer on 308 triples.

This gives the generative form of Q1 over the univalent lax-grid model. The admissible data goals are contextual formula sequents at the distinguished corollas and at exposed vertices of the two-vertex pastings, with the relative sphere and collar boundaries above. Exactness uses the initial data model (3). It does not require the substituted formula to have finitely many cells or an algorithm for finding a derivation of an inhabited goal.
