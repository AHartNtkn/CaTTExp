# Free higher categories with directed interchange: are all cells built from primitive coherences?

*A self-contained problem. No background beyond standard higher category theory is assumed. Everything in §2 is proved and is not re-asked. The constructions summarized in (K43)–(K53) are defined in full in the round-5 answers, reproduced in the repository file `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r5-2026-11-20.md` (Responses 1–34), with Responses 8–9 of `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r4-2026-11-19.md`. (K52)–(K53) are from `-r6-2026-11-20.md`, (K54)–(K57) from `-r7-2026-11-20.md`, (K58)–(K64) from `-r8-2026-11-20.md`, (K65)–(K73) from `-r9-2026-11-20.md`, and (K74) from `-r10-2026-11-20.md` (Response 3), all in the same folder.*

**What we are asking for.** On free objects of finite pasting diagrams there is a least admissible theory V ((K58)), with four offered settings V + S, S ⊆ {R, inner law}, whose equality and reachability are decidable ((K74)). We now ask whether every cell of these free objects is *built from primitive coherences*: cells that are canonical over their own pasting diagram (Definition in §4). This is the exact condition under which a CaTT-style type theory, whose only term former names one primitive cell per pasting diagram and then substitutes, can name every cell of the free object. A uniform argument over all diagrams, degrees and settings is wanted, not further cases.

## 1. Setting

**Globular sets and composition.** Work with reflexive globular sets with source and target maps s, t, identities, and for k < n a partial composition ∗ₖ of n-cells along k-cells. Composition is written in **diagrammatic order** (u ∗ₖ v means "first u, then v"), and it is strictly associative and unital. The boundary formulas hold: for k < m and (m+1)-cells u, v,

```
s(u ∗ₖ v) = s(u) ∗ₖ s(v),     t(u ∗ₖ v) = t(u) ∗ₖ t(v).        (B)
```

For k < l < n and n-cells in interchange position put `L(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₗ b) ∗ₖ (c ∗ₗ d)` and `R(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₖ c) ∗ₗ (b ∗ₖ d)`. A strict ω-category requires L = R.

**The Gray case (directed interchange along 0-cells only).** In a category enriched in strict ω-categories with the lax Gray tensor product (Ara–Maltsiniotis, arXiv 1607.00668), for 2-cells α : f ⇒ f′ : x → y and β : g ⇒ g′ : y → z there is a 3-cell

```
χ_{α,β} : (f ◁ β) ∗₁ (α ▷ g′)  ⇛  (α ▷ g) ∗₁ (f′ ◁ β)
```

and nothing in the reverse direction. Composition along k-cells with k ≥ 1 is strict. For a finite globular pasting diagram Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ, the free such structure has homs ν(λΘᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘⱼ). Here λ is Steiner's directed complex, ⊗ is Steiner's tensor with `∂(x ⊗ y) = ∂x ⊗ y + (−1)^{|x|} x ⊗ ∂y`, and ν is Steiner's functor to strict ω-categories (Steiner, HHA 2004). Each hom is generated by tuples of cells, and a cell is determined by the set of generators it uses.

**The aim** is the same for directed interchange along k-cells for **every** k, including inside homs.

## 2. What is already known (all proved)

**(K1) Raw higher interchangers are impossible.** If every pair of n-cells in interchange position had an (n+1)-cell L → R, then applying this to identities and using (B) forces L = R one dimension down. That contradicts directedness.

**(K2) A single comparison for each simultaneous variation is impossible.** The obvious repair of (K1) adds, for 3-cells A : a ⇛ a′, …, D : d ⇛ d′ in interchange position, a 4-cell between the parallel 3-cells

```
L(A,B,C,D) ∗₂ χ_{a′}     and     χ_a ∗₂ R(A,B,C,D).        (N)
```

This cannot be required for every instance. Take Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², with two 3-cells A : α ⇛ α′ and B : β ⇛ β′ side by side along a 0-cell, and the instance (A, 1, 1, B). Its two sides map to the hom 2-cells

```
U = (v₀ ∘₀ u₁) ∘₁ c₁₁,     V = c₀₀ ∘₁ (u₀ ∘₀ v₁)
```

of ν(λD² ⊗ λD²), where `u_j = A ⊗ g_j`, `v_i = f_i ⊗ B` and `c_ij = α_i ⊗ β_j`. There is no 4-cell between U and V in either direction: the degree-3 chain would need a positive coefficient at u₀ (or at v₀), but every basis element of degree 3 has a non-positive coefficient there.

**(K3) The two variables have opposite naturality directions.** The degree-3 basis elements are `r_j = A ⊗ β_j` and `t_i = α_i ⊗ B`. Their differentials are

```
∂r_j = c_1j + u₁ − c_0j − u₀,     ∂t_i = c_i0 + v₁ − c_i1 − v₀.
```

So when only A varies, the Gray structure has a naturality 4-cell from the "χ first" side to the "variation first" side (V ⇛ U). When only B varies, it goes the other way (U ⇛ V). The simultaneous variation is the 5-cell A ⊗ B. It goes between two routes of 4-cells, `S → V → T` ⇛ `S → U → T`, where S and T are the composites through c₀₁ and c₁₀. In short, the higher structure is **tensor-shaped**: one Leibniz-signed naturality cell per variable, and higher cells between routes.

**(K4) Supports must use an expanded basis.** If every composite's support were the union of the supports of its parts, L and R would always have equal support. Then "support determines the cell" would force strict interchange.

**(K5) One finite basis forces finitely many cells.** If one finite set B admits injective support maps in every dimension, the structure has finitely many non-identity cells in total.

**(K6) No single directed complex presents the answer, and neither does a hom-wise family of them.** In ν(K), the two composites defining χ have the same chain table, so they are equal. A description must therefore be an iterated tower: objects, then homs presented again by objects and homs, and so on.

**(K7) The first uniform case through hom-dimension 3.** For Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², directing interchange inside the hom 𝒦(x, z) adds exactly two generators of hom-dimension 3 beyond the 25 tensor generators. They are the interchangers χ₁ of (A⊗g, f′⊗B) and χ₂ of (f⊗B, A⊗g′), the only pairs of hom-2-cells composable along a hom-0-cell. Their two sides have different supports. Neither occurs in the boundary of A ⊗ B.

**(K8) The (K7) 3-skeleton cannot be extended by 4-generators alone.** Write h_i^j = α_i ⊗ g_j, k_j^i = f_i ⊗ β_j, P_ij = k_j^0 h_i^1, Q_ij = h_i^0 k_j^1, so c_ij : P_ij ⇒ Q_ij. The tensor 3-generators are r_j : c_0j ; a_j^+ ⇛ a_j^- ; c_1j and t_i : b_i^- ; c_i1 ⇛ c_i0 ; b_i^+ (a_j^±, b_i^± the whiskered edge cells u_j, v_i), and the interchangers are χ₁ : b_0^+ ; a_1^+ ⇛ a_0^+ ; b_1^+ and χ₂ : a_0^- ; b_1^- ⇛ b_0^- ; a_1^-. The six composite hom-2-cells P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ are S = b_0^- ; c_01 ; a_1^+, V₀ = c_00 ; b_0^+ ; a_1^+, V₁ = c_00 ; a_0^+ ; b_1^+, T = a_0^- ; c_10 ; b_1^+, U₀ = a_0^- ; b_1^- ; c_11 and U₁ = b_0^- ; a_1^- ; c_11. Whiskering gives t̄₀ : S ⇛ V₀, χ̄₁ : V₀ ⇛ V₁, r̄₀ : V₁ ⇛ T, r̄₁ : S ⇛ U₁, χ̄₂ = χ₂ ∗₁ 1_{c₁₁} : U₀ ⇛ U₁ and t̄₁ : U₀ ⇛ T. Since ∂(A⊗B) = t₁ − t₀ + r₁ − r₀, requirement 4 forces a 4-cell between the route t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀ from S to T and a route from S to T using r̄₁ and t̄₁. In the free 3-skeleton with exactly the 25 tensor generators plus χ₁, χ₂ and no 2-equations, the first route exists, but the second would have to go S ⇛ U₁ and then U₁ ⇛ U₀ against χ̄₂: in the directed graph of these composites the only S → T path is t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀. So no list of 4-generators recovers A ⊗ B. The naturality cells of χ₁, χ₂ are trivial here (the edge homs have no 3-generators).

The repair must change dimension-3 data: an extra 3-generator such as h : S ⇛ U₀ whose image under the Gray quotient is r̄₁, or a 2-equation such as U₀ = U₁. Inverting χ₂ would also work but breaks requirement 3.

**(K9) The orientation of χ₁, χ₂ in (K7) is forced by substitution (proved).** One might try to avoid (K8) by orienting every inner interchanger by the factor order of the enclosing tensor (always "Θ₂-cell first ⇛ Θ₁-cell first"), which keeps χ₁ and reverses χ₂. This fails requirement 5. The free object on Σ(D² ∨ D²) has, inside its hom, the interchanger of its two 2-cells A′ (left) and B′ (right), oriented as in the Gray case: "B′ first ⇛ A′ first". The assignment A′ ↦ f₀ ⊗ B and B′ ↦ A ⊗ g₁ (with the hom-0-cells and hom-1-cells sent to f₀⊗g₀, f₀⊗g₁, f₁⊗g₁ and to the matching edges) respects all boundaries, so substitution sends that interchanger to a 3-cell from "A ⊗ g₁ first" to "f₀ ⊗ B first", which is exactly χ₂ in its (K7) orientation. The pair χ₁ is forced the same way via A′ ↦ A ⊗ g₀, B′ ↦ f₁ ⊗ B. So χ₂ cannot be reversed: adding the reversed cell as well breaks requirement 3, which is the "inverting χ₂" option above. Inner interchangers are oriented by position (right cell first ⇛ left cell first), as at the outer level, and some repair of dimension-3 data is needed (see (K10)).

**(K10) The smallest case is repaired by a factorization.** The six hom-2 composites P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ of (K8) are the maximal paths of an oriented 3-cube with coordinates A (edges a_j^±), B (edges b_i^±) and C (edges c_ij): S = BCA, V₀ = CBA, V₁ = CAB, T = ACB, U₀ = ABC, U₁ = BAC. The six whiskered hom-3 cells t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀, r̄₁, χ̄₂, t̄₁ are its six square faces (adjacent transpositions); every proper interval carries at most one of them. Add one hom-3 generator and one hom-3 equation:

```
h : S ⇛ U₀,        r̄₁ = h ∗₂ χ̄₂.        (F)
```

Then the maximal hom-3 interval is the free category on S → V₀ → V₁ → T, S → U₀ → U₁, U₀ → T, with 11 non-identity cells with pairwise distinct label sets, two routes S → T (t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ and h ; t̄₁), and no reverse of χ̄₁ or χ̄₂. Killing the inner interchangers gives exactly the free Gray diamond with q(h) = q(r̄₁), and A ⊗ B lifts to one 4-generator W : t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ ⇛ h ; t̄₁ with no non-identity contexts. Supports use one label per whiskered context, so r̄₁ is no longer a basis element (its support is that of h χ̄₂). The equation (F) cannot be dropped or replaced by a 4-cell h χ̄₂ ⇛ r̄₁ without a new class of cells killed by the quotient. h is not an interchanger: S and U₀ each contain a c_ij, and every hom-2 path containing a c_ij spans the whole external hom-0 interval.

h is **not forced**. The mirror repair k : U₁ ⇛ T with t̄₁ = χ̄₂ ∗₂ k works equally well, and so does the symmetric repair with both h and k and both equations (12 cells, second route h ; χ̄₂ ; k, same exact Gray quotient). The alternative 2-equation U₀ = U₁ makes χ̄₂ an endomorphism and needs further equations (at least χ̄₂² = χ̄₂ under set supports), so it is not preferred.

**(K11) The first larger case, ΣD³ ∨ ΣD², is completed through its top cell.** Write the first factor as α₀, α₁ : f₀ → f₁, A⁰, A¹ : α₀ ⇒ α₁, Ω : A⁰ ⇛ A¹, and the second as β₀, β₁ : g₀ → g₁, B : β₀ ⇒ β₁. The maximal hom-3 interval is two copies (ε = 0, 1) of the repaired hexagon of (K10), with arrows a^ε, b^ε, c^ε, h^ε, e^ε, f^ε, joined by six variations z_X : X⁰ → X¹ (Ω whiskered in place of the A-edge of X).
- **Hom-4 faces.** W^ε = A^ε ⊗ B : a^ε b^ε c^ε ⇛ h^ε f^ε. R_j = Ω ⊗ β_j. The naturalities N₁, N₂ of χ₁, χ₂ have opposite directions: Ω varies the left input of χ₁ and the right input of χ₂. Two inner interchangers along a hom-1 cell, 𝖠 : z_S a¹ ⇛ a⁰ z_{V₀} (of t₀ with Ω) and 𝖥 : f⁰ z_T ⇛ z_{U₀} f¹. Writing 𝖡, 𝖢, 𝖣, 𝖤 for the whiskered N₁, R₀, R₁, N₂, the maximal hom-4 interval has the seven hom-3 paths x₁ … x₇ from S⁰ to T¹.
- **Added data.** Two hom-4 generators and two equations:
  ```
  H : h⁰ z_{U₀} ⇛ z_S h¹,                H e¹ = (h⁰ 𝖤) ; 𝖣                 (variation of the factorization)
  K : a⁰ z_{V₀} b¹ c¹ ⇛ z_S h¹ f¹,       z_S W¹ = (𝖠 b¹ c¹) ; K            (k-type factorization in hom-dimension 4)
  ```
  Then there are exactly two routes x₁ ⇛ x₇: L = (W⁰ z_T) ; (h⁰ 𝖥) ; (H f¹) and R = (a⁰ b⁰ 𝖢) ; (a⁰ 𝖡 c¹) ; K. The top generator Z = Ω ⊗ B : L ⇛ R has the orientation of ∂(Ω ⊗ B) = W¹ − W⁰ − R₁ + R₀.
- **Checks.** On the maximal interval there are 68 hom-3 cells and 98 hom-4 cells, all with distinct supports; the graphs are acyclic, and the reductions have no overlaps. The Gray quotient is exact: q(H) = q(R₁ whiskered), q(K) = q(z_S W¹), and the surviving tensor generators are R₀, R₁, W⁰, W¹.
- **Forcing substitution.** A⁰, A¹ ↦ A, Ω ↦ 1_A sends H ↦ 1_h, K ↦ W and Z ↦ 1_W, so K is not a cell that every degeneration kills.
- **The source/sink pattern.** In both the h-repair and the k-repair of (K10), the face 𝖠 (whose pair flips relative to the tensor faces) creates a second source and a second sink one dimension up. With source-joining (h) in hom-dimension 3, the one-face joining that works in hom-dimension 4 is sink-joining (K); a source-joining alternative would have to factor a composite. Each existing factorization also needs a variation cell and equation under each higher variable (H).

**(K12) The source/sink count fails in the ambient graph.** Reversing one arrow u → v of a finite directed graph changes the number of sources by 1[d⁻(v)=1] − 1[d⁻(u)=0], and the number of sinks by 1[d⁺(u)=1] − 1[d⁺(v)=0].
- Take the interval of λD² ⊗ λD² ⊗ λD² with hom-1 boundary (β₀α₀)γ₀ → (α₁β₁)γ₁. Its hom-2 paths are the 24 orderings of A, B (updates of the first two factors), C (the crossing βα → αβ) and D (the third-factor update).
- Its arrows are BC → CB, CA → AC and DX → XD. The arrow between A and B is positional: AB → BA when C follows the pair, and BA → AB when C precedes it.
- Compare with the graph that uses BA → AB everywhere, which has one source, DBCA, and one sink, ACBD. The positional graph has sources {DBCA, DABC} and sinks {ACBD, BACD}.
- Of the three reversed arrows, BADC → ABDC creates neither a source nor a sink.

So a flipped face does not create one extra source/sink pair in the full comparison graph. It does so only for an isolated polygon.

**(K13) Local factorization does not force alternation.** For a simple oriented polygon with arms pᵢ : sᵢ → tᵢ and qᵢ : sᵢ₊₁ → tᵢ (indices mod r), add hᵢ : s₀ → sᵢ for 1 ≤ i < r, with p₀ = h₁;q₀ and hᵢ;pᵢ = hᵢ₊₁;qᵢ for 1 ≤ i < r − 1. The boundary telescopes to h_{r−1}+p_{r−1}−q_{r−1}, a pair of parallel paths s₀ → t_{r−1}. So source-joining alone always works locally. For r = 2 this is (K10). For r ≥ 3 the equations identify two composites; this is harmless locally (K16).

**(K14) The triple tensor through hom-dimension 3.**
- *Words.* Write a, b, c for the hom-2 updates of the three factors. Start from hom-1 order zyx, with crossings p : yx → xy, q : zx → xz and r : zy → yz. The tensor hom-3 generator is τ : rqp → pqr.
- *The maximal interval.* Its hom-2 words are the 240 orderings of a, b, c, p, q, r whose crossings occur as rqp or pqr. Its hom-3 arrows are:
  - the tensor naturalities, where the lower-index update moves earlier past a crossing involving its factor and the higher-index update moves later;
  - the inner interchangers, oriented right-first ⇛ left-first by the current hom-1 order;
  - the contextual instances of τ.

  That makes 240 + 240 + 24 = 504 arrows.
- *Added data.* Six families of new hom-3 arrows, each with an equation old = new ; inner, applied in every typed context:

  | New arrow | Factors | Contexts |
  |---|---|---|
  | h_p : bpa → abp | bpa → bap | 8 |
  | h_q : cqa → acq | cqa → caq | 8 |
  | h_r : crb → bcr | crb → cbr | 8 |
  | λ_x : rqa → arq | rqa → raq | 12 |
  | h_z : crqp → pcqr | the τ-instance crqp → cpqr | 6 |
  | μ : crqa → acrq | cλ_x, that is crqa → carq | 3 |

  μ factors a previously added joining arrow. Without it the interval has two sources, crqbpa and acrqbp, and a composite-valued substitution A ↦ a, B ↦ c;r of the binary h-repair has no image.
- *Result.* The retained basis has 504 arrows, the graph is acyclic, and there are 40,172 non-identity hom-3 paths with pairwise distinct edge sets. The unique source is crqbpa. There are **ten sinks**: apqbrc, apqcbr, bapqrc, braqpc, brcaqp, capqbr, cbapqr, cbraqp, raqpbc and rcaqpb. Killing the inner interchangers gives the same quotient as the unrepaired presentation.
- *Hom-4 lifts.* The eighteen tensor hom-4 generators have typed lifts. Six of them, of pattern (2,2,0), are as in (K10). The other twelve take the three shapes W_x, W_y, W_z of patterns (2,1,1), (1,2,1) and (1,1,2), with four choices each:
  - W_x : rqpa → pqra → pqar → paqr → apqr ⇛ rqpa → rqap → arqp → apqr, through λ_x p;
  - W_y : rqbp → rbqp → brqp → bpqr → pbqr → pqbr ⇛ rqbp → rqpb → pqrb → pqbr;
  - W_z : crqp → rcqp → rqcp → rqpc → pqrc ⇛ crqp → pcqr → pqcr → pqrc, through h_z.

**(K15) The interchanger-of-a-composite convention.** The substitution in (K14) assumes that crossing against a composite hom-1 path is the composite of the pairwise crossings. That holds in Gray categories, but it does not follow from (B), associativity and units alone.

**(K16) Source-only polygons keep injective supports (proved).** In (K13), every relation runs from the unique source s₀ to a sink, so no relation has a context. The presented category is thin except at (s₀, t_{r−1}), which has two arrows. Orienting the relations gives a one-step normal form, and the edge set of the normal form is an injective support for every r and every arm length. A union-preserving injective invariant with 2|V| + 1 labels also exists. Caveat: overlapping systems such as a;c = b;d, a;d = b;c have no injective union-support.

**(K17) Only one sink matters, and the top cell lifts (proved).**
- *The sink.* Undirected inner interchanges split the 240 words of (K14) into 54 classes. These are the fibres of the tensor hom-2 chain. Only one of the ten sinks, T = apqbrc, lies in the class of the top atom's hom-2 target. The source is S = crqbpa.
- *Paths.* The words reaching T carry 244 arrows and 1,226 paths S → T, with 25 distinct tensor hom-3 chains.
- *The lift.* Keeping all hom-3 data, the top cell lifts as follows:
  - 24 hom-4 operations A_{ij} : P_i ⇒ P_j between chosen paths, 15 of them new and 9 literal whiskerings of the W-cells;
  - six parallel hom-4 routes R₀ … R₅ : P₂₄ ⇒ P₀;
  - six hom-5 generators F_x^±, F_y^±, F_z^± between those routes;
  - one hom-6 generator Ω : F_x^- F_y^- F_z^- ⇒ F_z^+ F_y^+ F_x^+, whose image is the atom x₂ ⊗ y₂ ⊗ z₂ in every dimension.
- *Scope.* This is an extension with a map to ν(K), not yet one whose inner-interchange quotient is ν(K).

**(K18) Source-only completion of a comparison graph (proved).** Let Q be a finite quiver whose generating arrows are split into inner arrows E and positive arrows P, with E acyclic and a rank φ : V(Q) → ℤ constant along E and strictly increasing along P. For every α : s → t in P and every literal directed E-path R : u → t, adjoin j_{α,R} : s → u (with j_{α,1_t} = α), and impose j_{α,e;R} ; e = j_{α,R} for e : u → v in E and R : v → t. Then:
- the set of generators is finite and does not depend on the order of saturation;
- read as contractions (positive arrow followed by an inner arrow), the equations are confluent and terminating, so every path has a unique normal form;
- the completion is acyclic, normal forms have injective edge-set supports, the original path category embeds, and killing E gives exactly the original inner quotient.

On the 240-word interval of (K14) a rank with values 0, …, 7 exists; there are 264 positive seed arrows and 877 positive generators after completion (613 new, 1,117 arrows in all), and 54 inner classes. The completion is natural under maps of comparison categories that send positive seeds to positive seeds and inner arrows to inner paths.

**(K19) The completion is not natural under generator-to-composite maps (proved).** In (K14), take x = bacrqp, u = abpcqr, w₁ = abcpqr, w₂ = bapcqr, v = bacpqr, with inner arrows p : u → w₁, e : w₁ → v, q : u → w₂, f : w₂ → v, and the tensor arrow τ : x → v with its factorization τ = h_z ; f. The completion has j_{τ,e} : x → w₁ with j_{τ,e} ; e = τ. The comparison-category map fixing inner arrows and sending τ ↦ h_z ; f does not extend: an image H : x → w₁ with H ; e = h_z ; f would be j_{h_z,R} ; P with inner paths R, P and P ; e = R ; f as literal inner words, impossible since e ≠ f (contractions preserve the ordered list of positive seeds). This map has not been realised as a substitution of globular generators, so it does not refute requirement 5.

**(K20) Two-sided division is not confluent (proved).** In (K14), with tensor arrows α : bapcqr → bapqcr and β : abpqcr → abpqrc and the inner arrow e : abpqcr → bapqcr, adjoining both a source division h : bapcqr → abpqcr (h ; e = α) and a sink division k : bapqcr → abpqrc (e ; k = β) gives the critical pair h ; e ; k → α ; k and → h ; β, both irreducible and distinct. The presented category satisfies the composite–composite equation α ; k = h ; β. It is not of the crossed shape a;c = b;d, a;d = b;c.

**(K21) Hom-4 comparisons and exactness through hom-dimension 3 (proved).**
- *Disjoint operations.* For two hom-3 operations α (left) and β (right) on disjoint intervals of a hom-2 word, D_{α,β} : (β ; α^β) ⇛ (α ; β^α), right region first ⇛ left region first. On the 240-word interval there are 258 distinct such boundary pairs (50 need eliminated operations expanded through the factorization equations of (K14)).
- *Inner naturality.* For a hom-3 operation α : U ⇛ V and an independent hom-2 update z, comparing "slide z across U, then α" with "α, then slide z across V": for factor degrees (2,1) inner crossing first ⇛ operation first, for (1,2) the reverse. This agrees with the Leibniz signs of (K3). There are 62 distinct local comparisons N. They use (K15) as a convention.
- *Exactness.* On the top atom's hom-1 boundary, imposing I = D = N = 1 (I the inner interchangers) gives 54 hom-2 classes and 874 hom-3 path classes, which are exactly the numbers of 2-cells and 3-cells of ν(λD² ⊗ λD² ⊗ λD²) with that 1-boundary (identities included). Without N it is not exact: the single-arrow equations leave 152 generator classes over 116 tableaux, and two-arrow equations cannot merge them.
- The D and N comparisons introduce no reverse path in the comparison graph they generate on the raw hom-3 paths.

**(K22) The fifteen fresh hom-4 lifts of (K17) (proved after the lower quotient).** After I = D = N = 1, every one of the 15 new operations A_{ij} has an expression through an existing W-cell (W_x, W_y, W_z or their (2,2,0)-type analogues W_{xy}, W_{xz}) with prefix and suffix whiskerings and the same quotient boundary. Three of them can be defined before quotienting as a whiskered W-cell followed by forward D-comparisons. A fourth, A_{2,1}, is identified through one new hom-4 generator H and one hom-4 equation whose two sides reduce, after the lower quotient, to H̄ = 1 ∗₂ W̄. The remaining eleven are identified only in the quotient. Support injectivity in hom-dimensions 4 and 5 and exactness through hom-dimension 6 are not established.

- In particular V + {R} and V + {inner composite-crossing law} are each admissible.

**(K74) Decidability in the four settings (proved).** For every S ⊆ {R, inner law}, every finite pasting diagram Γ and every degree, equality and reachability in F_{V+S}(Γ) are decidable, with normal forms (rank selection over the comparison to T₁₂, A-labelled raw lifts, connectivity of one-step replacements) and computable, functorial comparison maps F_{V+S}(Γ) → F_{V+S′}(Γ) for S ⊆ S′. Reachability is breadth-first search over the finite vertex list at fixed lower faces and conserved weight; some free objects have loops (a directed loop H of degree 4 on Σ[2] ∨ Σ[2] with pairwise distinct powers), so no length bound is used.

## 3. Requirements

A **tensor-shaped lax-interchange ω-category** has, for every k < l, elementary interchangers χ : L ⇛ R for (l+1)-cells with no reverse cell in free objects; one naturality cell per varying argument, oriented by the Leibniz sign; higher cells between routes of lower naturality cells; and coherence equations to be determined. The free objects F(Γ) must satisfy:
1. an explicit combinatorial description by generators with explicit boundaries;
2. two cells of the same dimension using the same generators, in the expanded basis, are equal;
3. every elementary interchanger exists and its reverse does not;
4. making all interchangers with k ≥ 1 identities gives the free Gray-enriched category, and making all interchangers identities gives the free strict ω-category;
5. closure under substitution of cells for generators.


## 4. Questions

*Conventions.* For a finite pasting diagram P (a finite globular pasting diagram, equivalently a Batanin tree), write F(P) = F_{V+S}(P) and π : F(P) → 𝔉(P) for the projection of requirement 4 to the free Gray-enriched category, whose homs are ν(λΘᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘⱼ) with Steiner bases of tuples of generators. A cell x of 𝔉(P) has **full Gray support** if its downward-closed support is the whole basis. A **substitution** σ : P → Γ assigns to each generator of P a cell of F(Γ), compatibly with boundaries; by requirement 5 it induces F(σ) : F(P) → F(Γ).

**Definition (primitive cells).** A cell c : a → b of F(P) is **primitive over P** if either
- (operation) π(c) is the top cell τ_P of 𝔉(P) (the Gray tensor of the composites of the factors), and c is the **only** cell a → b of F(P) whose projection has full Gray support; or
- (coherence) c is an identity 1_a, and every generator of P occurs in the projected support of π(a).

Let 𝒯 be the least family of cells (over all finite pasting diagrams Γ) that contains every generator and is closed under: if c is primitive over P and σ : P → Γ sends every generator of P to a cell of 𝒯, then F(σ)(c) ∈ 𝒯.

**Q1 (main).** For every S ⊆ {R, inner law} (or, better, for every admissible theory), is every cell of F_{V+S}(Γ), for every finite pasting diagram Γ and every degree, in 𝒯? Equivalently: is F_{V+S}(Γ) generated, under substitution, by primitive cells? Prove it uniformly, or give a counterexample (a diagram, a degree and a cell outside 𝒯) and characterise 𝒯.

**Q2 (only if Q1 fails because of non-uniqueness).** Replace "the only cell a → b with full Gray projection" in the operation clause by "a chosen such cell", fixed once per (P, a, b) and stable under the automorphisms of P (for instance the first witnessing path of minimal length in a fixed enumeration of the generating steps of (K74)). Is every cell then in the resulting family for *some* choice, or for every choice? In particular, on Γ = Σ[2] ∨ Σ[2], is the loop H in 𝒯, and for which choices?

**Q3 (the same for loops).** Is every cell x : a → a of F_{V+S}(Γ) whose projection is an identity in 𝒯 (for example the loop H and its powers)?

## 5. What counts as a solution

A proof that 𝒯 is everything for the four settings (preferably for every admissible theory), or an explicit cell outside 𝒯 with a proof, together with a description of the closure; or a published result.

## Researcher Response 1

**Q1 and Q3 have negative answers in all four settings.** The binary join
on two consecutive 3-globes is missing in degree four. There are also proper
5-loops, with identity Gray projection, all of whose positive powers are
missing. Both obstructions persist if one admits *every* lift in the
operation clause. On the particular two-by-two grid of Q2 the answer depends
on the rule. Only the identity power of $H$ belongs to the original
family. Every choice in Q2 generates $H$ and all its powers.

We use the equality $\pi c=\tau_P$ in §4 in its stated degree. For
$P=\bigvee_i\Sigma\Theta_i$ with at least one span, put
$d(P)=1+\sum_i\dim\Theta_i$. To see that this is the degree of $\tau_P$,
choose a maximal-dimensional generator in each factor and take their
tensor. Its degree in the long hom is the sum of their dimensions.
An identity elevation of $\tau_P$ has the same closed support in a
higher degree. The equality of degrees in the operation clause matters
for the bare inner exchange.

### A missing 4-cell, independent of choices

Write $D(X)$ for the degree-four values expressible using 4-generators,
identities, the two outer prisms $r,t$, the inner exchange $\iota$,
chronological composition and fixed whiskers. Allow all matching lower
cells as arguments. By (K30), (K32) and (K45), the only further proper
letter to consider in this degree is the outer join $h$. The triangle is
already $r(\chi,-)=t(-,\chi)$.

In an outer interval of total hom height at most one, every 3-cell is an
identity. Indeed the 3-letters are generating changes and outer crossings.
The first needs a height-two factor and the second needs two positive
factors. A proper $h$ needs proper 3-inputs on disjoint outer intervals.
Thus a generic operation in degree four, whose total hom height is three,
has a spelling in $D(P)$. The unit cases of $h$ are a prism or an
identity. Substituting $D$-values for its 4-generators preserves this
spelling, and substituting into a coherence gives an identity. Induction on
generation therefore puts every generated degree-four value in $D(X)$,
even with all lifts allowed. This uses no hypothesis on the expressibility
of the generic faces.

Take $L=\Sigma D^2\vee\Sigma D^2$, with generating changes $A,B$ and
crossing $C$. In the interval of (K32) each 3-history schedules $A,B,C$
once. The $D(L)$-steps on these schedules are exactly
$$
 BCA\longrightarrow CBA\longrightarrow CAB\longrightarrow ACB,\qquad
 BCA\longrightarrow BAC,\qquad ABC\longrightarrow BAC,\qquad
 ABC\longrightarrow ACB .
$$
The prisms move $BC$ to $CB$ or $CA$ to $AC$. The inner square
puts the spatially left change first, and the spatial order changes at
$C$. There is no further slot to vary or subdivide. The common
degree-three presentation distinguishes the six schedules. From $BCA$
one cannot reach $ABC$ in this graph, whereas (K45) supplies
$h(A,B):BCA\to ABC$. This proves Q1, including the failure for every
proposed choice in Q2. The same interval was computed for the syntactic
head question in [Response 13 of the surface analysis](../answered/semistrict-surface-conservativity-r2-2026-11-25.md#researcher-response-13).

### An exact description of the closure in every degree

Here is an effective increasing union of finite sets describing
$\mathcal T(\Gamma)_j$. It uses the unrestricted free tables and involves
no oracle for primitivity or generation. First compute a test on an arity
and a pair $a,b$ of proposed faces. Take the finite contextual-step graph
of (K74) for reaching $b$ from $a$. Use proper steps. At each vertex
also record the accumulated Gray cell, initialized at $1_{\pi a}$ and
updated by composition with the projected step. This is a finite product
graph since the Gray tables in fixed degree are finite by their Steiner
supports. Retain the states on walks from the initial state to a state over
$b$ with full support. Use *all* those final states, including the
initial state when it qualifies.

A directed cycle in this retained graph gives two full-support values of
different positive-step weights by traversing the cycle one extra time.
Here the additive top weight is pulled back from $T_{12}$, positive on
each proper step by (K50), (K58) and (K74). In the acyclic case, list the
finitely many accepting paths, including the empty path when applicable,
and identify their values by (K74). Record an operation exactly when there
is one class and its projection is $\tau_P$. This also proves necessity
of the test since every free cell has a contextual path spelling.

Fix a finite-alphabet coding of diagrams and expressions. At cutoff $N$
list all well-typed expressions of length at most $N$, in degrees at most
$j$, on trees of code length at most $N$, including the query tree in
the list. Identify entries on each tree by free equality. On the listed
arities run the preceding test at all listed pairs, using the *entire*
graph for the test. Also record $1_a$ for each listed base $a$ passing
the projected generator-occurrence test. On $\Gamma$, mark the listed
generators. For every recorded operation or identity, try every compatible
tuple of marked entries indexed by **all** its arity generators. Evaluate
on the tuple and mark any equal listed result. Iterate to stability. Take
the union over $N$ of the marked degree-$j$ entries.

Each mark has a derivation by §4. Conversely every derivation has a finite
tree of applications on $\Gamma$, with finitely many generic heads,
generic faces and argument values. All have finite spellings and fit at
one cutoff. Its entries are marked from the leaves up. Arity heights in
such a derivation never exceed the output degree of the head, as follows
from $d(P)$ for an operation and from occurrence in the projected base
for a coherence. The procedure needs no earlier mark for that generic
base. In particular the description applies even to identities on bases
whose generation has never been established. It gives a uniform positive
membership search.

### Why the unique-lift rule misses $H$

Write $G=\Sigma[2]\vee\Sigma[2]$. In fact every degree-four value of
$\mathcal T(G)$ is an identity. We prove this by induction on the last
application. There are no 4-generators in $G$, and a coherence gives an
identity. For an operation let its degree-four arguments already be
identities. Each outer span maps to an ordered interval of objects of
$G$. For an outer prism to survive, its 3-change would have to act
on one interval while a proper 2-step acts on a disjoint one, and neither
short span of $G$ has a proper 3-cell. By the height bound it remains
to test whether a *unique* full lift can contribute a surviving
$\iota$. The positive hom heights of its arity partition three.

We give the schedule details of this test. In a height-one column the
2-steps form a line of slots. A height-two column has a line of slots
with a string of generating 3-changes at each slot, possibly empty.
Expand every 3-history into generating changes and elementary crossings
using the two outer subdivisions (K30). Each atomic 3-step contributes
a positive basis element to its Gray chain, so reading the chain of a
prescribed face accounts for every such step. Crossing two blocks of slots
preserves the order within each block. At fixed 2-faces the possible
expansions are schedules of adjacent swaps. Fixed outer 1-arrows can be
carried throughout. They have no events, and (K30)'s whiskering and (K58)'s
exterior and intervening transports place the same calls at these fixed
arrows. We use the following fact about two blocks several times.

In a complete rectangle of crossings, index the rows by the left-factor
slots in their order in the 2-word. To put a smaller row earlier, swap
two consecutive out-of-row-order crossings. They use different slots on
both sides, since the order on each shared slot is prescribed. The
smaller row is spatially left, so this is a forward $\iota$. This sorts
any schedule to row order. Sorting the other way by *inverse* moves gives
the other extreme, column order (on a fixed column the row order is
forced). Equivalently the two priority orders of the rectangle poset take
opposite orders on **every** incomparable pair. Both extremes spell the
same block $\chi$ by (K30). Hence any complete schedule can reach that
block and the block can reach any complete schedule. If both side lengths
are at least two, obtain a positive loop by subdividing off a
two-by-two subrectangle and inserting the whiskered (K34) loop there.
Its weight is positive by (K45). Any full lift whose faces can be joined
through such a block in a fixed 3-context fails uniqueness, by inserting
that loop into the path. This observation applies when the path projects
to $\tau_P$, since the inserted inner step projects to an identity.

**One height-three column.** In the single active column the proper
4-steps are literal 4-globe updates and inner exchanges. A 3-history
lists the generating changes at each slot in order, interleaved across
slots. A 4-globe replaces the version of one such change by its next
parallel version. Full support uses a literal 4-globe. If a route also
uses an inner exchange, perform all the version updates first and then
the same exchanges, or perform the exchanges first and all updates last.
Changing the version preserves the two faces of
the change and exchanging adjacent disjoint blocks transports its
position. Keep the order of successive updates of a version. Both routes
project to the same strict-hom paste. They are different in the four
settings. On this column the chronological list of literal 4-globes
separates gaps of inner exchanges. The structural laws preserve the
list and the optional subdivisions replace a proper block exchange by
proper exchanges in the same gap. No proper outer law is enabled. Thus
a nonempty exchange gap before the first globe cannot equal an empty
one. A unique head here uses only globe updates and specializes to an
identity.

**A height-two column and a line.** First put the moving column on the
left. Write $m$ for its number of slots, $n$ for the length of the
stationary line. Write $u,v$ for the orders of moving changes in the
two proposed 3-faces, read by forgetting the crossings. In an expanded
source face each change at row $i$ follows all $n$ crossings of that
row. In a target face it precedes them all. Indeed the Gray faces of the
total are the source and target of the prism on the entire moving paste
and stationary line. Expansion uses every required change and crossing
once. The order of a change and a crossing on its own row is visible in
the version of the row at the crossing in the projected Steiner table.
The following construction works for every such pair admitting a lift.

1. From a source spelling extract its first moving change, say at row
   $i$, to the front. The crossing prefix contains the complete first
   $i$ rows. Sort those rows to the front of the prefix by the forward
   disjoint swaps above and put that rectangle in block form. The
   remaining prefix uses rows to the right of $i$, so the change can
   pass it towards the front by forward inner swaps. Use $r$ to move
   the change through the block of the first $i$ rows, whiskering it
   by the other slots in those rows. Repeat in the remaining suffix.
   This gives $u$ followed by a complete rectangular crossing.
2. At this stage one can change $u$ to $v$. Contracting the stationary
   column in the given lift gives a path of inner exchanges of the moving
   changes. For reachability each such swap of disjoint blocks can be
   executed by successive adjacent right-first to left-first swaps,
   regardless of any subdivision equation.
3. From $v$ followed by a block one can reach the prescribed target.
   Choose the order of crossings in that target using the complete
   rectangle fact. Interleave them into $v$ in the prescribed order.
   Each crossing that must precede a pending change lies in a strictly
   smaller row, since a crossing of the same or a larger row would require
   a crossing of that change's row already to have occurred. Moving
   the crossing earlier is therefore a forward inner swap.

All the constructed paths between the prescribed faces project to the
total. Each moving change crosses the stationary line by its prism once,
with the other rows fixed. In the Gray hom these contribute exactly the
tensor of that change with each stationary edge and the inner steps
contribute zero. This is the top chain of the total, and its lower rows
are fixed by the prescribed faces. In particular, if $m,n\ge2$, step 1
exhibits the block where the positive loop can be inserted.

There is a second way to perform step 2 if $u\ne v$. Execute its
adjacent swaps *before* step 1. For a desired swap of successive moving
changes at rows $j>i$, any intervening crossings have row greater
than $j$, since all crossings of row $j$ preceded its change, and hence
so did all crossings of earlier rows. Move the row-$i$ change earlier
over the intervening crossings, then swap the two changes. This keeps
the source-face condition. Iterate, extract the changes by step 1 and
finish by step 3. These two lifts are unequal. Mark a pair of changes
whose order switches and one stationary edge. In the early route the
two changes exchange after both have crossed the marked edge in the
2-word. In the late route they exchange before either has crossed it.
To check that this distinguishes values, retain just the two marked
changes at their two slots and one stationary edge by contraction. On
this strip a 3-history has a unique atomic spelling: the moving updates
are literal and a crossing against one stationary edge splits in its
prescribed row order. Count exchanges of the marked pair with that edge
on their right. For an exchange of two histories, read the pair at its
individual swap in a subdivision into atomic swaps. Its position is
well-defined even when a block split is optional. At that swap exactly
the two prefixes internal to the respective operands have executed. They
act on disjoint spatial intervals, so either order gives the same 2-word.
Consequently dividing either operand partitions the same count. Balancing
and fixed whiskering preserve the marked positions, and the
extra exchanges in the varying-whisker laws each have a crossing as one
operand. No proper join is possible. These exhaust the positive
degree-four laws here by (K30), (K45) and (K58). The pair changes order
only once under the oriented swaps. Thus the early and late counts are
one and zero.

For a right moving column the identical path argument is obtained by
reflecting the slot word, interchanging the two outer factors and reading
each 4-route backwards. At the level of schedules this exchanges the
source of $r$ with the target of $t$, and right-first before a forward
swap with left-first after the reflected swap. It exchanges the two
prism subdivision and varying-whisker checks. Only prisms, inner squares
and the two outer crossing subdivisions enter this argument.

Now specialize to $G$. If the moving column covers both actual spans,
the stationary column collapses. A surviving inner square has a change
from each moving operand and reverses their relative order. The moving
row positions are fixed, so forward swaps cannot undo that reversal.
This requires $u\ne v$, just excluded for a unique head. Otherwise
all its generating 3-changes specialize to identities. A surviving
inner square must then exchange disjoint crossing histories. Each
operand needs a slot from each of the two columns, hence $m,n\ge2$,
also excluded. Subdivision inside a substituted slot does not change
the disjointness of the two generic slot intervals.

**Three lines.** Denote their ordered blocks of slots by $A,B,C$
from left to right along the outer spine. In the initial 2-word they
occur as $CBA$. For every choice of one slot per block the total's
3-source orders its crossings $BC,AC,AB$ and its target orders them
$AB,AC,BC$, by the triangle of (K30). These orders follow also by
restricting the projected faces to that triple. The expanded histories
have each inter-block pair crossing exactly once.

From any source spelling move all $BC$ crossings to the front. No
$AB$ crossing can precede an unfinished $BC$, since the first $AB$
uses the last $B$-slot, which must already have crossed every
$C$-slot. An intervening $AC$ that precedes a pending $BC$ can
be interchanged with it when adjacent. The two crossings are disjoint,
and the $C$-slot meeting $A$ has already passed all of $B$, placing the $BC$ crossing spatially left. Thus the required swaps are
forward. The initial $BC$ rectangle and the remaining crossing of
$A$ with the ordered block $BC$ are both complete. Put each in
block form. From a target spelling the reverse procedure using
*inverse* inner swaps moves all $AB$ crossings to the front. The
first $BC$ needs the first $B$-slot to have crossed all of $A$,
and a pending $AB$ is spatially right of an intervening $AC$.
The remaining rectangle crosses the ordered block $AB$ with $C$.
The two resulting block spellings are joined by the full triangle
$t(A,\chi(B,C))=r(\chi(A,B),C)$. Reading the target preparation
backwards therefore finishes a full lift to the prescribed target.

If two line lengths are at least two, one of the rectangles on this
route admits the positive loop, since the source rectangles have sizes
$|B|\times|C|$ and $|A|\times(|B|+|C|)$.
Under a substitution to $G$, at most two outer columns can occupy
distinct nonempty object intervals. A surviving inner exchange needs
a crossing in each of two disjoint operands. Both occupied columns
must therefore have at least two generic slots. The loop on the
constructed route excludes uniqueness.

This exhausts the partitions of three and proves the induction on
$G$. By (K45) and comparison to $T_{12}$, $H^k$ is proper for
every $k>0$. Its zeroth power is the coherence on the block crossing, whose
projection uses all four generating advances.

### Why every choice generates $H$

Use the single outer column $D^4 *_1 D^3$. On its left temporal
interval put $U:A_0\to A_1$ in degree four, and on its right interval
put the generating 3-change $B$. Use as faces the right-first schedule
on $A_0,B$ and the left-first schedule on $A_1,B$. Updating the
version and then exchanging the changes gives a lift of the proper
4-dimensional total. Its value in the strict Gray hom is $U$ pasted with
the identity on $B$. Collapse $U$ to $1_A$ and both versions to
$A$ on $D^3 *_1 D^3$. Between the resulting two schedules there is
exactly one 4-cell. There are just two generating changes, one forward
inner exchange and no further proper letter. Thus **every** choice at
the padded faces specializes to $\iota(A,B)$. The collapse uses only
generators and the identity of a generator, available by coherence on
its globe.

The outer elementary square on two 2-globes is unique on its generic
faces and gives each crossing used in (K34). The structural operations
needed there are available at their exact values as well. Top composition
uses a temporal stack of two globes and a fixed whisker uses a globe
with a single adjacent slot of dimension one above the cut. On these
arities there is just the indicated order of the top changes, with the
lower slot stationary. No two disjoint changes are available for an
exchange at that cut. The resulting full top paste is unique (at cut
zero the exterior 1-arrow is a point factor in the tensor). Through degree
three every cell is a structural paste of variables and elementary outer
crossings. This supplies all faces needed for the substitutions.
One can therefore substitute the two outer crossings into the padded-square
construction and put its value between the fixed corners $p,q$.
This gives precisely $H$. Top composition gives every power. The
argument uses only the forced image after collapse, so it applies to
every choice satisfying Q2's condition.

### A loop missed even with every lift

On three consecutive outer blocks take two copies $L_1,L_2$ of
$L$ above and the arity $R$ of the weight-one 5-loop $J$ of
(K69). Explicitly $R$ has two consecutive outer spans, two
top-composable hom 2-globes on the first and a two-edge line on the
second. The two subdivisions of the outer unary have the same first
and last moves and exchange the order of the middle two. The contextual
exchange of these middle moves gives $J$, with charge one in
$T_{12}$. [Construction and charge](../answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r9-2026-11-20.md#power-splits-on-actual-pasting-diagrams)

On the wedge of these blocks form the 5-cell
$$
                 X=(1_{h_1} *_0 1_{h_2}) *_0 J .
$$
It is a loop by the face formula. Its projection is an identity since
$J$ is an inner exchange in context. Collapsing everything except
$R$ gives $J$, so $X^k$ is proper for every $k>0$.
Collapsing to either $L_i$ sends its 4-source to $h_i$ by strict units.

Suppose such a power were generated, allowing every lift. The wedge has
no 5-generator, and the output is proper, so its final head is a
5-operation on some $P$ of total hom height four. Spell its 4-source
in the degree-four presentation, using the generating skeleton through
degree four. Under either collapse every assigned 4-generator has value
in $D(L_i)$. To see this, compose the generation derivation for its assignment
with the collapse, which sends generators to generators or iterated
identities on vertices. Such post-substitution preserves generation by
induction on the derivation. Since $h_i\notin D(L_i)$, at least one
native join in this source spelling must remain proper after collapse
to $L_i$. Otherwise the argument spellings and the specialized source
give a $D(L_i)$-expression.

The two operands of that surviving join must act on the two distinct
short spans of $L_i$. On either span the only proper 3-change is its
generating globe. In an expanded generic 3-operand some 3-variable
must supply that change, since a crossing restricted to a single short span
has a unit operand. Thus one generic outer column of height at least
two supplies the left change and a strictly later such column supplies
the right change. Under the original substitution their object
intervals contain the respective actual short spans of $L_i$.

There can be at most two height-two-or-higher columns in $P$. Hence
the *same* earlier column must supply both left changes and the same
later column both right changes. But the right span of $L_1$
precedes the left span of $L_2$. Ordered images of two consecutive
groups of outer columns have disjoint interiors, so the earlier image
cannot cover the left span of $L_2$ while the later one covers the
right span of $L_1$. This contradiction proves the claimed exclusion
for every positive power and finishes Q3.
