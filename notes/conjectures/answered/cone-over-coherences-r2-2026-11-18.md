# Cones over coherence terms in CaTT (N^cone), round 2

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Round 1 and its verified answer are in `answered/cone-over-coherences-2026-11-18.md`. Questions are at the end, smallest first.*

## Background

**CaTT.** Contexts list variables `x : A`, where a type is `⋆` or `a →_A b` with a, b terms of type A. Terms are variables or `coh_{Q,B}[γ]`, where Q is a *pasting context* (a context presenting a globular pasting diagram), B is a type over Q satisfying the usual side conditions (either both boundary terms are full in Q, the *coherence case*, or each is full in the matching boundary of Q, the *composition case*), and `γ : Δ ⊢ Q` is a substitution. Reference: Finster–Mimram, *A type-theoretical definition of weak ω-categories* (LICS 2017); Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, *Globular weak ω-categories as models of a type theory* (2021). Composition order below is diagrammatic, and `u ∗_k w` denotes the binary composite along a k-cell, which is itself a composition coherence over two disks glued along a k-disk. For a term t of dimension m ≥ 1 write `σt, τt` for its source and target, and `σʲt` for the iterated source.

**Cone over a globular context.** Call a context *globular* if every variable's type is `⋆` or `a → b` with a, b variables; every pasting context is globular. For globular Γ, the *specified cone* `Cone(Γ; c)` extends Γ by an object `c : ⋆` and one variable `p_t` for each variable t of Γ, in order:

```
p_x : c → x                                                   (dim x = 0)
p_t : p_{τt}  →  p_{σt} ∗_{m−1} (p_{σ²t} ∗_{m−2} ( … (p_{σᵐt} ∗₀ t)))      (dim t = m ≥ 1)
```

So `f : x → y` gets `p_f : p_y ⇒ p_x ∗₀ f`, and `α : f ⇒ g` gets `p_α : p_g ⇛ p_f ∗₁ (p_x ∗₀ α)`. This is a well-formed context for every globular Γ (by induction on the variables: each composite in the target is well formed because the projections of the boundary variables already have the matching types). It is the cone of T. J. Mikhail's Thm 3.3 (*A type-theoretic definition of lax (∞,∞)-limits*, arXiv 2412.13310), lax at the vertices. When Γ is a pasting context, the strict ω-category freely generated by `Cone(Γ; c)` is the Ara–Maltsiniotis join `c ⋆ Γ`, with `p_t ↦ c ⋆ t`.

**Cones over arbitrary contexts.** In a general context a variable may have composite boundaries, e.g. `τ : f ∗₀ g ⇒ h`. The cone variable over τ then needs a cone cell `P(f ∗₀ g)` over the *term* `f ∗₀ g`. Extend p from variables to all terms:

```
P(x)              := p_x
P(coh_{Q,B}[γ])   := Π(Q, B)[Cone(γ)]
```

where `Cone(γ) : Cone(Γ; c) ⊢ Cone(Q; c)` sends `q ↦ γ(q)`, `c ↦ c`, `p_q ↦ P(γ(q))`, and `Π(Q, B)` is a chosen term of `Cone(Q; c)`. For a term t of dimension m ≥ 1 put

```
T(t) := P(σt) ∗_{m−1} (P(σ²t) ∗_{m−2} ( … (P(σᵐt) ∗₀ t))).
```

For `c₀ := coh_{Q,B}[id_Q]`, of dimension n, the term `Π(Q, B)` must have type

```
Π(Q, B) : P(τc₀) → T(c₀)          (an (n+1)-term of Cone(Q; c)).
```

This type is well formed once P is defined on the boundary terms of c₀, which have dimension < n. So the definition is a recursion on dimension. Within a dimension, the boundary terms of an n-coherence may themselves contain n-dimensional subterms only in the composition case, through lower-dimensional factors, so the recursion is well founded with the order "lower dimension first, then structural" .

## Statement

> **Conjecture N^cone.** For every pasting context Q and every type B such that `coh_{Q,B}` is a valid coherence, there is a term `Π(Q, B)` of `Cone(Q; c)` of type `P(τc₀) → T(c₀)`, where P on the boundary of c₀ is defined from Π at lower dimensions as above. Moreover Π can be chosen so that, in the free strict ω-category on `Cone(Q; c)`, `π(Π(Q, B)) = c ⋆ π(c₀)` (the join of c with the strict image of c₀).

The strict-image clause is part of the statement because the inductive step uses it: it guarantees that `P(u)` has strict image `c ⋆ π(u)` for every term u.

Consequence: for every CaTT context Γ, the extension `Cone(Γ; c)` (with P in place of p in the types of the cone variables) is a well-formed context. This is a precise form of Mikhail's Conjecture 3.6 ("cones exist over arbitrary contexts"), restricted to a specified construction.

## What is known

1. **Dimension 1 (proved).** For the composite of a chain `x₀ —f₁→ … —f_m→ x_m`, Π is the vertical composite of the whiskered cone generators `p_{f_m}`, `p_{f_{m−1}} ∗₀ f_m`, … followed by the associator coherence κ over `c → x₀ → … → x_m`. Machine-checked on `τ : f ∗₀ g ⇒ h`.

2. **Every relative-coherence witness in a join context strictifies to an identity (round 1, proved).** The free strict ω-category on `Cone(Q; c)` is the join `c ⋆ Q`. This join is ν(K) for a strong Steiner complex K, because joins of strong Steiner complexes are strong Steiner (Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches*). In a strong Steiner complex every positive cycle is zero: take a minimal basis element b of its support and a ∈ supp ∂⁻b; only a b′ with a ∈ supp ∂⁺b′ could cancel a, and that b′ would satisfy b′ < a < b. So every endo-cell of c ⋆ Q is an identity.

3. **Reduction to relative coherence (round 1, proved).** Write **RC(r) for Δ** for the statement: any two parallel r-terms of Δ with equal strict images are connected by an (r+1)-term. Then:

   > If RC(1), …, RC(n) hold for `Cone(Q; c)` for every pasting context Q, then N^cone holds through dimension n, in both the coherence case and the composition case.

   Proof.
   - RC(1..n) lift every strict (n+1)-cell to a term. Lift generators, identities and binary composites, inserting RC corrections to make the gluings literal; by item 2 those corrections strictify to identities.
   - A transport lemma then corrects the r-faces of the lift one dimension at a time, using (λ_r ∗_r W) ∗_r ρ_r with λ_r, ρ_r from RC(r). The strict image is unchanged, and no correction is inverted.
   - Applied to `d = c ⋆ π(c₀)`, this gives `Π(Q, B) : P(τc₀) → T(c₀)` with `π(Π) = c ⋆ π(c₀)`.

4. **Dimension 2 holds (proved).** RC(1) holds in every CaTT context: parallel 1-terms with the same strict path pull back to the chain context of that path. RC(2) holds in every CaTT context (paper proof: sequentialise both 2-terms into whiskered 2-generators and connect them by exchange moves, each a coherence over "chain, 2-disc, chain, 2-disc, chain"). So item 3 gives N^cone in dimension 2. There is also an explicit term for the horizontal composite `α ∗₀ β` that uses no RC: a chain of eight 3-cells built from p_β, p_α ∗₀ g′, a whiskering-distribution coherence, an exchange coherence, a coherence over the pasting context `c → x ⇒ y ⇒ z`, and reassociations. Its strict image is `p_β ∗₁ (p_α ∗₀ g′) = c ⋆ (α ∗₀ β)`.

5. **A related case that is settled.** Relative coherence in dimension 3 is known for one context that is not a pasting context: the lax square, with objects x⁻, x⁺, y⁻, y⁺, edges x↑, y↑, f⁻, f⁺ and one 2-cell F : f⁻ ∗₀ y↑ ⇒ x↑ ∗₀ f⁺. RC(3) holds there. The proof:
   - For a 2-term with strict image F, exactly one 0-column of its arity carries F and spans x⁻ → y⁺. Every other column sits at the point x⁻ or y⁺ and factors literally through a point context.
   - This factorisation is stable under substitution.
   - It gives normalisers T → (κ · F) · κ′ that are compatible with every 3-term, by induction on syntax.
   
   The step that uses the square is that x⁻ is the unique source and y⁺ the unique sink, so the discarded columns are point-supported. In `c ⋆ Q` the apex c is the unique source object, but there is in general more than one sink.

6. **Strict case.** In strict ω-categories, `c ⋆ −` is functorial on cells (Ara–Maltsiniotis), so the strict analogue of N^cone holds in every dimension.

7. **What is not enough.** Loopy contexts can fail RC(3): in (x, a : 1_x → 1_x) the weak free object groupoidifies to S² and the strict one to K(ℤ, 2). So a proof must use the loop-freeness of `c ⋆ Q`.

## Questions

1. **RC(3) for the smallest join context, Cone(D²; c).** Here D² = (x, y, f, g, α : f ⇒ g). The first instance is the unitor `c₀ : α ⇛ α ∗₁ 1_g`, for which a 4-term `P(α ∗₁ 1_g) → P(α) ∗₂ (p_f ∗₁ (p_x ∗₀ c₀))` is required. Then prove RC(3) for `Cone(D²; c)` in general. Does an active-column factorisation as in item 5 work, with the apex c in the role of the unique source?
2. **RC(3) for every join context Cone(Q; c), Q a pasting context.** By item 3 this gives N^cone in dimension 3. Which property of `c ⋆ Q` does the proof use?
3. **RC(n) for join contexts**, or a direct construction of `Π(Q, B)` in all dimensions, for example by induction on Q using the join's decomposition `c ⋆ (Q ∨ Q′)`, or via the cylinder `D¹ ⊗ Q` contracted at its source end with weak units in place of a strict quotient.
4. **Fixed dimension.** Does RC(n) for all join contexts imply RC(r) for all r < n there? (Item 3 needs RC in every dimension up to n.)

## What would count as a solution

Explicit terms for question 1; a proof of RC(3) or RC(n) for join contexts; a counterexample (a join context with two parallel terms of equal strict image and no term between them); or a reference that constructs cones over coherence terms in CaTT or another algebraic presentation of weak ω-categories.

# Researcher Response

**The displayed unitor instance has an explicit 4-term. Question 4 also has a positive answer:** for the standard recursively defined binary compositions,
\[
\mathrm{RC}\bigl(r+1,\operatorname{Cone}(\Sigma Q;d)\bigr)
\Longrightarrow
\mathrm{RC}\bigl(r,\operatorname{Cone}(Q;c)\bigr).
\]
The second result uses a syntactic dimension-lowering translation with a literal left-inverse property.

I give both constructions below. They do not prove general \(\mathrm{RC}(3)\) for \(\operatorname{Cone}(D^2;c)\).

Throughout, binary compositions are the direct mixed-dimensional operations defined recursively from their pasting contexts, compatible with suspension. These are the conventions of BMOSV §2.2–2.3. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.11620?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 1. The explicit unitor instance

Write
\[
a=p_x,\qquad b=p_y,\qquad u=p_f,\qquad v=p_g,
\]
and
\[
M=p_\alpha:
v\longrightarrow u*_1(a*_0\alpha).
\]
Let
\[
c_0:\alpha\longrightarrow\alpha*_11_g
\]
be the specified unitor coherence over \(D^2\).

We first choose the generic dimension-2 identity and vertical-composition schemes. This is necessary because \(P(\alpha*_11_g)\) must result from substitution into a generic scheme.

### 1.1. Four generic 3-cell operations

Choose the following direct coherence instances:

| Operation | Type | Generic pasting context |
|---|---|---|
| \(R_z\) | \(z\to z*_11_{t_1z}\), for a 2-cell \(z\) | \(D^2\) |
| \(H_{r,s,t}\) | \((r*_1s)*_1t\to r*_1(s*_1t)\) | \(D^2*_1D^2*_1D^2\) |
| \(\eta_{a,f}\) | \(1_{a*_0f}\to a*_01_f\) | \(D^1*_0D^1\) |
| \(\chi_{a,\alpha,\beta}\) | \((a*_0\alpha)*_1(a*_0\beta)\to a*_0(\alpha*_1\beta)\) | \(D^1*_0(D^2*_1D^2)\) |

In every row the endpoints are literally parallel and have full support in the indicated context. Thus these are instances of the full-support coherence rule, whose precise formulation is BMOSV Figure 3. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.11620?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For 3-cells, abbreviate
\[
X;Y:=X*_2Y.
\]
Parentheses remain significant.

Choose the generic identity scheme
\[
\boxed{
P(1_f)=
R_{p_f};\bigl(p_f*_1\eta_{p_x,f}\bigr).
}
\tag{1}
\]

For generic vertically composable 2-cells
\[
\alpha:f\Rightarrow g,\qquad \beta:g\Rightarrow h,
\]
put
\[
A=a*_0\alpha,\qquad B=a*_0\beta,
\]
and write
\[
M=p_\alpha:p_g\to p_f*_1A,\qquad
N=p_\beta:p_h\to p_g*_1B.
\]
Choose
\[
\boxed{
P(\alpha*_1\beta)
=
N;\Bigl((M*_1B);
  \bigl(H_{p_f,A,B};(p_f*_1\chi_{a,\alpha,\beta})\bigr)\Bigr).
}
\tag{2}
\]
Its exact type is
\[
p_h\longrightarrow p_f*_1\bigl(a*_0(\alpha*_1\beta)\bigr).
\]

Both choices have the required strict images: (1) becomes an identity, and (2) becomes the strict vertical cone composite. Formula (2) is the vertical construction already described in the question, with its parentheses fixed.

### 1.4. The term

For two parenthesizations \(U,V\) of the same ordered list of composable 3-cells, let
\[
\operatorname{br}(U,V):U\to V
\]
denote the coherence over the generic chain of 3-globes, followed by substitution of that list. This defines a specific term; it does not assert judgmental associativity.

The successive 3-boundaries of the construction are
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_0&=(r;e);(m;(h;k)),\\
T_1&=r;((e;m);(h;k)),\\
T_2&=r;((p;q);(h;k)),\\
T_3&=(r;p);(q;(h;k)),\\

\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
\]
Every \(T_i\) has the literal type
\[
v\longrightarrow u*_1D.
\]

Define \(E_i:T_i\to T_{i+1}\) by
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_0&=\operatorname{br}(T_0,T_1),\\
E_1&=r*_2\bigl(\mathcal I*_2(h;k)\bigr),\\
E_2&=\operatorname{br}(T_2,T_3),\\
E_3&=\mathcal N*_2\bigl(q;(h;k)\bigr),\\

E_5&=M*_2\bigl(R*_2(\mathcal A*_2k)\bigr),\\
E_6&=M*_2\operatorname{br}\bigl(R;((j;t);k),(R;j);(t;k)\bigr),\\
E_7&=M*_2\bigl(\mathcal U*_2(t;k)\bigr),\\
E_8&=M*_2\mathcal F,\\
E_9&=M*_2(u*_1\mathcal K).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
For \(E_0,E_2,E_4,E_6\), the generic chains have respective factor lists
\[
[r,e,m,h,k],\quad
[r,p,q,h,k],\quad
[M,R,q,h,k],\quad
[R,j,t,k].
\]

Finally, set
\[
F_0=E_0,\qquad F_{i+1}=F_i*_3E_{i+1}\quad(0\le i<9),
\qquad
\Pi_{\mathrm{unit}}=F_9.
\tag{10}
\]
Equations (8)–(9) verify every gluing, giving
\[
\boxed{
\Pi_{\mathrm{unit}}:
P(\alpha*_11_g)
\longrightarrow
P(\alpha)*_2
\bigl(p_f*_1(p_x*_0c_0)\bigr).
}
\tag{11}
\]

All auxiliary 4-cells are coherences over pasting contexts of dimension at most 3, so their strict images are identities. Both endpoints of (11) strictify to \(p_\alpha\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
\pi(\Pi_{\mathrm{unit}})
=
1_{p_\alpha}
=
c\star\pi(c_0).
}
\tag{12}
\]

This proves the specified first instance of question 1, for the generic lower-dimensional choices (1)–(2). It works for any specified coherence \(c_0\) of the displayed type over \(D^2\).

## 2. Fixed dimension: a positive answer

Put
\[
C_Q=\operatorname{Cone}(Q;c).
\]
I will prove the stronger, local implication
\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{RC}(r+1,C_{\Sigma Q})
\Longrightarrow
\mathrm{RC}(r,C_Q).
}
\tag{13}
\]

The essential construction lowers the dimension of terms crossing a chosen \(0/1\) cut. It is a translation of syntax, not a judgmental identification of weak units.

### 2.1. Colored contexts and the cut translation

Assign each 0-variable of a context \(\Gamma\) a color in \(\{0,1\}\), requiring every generating 1-cell to have nondecreasing endpoint colors.

Call a positive-dimensional term \(t\) **crossing** if
\[
\epsilon(s_0t)=0,\qquad \epsilon(t_0t)=1.
\]
Call it **same-side** if its endpoint colors agree.

We need the following locality fact:

> A same-side term has no crossing variable in its dependency-closed support.

Here is a proof that also handles arbitrary substitutions. More generally, assign ordered integer ranks to the 0-variables and require generating 1-cells to be nondecreasing. Prove by mutual induction on declarations and terms that every 0-variable in a term’s dependency-closed support has rank between the ranks of its 0-source and 0-target.

The variable case follows from its declared boundaries. For a coherence over a pasting context \(P\), apply the induction hypothesis to its generic boundary terms. Full support forces their 0-boundaries to be the first and last root vertices of \(P\). The boundary-support alternative has the same consequence; for a 1-dimensional pasting context it selects those vertices directly. Successive root vertices remain nondecreasing after substitution, because each intervening generating 1-cell is substituted by a term already covered by the induction.

For Boolean ranks, equal outer colors therefore force every substitution argument to be same-side. Induction excludes crossing variables from those arguments.

Now define \(D\Gamma\) by deleting all 0-variables and all same-side variables, and lowering each crossing declaration by one dimension. For crossing types, set
\[
D(s\to_\star t)=\star,
\]
and, when the endpoints have positive dimension,
\[
D(s\to_A t)=D(s)\to_{D(A)}D(t).
\tag{14}
\]
A crossing variable is sent to its retained, lower-dimensional variable.

The coherence case requires a particular choice.

### 2.2. Decoding a coherence

Let
\[
t=\operatorname{coh}_{P,A}[\sigma]
\]
be crossing, of dimension \(d\ge1\). Pull the colors back to the **generic arity** \(P\) using \(\sigma\) on 0-variables.

Write its root decomposition
\[
P=\bigvee_{i=1}^{k}\Sigma B_i.
\tag{15}
\]
This description of pasting diagrams, together with the corresponding rootwise boundary formulas, is Benjamin–Markakis, Definitions 2–3. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01611?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The root colors form a nondecreasing sequence beginning in \(0\) and ending in \(1\). Exactly one branch crosses the cut; write
\[
D(P)=B_j=:B.
\]

Compute \(D(A)\) and \(D(\sigma)\) recursively. Choose a term \(F\) over the generic reduced arity \(B\) as follows:

1. If \(d=1\), then \(B\) is a point. Take its sole variable.
2. If \(d>1\), \(k>1\), \(B\) is a \((d-1)\)-disk, and \(D(A)\) is **literally** the declared type of its top variable \(z\), take \(F=z\).
3. Otherwise take
   \[
   F=\operatorname{coh}_{B,D(A)}[\mathrm{id}_B].
   \]

Define
\[
D(t)=F[D(\sigma)].
\tag{16}
\]

The test in clause 2 occurs **before substitution**, in the generic colored arity. Also, the condition \(k>1\) is essential: it prevents contraction of a suspended raw coherence.

#### Admissibility

Use dependency-closed supports. The simultaneous induction gives
\[
\operatorname{supp}(Dt)
=
D\bigl(\operatorname{supp}(t)\cap
      \{\text{crossing variables}\}\bigr).
\tag{17}
\]
Same-side arguments contribute no crossing variables by locality. Each generic choice of \(F\) uses all of \(B\); for the disk-variable choice, this follows because the top variable’s dependencies are the entire disk.

For the fallback coherence:

- Full support in \(P\) becomes full support in \(B\).
- In the composition case, put \(m=\dim P\). If \(\dim B=m-1\), cutting the two \((m-1)\)-boundaries of \(P\) gives the two \((m-2)\)-boundaries of \(B\).
- If \(\dim B<m-1\), both cut boundary supports are all of \(B\), so the decoded type satisfies the full-support coherence alternative.

The dimension-1 case is handled separately because CaTT has no 0-dimensional coherence constructor. Thus (16) always has the type specified by (14).

#### Substitution compatibility

For every color-preserving substitution \(\theta\),
\[
\boxed{
D(t[\theta])\equiv D(t)[D(\theta)].
}
\tag{18}
\]
At a coherence node, \(\sigma\) and \(\sigma\theta\) induce the same coloring of the generic arity. They therefore select the same branch and the same clause of (16). The remaining equality follows by induction on the substitution arguments.

Testing the disk condition after substitution would not justify (18); the generic test is what makes this argument work.

### 2.3. Suspension and composition

Give the two suspension vertices colors \(0,1\). Then
\[
\boxed{
D(\Sigma\Gamma)\equiv\Gamma,\qquad
D(\Sigma A)\equiv A,\qquad
D(\Sigma t)\equiv t.
}
\tag{19}
\]
Indeed, a suspended coherence has dimension at least 2 and a generic arity with exactly one root branch. Neither special clause of (16) applies, so the original coherence constructor is reconstructed literally. The syntax-level suspension used here is the one displayed in BMOSV §2.3. For the standard direct compositions, two further literal equations hold:
\[
D(e*_0u)\equiv D(u)
\tag{20}
\]
when \(e\) is a same-side 1-cell followed by a crossing term \(u\), and
\[
D(u*_kv)\equiv D(u)*_{k-1}D(v)
\qquad(k>0)
\tag{21}
\]
when \(u,v\) are crossing and composable.

To check (20), use the generic arity \(D^1\vee D^d\), where \(d=\dim u\). Its crossing branch lowers to \(D^{d-1}\). For \(d=1\), clause 1 applies. ### 2.4. Raising a cone into the next join

\[

\[

\Sigma c&\mapsto p_S,\\
\Sigma q&\mapsto p_N*_0\bar q
&&\text{for each base generator }q,\\
\Sigma p_q&\mapsto p_{\bar q}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{22}
\]
Here \(U,V\) are the two suspension vertices of \(\Sigma C_Q\).

**The globularity of \(Q\) is used in this typing check.** A generating boundary of \(q\) is a variable. Consequently, the immediate boundaries of \(p_N*_0\bar q\) are exactly the images under (22) of the suspended boundaries of \(q\).

For a projection generator \(p_q\), suspension raises every composition index in its specified cone type by one. Substitution (22) inserts the final whisker \(p_N*_0\bar q\). The resulting right-associated expression is exactly the declared type of \(p_{\bar q}\) in \(J_Q\).

Define
\[
L_Q(t):=(\Sigma t)[e_Q].
\tag{23}
\]
This formula defines \(L_Q\) on arbitrary terms. The whiskering formula in (22) is asserted only for base generators.

Color \(J_Q\) by
\[
\epsilon(d)=\epsilon(N)=0,\qquad \epsilon(S)=1.
\]
Then, after the indicated variable renaming,
\[
D(J_Q)\equiv C_Q.
\tag{24}

Indeed:

- \(d,N,S,p_N\) are deleted;
- \(\bar q\) lowers to \(q\);
- \(p_S\) lowers to \(c\);

Equations (20)–(21) verify the declared types in (24): the final \(p_N\)-whisker disappears under \(D\), and every remaining composition index decreases by one.

On the variables in (22), \(D(e_Q)\) is the identity substitution. Therefore (18)–(19) give the required literal retraction:

\boxed{

\tag{25}
