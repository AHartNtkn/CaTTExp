# Conjecture: the cartesian product of globes is the Gray product with mixed cells inverted

*Self-contained problem statement. No knowledge of the surrounding project is assumed. Partial results and proof attempts are in §3–§4. Status: **SOLVED (2026-11-18).** C1 and C1′ are proved, in (∞,ω) and every finite n, by an external researcher who completed the §7 sketch; see "Researcher Response" and "Verification" at the end. C1″ holds with "locally full" deleted: strong cells are those whose adjunct inverts all mixed cells, including those on boundary faces.*

## 1. Setting

- Cat_n denotes the ∞-category of (∞,n)-categories, for 1 ≤ n ≤ ∞. The case n = ∞ (or ω) is the one of interest, but finite n is already open beyond n = 2.
- Dᵏ is the walking k-cell (the k-globe). It has two cells in each dimension below k and one cell in dimension k.
- Dᵏ ⊗ Dˡ is the **(lax) Gray tensor product** of Campion, *The Gray tensor product of (∞,n)-categories* (arXiv 2311.00205). It is the unique monoidal biclosed structure on Cat_n for which the inclusion of Gray cubes is strong monoidal (Campion, Thm. A). Equivalently, it is the Day-convolution Gray product of Chanavat on diagrammatic sets (arXiv 2505.01387).
  - Dᵏ ⊗ Dˡ is freely generated by the regular directed complex whose cells are pairs x ⊗ y, where x is a cell of Dᵏ and y is a cell of Dˡ.
  - The cell x ⊗ y has dimension dim x + dim y.
  - Its boundary is given by Steiner's formula ∂^α(x⊗y) = (∂^α x ⊗ y) ∪ (x ⊗ ∂^{(−1)^{dim x} α} y), or in full by Chanavat (2505.01387), Prop. 2.16.
- A generating cell x ⊗ y is **mixed** if dim x ≥ 1 and dim y ≥ 1.
- The pt is the unit for ⊗, and the projections A ⊗ B → A ⊗ pt ≅ A and A ⊗ B → pt ⊗ B ≅ B induce a canonical comparison map

  ```
  c_{A,B} : A ⊗ B → A × B .
  ```

- For a set S of cells of X, X[S⁻¹] denotes the localisation of X inverting S. It is the pushout of X along ∐ Dᵏ → ∐ Eᵏ, where Eᵏ is Dᵏ with its top cell made invertible.

## 2. Statement

**Conjecture C1.** For all k, l ≥ 0, the comparison map c factors through an equivalence

```
(Dᵏ ⊗ Dˡ)[mixed⁻¹]  ≃  Dᵏ × Dˡ .
```

**Equivalent forms**, given §3.1:

- **(C1′)** For all A, B ∈ Cat_n, c_{A,B} exhibits A × B as the localisation of A ⊗ B at the images of the mixed generators, along all maps Dⁱ → A and Dʲ → B.
- **(C1″)** For all B, C, the cartesian internal hom Fun(B, C) is equivalent to the locally full sub-object of the right lax internal hom Fun^lax(B, C). That sub-object has the same objects and keeps exactly those higher cells whose naturality cells (images of mixed generators) are invertible. In words: *strong transformations are exactly the lax transformations with invertible naturality cells*, in every dimension.

**Why it matters.** C1″ is the precise sense in which "pseudo = lax + invertibility marking". It lets a single syntax for lax cylinders also serve for pseudo ones, by marking cells invertible.

## 3. What has been proved

### 3.1 Reduction to globes (proof)

Let Θ = Θ_n be Joyal's category of globular pasting diagrams. It is a dense full subcategory of Cat_n, and each of its objects is a globular sum, i.e. an iterated pushout of globes along globes.

1. For T, U ∈ Θ, set L(T, U) := (T ⊗ U)[mixed⁻¹], where the mixed cells are the generators x ⊗ y of the product complex with dim x, dim y ≥ 1. There is a map L(T, U) → T × U, because c inverts mixed cells. In a cartesian product, the composites (x, id)·(id, y) and (id, y)·(x, id) are both canonically equivalent to (x, y), so the image of the generator x ⊗ y, which mediates between them, is invertible. This is standard, but a fully careful proof needs a choice of model.
2. Extend L to Cat_n × Cat_n by left Kan extension along Θ × Θ ⊂ Cat_n × Cat_n. The result L̃ preserves colimits in each variable. Since ⊗ preserves colimits in each variable (it is biclosed), and localisation commutes with colimits, L̃(A, B) is the localisation of A ⊗ B at the images of mixed cells.
3. The cartesian product also preserves colimits in each variable, because Cat_n is cartesian closed. So L̃(A, B) → A × B is a natural transformation between functors that are cocontinuous in each variable. It is an equivalence for all A, B if and only if it is an equivalence on Θ × Θ.
4. Globular sums are colimits of globes, and both sides preserve those colimits in each variable. So it is enough to check globes. ∎

Hence **C1 ⇔ C1′**. C1′ ⇔ C1″ by currying and Yoneda in the first variable.

### 3.2 Trivial cases (proof)

If k = 0 or l = 0, there are no mixed cells and D⁰ = pt is the unit for both products.

### 3.3 The case k = l = 1 (proof)

- **The Gray square.** D¹ ⊗ D¹ is the free (∞,n)-category on four objects, four arrows a, b, c, d forming two paths a·b and c·d from (0,0) to (1,1), and one 2-cell τ : a·b ⇒ c·d. As a pushout:

  ```
  D¹⊗D¹ = (P₁ ⊔ P₂) ⊔_{∂D²} D²
  ```

  where P₁ and P₂ are copies of D¹ ∨ D¹ glued at their endpoints, and ∂D² → P₁ ⊔ P₂ picks the two composites.
- **Inverting τ.** This replaces D² by E² ≃ D¹. So (D¹⊗D¹)[τ⁻¹] ≃ P₁ ⊔_{D¹} P₂, glued along the two composite edges.
- **Moving to Cat₁.** The inclusion Cat₁ ⊂ Cat_n has both adjoints, so it preserves pushouts and products. The pushout P₁ ⊔_{D¹} P₂ is computed in Cat₁, where each Pᵢ with its composite edge is Δ² (Segal condition). So the result is Δ² ⊔_{Δ¹} Δ² ≃ Δ¹ × Δ¹ = D¹ × D¹. ∎

### 3.4 Dimension 2 (literature)

Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari (arXiv 2006.14495), Prop. 2.12 prove, for ∞-bicategories, that the Gray product with additional triangles marked thin maps by an equivalence to the cartesian product. We believe this gives C1 for the (∞,2)-truncations of all Dᵏ ⊗ Dˡ, i.e. for n = 2. The precise matching of their thin-triangle marking with "mixed generators" has not been checked line by line.

## 4. A proof strategy for the general case (attempted; stalls at Lemma S3)

**Induction on k for l = 1**, using suspension. Write ΣC for the suspension, so Dᵏ = ΣDᵏ⁻¹.

- **(S1)** Loubaton (arXiv 2406.05425, Ch. 2) gives ΣC ⊗ D¹ as a colimit of D¹ ∨ ΣC, Σ(C ⊗ {0}), Σ(C ⊗ D¹), Σ(C ⊗ {1}) and ΣC ∨ D¹. This is available.
- **(S2)** The analogous colimit formula for ΣC × D¹, with Σ(C ⊗ D¹) replaced by Σ(C × D¹), and with the 2-cell-level gluing made invertible. We believe this is routine but have not written it out.
- **(S3)** Mixed cells of ΣC ⊗ D¹ are the cells (Σx) ⊗ i for *every* cell x of C, including objects of C. Inside the hom from (0,0) to (1,1) they become *all* cells of the form x ⊗ i in C ⊗ D¹, including the components. The step therefore needs

  ```
  (C ⊗ D¹)[ {x ⊗ i : x ∈ C}⁻¹ ]  ≃  C .
  ```

  This is plausible: Chanavat (2505.01387) shows that tensoring with the walking equivalence is a cylinder. It reduces to showing that the sub-object of Fun^lax(D¹, T) on cells with all components and naturality cells invertible is equivalent to T. **This is where the attempt stalls.**

**General l.** This would need a formula for ΣC ⊗ ΣC′. We have not found one in the literature. Ara–Maltsiniotis (Mém. SMF 165) relate Gray products, joins and suspensions in the strict setting, which may be the right model.

**Alternative routes.**
- Verity's complicial sets, where both products are explicit.
- The strict analogue: Steiner complexes, with C1 holding up to folk weak equivalence, transported along a comparison of strict Gray products with Campion's.

## 5. Why it matters

Strong (pseudo) natural transformations are expected to be exactly the lax transformations with invertible naturality cells. C1 makes this precise in every dimension. A syntax that describes lax cylinders could then describe pseudo ones by marking some generators invertible, so a single shape language serves both.

## 6. What would count as a solution

Any of the following:
- a proof of C1 for all k and l, or of lemma S3 together with a general-l argument;
- a counterexample, e.g. specific k and l for which the localisation is not equivalent to Dᵏ × Dˡ;
- a reference where this is proved, in any model of (∞,n)-categories.

## 7. Literature status (searched 2026-11-18)

**Status: partly solved in the literature. The case l = 1 is proved, for every A and every (∞,1)-category B. The general case seems to follow from published results by a short argument (sketch below, not yet checked line by line).**

**Proved results.**
- **l = 1, any k (in fact any A).** Loubaton, *Categorical theory of (∞,ω)-categories* ([arXiv 2406.05425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05425)), Prop. 3.1.3.1, gives C♯ ⊗ K♯ ≃ C♯ × K♯ for every (∞,ω)-category C and every (∞,1)-category K. The same result is in his thesis ([arXiv 2307.11931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11931), Ch. 5).
  - Here C♯ marks every cell of positive dimension. By Construction 3.1.2.1, the underlying (∞,ω)-category of C ⊗ K♯ is the pushout of C ⊗ K along ∐ Dⁿ ⊗ K → ∐ τⁱₙ(Dⁿ ⊗ K), one summand for each marked n-cell of C. The intelligent truncation τⁱₙ inverts the cells of dimension above n. For K = D¹ the only such cell of Dⁿ ⊗ D¹ is the mixed generator x ⊗ i.
  - So the underlying statement is exactly (C ⊗ D¹)[{x ⊗ i : dim x ≥ 1}⁻¹] ≃ C × D¹. This is C1′ with B = D¹, and with A arbitrary. Loubaton states it this way in his introduction: "the underlying (∞,ω)-category of A♯ ⊗ [1]♯ is A × [1]."
  - **Lemma S3 of §4 follows.** Also invert the components x ⊗ i for objects x. That turns C × D¹ into C × E¹ ≃ C.
  - Loubaton's Gray cylinder is the cocontinuous extension of the strict Steiner Gray cylinder on Θ. By Campion's Cor. 3.5 (see below) it therefore agrees with Campion's ⊗ D¹.
- **n = 2.** Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari ([arXiv 2006.14495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14495)), Prop. 2.12, already cited in §3.4.
- **The comparison map is an epimorphism, and the strong-to-lax inclusion is a monomorphism, in all dimensions.** Gepner–Heine, *Oriented category theory* ([arXiv 2510.10504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10504)), §4.1:
  - Thm 4.1.9: Fun(A, C) → Fun^lax(A, C) and Fun(A, C) → Fun^oplax(A, C) are inclusions. That is, they are embeddings on maximal subspaces and on all mapping spaces.
  - Thm 4.1.10: A₁ ⊠ ⋯ ⊠ Aₙ → A₁ × ⋯ × Aₙ is an epimorphism.
  - This gives the "inclusion" half of C1″. It does not identify the image as the cells with invertible naturality cells.
- **Comical and complicial models.** In Campion–Kapulkin–Maehara ([arXiv 2005.07603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07603)), Def. 2.13 and Remark 2.14, the *pseudo* Gray tensor of marked cubical sets is by definition the lax one with exactly the mixed cubes x ⊗ y (dim x, dim y ≥ 1) marked. Its triangulation is the cartesian product of marked simplicial sets (§1, Notation 1.27, and Thm 6.10). Doherty–Kapulkin–Maehara ([arXiv 2106.09428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09428)) prove that triangulation is a Quillen equivalence. In the saturated models, marking a cell is localising at it.
  - So C1 holds in the comical model, for its own lax Gray tensor.
  - Campion (2311.00205, introduction) notes that no comparison is known between the comical or complicial Gray tensor and his universal one for n ≥ 3. Keidar–Neuhauser ([arXiv 2606.27574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27574)) compare a new lax-grid model with Campion's, but not with the comical one.

**Sketch of the general case from these results (to be checked).**
1. Campion ([arXiv 2209.09376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.09376)): the Gray cubes are dense in flagged (∞,ω)-categories. Run the reduction of §3.1 with cubes in place of Θ. It then suffices to prove C1 for A = □ᵃ and B = □ᵇ, where □ᵐ = (D¹)^{⊗m}.
2. Induct on b, writing □ᵇ = □ᵇ⁻¹ ⊗ D¹. Loubaton, Prop. 3.1.3.2: (D♯ × C) ⊗ K♯ ≃ D♯ × (C ⊗ K♯). A cell of a product is marked when both components are. With D = A, C = (□ᵇ⁻¹)♭ and K = D¹, this reads:

   A × (□ᵇ⁻¹ ⊗ D¹) ≃ ((A × □ᵇ⁻¹) ⊗ D¹)[{(x, b) ⊗ i : dim x ≥ 1, b an object}⁻¹].
3. By induction, A × □ᵇ⁻¹ ≃ (A ⊗ □ᵇ⁻¹)[mixed⁻¹]. Now − ⊗ D¹ preserves pushouts, and Eᵏ ⊗ D¹ ≃ Dᵏ⁻¹ ⊗ D¹. So tensoring with D¹ inverts s ⊗ 0, s ⊗ 1 and s ⊗ i for every mixed cell s = x ⊗ y.
4. The cells inverted in steps 2 and 3 together are exactly x ⊗ w with dim x ≥ 1 and w a cell of □ᵇ⁻¹ ⊗ D¹ of dimension ≥ 1. These are the mixed cells of A ⊗ □ᵇ. ∎ (sketch)

The steps that need care:
- the density reduction with cubes, including functoriality of "invert images of mixed generators" along degeneracies, where degenerate images are identities;
- flagged versus complete (∞,ω)-categories;
- the marking convention on products in Loubaton.

**Recommended next step:** check this sketch in detail. If it holds, C1 becomes a theorem with a literature proof.

# Researcher Response

**C1 and C1′ follow from the cited results.** The cube induction in §7 can be completed, but it needs two lemmas: one describing tensoring a localisation with the interval, and one describing the marking generated by the pure cells of the first factor. The extension from cubes should then be made directly through mapping spaces, without assuming that the proposed localisation functor preserves colimits.

**C1″ needs a terminology correction:** its description by invertible naturality cells is correct, with the same condition imposed on boundaries. The resulting subcategory is generally **not locally full** when \(n\ge3\).

Here is the proof. All equivalences are compatible with the canonical comparison maps.

## 1. Setting and compatibility of the cylinders

Work first in the **Rezk-complete** \(\mathrm{Cat}_{\omega}\). Write
\[
I=D^1,\qquad Q_b=I^{\otimes b},
\]
and let \(u_r\) be the top cell of \(D^r\).

Loubaton’s cylinder agrees with Campion’s. Indeed, Loubaton’s Proposition 2.3.3.1 and Theorem 2.3.3.22 identify its restriction to \(\Theta\), naturally, with the strict Steiner cylinder. Campion’s Example 3.2, Corollary 3.5 and Corollary 3.15 give the same restriction. Density of \(\Theta\) and preservation of colimits extend this identification to every input. Thus the Loubaton cylinder formulas used below apply to Campion’s tensor. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

We will use the following elementary observation repeatedly.

**Colimits of localisation maps.** Suppose \(X_j\to Y_j\) is a natural diagram whose \(j\)-th map localises a family \(S_j\). Then
\[
\operatorname*{colim}_j X_j\longrightarrow
\operatorname*{colim}_j Y_j
\]
localises the union of the images of the \(S_j\).

To prove this, map into an arbitrary \(Z\). Each
\[
\operatorname{Map}(Y_j,Z)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Map}(X_j,Z)
\]
identifies its source with the full subspace of maps inverting \(S_j\). Taking limits identifies the resulting source with maps inverting every image of every \(S_j\). This proves the universal property, including coherent uniqueness of factorisations.

## 2. Tensoring a localisation with the interval

The missing assertion in §7, step 3, is the following.

### Lemma 1

If \(X\to X[S^{-1}]\) localises positive-dimensional cells \(s\), then
\[
X\otimes I\longrightarrow X[S^{-1}]\otimes I
\]
is the localisation at
\[
\boxed{\{s\otimes0,\ s\otimes1,\ s\otimes i:s\in S\}.}
\tag{1}
\]

Here and below, “localisation at” specifies generators for the universal property; further cells can become invertible as consequences.

### Proof

Let
\[
q_r:D^r\longrightarrow D^{r-1}
\]
be the map sending the top \(r\)-cell to an identity. We first prove that
\[
q_r\otimes I
\]
localises
\[
u_r\otimes0,\qquad u_r\otimes1,\qquad u_r\otimes i.
\tag{2}
\]

There is no ambiguity about the localisation map \(q_r\): take
\[
q_r=\Sigma^{r-1}(I\to\mathrm{pt}).
\]
Suspension preserves cell-localisations. For specified endpoints \(a,b\) in \(Z\), this follows from
\[
\operatorname{Map}_{\partial I/}(\Sigma X,Z)
\simeq
\operatorname{Map}(X,\operatorname{hom}_Z(a,b)).
\]
Inverting \(\Sigma s\) on the left is exactly inverting \(s\) in the hom on the right. This argument does not require the unpointed suspension to preserve all colimits.

**Base case \(r=1\).** In \(I\otimes I\), invert the two endpoint copies \(i\otimes0\) and \(i\otimes1\). Loubaton’s Proposition 2.3.3.6 identifies the resulting pushout with
\[
(I\otimes I)
\amalg_{I\otimes\{0\}\,\amalg\,I\otimes\{1\}}
(\{0\}\amalg\{1\})
\simeq \Sigma I=D^2.
\tag{3}
\]
The square generator becomes the top cell of \(D^2\). Inverting it gives \(D^1\), and the composite map is \(q_1\otimes I\). [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Induction step.** Loubaton’s Proposition 2.3.1.10 supplies the natural colimit decomposition
\[
(\Sigma C)\otimes I
\simeq
\operatorname*{colim}
\left(
I\vee\Sigma C
\longleftarrow \Sigma C
\longrightarrow \Sigma(C\otimes I)
\longleftarrow \Sigma C
\longrightarrow \Sigma C\vee I
\right).
\tag{4}
\]
The maps into the outer terms are whiskerings; those into the middle term come from the interval endpoints. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For \(r\ge2\), apply (4) to
\[
q_{r-1}:D^{r-1}\longrightarrow D^{r-2}.
\]
The resulting map of colimits is \(q_r\otimes I\).

The outer and overlap maps localise top \(r\)-cells. Their images in \(D^r\otimes I\) are the two endpoint cells in (2), or whiskerings of those cells.

By induction and the suspension observation, the middle map
\[
\Sigma(q_{r-1}\otimes I)
\]
localises
\[
\Sigma(u_{r-1}\otimes0),\quad
\Sigma(u_{r-1}\otimes1),\quad
\Sigma(u_{r-1}\otimes i).
\]
The first two map to whiskerings of \(u_r\otimes0\) and \(u_r\otimes1\). The third maps to \(u_r\otimes i\). Whiskering preserves invertibility, so the union of these conditions is equivalent to (2). The observation about colimits of localisations completes the induction.

Finally, write the original localisation as
\[
X[S^{-1}]
\simeq
X\amalg_{\coprod_{s\in S}D^{|s|}}
\coprod_{s\in S}D^{|s|-1}.
\]
Tensoring this pushout with \(I\) and applying (2) proves (1). \(\square\)

This supplies what the absolute equivalence
\[
E^r\otimes I\simeq D^{r-1}\otimes I
\]
alone does not establish: the universal property of the specified map **under \(D^r\otimes I\)**.

## 3. The marking needed for the cube step

Let \(U\) forget Loubaton’s markings. For a category \(X\) and a family \(T\) of positive-dimensional cells, write \(X_{\langle T\rangle}\) for \(X\) with the marking generated by \(T\).

### Lemma 2

There is a canonical equivalence
\[
\boxed{
U\bigl(X_{\langle T\rangle}\otimes I^\sharp\bigr)
\simeq
(X\otimes I)[(t\otimes i)^{-1}:t\in T].
}
\tag{5}
\]

### Proof

Let \((D^r)^{\mathrm t}\) mark the top cell, together with the required identity cells. Freely adjoining the markings gives a pushout
\[
X_{\langle T\rangle}
\simeq
X^\flat
\amalg_{\coprod_{t\in T}(D^{|t|})^\flat}
\coprod_{t\in T}(D^{|t|})^{\mathrm t}.
\tag{6}
\]

Both the marked cylinder and \(U\) preserve colimits. Construction 3.1.2.1 and Propositions 3.1.2.3–3.1.2.4 identify the image of each attaching map in (6) with
\[
D^r\otimes I\longrightarrow\tau_r^i(D^r\otimes I).
\]
This localises its sole generating cell above dimension \(r\), namely \(u_r\otimes i\). Applying \(U(-\otimes I^\sharp)\) to (6) proves (5). [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

In particular, marking closure introduces no additional independent localisation conditions.

### Applying the lemma to \(A^\sharp\times Q^\flat\)

For this step let \(A,Q\) be strict Gray cubes. Put \(M=A\times Q\), and let \(T\) consist of the cells
\[
(x,1_b^r),
\qquad
x\text{ a generating }r\text{-cell of }A,\quad
r>0,\quad b\in Q_0.
\tag{7}
\]
Here \(1_b^r\) is the iterated identity \(r\)-cell at \(b\).

We claim that
\[
\boxed{M_{\langle T\rangle}=A^\sharp\times Q^\flat.}
\tag{8}
\]

The product marking is coordinatewise. Consequently, its marked \(r\)-cells are pairs
\[
(a,1_q),
\qquad a\in A_r,\quad q\in Q_{r-1},
\tag{9}
\]
including cases where \(q\) has positive dimension. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

These additional cells are generated by (7). Read the following \(0\)-composition from left to right:
\[
(a,1_q)
=
(1_{s_0a}^{r},1_q)
*_0
(a,1_{t_0q}^{r}).
\tag{10}
\]
The first factor is the identity \(r\)-cell on
\[
(1_{s_0a}^{r-1},q).
\]
Thus (10) is a lower-dimensional whiskering of a pure \(A\)-cell at a vertex of \(Q\). Pure composite \(A\)-cells are generated by the corresponding pure generating cells. Conversely, the coordinatewise marking satisfies the marking axioms and marks every cell in (7). This proves (8).

Now apply Loubaton’s Proposition 3.1.3.2 with \(D=A\), \(C=Q^\flat\) and \(K=I\):
\[
(A^\sharp\times Q^\flat)\otimes I^\sharp
\simeq
A^\sharp\times(Q^\flat\otimes I^\sharp).
\tag{11}
\]
Combining (5), (8) and (11), and forgetting markings, gives
\[
\boxed{
\bigl((A\times Q)\otimes I\bigr)
\left[
\bigl((x,1_b^{|x|})\otimes i\bigr)^{-1}
\right]
\simeq
A\times(Q\otimes I),
}
\tag{12}
\]
where \(x\) ranges over positive-dimensional generators of \(A\), and \(b\) over vertices of \(Q\). The map in (12) is canonical. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This proves the intended assertion in §7, step 2. The product marks more cells than the displayed family, but that family **generates** its marking.

## 4. Cube induction

Fix a Gray cube \(A\). We prove, by induction on \(b\), that
\[
c_{A,Q_b}:A\otimes Q_b\longrightarrow A\times Q_b
\]
localises the mixed generating cells.

For \(b=0\), both sides are \(A\).

Suppose the assertion holds for \(Q=Q_b\). Factor the next comparison as
\[
A\otimes Q\otimes I
\xrightarrow{\,c_{A,Q}\otimes I\,}
(A\times Q)\otimes I
\longrightarrow
A\times(Q\otimes I).
\tag{13}
\]

By Lemma 1, the first map localises
\[
x\otimes y\otimes0,\qquad
x\otimes y\otimes1,\qquad
x\otimes y\otimes i
\]
for generating \(x,y\) with \(|x|,|y|>0\).

By (12), the second map localises the images of
\[
x\otimes b\otimes i,
\qquad |x|>0,\quad b\in Q_0.
\]

Together these are exactly the mixed generators for the two factors \(A\) and \(Q\otimes I\):

| Generator of \(Q\otimes I\) | Required mixed generator | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| \(y\otimes0\) or \(y\otimes1\), \(|y|>0\) | \(x\otimes y\otimes0\) or \(x\otimes y\otimes1\) | First |
| \(y\otimes i\), \(|y|>0\) | \(x\otimes y\otimes i\) | First |
| \(b\otimes i\), \(b\in Q_0\) | \(x\otimes b\otimes i\) | Second |

Each second-stage generator has the indicated lift to the original source. Therefore the composite localises the union of these families.

The composite in (13) is \(c_{A,Q\otimes I}\): its projection to \(A\) collapses \(Q\) and \(I\), and its projection to \(Q\otimes I\) collapses \(A\). This proves the induction.

Hence, for every pair of cubes,
\[
\boxed{
(Q_a\otimes Q_b)[\mathrm{mixed}^{-1}]
\simeq Q_a\times Q_b.
}
\tag{14}
\]

## 5. Extending from cubes without assuming cocontinuity of localisation

Campion’s cube-density theorem applies directly to complete \(\mathrm{Cat}_\omega\), not only to flagged categories: this is Corollary 2.6 of *Cubes are dense in \((\infty,\infty)\)-categories*. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.09376?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For arbitrary \(A,B\), define the intrinsic family
\[
S(A,B)=
\left\{
(a\otimes b)(u_p\otimes u_q)
\;\middle|\;
p,q>0,\ a:D^p\to A,\ b:D^q\to B
\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]

Use the canonical cube-density presentations
\[
A\simeq\operatorname*{colim}_{Q\to A}Q,
\qquad
B\simeq\operatorname*{colim}_{R\to B}R.
\]

Both products preserve colimits separately. Consequently, \(c_{A,B}\) is the colimit of the canonical cube comparisons \(c_{Q,R}\). By (14) and the observation in §1, it is a localisation at the family \(V(A,B)\) obtained by mapping all mixed cube generators into \(A\otimes B\).

Two facts identify its universal property with localisation at (15).

**First, \(V(A,B)\subseteq S(A,B)\).** Each generating cell of a cube has a characteristic globe map. Tensoring two such maps expresses its mixed generator as a member of (15).

**Second, \(c_{A,B}\) inverts all of \(S(A,B)\).** By naturality, it suffices to inspect
\[
D^p\otimes D^q\longrightarrow D^p\times D^q.
\]
The mixed top cell has dimension \(p+q>\max(p,q)\). The strict product has no nonidentity cells in that dimension, so its image is an identity.

It follows that, for every \(Z\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Map}(A\times B,Z)
&\simeq
\{F:A\otimes B\to Z\mid F\text{ inverts }V(A,B)\}\\
&=
\{F:A\otimes B\to Z\mid F\text{ inverts }S(A,B)\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
For the second equality: an \(S\)-inverting map inverts \(V\), hence factors through \(c_{A,B}\); every such factorisation inverts \(S\), because \(c_{A,B}\) does.

Thus
\[
\boxed{
(A\otimes B)[S(A,B)^{-1}]
\simeq A\times B.
}
\tag{17}
\]

This proves **C1′**.

For \(A=D^k\), \(B=D^\ell\), every nonidentity cell is a generating face. Degenerate globe maps contribute identities to (15). Thus (15) imposes exactly the inversions of the mixed generators specified in C1. Therefore
\[
\boxed{
(D^k\otimes D^\ell)[\mathrm{mixed}^{-1}]
\simeq D^k\times D^\ell.
}
\tag{18}
\]

This also resolves the functoriality issue in §7. Arbitrary cube maps need not send generators to generators; they can send them to composites. The proof uses the natural comparison and its mapping-space universal property, so literal preservation of the generating family is unnecessary.

Likewise, the assertion in §3.1 that the proposed localisation preserves colimits should be treated as a **consequence of (17)**. Being a left Kan extension along a dense inclusion does not alone establish that assertion.

## 6. Finite \(n\)

Let \(\tau_n:\mathrm{Cat}_\omega\to\mathrm{Cat}_n\) be the reflector. Campion’s reflected tensor satisfies
\[
A\otimes_n B\simeq\tau_n(A\otimes_\omega B).
\]
This is supplied by the monoidal-localisation results in Theorem 3.14 and Corollary 3.15. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00205?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Take \(A,B\in\mathrm{Cat}_n\) and apply \(\tau_n\) to (17). Since the reflector preserves colimits, it preserves the localisation pushout. Since \(A\times B\) is already in \(\mathrm{Cat}_n\),
\[
\boxed{
(A\otimes_n B)[\mathrm{mixed}^{-1}]
\simeq A\times B.
}
\tag{19}
\]

For globes above dimension \(n\), interpret \(D^k\) through the same reflector. This proves the finite-dimensional versions as well.

## 7. The exact internal-hom statement

Currying (17) gives, naturally in \(A\),
\[
\operatorname{Map}\bigl(A,\operatorname{Fun}(B,C)\bigr)
\simeq
\left\{
\Phi:A\otimes B\to C
\;\middle|\;
\Phi\text{ inverts }S(A,B)
\right\}.
\tag{20}
\]

Consequently, the correct formulation of C1″ is:

> \(\operatorname{Fun}(B,C)\) is the sub-\((\infty,n)\)-category of \(\operatorname{Fun}^{\mathrm{lax}}(B,C)\) with the same objects whose \(r\)-cells \(\sigma\) are precisely those for which the adjunct
> \[
> \Phi_\sigma:D^r\otimes B\to C
> \]
> sends every mixed cell to an equivalence.

This tests positive-dimensional faces of \(D^r\) as well as its top cell. Equivalently, impose the condition recursively on the source and target, then require invertibility of the naturality cells involving the top \(r\)-cell.

### Why “locally full” must be removed

There is a counterexample already with
\[
B=I,\qquad C=D^3.
\]
Write the nonidentity cells of \(D^3\) as
\[
f,g:x\to y,\qquad
a,b:f\Rightarrow g,\qquad
\theta:a\Rrightarrow b.
\]

Let \(F,G:I\to D^3\) be constant at \(x,y\). The constant transformations with components \(f\) and \(g\) have identity naturality cells, so both are strong.

There is a lax modification between them with endpoint components \(a,b\) and naturality \(3\)-cell \(\theta\). Explicitly, it is classified by a map
\[
D^2\otimes I\longrightarrow D^3
\]
sending the two mixed \(2\)-generators to identities and the mixed \(3\)-generator to \(\theta\). Swap \(a,b\) if the chosen lax convention reverses its orientation.

Since \(\theta\) is noninvertible, this modification is excluded from the strong internal hom. Thus the selected hom subcategory is not full on its selected transformations.

The statement **“strong transformations are lax transformations with invertible naturality cells, in every dimension”** holds with the recursive boundary condition above.

## 8. Lemma S3

S3 follows as well. First apply the interval case of (17):
\[
(C\otimes I)
[(x\otimes i)^{-1}:|x|>0]
\simeq C\times I.
\]
Then invert the component arrows indexed by the objects of \(C\). A strong transformation is an equivalence precisely when its object components are equivalences; Loubaton states this criterion in Proposition 2.2.1.59. Hence this second localisation is \(C\times E^1\simeq C\). [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Therefore
\[
\boxed{
(C\otimes I)
[(x\otimes i)^{-1}:|x|\ge0]
\simeq C.
}
\]

**The resulting status is:** C1 and C1′ are proved by the argument above; the intended cellwise assertion of C1″ is proved; “locally full” in C1″ should be deleted.
