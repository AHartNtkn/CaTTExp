# Interpreting CaTT in (∞,ω)-categories, round 5: the unit on finite computads and the directed coherator

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Rounds 1–4 and their verified answers are archived in `answered/catt-models-in-infinity-omega-2026-11-18.md` and `answered/catt-models-in-infinity-omega-r2-2026-11-18.md` through `-r4-2026-11-18.md`; nothing answered there is re-asked. The smallest open case is Q1.*

## Background

**CaTT.** CaTT (Finster–Mimram, LICS 2017) is a dependent type theory with one sort former, `⋆` and `arr A t u` (cells of type A from t to u), and one term former, `coh`. For a *pasting context* Θ (a context presenting a globular pasting diagram) and two parallel terms u, v over Θ satisfying a support (free-variable) condition, `coh Θ (u → v) σ` is a cell, for every substitution σ into Θ. Substitution is strict and syntactic. The models of CaTT are exactly Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis weak ω-categories: presheaves on the CaTT coherator sending globular sums to globular products (Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, arXiv 2106.04475, §3).

**A directed variant.** A variant L of CaTT keeps the same syntax but changes the side condition of `coh` so that, across objects, interchange is a directed cell (lax, not invertible). Its intended strict models are strict ω-categories with lax transformations (Ara–Maltsiniotis's ∞-Cat_lax, arXiv 1607.00668, Ex. C.18), and its intended homotopical models are oriented (∞,ω)-categories in the sense of Gepner–Heine (arXiv 2510.10504), whose homs carry Campion's Gray tensor (arXiv 2311.00205). The strict soundness of L is proved, using that cells of free strict ω-categories on Steiner complexes are determined by their supports.

**What is known about the homotopy theory.**
- Strict ω-categories have the Lafont–Métayer–Worytkiewicz model structure, cofibrantly generated by the boundary inclusions ∂Oₙ → Oₙ (arXiv 0712.0617, §4).
- Leinster's operad is the universal cofibrant replacement of the strict ω-category operad for that weak factorisation system (Garner, arXiv 0804.2663). Markakis extends this to generalised signatures: free algebras on computads are the cofibrant objects (arXiv 2303.11978).
- For Batanin–Leinster weak ω-categories, Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara prove 2-out-of-3 for ω-weak equivalences and call a (left semi-)model structure future work (arXiv 2406.13240, introduction). We know of no model structure on weak ω-categories.
- Cat_ω (Loubaton, arXiv 2406.05425, the inductive notion lim Cat_n) is presentable and cartesian closed (Prop. 2.2.1.54). Campion's Gray tensor is built on flagged (∞,∞)-categories and transferred to Cat_n for n ≤ ω and to the version with coinductive equivalences.
- CaTT's invertible cells are the coinductively invertible ones (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2406.12127).

**What round 1 established.**
- **A relative category.** Models of CaTT are equivalent to Batanin–Leinster weak ω-categories (Benjamin–Markakis–Sarti, arXiv 2405.00398, §7). With W the Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara ω-weak equivalences (2-out-of-6, arXiv 2406.13240 Thm 3.3.3, Cor. 3.3.4), 𝓗_CaTT := Mod(CaTT)[W⁻¹] exists. FHM (arXiv 2511.09849) characterise trivial fibrations as W ∩ ω-equifibrations (Prop. 4.1.5) and equifibrations as J-injectives (Thm 4.3.1). No (semi-)model structure is known.
- **Strict coherent choice, one direction.** Let 𝓜 be the injective complete Segal Θ_ω-space model category and 𝒜 its algebraically fibrant objects (Nikolaus). For each globular pasting shape P, yP is discrete and fibrant, so F Sp(P) → yP is a trivial fibration in 𝒜. Lifting the CaTT coherator's cell attachments against these gives R : 𝕋 → 𝒜 preserving globular sums, and S(Y) := 𝒜(R(−), Y) is a CaTT model with strict substitution and S(Y)_n = (UY)(D_n)₀. S sends weak equivalences to W, so it induces Cat_ω → 𝓗_CaTT. It has a formal left adjoint 𝓛_R(A) = ∫^P A(P) · R(P).
- **(2,2)-categories are presented** (Campbell, arXiv 1907.01999: 2-truncated (∞,2)-categories are bicategories; strictify).
- **Obstruction to the raw inductive nerve.** The coherent walking ω-equivalence E (Hadzihasanovic–Loubaton–Ozornova–Rovelli, arXiv 2404.14509, Thm 1.33) is a gaunt strict ω-category with E → 1 a folk weak equivalence, so no functor 𝓗_CaTT → Cat^ind_ω agrees with the raw inductive nerve on strict ω-categories. The Street nerve marking coinductively invertible cells does send E → 1 to an equivalence (Henry–Loubaton, arXiv 2301.11424, Prop. 4.58).
- **Free shapes agree.** Finite globular pasting shapes and their Gray products are the same free objects in the inductive and coinductive theories (Campion, arXiv 2311.00205, Cor. 3.5, Thm 3.14, Cor. 3.15).

**What round 2 established.**
- **S factors through coinductive completion.** Write Q : Cat^ind_ω ⇄ Cat^coind_ω : j for coinductive completion. By Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde (arXiv 2603.11005: Lemma 5.9, Prop. 5.29 and 5.30, Cor. 5.31), completion is the localisation at ∞-surjective maps, and its units are ∞-surjective. S₀ sends every ∞-surjective map to W, because for an 𝒜-fibration, π₀-surjectivity of the Kan fibration Map(yDₙ, UY) → Map(Bₙ, UY) ×_{Map(Bₙ,UZ)} Map(yDₙ, UZ) gives strict globular boundary lifts, which are FHM trivial fibrations. Hence S ≃ S̄ ∘ Q with S̄ = S j.
- **The underived left adjoint is not relative.** The coend 𝓛_R ⊣ S₀ is an ordinary adjunction. For gaunt strict C with discrete nerve NC (canonically algebraically fibrant), π₀ Map(𝓛_R A, NC) ≅ Mod(CaTT)(A, C). Applied to the coherent walking equivalence w : E → 1, this would make Mod(1, E) → Mod(E, E) a bijection if 𝓛_R(w) were an equivalence, which is false. So 𝓛_R does not send W to inductive equivalences.
- **S₀ preserves categorical level.** If Y presents an (∞,n)-category, every cell of S₀(Y) above dimension n is coinductively invertible. The proof extends the cell strictly over N(Σ^{k−1} I), then uses that FHM weak equivalences reflect invertibility.
- **A necessary test for reconstruction.** If the ordinary counit ε_Y : 𝓛_R S₀(Y) → Y is an equivalence, then 𝒜(Y, NC) → Mod(CaTT)(S₀Y, C) is bijective for every gaunt strict C. In particular, for contractible Y every strict CaTT map S₀(Y) → E must be constant.
- **Marked targets.** Marking the coinductive equivalences of C does not change C. Localising at them gives QC (ORW Prop. 5.29).
- **Directed version, conditional.** Suppose the coherator of L has a Gray-enriched cellular presentation, and suppose the hom maps K(Q_a, Q_b) → K(Q_a, Y_b) are trivial fibrations. Then lifting into the enriched category of commuting squares gives an enriched R_L that strictly preserves composition. Still to be supplied are arity colimits in the semantic category, ordered tensor domains D^r ⊗ D^s (for example via a coloured operad), and invertibility declarations.

**What round 3 established.**
- **The underived counit fails.** For every gaunt strict C with C → 1 a folk weak equivalence there is a contractible algebraically fibrant Y with S₀(Y) ≅ C (take Y = cosk₀ of the right Kan extension of C's reflexive globular set from the globes to Θ_ω, with fillers chosen to make NC → Y algebraic). For C = E this gives a non-constant S₀(Y) → E, so ε_Y : 𝓛_R S₀ Y → Y is not an equivalence. This is compatible with an equivalence 𝓗_CaTT ≃ Cat^coind_ω, since E ≃ 1 in 𝓗_CaTT and Q(NE) ≃ 1.
- **A conditional derived adjunction.** Let K be the computadic cofibrant-replacement comonad on Mod(CaTT) (Markakis, arXiv 2303.11978, Prop. 19, Cor. 21–22; its counit q and comultiplication are in W and K preserves and reflects W, Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara arXiv 2406.13240 Prop. 4.2.2). Assume

  ```
  (H)  f : B → B′ in W between computad-cofibrant models  ⇒  QU𝓛_R(f) is an equivalence.
  ```

  Then the co-Kleisli category of K (weak maps, Bourke–Garner) gives an adjunction Λ : 𝓗_CaTT ⇄ Cat^coind_ω : S̄ with Λ[A] ≃ QU𝓛_R(KA).
- **The reconstruction condition.** For Y presenting jC, the derived counit is QU(d_Y), with d_Y = ε_Y ∘ 𝓛_R(q_{S₀Y}). Since a map into a coinductively complete target is inverted by Q iff it is ∞-surjective, the counit condition is

  ```
  (R)  U𝓛_R K S₀ Y → UY is ∞-surjective.
  ```

- **The equivalence conjecture.** (H), (R) for every coinductively complete C, and the unit condition "A ← KA → S₀𝓛_R KA is an equivalence in 𝓗_CaTT" (for instance η_{KA} ∈ W) together imply 𝓗_CaTT ≃ Cat^coind_ω.
- **A correction.** Full faithfulness of S̄ restricted to a full subcategory 𝓑 implies that the counits at 𝓑 are equivalences only if Λ S̄(𝓑) ⊆ 𝓑.
- **Directed tensor arities.** For L, binary global operations through global dimension 3 have hom tensor domains D⁰ ⊗ D⁰, D¹ ⊗ D⁰, D⁰ ⊗ D¹, D² ⊗ D⁰, D⁰ ⊗ D² and D¹ ⊗ D¹. The last gives the directed interchanger. For α : f₀ ⇒ f₁ : x → y and β : g₀ ⇒ g₁ : y → z, in execution order, it goes (f₀◁β) ; (α▷g₁) ⇛ (α▷g₀) ; (f₁◁β): β first, then α, to α first, then β. In global dimension 4, the interchanger's naturality and the cube of three interchangers are 4-cells in an untruncated theory but equalities in a strict 3-truncation.

**What round 4 established.**
- **Units on representables and graphs.** α_G : F_w G → F_s G is a globular trivial fibration for every globular set G, since B → T is cartesian and contractible. Hence η_{h_P} ∈ W for every coherator representable h_P = F_w G_P, and η_{F_w G} ∈ W for every graph G. The graph case uses 𝓛_R F_w G ≅ F|G| ≃ N Path(G).
- **Detection.** For f : X → Y in 𝒜, S₀(f) ∈ W ⇒ QU(f) is an equivalence. Algebraic identities and composites agree with geometric ones relative to boundaries, so algebraic invertibility gives geometric coinductive invertibility (ORW Prop. 5.42), and Q inverts the resulting maps (ORW Prop. 5.30).
- **One assertion suffices.** Let (U) be the assertion η_B ∈ W for every computad-cofibrant B. Then (U) implies (H), (R) and 𝓗_CaTT ≃ Cat^coind_ω. For (R), S₀(d_Y)η_{KA} = q_A. Moreover (U) reduces to
  ```
  (11)  η_{A_𝔠} ∈ W for every finite computad 𝔠,
  ```
  by filtered colimits of finite subcomputads (the algebraic colimit is W-equivalent to S₀ of the 𝒜-colimit; FHM Prop. 3.4.4). (11) is an infinite family.
- **The (∞,1) fragment.** Free models on computads with bounded generator dimensions and invertible cells above dimension 1 are free on graphs, so (H) holds between them. (R) for general Kan complexes or quasi-categories is not obtained: finite subcomputads of K S₀ Y can omit the inverse witnesses.
- **L, ordinary presentation.** The coherator of L is the colimit of free globular extensions, adjoining each admissible head once. Through global dimension 3 the generators are classified by active hom-dimension d:
  - r = 1: operation heads with d = 0;
  - r = 2: operation heads with d = 1, and coherence heads with d = 0;
  - r = 3: operation heads with d = 2, either one factor of dimension 2 or two of dimension 1 (the interchangers), and coherence heads with d ≤ 1.

  Whole-grid interchangers are separate generators. Composites of elementary ones are derived, and the two are linked by dimension-4 coherence heads when formally parallel. In dimension 4, the operation profiles are (3), (2,1), (1,2) and (1,1,1), the last being the three-interchanger cube. No Gray-enriched presentation was given.

**The coh rule of L (two-tier).** Contexts are CaTT pasting contexts. Each decomposes uniquely as Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ, with objects a₀, …, aₙ and Θᵢ the pasting scheme in the hom from aᵢ₋₁ to aᵢ.
- **Gray unfolding.** U_{ij}(Γ) := Θᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ Θⱼ (factors in execution order) is the Steiner tensor of the Steiner complexes λ(Θ). Its generators are tensors of generators, dimensions add, and the boundary is the Leibniz formula. It is strongly loop-free, so its cells are determined by their supports.
- **Supports.** For a term t : A with endpoints aᵢ, aⱼ, supp(t : A) is the downward-closed set of generators of U_{ij}(Γ) used by the strict image of t and of the cells in its type, computed in the free strict Gray-enriched ω-category on Γ.
- **The rule.** coh_{Γ, t →_A u}[σ] is well formed when either
  - (operation) supp(t : A) = ∂⁻U_{ij}(Γ), supp(u : A) = ∂⁺U_{ij}(Γ), and dim A = dim U_{ij}(Γ) (so the cell has dimension dim U_{ij}(Γ) + 1); or
  - (coherence) supp(t : A) = U_{ij}(Γ) = supp(u : A).

  **Here (i, j) = (0, n) is required** (round 4 showed that proper intervals let a 1-head over ΣD⁰ ∨ ΣD^{m−1} depend formally on an m-dimensional argument, destroying the filtration by output dimension).
- **Consequences.**
  - On a single hom (n = 1) this is CaTT.
  - For Γ = ΣD¹ ∨ ΣD¹ there is no horizontal composite of α and β. There is an operation-case 3-cell (f◁β) ; (α▷g′) ⇛ (α▷g) ; (f′◁β), and none in the reverse direction.
  - The intended strict model is ∞-Cat_lax (strict ω-categories with lax transformations), where the rule is sound.
  - "Two-tier" means interchange is invertible inside a hom and directed across objects.

## Questions

**Q1 (the unit on finite computads, smallest open case).** Prove η_{A_𝔠} ∈ W for a finite computad 𝔠 that is not free on a globular set or a graph. The smallest such case is a single 2-generator α : f ∘ g ⇒ h between composite 1-terms. More generally, does S₀𝓛_R send a generator attachment (a pushout of F_w(∂Dₙ) → F_w(Dₙ) along parallel terms) to a W-equivalence onto the corresponding homotopy pushout? Such an excision statement, with (1), would give (11) by induction on the number of generators.

**Q2 ((R) directly for spaces).** For Y the nerve of a Kan complex, is U𝓛_R K S₀ Y → UY ∞-surjective? Prove it directly, without (U). This is a statement about cells being lifted up to invertible cells, which may be accessible because all cells of S₀Y are invertible.

**Q3 (saturation).** Does it suffice to prove the unit statements only up to the saturation of W, that is, in 𝓗_CaTT? Is W saturated? Does the reduction (9) hold for the saturation?

**Q4 (the enriched coherator of L).** With (i, j) = (0, n) required, give a Gray-enriched (or coloured-operadic) cellular presentation of L's coherator through global dimension 3. It should have:
- Gray-enriched homs between arities;
- enriched attaching maps, with the whole-grid interchangers either as generators or as enriched composites;
- tensor-arity colimits in the semantic category.

Then apply the round-2 lifting theorem to obtain R_L, or identify the step that fails.

## What would help most

An excision lemma for Q1; a direct proof of Q2; the enriched presentation for Q4 in low dimensions.

# Researcher Response

Taking the results of rounds 1–4 as hypotheses, I obtain the following partial results.

- **Q1:** The realization of the triangular computad is equivalent to its free strict \(2\)-category. Its unit is therefore in \(W\) exactly when a specific strictification map is in \(W\). That strictification map has globular boundary lifting through dimension \(3\). I do not establish the remaining higher-dimensional lifting.
- **Q2:** For a space \(X\) presented by \(Y\), \(QU\mathcal L_RKS_0Y\) is itself a space. The proposed reconstruction map induces a bijection on \(\pi_0\), isomorphisms on \(\pi_1\), and surjections on every homotopy group. Higher injectivity remains unproved.
- **Q3:** Units in \(W^{\mathrm{sat}}\) give an equivalence with a precisely specified further localization of the geometric theory. Identifying that localization with \(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{coind}}_\omega\) requires an additional detection statement. Neither saturation nor the finite-computad reduction for saturation follows formally from the closure properties listed in the question.
- **Q4:** Gepner–Heine’s suspension-and-wedge construction supplies the semantic tensor arities. The enriched attaching maps remain missing. There is also an obstruction to a strict Gray enrichment that identifies its cells with the raw syntax.

## 1. Q1: an exact calculation for the triangular computad

Fix a chosen binary composition term \(m\), with \(m(f,g)\) denoting execution of \(f\) followed by \(g\). Let \(A_\triangle\) be freely generated by
\[
x\xrightarrow{f}y\xrightarrow{g}z,\qquad
h:x\to z,\qquad
\alpha:m(f,g)\Rightarrow h.
\]

Let \(P=[2]\) be the globular pasting shape consisting of \(f,g\). There is a pushout of CaTT models
\[
A_\triangle
\cong
h_P\amalg_{h_{D_1}}h_{D_2},
\tag{1}
\]
where the first map selects \(m(f,g)\), and the second is the source-face inclusion. The latter map freely adjoins the target \(1\)-cell and then the \(2\)-cell, so it is a computadic cofibration.

Define the strict \(2\)-category \(T_\triangle\) by
\[
T_\triangle(x,y)=\{f\},\qquad
T_\triangle(y,z)=\{g\},\qquad
T_\triangle(x,z)=
\bigl(gf\xrightarrow{\alpha}h\bigr).
\]
Its endomorphism categories are terminal, its reverse hom categories are empty, and composition sends \((f,g)\) to \(gf\). Write
\[
\pi:A_\triangle\longrightarrow T_\triangle
\]
for the canonical strictification map.

### 1.1 The geometric realization is \(NT_\triangle\)

There is an actual pushout of discrete \(\Theta_\omega\)-presheaves
\[
N_\Theta T_\triangle
\cong
yP\amalg_{yD_1}yD_2.
\tag{2}
\]

Here is the pointwise verification. A \(\Theta\)-shape has the form
\[
[m](\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_m).
\]
A strict functor from it to \(T_\triangle\) gives a nondecreasing sequence of \(0\)-cells in \(\{x,y,z\}\).

- If that sequence uses \(y\), no adjacent pair can map directly from \(x\) to \(z\). Every adjacent hom therefore maps into the subcategory \(P\). Strict composition forces the whole functor to factor through \(P\).
- If the sequence omits \(y\), the functor factors through the full subcategory on \(\{x,z\}\), which is \(D_2\).
- The intersection of these two subcategories is the walking arrow \(gf\), namely \(D_1\).

Thus every simplex belongs to one of the two subpresheaves, with precisely the intersection asserted in (2).

Applying \(\mathcal L_R\) to (1) gives
\[
\mathcal L_R A_\triangle
\cong
RP\amalg_{RD_1}RD_2.
\tag{3}
\]
This computes a homotopy pushout: the three \(R\)-values are cofibrant, and \(RD_1\to RD_2\) is a cofibration. The compatible comparisons \(q_P:RP\to yP\) from the construction of \(R\) compare its defining span with the span in (2). The latter span consists of monomorphisms and also computes a homotopy pushout.

Consequently the canonical algebraic map
\[
c_\triangle:\mathcal L_R A_\triangle\longrightarrow N_\Theta T_\triangle
\]
is a weak equivalence.

Compatibility with the adjunction gives
\[
S_0(c_\triangle)\,\eta_{A_\triangle}=\pi.
\]
Since \(S_0(c_\triangle)\in W\), two-out-of-three yields the exact reduction
\[
\boxed{\quad
\eta_{A_\triangle}\in W
\iff
\pi:A_\triangle\to T_\triangle\in W.
\quad}
\tag{4}
\]

Thus the geometric pushout in this example is determined. The remaining issue is the algebraic strictification map.

### 1.2 Strictification has boundary lifting through dimension \(3\)

There is a further calculation of \(\pi\).

All cells of \(A_\triangle\) above dimension \(2\) are coinductively invertible: the computad has no generators in those dimensions, so Benjamin–Markakis’s support criterion applies. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12127?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Form \(\tau^{\mathrm{alg}}_2A_\triangle\) by retaining its \(0\)- and \(1\)-cells and quotienting its \(2\)-cells by
\[
a\sim b
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{there exists a \(3\)-cell }a\Rightarrow b.
\]
This is an equivalence relation because identities, inverses and composition exist for the \(3\)-cells. It is also compatible with the \(2\)-dimensional operations: changing a \(2\)-input along a \(3\)-cell extends the corresponding pasting arity by a \(3\)-cell, and operadic contraction supplies the comparison between the two outputs.

This quotient is the reflection into \(2\)-dimensional CaTT models. Indeed, every map to such a model identifies the source and target of every \(3\)-cell, and hence factors uniquely through the quotient.

For ordinary CaTT, the \(1\)-operation operad is the free planar-tree operad: there is one generating \(n\)-ary composition head for each \(n\geq0\). After quotienting \(2\)-operations by their \(3\)-dimensional comparisons, there is exactly one \(2\)-operation for each arity and pair of parallel \(1\)-operations. This is the operad described in Leinster’s proof of Theorem 4.8.3, whose algebras are unbiased bicategories; the relevant truncation comparison is Theorem 4.7.2. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0011106?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

It follows from freeness that \(\tau^{\mathrm{alg}}_2A_\triangle\) is the free unbiased bicategory on the displayed triangle. Concretely:

- its \(1\)-cells are formal composition trees in \(f,g,h\);
- for parallel \(1\)-terms \(u,v\), its \(2\)-cells are the \(2\)-cells
  \[
  \operatorname{flat}(u)\Rightarrow\operatorname{flat}(v)
  \]
  in \(T_\triangle\).

The corresponding description for free bicategories on \(2\)-computads is Fairbanks–Shulman, Corollary 5.7. The unbiased version uses trees with arbitrary finite arities instead of only binary and nullary nodes; the coherence argument is unchanged. [Springer Nature Link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10485-026-09863-1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Therefore
\[
\tau^{\mathrm{alg}}_2A_\triangle\longrightarrow T_\triangle
\]
is a biequivalence, locally fully faithful on \(2\)-cells. More explicitly, \(\pi\) has the right lifting property against globular boundary inclusions in dimensions \(0,1,2,3\):

- dimensions \(0,1\): every required vertex or path has a syntactic representative;
- dimension \(2\): the displayed description supplies a representative of every required \(2\)-cell;
- dimension \(3\): two parallel \(2\)-cells with equal strict images determine the same quotient class, so an invertible \(3\)-cell joins them.

Since \(T_\triangle\) has only identity cells above dimension \(2\), the remaining condition is exactly
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{For every }r\geq3\text{ and every parallel pair }
a,b\in(A_\triangle)_r,\\
\text{there exists an }(r+1)\text{-cell }a\Rightarrow b.
\end{gathered}}
\tag{5}
\]

I do not prove (5). Invertibility of all cells above dimension \(2\) does not imply the existence of cells between every parallel pair.

This locates the first missing test at **parallel \(3\)-cells and their \(4\)-cell fillers**. The fact that realization sends the attachment to a homotopy pushout does not establish that test, and hence does not yet establish the requested excision lemma.

## 2. Q2: direct reconstruction results for spaces

Let
\[
B=KS_0Y,\qquad q:B\to S_0Y,
\]
and put
\[
X=QUY,\qquad Z=QU\mathcal L_RB.
\]
Assume \(X\) is a space. Let
\[
\bar d:Z\longrightarrow X
\]
be induced by
\[
d=\varepsilon_Y\,\mathcal L_R(q).
\]

The following can be proved without the general unit assertion:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&Z\text{ is a space};\\
&\pi_0(\bar d)\text{ is bijective};\\
&\pi_1(\bar d)\text{ is an isomorphism at every basepoint};\\
&\pi_n(\bar d)\text{ is surjective for every }n\geq1.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{6}
\]

### 2.1 Why \(Z\) is a space

Every positive-dimensional cell of \(S_0Y\) is invertible. Since \(q\in W\) reflects invertibility, the same holds in \(B\). The reflection property is FHM Proposition 3.1.7. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13240?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

By the semantic compatibility established in round 4, interpreted cells of \(B\) become invertible after applying \(Q\). In particular, the top cell and every positive-dimensional face of each characteristic generator globe
\[
D_n\longrightarrow Z
\]
are invertible.

Now use the core inclusion
\[
i:Z^\simeq\longrightarrow Z.
\]
This is a monomorphism in the ambient \(\infty\)-category. One way to verify this is in complete Segal \(\Theta\)-spaces: completeness identifies the identity map from the preceding globe space with the union of components consisting of equivalence cells. Iterating gives the core inclusion on every globe, and monomorphisms are detected on globe evaluations. The relevant statements are Rezk §11.14 and Corollary 11.17, and Loubaton §4.2.1.55. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.3602?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Thus a globe map all of whose positive-dimensional cells are invertible has a contractible space of factorizations through \(i\).

Apply this to the accepted homotopy cell presentation of \(Z\). For a map \(V\to Z\), write
\[
\operatorname{Lift}(V)
=
\operatorname{Map}_{/Z}(V,Z^\simeq).
\]
Every such space is empty or contractible. At a cell attachment
\[
V'=V\amalg_{\partial D_n}D_n
\]
we have
\[
\operatorname{Lift}(V')
\simeq
\operatorname{Lift}(V)
\times^h_{\operatorname{Lift}(\partial D_n)}
\operatorname{Lift}(D_n).
\]
The first and last spaces are contractible by induction and by invertibility of the characteristic globe. Their restrictions make the middle space nonempty, hence contractible. At limit stages, the lift space is a limit of contractible spaces.

Consequently \(\mathrm{id}_Z\) factors through \(i\). A monomorphism with a section is an equivalence, so \(Z^\simeq\simeq Z\).

This argument uses only characteristic generator globes. It does not require every geometric cell to have an algebraic name.

### 2.2 The resulting CW presentation

Groupoidification preserves colimits. It sends \(D_n\) to a contractible space and \(\partial D_n\) to \(S^{n-1}\), the latter by the recursive description
\[
\partial D_n=D_{n-1}\amalg_{\partial D_{n-1}}D_{n-1}.
\]
Therefore the computadic filtration gives a CW model for \(Z\), with one \(n\)-cell for each \(n\)-generator of the computad underlying \(B\).

In particular, every based loop in \(Z\) is homotopic to a finite word of generator edges and reversed generator edges. This is the ordinary cellular approximation argument; see Hatcher, Theorem 4.8 and Proposition 1.26. [pi.math.cornell.edu](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATch4.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 2.3 Surjectivity on every homotopy group

Choose \(x\in B_0\). A class
\[
u\in\pi_n(X,qx)
\]
can be represented by a geometric \(n\)-cell with the prescribed iterated identity boundary at \(qx\), relative to a chosen identity \(n\)-cell.

The algebraic identity tower at \(x\) supplies a strict lift of this boundary. Since \(q\) is a globular trivial fibration, the representative lifts to an algebraic \(n\)-cell of \(B\) with that exact boundary. Interpret this cell through \(\eta_B\), and glue it to the interpreted reference identity cell along their common boundary. This gives a based sphere in \(Z\).

The identity
\[
S_0(d)\,\eta_B=q
\tag{7}
\]
shows that its image represents \(u\). This proves surjectivity on \(\pi_n\) for every \(n\geq1\). The argument uses compatibility of algebraic identities with geometric identities relative to boundaries; it does not require a chosen algebraic identity to equal a simplicial degeneracy strictly.

Surjectivity on \(\pi_0\) follows from \(q_0\). If two source vertices have images in the same component, lift a geometric \(1\)-cell between their images through \(q\). Its interpretation joins those vertices in \(Z\), proving injectivity on \(\pi_0\).

### 2.4 Injectivity on \(\pi_1\)

Let a based loop \(\gamma\) in \(Z\) map to a nullhomotopic loop in \(X\). By the CW presentation, represent \(\gamma\) by a word of generator edges and their reversals.

Replace each reversed edge by the interpretation of an algebraic inverse in \(B\). The inverse witnesses give the requisite path homotopies. Compose the resulting algebraic \(1\)-cells. Compatibility of interpretation with composition shows that \(\gamma\) is represented by one algebraic cell
\[
a:x\to x.
\]

Its image \(q(a)\) is nullhomotopic, so there is a geometric \(2\)-cell from \(q(a)\) to the chosen algebraic identity at \(qx\). Lift this through \(q\), with prescribed boundary \(a,1_x\). Interpreting the lift gives a nullhomotopy of \(\gamma\).

### 2.5 The precise remaining statement

The same proof would establish higher injectivity if one proved:

> **Spherical representation condition.** Every class of
> \[
> \pi_n(QU\mathcal L_RB,x)
> \]
> for \(n\geq2\) is represented by the interpretation of an algebraic \(n\)-cell with iterated identity boundary.

Only the cases \(B=KS_0Y\) are needed for Q2. Given this condition, a target nullhomotopy lifts through \(q\) as an \((n+1)\)-cell, exactly as above.

Ordinary cellular approximation does not prove this condition. It moves a sphere into the \(n\)-skeleton; it does not express an arbitrary spherical cellular diagram as one algebraic globular cell.

Cisinski’s reconstruction theorem is relevant but does not directly fill this gap. His Theorem 4.4 uses the realization and weak equivalences associated with Berger’s simplicial algebra model. Applying it here requires a comparison of that derived realization with \(QU\mathcal L_R\), and a proof that the chosen \(K\)-replacement is a weak equivalence for that model. Those comparisons are not supplied by the globular trivial-fibration property of \(q\). [math.univ-toulouse.fr](https://www.math.univ-toulouse.fr/~dcisinsk/highergr.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Thus (6) does not yet imply (R), even when \(X\) is a \(1\)-type: higher homotopy groups of the source could remain.

## 3. Q3: saturated units and the finite-computad reduction

Write
\[
\mathcal D=\operatorname{Mod}(\mathrm{CaTT}),\qquad
\mathcal H=\mathcal D[W^{-1}],
\]
and let \(W^{\mathrm{sat}}\) be the class of actual morphisms whose images are equivalences in \(\mathcal H\).

### 3.1 What saturated units do imply

Assume
\[
\eta_B\in W^{\mathrm{sat}}
\qquad
\text{for every computad-cofibrant }B.
\tag{8}
\]
Define
\[
V=\{f\in\operatorname{Mor}(\mathcal A)\mid S_0f\in W^{\mathrm{sat}}\},
\qquad
T=\mathcal L_RK.
\]

Then there is an equivalence
\[
\boxed{\mathcal H\simeq\mathcal A[V^{-1}].}
\tag{9}
\]

This follows formally, without a derived-adjunction theorem.

For \(f:X\to X'\) in \(W\), naturality gives
\[
S_0(Tf)\,\eta_{KX}
=
\eta_{KX'}\,Kf.
\]
The units and \(Kf\) belong to \(W^{\mathrm{sat}}\), so \(Tf\in V\). Thus \(T\) and \(S_0\) descend to the indicated localizations.

The natural spans
\[
A\xleftarrow{q_A}KA\xrightarrow{\eta_{KA}}S_0TA
\]
become natural equivalences. In the other direction, put
\[
\beta_Y
=
\varepsilon_Y\,\mathcal L_R(q_{S_0Y})
:
TS_0Y\longrightarrow Y.
\]
Equation (7), with the appropriate indices, gives
\[
S_0(\beta_Y)\,\eta_{KS_0Y}=q_{S_0Y},
\]
so \(\beta_Y\in V\). These two natural equivalences prove (9).

Let
\[
V_{\mathrm{geom}}
=
\{f\mid QUf\text{ is an equivalence}\}.
\]
The established factorization through \(Q\) implies
\[
V_{\mathrm{geom}}\subseteq V.
\]
Hence (9) identifies \(\mathcal H\) with a **further localization** of \(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{coind}}_\omega\).

To recover exactly the intended target, the additional assertion needed is
\[
\boxed{
S_0f\in W^{\mathrm{sat}}
\ \Longrightarrow\
QUf\text{ is an equivalence}.
}
\tag{10}
\]
This is saturation-level detection. Round 4 supplies the implication with \(W\), which does not by itself establish (10).

Thus saturated units suffice **together with (10)**. By themselves they establish (9).

### 3.2 Saturation of the FHM class is not established here

I could not verify a theorem asserting \(W=W^{\mathrm{sat}}\) for the FHM class. Its two-out-of-six, retract and filtered-colimit closure properties do not formally imply saturation.

A precise additional condition would suffice:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Pushouts of maps in }\operatorname{Cof}\cap W
\text{ remain in }W.
}
\tag{11}
\]
Indeed, use the computadic weak factorization system
\[
(\operatorname{Cof},\operatorname{TF}).
\]
Every \(w\in W\) factors functorially as \(w=pi\), where \(p\in\operatorname{TF}\subseteq W\); two-out-of-three gives \(i\in\operatorname{Cof}\cap W\). Trivial fibrations are pullback-stable. Condition (11) supplies pushout stability of the other factor.

These are the three-arrow-calculus conditions of Barwick–Kan §2.1(c). Together with two-out-of-six they imply saturation, by their §3.1. A full model structure is unnecessary for this argument. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.2512?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### 3.3 Why the formal closure arguments fail

Here is an explicit counterexample to deriving saturation from the listed closure properties.

Take the poset generated by
\[
A\xrightarrow f B\xrightarrow g C,\qquad
B'\xrightarrow p B,\qquad
B'\xrightarrow k C'\xrightarrow q C,\qquad
C'\xrightarrow h D,
\]
and adjoin a bottom and a top. In particular,
\[
gp=qk.
\]
Let
\[
W_{\mathcal P}
=
\{\text{identities}\}\cup\{gf,p,q,hk\}.
\]

This eight-element poset is a complete lattice. The class \(W_{\mathcal P}\) has two-out-of-three and two-out-of-six: nondegenerate triangles have at most one \(W_{\mathcal P}\)-edge, and the only nonidentity \(W_{\mathcal P}\)-arrows with nontrivial factorizations are \(gf\) and \(hk\); these do not give overlapping \(W_{\mathcal P}\)-composites in a three-arrow chain. Retract closure is automatic in a poset. Filtered-colimit closure holds because every filtered diagram in its finite arrow poset has an attained greatest value.

Nevertheless \(g\notin W_{\mathcal P}\) becomes invertible:

- \(gf\) invertible gives \(g\) a right inverse;
- \(hk\) invertible gives \(k\) a left inverse;
- \(g=qkp^{-1}\) therefore has a left inverse.

One may also take \(K=\mathrm{Id}\), arising from the weak factorization system \((\text{all maps},\text{isomorphisms})\). Thus the stated comonad properties do not repair the implication.

There is a corresponding counterexample to filtered-colimit closure of the saturation. Take copies of this six-element diagram indexed by \(n\in\mathbb N\), add
\[
B_n\leq B_{n+1},\qquad C_n\leq C_{n+1},
\]
and add
\[
B_\infty=\sup_nB_n,\qquad
C_\infty=\sup_nC_n,\qquad
B_\infty\leq C_\infty,
\]
together with a global bottom and top. Use only the order relations generated by these requirements. This is an algebraic complete lattice: all elements except \(B_\infty,C_\infty\) are compact, and those two are the displayed directed suprema.

Let \(W_\infty\) consist of identities and
\[
A_n\to C_n,\quad
B'_n\to B_n,\quad
C'_n\to C_n,\quad
B'_n\to D_n
\]
for all \(n\). It again satisfies two-out-of-three, two-out-of-six and retract closure. It is filtered-colimit closed: the only comparisons between distinct nonidentity \(W_\infty\)-arrows are
\[
(B'_n\to B_n)\leq(C'_n\to C_n).
\]
A filtered diagram of \(W_\infty\)-arrows is therefore cofinally constant at a nonidentity arrow, or cofinally a diagram of identities.

Each \(g_n:B_n\to C_n\) belongs to \(W_\infty^{\mathrm{sat}}\), by the preceding argument. Their colimit does not. Define a monotone functor to \([1]\) by sending \(C_\infty\) and the top to \(1\), and everything else to \(0\). It inverts \(W_\infty\), but sends
\[
g_\infty:B_\infty\to C_\infty
\]
to the noninvertible arrow \(0\to1\). Hence
\[
g_\infty\notin W_\infty^{\mathrm{sat}}.
\]

Every arrow in a poset is a monomorphism. Thus the failure persists for filtered diagrams of monomorphisms, even in a locally finitely presentable category.

These examples refute the formal implications, not saturation of the specific FHM class.

### 3.4 Consequence for finite computads

FHM Proposition 3.4.4 proves filtered-colimit closure for \(W\), which is what the round-4 finite-subcomputad argument uses. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13240?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Replacing the finite unit statements by membership in \(W^{\mathrm{sat}}\) requires a new input: closure of \(W^{\mathrm{sat}}\) under the particular filtered colimits used there, or preservation of those colimits by the localization \(\mathcal D\to\mathcal H\). The preceding example shows why that input cannot be omitted.

## 4. Q4: semantic tensor arities exist; the enriched attachments are the missing step

### 4.1 The required semantic arity colimits

Let \(\mathcal V\) be the chosen presentably monoidal category of higher categories with its Gray tensor, using the question’s execution-order convention.

For \(A\in\mathcal V\), its enriched suspension \(\Sigma A\) has two \(0\)-cells, hom \(A\) from the first to the second, unit endomorphism homs, and initial reverse hom.

For
\[
\Gamma=\Sigma\Theta_1\vee\cdots\vee\Sigma\Theta_n,
\]
form the enriched wedge
\[
\mathscr G(\Gamma)
=
\Sigma|\Theta_1|
\amalg_{\{a_1\}}
\Sigma|\Theta_2|
\amalg_{\{a_2\}}\cdots
\amalg_{\{a_{n-1}\}}
\Sigma|\Theta_n|.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr G(\Gamma)(a_i,a_j)
\simeq
|\Theta_{i+1}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|\Theta_j|
\qquad(i<j).
}
\tag{12}
\]
This is exactly Gepner–Heine, Corollaries 2.2.6 and 2.2.8, with their wedge defined in Definition 2.2.7. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10504?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Thus the semantic tensor arities have an explicit colimit construction. For example, the boundary-to-tensor map for two globes is
\[
(\partial D^r\otimes D^s)
\amalg_{\partial D^r\otimes\partial D^s}
(D^r\otimes\partial D^s)
\longrightarrow D^r\otimes D^s.
\tag{13}
\]
The construction uses separate cocontinuity of the tensor; Campion supplies the relevant monoidal structures and their transfer to the stated higher-categorical targets. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00205?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The full-interval rule therefore has a precise semantic arity interpretation. This does not yet specify enriched homs between the syntactic arities.

### 4.2 Why an ordinary head is not an enriched cell attachment

An ordinary coherence head
\[
h:D_r\longrightarrow\Gamma
\]
is an ordinary arrow of the coherator. In an enriched coherator it would initially be a **\(0\)-cell of**
\[
\mathbb T_L(D_r,\Gamma).
\]
It is not automatically an \(r\)-cell of
\[
\mathbb T_L(D_0,\Gamma).
\]

To attach it in the second sense, one needs a cell-representation statement
\[
\mathbb T_L(D_r,\Gamma)_0
\cong
\bigl(\mathbb T_L(D_0,\Gamma)\bigr)_r,
\tag{14}
\]
natural in \(\Gamma\) and compatible with faces.

Attaching a \(0\)-cell to the first enriched hom merely creates an ordinary arrow. It does not impose its required equations
\[
h\sigma=t,\qquad h\tau=u.
\]
Attaching an \(r\)-cell to the second hom supplies the desired boundary data, but only becomes the original syntactic head after establishing (14).

That is the specific step not supplied by the ordinary cellular presentation.

### 4.3 An obstruction to the direct strict enrichment

Suppose one requires:

1. enrichment in **strict** \(\omega\)-categories with the Gray tensor;
2. underlying ordinary category equal to the raw coherator of \(L\);
3. the natural, boundary-compatible identifications (14).

These requirements are inconsistent already in dimension \(1\).

Let
\[
P_2=D_1\amalg_{D_0}D_1
\]
be the arity of a composable pair. Strict composition in
\(\mathbb T_L(D_0,\Gamma)\), natural in \(\Gamma\), would give by Yoneda a term
\[
c:D_1\longrightarrow P_2.
\]
Likewise strict identities would give
\[
i:D_1\longrightarrow D_0.
\]

The generic term \(c(f,g):x_0\to x_2\) cannot be a variable: neither variable arrow in \(P_2\) has those endpoints. It therefore has a `coh` head.

Strict right unitality would require
\[
c(f,i(y))=f
\tag{15}
\]
as raw terms. But substitution preserves the outer `coh` head on the left, while the right side is a variable. CaTT has no term equation identifying these expressions; changing the support condition to the stated rule of \(L\) does not add one. The absence of equations between CaTT operations is explicit in Benjamin–Markakis–Sarti’s description of the syntax. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.00398?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Thus this strict cell-representing enrichment cannot be the raw theory. Weak enrichment in Campion’s monoidal \(\infty\)-category remains a separate possibility; the argument does not rule it out.

### 4.4 Whole-grid interchangers impose another compatibility requirement

In a strict Gray-enriched category, the structural interchanger for a composite is a prescribed composite of structural interchangers. Schematically,
\[
\chi(\alpha_1;\alpha_2,\beta)
=
\operatorname{paste}
\bigl(\chi(\alpha_1,\beta),\chi(\alpha_2,\beta)\bigr),
\tag{16}
\]
with the required whiskerings. These are equations in the strict theory; Morehouse’s Definition 2.4, especially (2.15)–(2.18), states the composite and naturality laws. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.08783?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Your raw whole-grid head is a separate generator. Identifying it with the structural interchanger on the left of (16) would impose an equation with the elementary composite on the right. The raw untruncated theory instead retains both \(3\)-terms and connects formally parallel full-support terms by a \(4\)-cell.

A weak enriched presentation must retain that distinction. It cannot simply declare every whole-grid head to be the structural Gray interchanger and inherit the strict equations.

### 4.5 What this leaves for the lifting theorem

The semantic arity colimits are supplied by (12)–(13). The remaining input is an enriched or coloured-operadic presentation that specifies:

- the enriched homs between arities;
- how syntactic cells correspond to cells in those homs;
- enriched attaching maps with the prescribed boundaries;
- the higher comparisons relating independent whole-grid heads to composites.

The ordinary head-by-head construction does not supply these data. The strict interpretation satisfying (14) is obstructed by (15). A weak enriched resolution could avoid that obstruction, but it would require a comparison with the raw theory and a proof that the comparison preserves the intended models.

Accordingly, the round-2 lifting theorem cannot yet be applied to obtain the requested \(R_L\): the missing hypothesis is the **enriched cellular source presentation**, not the existence of the semantic tensor-arity colimits.
