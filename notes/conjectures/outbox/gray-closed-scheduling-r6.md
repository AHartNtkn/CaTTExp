# Schedules in free Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-categories, round 6

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked. The smallest open case is Question 1.*

## Background

**Schedules.** Fix a finite poset P on labels {1, …, n}, with numerical order as the left/right order of incomparable labels. Let S(P) be the category with:
- objects: the linear extensions of P;
- generating arrows: swaps ji → ij of adjacent P-incomparable labels with i < j;
- equations: commuting squares for swaps at disjoint positions, and the braid hexagon for three pairwise incomparable labels.

Then S(P)(w, v) is a singleton if Inv_P(v) ⊆ Inv_P(w) and empty otherwise. Here Inv_P(w) is the set of pairs i < j, incomparable, with j before i in w. (Proof: move the first differing label left, then resolve the first swaps by a square or a hexagon and induct.)

**Gray monoids.** In the free strict Gray monoid (a monoid in 2-Cat for Gray's lax tensor) on a set of objects and generating 1-cells, fix the image in the free strict monoidal category, i.e. the string diagram. Then the hom-category between the 1-cells with that diagram is S(P). Here P is the diagram's dependency order, with left/right given by planarity. The 2-cells are whiskered interchangers (f ⊗ B′)∘(A ⊗ g) ⇒ (A′ ⊗ g)∘(f ⊗ B), "right factor first ⇒ left factor first".

**The ω-version.** The hom ω-category of the Steiner complex ν(I^⊗n), from bottom to top, is not thin: two hexagon 2-cells are joined by the 3-cell ***. Gray's 2-tensor is its intelligent 2-truncation (Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches*, A.25 and A.31). For a dependency poset P, the subcomplex K_P ⊆ I^⊗n spanned by the faces (order ideal J, S ⊆ min(P∖J)) has paths equal to the linear extensions of P. Its truncated hom is S(P).

## Setting: the biclosed version

Formulas are built from atoms with ⊗ and the residuals B\A and A/B, with no unit. 𝒢 is the free **Gray-monoidal biclosed strict 2-category** on a set of generating 1-cells fᵢ : Pᵢ → Qᵢ between formulas. Its structure:
- a strict 2-category with a Gray monoidal structure (lax interchangers, as above);
- closed structure: isomorphisms of hom-categories Φ : 𝒢(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ 𝒢(B, A\C), and the mirror one for /.
  - Φ is **strictly natural in the context variable B**.
  - Φ is natural in the **bound variable A only up to the interchanger**.
  - Strict naturality in A would force (f ⊗ B′)(A ⊗ g) = (A′ ⊗ g)(f ⊗ B), collapsing distinct schedules into one 1-cell. That is not wanted.

**Two warnings, both established.**
1. *Nesting is not invariant under naive normalization.* For a generator h : U → U with U = X\Y,

   ```
   h = Λ(ev) ∘ h = Λ(ev ∘ (1 ⊗ h))
   ```

   So h can be "ambient" or "inside a closure". β-reduction ev ∘ (1 ⊗ Λk) = k also changes nesting. Any invariant notion of "event inside a closure" must start from a βη-canonical form.
2. *Unit-free planar coherence.* In the free unit-free biclosed monoidal 1-category, a balanced sequent (each atom twice, opposite polarities) has at most one morphism, and exactly one when it is provable in the Lambek calculus with product. So the purely structural parts of 1-cells are unique.

## What is known (proved)

1. **A word invariant.** Sending each generator to its label gives an invariant W of 1-cell equality with values in words (composition order), with W(Λu) = W(u), W(ev) = ε and W(D ⊗ u) = W(u). It is realised by the one-object model whose 1-cells are words, with a unique 2-cell between words with the same multiset. So 1-cells with different generator order are distinct.
2. **Closure trees are not invariants of components.** With generators a : X → A and f : A ⊗ B → C, c = Λ_A(f), e = ev: the interchanger gives θ : s = e∘(a⊗U)∘(X⊗c) ⇒ t = e∘(A⊗c)∘(a⊗B), and t = f∘(a⊗B) by β. Every representative of s has a generator under currying (a Boolean biclosed poset rules out the needed structural prefix), while t is tensor-only. So any βη-normal form fixing tensor-only terms gives s and t different closure trees, though they are connected by a 2-cell. "Cross-level cells are generated by ordinary interchangers" does not imply "the canonical tree is invariant".
3. **A forward comparison.** For φ : P → C/B and b : X → B, the forward comparison α = e_B ∗ χ_{φ,b} : t₁ ⇒ t₂ exists, W distinguishes t₁, t₂, and Λ_X(t₂) = (C/b)∘φ by strict naturality in the context variable. Whether 𝒢(t₁, t₂) = {α} and 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ is open: the tensor-only schedule theorem does not control 2-cells built by currying, uncurrying and η-expansion.
4. **Mapping-space biclosedness is too weak for classifying 1-cells.** Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari (arXiv 2006.14495, Rem. 2.20): Map(X ⊗ Y, Z) ≇ Map(X, Fun^{opgr}(Y, Z)) in general; only cores agree.

5. **An integer model.** Let 𝓜 be the strict 2-category with:
   - objects the integers;
   - for q ≥ p, the hom 𝓜(p, q) equal to the poset (ℤ, ≤) of weights; otherwise empty;
   - composition adding weights;
   - w(p ⊗ u) = w(u) + p·δ(u) and w(u ⊗ q) = w(u), where δ(u) = target − source;
   - p\r = r − p and r/q = r − q, with left currying subtracting p·δ and right currying leaving the weight unchanged.

   𝓜 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category. An interchanger raises the weight by δ(f)·δ(g). A matrix-valued version (objects in ℤ^r) records ordered generator pairs.
6. **No reverse comparisons.** With distinct atoms, 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ and 𝒢(t, s) = ∅. For t₁ and t₂ the weights are 0 and 1, from |B| = 1, |C| = 2 and |P| = |X| = 0. For s and t they are −1 and 0. Every forward 2-cell between them uses **exactly one** interchanger whose two arguments each contain one generator. Every other interchanger in it has a generator-free argument.
7. **Undirected components.** π₀ 𝒢(A, C) ≅ 𝓑_Σ(A, C), the free ordinary unit-free biclosed monoidal category. Two 1-cells are connected by a zigzag of 2-cells iff their images there agree.
8. **The image and W do not classify 1-cells.** Take generators f : A → B and g : B → C with padding L, R, and let m = L ⊗ (g ∘ f) ⊗ R.
   - Currying g with the right padding gives u ⇒ m.
   - Currying g with the left padding gives m ⇒ v.
   - u, m and v have equal images in 𝓑_Σ, and W(u) = W(v).
   - Their weights (with |L| = |R| = |A| = 0, |B| = 1, |C| = 2) are −1, 0 and 1, so the three 1-cells are distinct.
   - The side of currying is data of the 1-cell, and schedules with only generator events cannot describe this component.

## Further observations (proved)

9. **Subtractive models cannot see interchangers with a structural argument.** Call a model *additive* if 1-cells carry a weight in an ordered abelian group, δ(u) = weight(target) − weight(source) is additive under composition and tensor, and an interchanger χ_{f,g} adds a bilinear term β(δ(f), δ(g)). If moreover the residuals are group subtraction (A\C = C − A, as in the model 𝓜 of item 5 and its matrix version), every structural 1-cell (ev, curried evaluations, units) has δ = 0, so every interchanger with a structural argument has weight 0. Such models cannot decide whether two 2-cells differing in those interchangers are equal. This blindness is specific to subtractive residuals; see item 13.
10. **The axioms do not make evaluation central.** For ev : A ⊗ (A\C) → C and a generator g : Y → Y′, compare (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y), related by χ_{ev,g}. Currying A on the left (using strict naturality in the context variable and the target) turns this into the same question for m_Y := Φ(ev ⊗ Y) and g, so currying only transports it. Likewise for χ_{ev,ev′} at independent positions. Unless centrality is imposed (as in Garner–Hirschowitz–Pardon's premonoidal construction), structural maps are non-central events in 𝒢. In the quotient 𝒢_c of 𝒢 in which every interchanger with a structural argument is an identity, Question 1 follows at once from item 6.

11. **Structural loops.** Take atoms A, B, C, D and U = A\(A⊗B), X = A⊗B, X′ = A⊗U, V = (C⊗D)/D, Y = C⊗D, Y′ = V⊗D. Let u = 1_A ⊗ Λ_A(1) : X → X′, e = ev : X′ → X, v = Λ^R_D(1) ⊗ 1_D : Y → Y′ and d = ev^R : Y′ → Y, so that e u = 1 and d v = 1 by β. Put T = X ⊗ Y and (composition rightmost first)

    ```
    H = (e ⊗ 1_Y)(1_{X′} ⊗ d)(u ⊗ 1_{Y′})(1_X ⊗ v) : T → T.
    ```

    - Whiskered interchangers give ι = (e⊗1) ∗ χ_{u,d} ∗ (1⊗v) : 1_T ⇒ H and π = [(e⊗1)(1⊗d)] ∗ χ_{u,v} : H ⇒ 1_T. The Gray axiom for χ_{u,dv} gives **π ∘ ι = 1**, so E = ι ∘ π is an idempotent on H.
    - **H^n are pairwise distinct.** In the model whose hom-categories are chaotic on the free group ⟨ℓ, r⟩ (objects integers, composition multiplication, left currying k ↦ ℓ^a k, right currying k ↦ r^a k), H ↦ ℓ⁻¹ r⁻¹ ℓ r.
    - All H^n have image 1_T in 𝓑_Σ, empty generator word and zero weight in every model of item 5. Since 2-cells H^m ⇒ 1_T ⇒ H^n exist, the category over 1_T is not isomorphic to any product of schedule categories.
    - This particular model does not satisfy strict equality of the two mixed curryings of (A\C)/B = A\(C/B). Item 14 removes that dependence.
    - **Forward comparisons.** Item 6's count factors each forward comparison as z₊ ∘ m ∘ z₋, with z_± using only interchangers with a generator-free argument and m a typed instance of the one generator–generator interchanger. Different typed instances of m must still be identified.
12. **Evaluation is not central in 𝒢.** In the same model with a third free generator γ for a generating 1-cell g, (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) ↦ ℓ⁻¹γ and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y) ↦ γℓ⁻¹, which are distinct. So χ_{ev,g} joins distinct 1-cells, and this uses only left closure. A complete invariant of 1-cells must schedule structural events (units, evaluations), not only generator events.

13. **A polarized model sees structural interchangers.** Let 𝓟 have objects (P, N) ∈ ℤ², A ⊗ B = A + B and A\C = C/A = C + swap(A), where swap(P, N) = (N, P). A 1-cell X → Y exists iff (P − N)(X) = (P − N)(Y), and it is an integer weight w. Put d(u) = P(source) − P(target).
    - Composition adds weights, w(A ⊗ u) = w(u) + P(A)·d(u) and w(u ⊗ B) = w(u).
    - Left currying is w ↦ w − P(A)·d(u), and right currying keeps the weight.
    - The homs are codiscrete.
    - 𝓟 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category in the sense above.
    - **ev is not central.** Here d(ev_A) = P(A) + N(A) ≠ 0. With A ↦ (1, 0), C ↦ (0, 0), X ↦ (1, 1), X′ ↦ (0, 0) and g : X → X′, the 1-cells (X′ ⊗ ev) ∘ (g ⊗ A ⊗ A\C) and (g ⊗ C) ∘ (X ⊗ ev) have weights 1 and 2. The same separates the two orders of two independent evaluations, with no generator involved.
    - Together with item 12, the central quotient 𝒢_c of item 10 is a proper quotient of 𝒢.

14. **Strict mixed currying does not help, and left closure alone suffices.**
    - *The model 𝒟.* Keep the chaotic free-group Gray monoid of item 11. Choose any k_c ∈ ⟨ℓ, r⟩ for c ∈ ℤ, put h_{a,c} = k_{c−a} k_c⁻¹, and let both curryings with bound object a and codomain c be f ↦ h_{a,c} f.
    - *Properties.* The multipliers telescope, so the two mixed curryings are equal, R_b R_a = R_{a+b}, and h_{0,c} = 1.
    - *Separation.* With |A| = |B| = 1, |C| = |D| = 2, k₁ = ℓ, k₄ = r⁻¹ and every other k_c = 1, H ↦ ℓ⁻¹r⁻¹ℓr. So H ≠ 1_T and the H^n are pairwise distinct even in the theory 𝒢_mix that imposes strict mixed currying.
    - *Left closure only.* Replace v and d by v_L = 1_C ⊗ Λ_C(1_{C⊗D}) and d_L = ev, through W = C\(C⊗D). The resulting H_L also maps to ℓ⁻¹r⁻¹ℓr. So the free left-closed theory already has non-identity structural loops 1_T ⇒ H_L ⇒ 1_T with π ∘ ι = 1.
15. **Countermodels for ι ∘ π = 1_H need non-invertible retractions.** In a Gray monoid, if f is strictly invertible, then χ_{f,g} is invertible, with inverse (f ⊗ B′) ∗ χ_{f⁻¹,g} ∗ (f ⊗ B). Hence in any model where u, e, v and d are strict isomorphisms, ι ∘ π = 1. That covers every refinement of the free-group, 𝒟 and 𝓟 models. A countermodel must have u ∘ e ≠ 1.
16. **Two different "centrality" questions.**
    - *Outer comparison.* For functors e, g between strict ω-categories, (e ⊗ 1)(1 ⊗ g) and (1 ⊗ g)(e ⊗ 1) are both e ⊗ g. So the interpretation of 𝒢's 1-cells as functors and its 2-cells as transformations sends every interchanger, generator or structural, to an identity, and cannot test directedness.
    - *Internal interchanger.* Evaluation applied to an interchanger of internal cells can be non-invertible. For S = J ⊗ J (J = [1]) with square γ : h₁v₀ ⇒ v₁h₀, let q : J → S/J curry 1_S and let α : v₀ → v₁ be the image of the arrow of J. Then e(χ_{α,u}) = γ.
17. **𝒢's two hom-category isomorphisms do not hold in the intended model.**
    - In S/J there is an arrow α : v₀ → v₁.
    - In J\S there is none: such an arrow would need the reverse of γ.
    - A single 2-category of strict ω-categories, with one orientation of transformation as 2-cells and ℋ(I, Z) ≅ Z, would identify both S/J and J\S with ℋ(J, S), identically on objects. That is impossible.

    So the Gray tensor's left and right closures are matched by 2-cells of opposite orientation (lax for one side, oplax for the other; Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari, Ex. 2.5). Campion's tensor gives (A\C)/B ≃ A\(C/B) canonically, but not literally.
18. **The correct structure is two-sided Gray enrichment.** For strict 2-categories with the Gray tensor, put ℍ(A, C)(P, Q) = 2Cat(P ⊗ A ⊗ Q, C), with probes P, Q on either side. Its one-sided restrictions are two different hom 2-categories: ℍ_ℓ (left probe, D_k ⊗ A → C) and ℍ_r (right probe, A ⊗ D_k → C). Then:
    - left currying is an isomorphism ℍ_r(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ ℍ_r(B, A\C), and right currying is an isomorphism ℍ_ℓ(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ ℍ_ℓ(A, C/B);
    - with probes on both sides, the bound argument absorbs the probe on its side: ℍ(A ⊗ B, C)(P, Q) ≅ ℍ(B, A\C)(P ⊗ A, Q) and ℍ(A ⊗ B, C)(P, Q) ≅ ℍ(A, C/B)(P, B ⊗ Q);
    - the squares 2Cat(J ⊗ A ⊗ J, C) give a strict double hom with mixed lax/oplax modifications.
19. **The free unit-free model 𝒢′_Σ.** Models have a probe sort and a data sort, adjunctions only between data types, and gluing along designated probe colimits (needed only for one-hole contexts). The free model 𝒢′_Σ on a signature Σ of generating 1-cells exists as a term model with an explicit universal property. The Gray model is a model. An erasure model (probes act by identities) shows that the free unit-free biclosed monoidal category 𝓑_Σ embeds faithfully in 𝒢′_Σ.
20. **What collapses and what survives in 𝒢′.**
    - Data arrows (functors) tensor bifunctorially, so the structural loops of items 11 and 14 are trivial: H = H_L = 1_T, and the maps ι, π of item 11 are identities.
    - Non-invertible Gray interchange survives one dimension up. For two transformations, the two schedules are related by the modification F(χ ▷ 1_A), which has no inverse in 𝒢′ (in the Gray model, with the atom A sent to the terminal 2-category, it is the 2-cell of the walking Gray square).
    - So in 𝒢′ the schedule structure of the Background lives on generators of probe degree J (transformations X ⊗ J → Y or J ⊗ X → Y, and more generally cells X ⊗ D_k → Y), with interchangers as modifications.

## Questions

Questions 1–3 are about 𝒢′ of items 18–20. Questions 4–5 are about the original 𝒢 of the Setting, whose closed structure is strict isomorphisms of hom-categories.

1. **(Schedules of transformations.)** In 𝒢′_Σ, let the generators include transformations (left- or right-parameter maps of probe degree J) as well as 1-cells. Fix an arrow of 𝓑_Σ and the 1-cells over it. Is the category of composites of generating transformations over it, with modifications as morphisms, equivalent to a product of schedule categories S(P), where P is the dependency order of the generating transformations and the swaps are the modifications F(χ ▷ 1)? Do structural events (units and counits of the two adjunctions, evaluations, the probe-absorption isomorphisms of item 18) enter P as events, and on which side?
2. **(Centrality at the transformation level.)** Item 12 shows evaluation is not central among 1-cells of 𝒢. In 𝒢′, is there an interchanger modification between a generating transformation and an evaluation, and if so in which direction? Which interchangers involving evaluation, units or currying survive as non-invertible modifications?
3. **(ω-version.)** Using two-sided enrichment over (∞,ω)-categories with the Gray tensor (probes D_k on either side), are the hom (∞,ω)-categories of the free unit-free biclosed object over a fixed arrow of 𝓑_Σ equivalent to products of ν(K_P)-homs, with structural events included?
4. **(Is ι ∘ π the identity in 𝒢?)** For H of item 11, or H_L of item 14, is ι ∘ π = 1_H in the original 𝒢? By item 15, a countermodel must have u ∘ e ≠ 1. If ι ∘ π = 1_H, are the isomorphisms H^n ≅ 1_T unique?
5. **(Forward uniqueness in 𝒢, smallest case.)** Prove 𝒢(t₁, t₂) = {α} and 𝒢(s, t) = {θ}, or show that they fail. This includes identifying the forward comparisons built from different typed instances of the crossing interchanger (item 11, last point).

## What counts as a solution

Any of the following:
- a description of the transformation-level hom categories for Question 1, or a counterexample to the S(P) form;
- a decision of Question 2;
- an answer to Question 3;
- a decision of Question 4;
- a proof or counterexample for Question 5;
