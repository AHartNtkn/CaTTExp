# A correct syntactic rule for Gray tensors of CaTT contexts with globes

*A self-contained problem. It assumes familiarity with the type theory CaTT, strict ω-categories and Steiner's directed complexes, and nothing else. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked.*

**What we are asking for.** A repaired rule, stated in CaTT's free-variable style, together with proofs of soundness and of existence for **every** context and **every** n. Patching the printed rule one example at a time is not what we want.

## 1. Setting

**CaTT** (Finster–Mimram, LICS 2017, arXiv 1706.02866). Types are `⋆` or `u →_A v`; terms are variables or `coh_{P, u →_A v}[σ]` with P a pasting context and either the coherence side condition `Var(u : A) = Var(P) = Var(v : A)` or the composition side condition `Var(u : A) = Var(∂⁻P)`, `Var(v : A) = Var(∂⁺P)`. Definitional equality is syntactic identity. Contexts are exactly finite computads, and terms are the cells of the free weak ω-category on them (Benjamin–Markakis–Sarti, arXiv 2405.00398). Every term t of a context M has a strict image π(t) in the free strict ω-category F(M).

**Mikhail's Gray judgment** (T. J. Mikhail, *A type-theoretic definition of lax (∞,∞)-limits*, arXiv 2412.13310, §4.2). For a context M and n ≥ 1 it derives contexts `M₁; …; M_{2n+1}`, meant to present `M ⊗ Dⁿ`:
- each variable x of M is duplicated into layers;
- at the odd layers a connecting cell `p_x : s → t` is added;
- s and t are not given by a formula. They are constrained only by free-variable conditions: τCond requires `FV(s) = FV(x) ∪ ⋃{FV(p_y) : p_y in the current layer, y ∝ τ(p)}`, and σCond the same for the primed copy x′ on the target side, where `y ∝ t` means "y occurs linearly in t at top dimension".

Mikhail proves that a derivation exists only for n = 1 and globular contexts M (Thm 4.4).

**Target.** For a context M whose free strict ω-category is ν(K) for a strongly loop-free Steiner complex K (for example a pasting context), the intended result is a context `M ⊗ Dⁿ` whose free strict ω-category is the Steiner tensor ν(K ⊗ λDⁿ), with one variable per generator x ⊗ y and boundaries given by the Leibniz formula `∂^α(x ⊗ y) = (∂^α x ⊗ y) ∪ (x ⊗ ∂^{(−1)^{dim x} α} y)`.

## 2. What is known

1. **The printed rule fails on the walking arrow.** Take M = (a, b : ⋆, f : a → b) with renamed copy (a′, b′, f′ : a′ → b′) and connectors u : a → a′, v : b → b′. At the f-step, τCond on the source side selects no connector (τ(f) = b, τ(u) = a′, τ(v) = b′), so FV(s) = {a, b, f}; σCond on the target side selects u (σ(f′) = a′), so FV(t) = {a, a′, b′, u, f′}. But f ∝ s forces s : a → b, so b ∈ FV(t), a contradiction. So no derivation exists for the intended square `p_f : (f ; v) ⇒ (u ; f′)`, which is a valid CaTT context.
2. **Reading the copied types literally does not help.** If x′ keeps the unrenamed type X (as the displayed rule writes), then f′ : a → b and p_f : f ⇒ f′ is derivable, but its strict ω-category has two parallel arrows a → b and two terminal vertices, so it is not the Gray square.
3. **A necessary repair.** Rename copied types, and use `y ∝ σ(p)` in the source-side selector while keeping `y ∝ τ(p)` on the target side. This fixes item 1's example, and nothing beyond it is proved.
4. **The linearity predicate does not enforce multiplicity one.** Its recursive clause counts substitution arguments that are themselves linear without excluding nonlinear uses elsewhere. For a loop ℓ : a → a and binary composition c: ℓ ∝ ℓ, ℓ ∝̸ c(ℓ, ℓ), but ℓ ∝ c(c(ℓ, ℓ), ℓ).
5. **The judgment style is permissive.** For the analogous cone judgment (same style of free-variable side conditions), non-canonical derivations exist that reuse earlier projections and do not present the intended join. So a correct rule must either pin down s and t up to a stated equivalence, or come with a specified construction.
6. **The strict side.** The Gray tensor of two free strict ω-categories is free, and the tensor of strong Steiner complexes is freely generated by its atoms (Ara–Lucas, arXiv 1909.13564, Cor. 3.11 and Prop. 3.7). Parity complexes are equivalent to strong Steiner complexes (Campbell, arXiv 2605.09427).
7. **The weak side.** For pasting contexts M, the free (∞,ω)-category on the Steiner tensor is Campion's Gray tensor of the free (∞,ω)-categories (Campion, arXiv 2311.00205, Ex. 3.2, Cor. 3.5; Chanavat, arXiv 2603.22242, Thm 3.29).
8. **Existence of typing terms.** For a generator x ⊗ y of the intended tensor, a CaTT type needs terms composing its boundary molecules. Such terms exist in all dimensions ≤ 4 of the tensor, by a normal-form argument for 2-dimensional cells (any two parallel 2-terms with equal strict image are connected, in every context). In higher dimensions their existence follows from the analogous connection statement for parallel terms in loop-free contexts one dimension lower. For n = 1 and arbitrary M (whose generators may have composite boundaries), the connecting cells are lax naturality cells of terms, known at depth ≤ 1 (Benjamin–Markakis–Offord–Sarti–Vicary, arXiv 2501.11620).

## 3. Questions

**Q1 (the rule).** Give side conditions for a Gray judgment `M ⊗ Dⁿ`, stated syntactically in CaTT's style (checkable on terms, no definitional equalities), and prove:
- **(soundness)** for every finite context M with F(M) = ν(K), K strongly loop-free Steiner, and every n, every context the judgment accepts has free strict ω-category ν(K ⊗ λDⁿ), with the connecting variables sent to the generators x ⊗ y;
- **(canonicity)** any two accepted contexts for the same M and n differ only in the choice of boundary terms, and those choices have equal strict images.

**Q2 (existence for every context and every n).** Give an explicit formula for the types of the connecting cells, for every finite context M and every n, and prove that it satisfies the rule of Q1. You may assume, as a black box, that parallel terms with equal strict image in loop-free contexts are connected (so that composites of boundary molecules exist), and you should say exactly where it is used. For contexts M that are not Steiner, state what the accepted context presents.

**Q3 (functoriality).** Show that the construction is natural in substitutions M′ ⊢ M (up to the choices in Q1's canonicity clause), and that `(M ⊗ D^m) ⊗ D^n` and `M ⊗ (D^m ⊗ D^n)` are related by substitutions both ways that are inverse up to higher cells.

## 4. What counts as a solution

A rule with proofs of Q1–Q2; a counterexample showing that no rule in the free-variable style can be both sound and complete (for example two contexts with the same free-variable data, one presenting the tensor and one not, for every choice of conditions of a stated kind); or a published reference.

## 5. Literature (searched 2026-11-18)

- Mikhail, arXiv 2412.13310 (§4.2, Thm 4.4); Mikhail's thesis, *Type Theory for (∞,∞)-Categories, and Decomposition Spaces* (UAB), has no general-n existence result.
- Ara–Lucas, arXiv 1909.13564; Campbell, arXiv 2605.09427; Steiner, HHA 2004.
- Campion, arXiv 2311.00205; Chanavat, arXiv 2603.22242.
- Benjamin–Markakis–Offord–Sarti–Vicary, arXiv 2501.11620.

# Researcher Response

**The permitted connection hypothesis is sufficient for Q1–Q2.** A uniform repair is to impose signed, dimension-indexed free-variable conditions on **universal loop-free templates**, then instantiate those templates by substitution. All templates are ordinary CaTT types and terms; they introduce no term constructor or definitional equality.

The construction below is effective: it uses searches through CaTT syntax whose termination is proved. It is not a claim that the templates admit a primitive recursive closed formula of bounded size.

There is one convention to distinguish. With negative boundary meaning source, your Leibniz formula gives
\[
\partial(f\otimes I)
=b\otimes I+f\otimes0-a\otimes I-f\otimes1,
\]
so its square is \(u;f'\Rightarrow f;v\). The square in Known 1 uses the opposite convention. Below I follow the **Target’s Leibniz formula**. Reversing the factor order throughout gives the other convention.

## 1. The syntactic invariant

For a context \(\Gamma\), put
\[
\operatorname{Var}_k(\Gamma)
=\{x\in\operatorname{Var}(\Gamma):\dim x=k\},
\qquad
\operatorname{FV}_k(t)
=\operatorname{Var}_k(\Gamma)\cap\operatorname{FV}(t).
\]

For an \(r\)-term \(t\), write \(t_k^\epsilon\) for its syntactic \(k\)-source or \(k\)-target, read recursively from its type. Set \(t_r^-=t_r^+=t\). These are actual terms, and globularity below the top dimension holds by syntactic identity.

Likewise, if \(A\) is a type of dimension \(r>0\), write
\[
\operatorname{bd}_k^\epsilon(A),\qquad k<r,
\]
for its endpoint terms. Explicitly, for \(A=u\to_Bv\),
\[
\operatorname{bd}_{r-1}^-(A)=u,\qquad
\operatorname{bd}_{r-1}^+(A)=v,
\]
and lower endpoints are read from \(B\).

The relevant data are the separate sets
\[
\operatorname{FV}_k\bigl(\operatorname{bd}_k^\epsilon(A)\bigr).
\]
Taking one union over the entire type would discard the required signs and dimensions.

### Lemma 1: top-dimensional support

For every context \(\Gamma\) and every \(r\)-term \(t\),
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{supp}([\pi(t)]_r)=\operatorname{FV}_r(t).
}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \([\pi(t)]_r\) is the \(r\)-chain in the linearization of \(F(\Gamma)\), whose basis consists of the \(r\)-generators.

**Proof.** Induct on \(t\).

For a variable, both sides are its singleton.

For
\[
t=\operatorname{coh}_{P,A}[\sigma]
\]
of dimension \(r\), the CaTT side conditions imply \(\dim P\le r\). If \(\dim P<r\), the generic strict image is an identity in dimension \(r\), and neither side has an \(r\)-generator.

If \(\dim P=r\), the generic strict composite has chain
\[
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{Var}_r(P)}z.
\]
After substitution, its chain is
\[
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{Var}_r(P)}[\pi(\sigma(z))]_r.
\]
All coefficients are nonnegative, so the support is the union of the supports of the summands. Apply the induction hypothesis. ∎

Suppose now that \(F(\Gamma)=\nu L\), with context variables identified with the atoms of a strong Steiner complex. Steiner’s Theorem 6.2(iii) says that every row of a cell’s tableau has coefficients zero or one. Consequently,
\[
(\pi t)_k^\epsilon
=
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{FV}_k(t_k^\epsilon)}z.
\tag{2}
\]
Thus the signed, dimension-indexed free-variable profile determines the **entire strict cell**. In particular, for parallel \(r\)-terms of the same literal type, equality of \(\operatorname{FV}_r\) alone implies equality of strict images. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0403237?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This is the fact that makes the proposed side conditions sound.

## 2. What the permitted connection theorem supplies

We first derive the relative lifting statement needed below. This step is essential: merely knowing that some weak representative exists would not make its boundaries match a prescribed type.

### Lemma 2: relative lifting in a loop-free context

Let \(\Delta\) be a finite CaTT context presenting a strong Steiner complex, and let \(c\in F(\Delta)\) be a strict \(d\)-cell.

1. There is a CaTT \(d\)-term \(u\) with \(\pi(u)=c\).
2. If \(a,b\) are parallel \((d-1)\)-terms satisfying
   \[
   \pi(a)=s(c),\qquad \pi(b)=t(c),
   \]
   there is a term of the **literal type**
   \[
   u:a\to_A b
   \]
   with \(\pi(u)=c\).

Here and below, “loop-free” in applications of your black box refers to these Steiner presentations and their dimensional truncations.

### Obtaining comparisons with identity strict image

A \(k\)-term uses no variable above dimension \(k\). Hence two parallel \(k\)-terms can be regarded as terms in the subcontext \(\Delta_{\le k}\).

Apply the permitted connection theorem there. A resulting \((k+1)\)-term has identity strict image, because \(F(\Delta_{\le k})\) has no nonidentity cells above dimension \(k\).

If “connected” means a finite zigzag rather than a directed filler, the same conclusion is available: each link has empty \((k+1)\)-dimensional support and is invertible, so the zigzag can be oriented and composed. The empty-support invertibility criterion is Benjamin–Markakis, Proposition 35. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12127?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Correcting the boundaries of an existing lift

Fix ordinary CaTT binary \(k\)-composition operations \(*_k\), with a fixed bracketing, whose outside \(k\)-faces are literally the outside \(k\)-faces of their arguments. Such operations are obtained from the pasting context of two globes glued along a \(k\)-face, choosing its boundary composites recursively. Identities and these pasting compositions use the ordinary CaTT rules. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.02866?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Suppose \(u\) is already a lift of \(c\). Correct its faces successively for
\[
k=0,1,\ldots,d-1.
\]

Let \(a_k,b_k\) be the desired \(k\)-source and \(k\)-target: for \(k<d-1\), they are the common lower faces of \(a,b\); for \(k=d-1\), they are \(a,b\).

Before stage \(k\), the lower faces have already been corrected. Therefore
\[
a_k,\ s_k(u)
\quad\text{and}\quad
t_k(u),\ b_k
\]
are literally parallel, with equal strict images. Obtain
\[
\alpha_k:a_k\to s_k(u),
\qquad
\beta_k:t_k(u)\to b_k
\]
in \(\Delta_{\le k}\), as above.

Replace \(u\), in diagrammatic composition order, by
\[
\boxed{
\bigl(1^{\,d-k-1}\alpha_k *_k u\bigr)
 *_k 1^{\,d-k-1}\beta_k.
}
\tag{3}
\]

This preserves \(\pi(u)\), makes its two \(k\)-faces literally \(a_k,b_k\), and preserves all previously corrected lower faces. After the last stage, its type is exactly \(a\to_A b\).

### Producing an initial lift

Express \(c\) using atoms, identities, and strict compositions. Such expressions exist by Steiner’s atomic generation theorem. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0403237?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Proceed by induction on that expression. Atoms and identities lift directly. For a composite \(c_1*_k c_2\), first obtain lifts \(u,v\). Their gluing \(k\)-faces have equal strict images.

Apply the same correction procedure to \(v\):

- below dimension \(k\), prescribe the faces of \(t_k(u)\);
- at dimension \(k\), correct only its source to \(t_k(u)\).

This produces \(v'\) with
\[
s_k(v')=t_k(u)
\]
literally and \(\pi(v')=\pi(v)\). The CaTT term \(u*_k v'\) is therefore well typed and lifts the strict composite.

This proves the first assertion. Applying (3) proves the second. ∎

**Every use of the assumed connection theorem in the construction below is an application of this lemma in a universal loop-free context.**

## 3. Universal boundary templates

Let the generators of \(D^p\) be
\[
a_i^-,a_i^+\quad(i<p),\qquad a_p,
\]
and similarly let those of \(D^q\) be \(b_j^\pm,b_q\). At the top, set
\[
a_p^-=a_p^+=a_p,\qquad b_q^-=b_q^+=b_q.
\]

Write \(r=p+q\). The atom \(a_p\otimes b_q\) has the explicit tableau
\[
\boxed{
\theta_{p,q;k}^{\epsilon}
=
\sum_{i+j=k}
a_i^\epsilon\otimes b_j^{(-1)^i\epsilon},
\qquad 0\le k\le r.
}
\tag{4}
\]
Out-of-range summands are omitted. This is Steiner’s tensor-atom formula; it also follows by applying the Leibniz differential and collecting adjacent terms. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0403237?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

We construct a CaTT type \(B_{p,q}\) for the product of a \(p\)-cell and a \(q\)-cell.

Set \(B_{0,0}=\star\). When one index is zero, use the ordinary globular type. This normalization ensures that tensoring a term with a vertex is literally a renamed copy of that term.

For \(p,q>0\), form a context \(U_{p,q}\) with one variable
\[
a_i^\epsilon\otimes b_j^\delta
\]
for every generator pair **except** \(a_p\otimes b_q\). Its variable of dimension \(i+j\) receives the previously constructed type \(B_{i,j}\), renamed along the appropriate globe-face inclusions.

Every such pair has dimension below \(r\). Thus \(U_{p,q}\) is defined using only earlier templates.

The boundary-template rule is:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
U_{p,q}\vdash B_{p,q}\ \mathrm{type},
\qquad \dim B_{p,q}=r,\\[2mm]
\operatorname{FV}_k
 \bigl(\operatorname{bd}_k^\epsilon(B_{p,q})\bigr)
=
\operatorname{supp}\theta_{p,q;k}^{\epsilon}
\quad
(0\le k<r,\ \epsilon\in\{-,+\}).
\end{gathered}
}
\tag{B}
\]

This is a finite CaTT-style free-variable test. It does not compare strict images.

### Soundness of (B)

Inductively, \(U_{p,q}\) presents the subcomplex of
\(\lambda D^p\otimes\lambda D^q\) generated by the proper pairs. By (2), condition (B) identifies every strict boundary row of the proposed new generator with the corresponding row of \(a_p\otimes b_q\).

Consequently, adjoining
\[
z:B_{p,q}
\]
adjoins exactly that tensor atom.

### Existence of \(B_{p,q}\)

Let \(S,T\) be the strict source and target of \(a_p\otimes b_q\), regarded as cells of \(F(U_{p,q})\).

Use Lemma 2 to lift \(S\) to a term \(s:A\). Since \(S,T\) are parallel, use its relative assertion to lift \(T\) to a term \(t:A\) with that exact same type. Then
\[
B_{p,q}:=s\to_A t
\]
satisfies (B).

This proves existence uniformly in \(p,q\).

## 4. Generic coherence templates and the actual rule

Boundary templates alone are insufficient for arbitrary contexts: a generator’s boundary can be a coherence term. We therefore also specify how every CaTT coherence constructor is translated.

Write
\[
\llbracket t,y\rrbracket
\]
for the translation of a term \(t\) against a globe generator \(y\). The following definitions are simultaneous; their well-founded order is given in §5.

For \(t\) of dimension \(p\) and \(y\) of dimension \(q\), define a substitution on the proper pairs by
\[
\boxed{
\Theta_{t,y}
 (a_i^\epsilon\otimes b_j^\delta)
=
\llbracket t_i^\epsilon,y_j^\delta\rrbracket.
}
\tag{5}
\]
Because the top pair is excluded, each entry has total dimension below \(p+q\).

Now consider a legal generic CaTT operation
\[
h=\operatorname{coh}_{P,A}[\operatorname{id}_P],
\qquad \dim h=p.
\]

Construct \(T_qP\), the proposed tensor of \(P\) with \(D^q\), using the \(B\)-templates. This step needs no translation of composite boundaries: the generators of the pasting context \(P\) have globular variable boundaries.

Let \(e_q\) be the top generator of \(D^q\). The coherence-template rule is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
T_qP\vdash
C_{P,A,q}:B_{p,q}[\Theta_{h,e_q}],\\[1mm]
\operatorname{FV}_{p+q}(C_{P,A,q})
=
\{z\otimes e_q:z\in\operatorname{Var}_p(P)\}.
\end{gathered}
}
\tag{C}
\]
If \(p>\dim P\), the set on the right is empty.

For \(q=0\), take \(C_{P,A,0}\) to be the renamed generic term \(h\).

### Why (C) is the correct free-variable condition

The strict \(p\)-chain of \(h\) is
\[
[\pi h]_p=\sum_{z\in\operatorname{Var}_p(P)}z.
\]
Thus the desired tensor cell has top chain
\[
\sum_{z\in\operatorname{Var}_p(P)}z\otimes e_q.
\]

Its strict boundaries are already forced by the exact type in (C), using the correctness of the lower-dimensional translations in (5). In the Steiner context \(T_qP\), equality of the top support and of the boundary rows determines the strict cell by Lemma 1 and the zero-or-one coefficient property.

Hence every accepted \(C_{P,A,q}\) has strict image
\[
\pi(h)\otimes e_q.
\]

### The formula for arbitrary contexts

Fix admissible \(B\)- and \(C\)-templates once. They are actual CaTT expressions, and repeated uses of a template use the same expression.

For a variable \(x\) of dimension \(p\) and a globe generator \(y\) of dimension \(q\), introduce one variable \(x_y\), of dimension \(p+q\), with type
\[
\boxed{
x_y:B_{p,q}[\Theta_{x,y}].
}
\tag{6}
\]

Define translation of terms by
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\llbracket x,y\rrbracket
&=x_y,\\
\llbracket \operatorname{coh}_{P,A}[\sigma],y\rrbracket
&=C_{P,A,q}[\widehat{\sigma}_y],
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{7}
\]
where \(q=\dim y\), \(j_y:D^q\to D^n\) selects the globe face \(y\), and
\[
\boxed{
\widehat{\sigma}_y(z\otimes v)
=
\llbracket \sigma(z),j_y(v)\rrbracket.
}
\tag{8}
\]

Equations (5)–(8) are the promised formula for **every connecting type**.

Order the output variables first by the dimension and fixed ordering of \(y\), then by the declaration order of \(x\) in \(M\). A type in (6) uses only earlier declarations:

- a proper face of \(y\) belongs to an earlier globe layer;
- at the same \(y\), a proper face term of \(x\) uses only variables declared before \(x\).

There are exactly
\[
(2n+1)\,|\operatorname{Var}(M)|
\]
variables. At a vertex of \(D^n\), (6) is the original declaration with its variables renamed.

### The judgment

A derivation of
\[
M\otimes D^n\rightsquigarrow T_nM
\]
records the finitely many templates it uses, checks (B) and (C), and builds the declarations by (5)–(8).

Thus its checks consist of:

- ordinary CaTT typing;
- literal substitution;
- dimensions;
- finite free-variable-set comparisons.

All template notation can be expanded into ordinary CaTT syntax. The judgment adds no equations to CaTT.

## 5. Well-foundedness and existence

Here is the dependency order. It is needed both to prove existence and to exclude circular use of term translation.

Proceed by total dimension \(r\).

### Phase 1: construct \(B_{p,q}\) for \(p+q=r\)

The proper-pair context \(U_{p,q}\) uses only \(B_{i,j}\) with \(i+j<r\). Its strict presentation is therefore already known.

Lemma 2 constructs \(B_{p,q}\), as in §3.

### Phase 2: construct the required generic contexts \(T_qP\)

For a generic operation \(h\) of dimension \(p\), its pasting context satisfies \(\dim P\le p\). Thus every generator of \(T_qP\) has dimension at most \(r=p+q\).

Because \(P\) is globular, its product declarations are obtained from the \(B\)-templates by variable renaming. No current-dimensional \(C\)-template is required. The strict presentation is
\[
F(T_qP)=F(P)\otimes D^q,
\]
a strong Steiner presentation.

### Phase 3: construct \(C_{P,A,q}\)

Every entry of \(\Theta_{h,e_q}\) has dimension below \(r\). Therefore its entries, their types, and their strict images are already available.

The assignment (5) is a literal substitution: the types of its entries match because the lower globe identities are syntactic identities. In particular,
\[
B_{p,q}[\Theta_{h,e_q}]
\]
is a well-formed type.

Its strict boundary is the boundary of the strict cell
\[
\pi(h)\otimes e_q.
\]
Apply Lemma 2 in the strong Steiner context \(T_qP\), with this exact prescribed type. The resulting term satisfies (C), by Lemma 1.

### Phase 4: translate arbitrary terms at total dimension \(r\)

The variable clause of (7) is immediate.

In the coherence clause, a call involving \(\sigma(z)\) either has smaller total dimension or, at equal total dimension, translates a proper syntactic subterm of
\(\operatorname{coh}_{P,A}[\sigma]\). The recursion is therefore well founded by
\[
(\text{total dimension},\text{syntax size})
\]
in lexicographic order, after the current-dimensional templates have been constructed.

At a bounded stage, work in the subcontext of product variables of the required dimensions and then weaken. A \(p\)-term cannot require a variable above dimension \(p\), so larger ambient generators introduce no dependency on later templates.

### Making all choices effective

Fix a size-lexicographic enumeration of nameless CaTT syntax. Define:

- \(B_{p,q}\) to be the first type satisfying (B), except for the stipulated unit cases;
- \(C_{P,A,q}\) to be the first term satisfying (C).

These predicates are decidable syntactic checks. CaTT’s syntax and typechecking do not require deciding a conversion relation between terms. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.02866?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The preceding existence proof shows that every search halts. For a fixed finite input, only finitely many templates are requested; their recursive prerequisites have lower total dimension.

This is a **total computable construction by unbounded minimization**. Its termination is proved, rather than assumed from enumeration.

## 6. Soundness, canonicity, and the non-Steiner case

For a fixed admissible template family, the typing and semantic induction gives
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{type}(\llbracket t,y\rrbracket)
=
B_{\dim t,\dim y}[\Theta_{t,y}],
\qquad
\pi(\llbracket t,y\rrbracket)=\pi(t)\otimes y.
}
\tag{9}
\]

The second equality means the image of the top atom of
\(D^{\dim t}\otimes D^{\dim y}\) under the strict maps selecting \(\pi(t)\) and \(y\).

For a variable, the type in (6) has exactly the strict attaching boundary of the product generator \(x\otimes y\). For a coherence term, (9) follows from the certified generic \(C\)-template and substitution.

The strict product has precisely these generators and attaching boundaries. One can see this directly by expressing \(F(M)\) as successive globe attachments and tensoring those attachments with \(D^n\). The relative product of globes freely adjoins the pairs consisting of the newly attached generator and a globe generator. Ara–Lucas’s pushout-product result supplies this relative freeness, and its Corollary 3.11 supplies general freeness of the tensor of free strict categories. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/1909.13564v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

It follows that
\[
\boxed{
F(T_nM)\cong F(M)\otimes D^n
}
\tag{10}
\]
by the isomorphism sending \(x_y\) to \(x\otimes y\).

### Q1: soundness

If \(F(M)=\nu K\) for strong Steiner \(K\), then
\[
F(T_nM)
\cong \nu K\otimes D^n
\cong \nu(K\otimes\lambda D^n).
\]
Every connecting variable has the required image.

Notice that this proof applies to **any** templates satisfying (B) and (C). It does not require them to be the least ones selected by the algorithm.

### Q1: canonicity

Any two accepted outputs have the same labeled generators, dimensions, and fixed declaration order. By (9), corresponding attaching terms have the same strict images in the tensor presentation.

Consequently, the identity on the labels induces a canonical strict isomorphism between their strictifications. Their differences are precisely choices of weak boundary terms with the same strict images.

Soundness and this canonicity assertion do not use the connection hypothesis. That hypothesis is needed to prove the existence of the certified templates.

### Q2: arbitrary finite \(M\)

Nothing in (5)–(8) assumes that \(M\) is Steiner. All applications of the connection theorem and all uses of Steiner tableaux occurred in the universal contexts \(U_{p,q}\) and \(T_qP\).

Thus (6) constructs the connecting types for every finite \(M\) and every \(n\), including contexts with composite boundaries, repeated variables, and loops.

For non-Steiner \(M\), the precise conclusion is:

> \(T_nM\) is a finite weak computad, with the explicitly constructed weak attaching terms, whose strictification is \(F(M)\otimes D^n\).

There is no assertion here that
\[
F(M)\otimes D^n
=
\nu\bigl(\lambda F(M)\otimes\lambda D^n\bigr)
\]
for arbitrary \(M\), or that this weak computad has an independently specified weak Gray universal property. Those assertions are unnecessary for Q1–Q2.

Multiplicity is preserved by the actual substitutions in (7)–(8). The free-variable tests are performed in the universal Steiner contexts, before substitution can identify distinct variables.

## 7. Q3: substitutions and compatible associativity

### Naturality in substitutions

For
\[
M'\vdash\sigma:M,
\]
define
\[
\boxed{
(T_n\sigma)(x_y)=\llbracket\sigma(x),y\rrbracket.
}
\tag{11}
\]

The key identity is literal:
\[
\boxed{
\llbracket t[\sigma],y\rrbracket
=
\llbracket t,y\rrbracket[T_n\sigma].
}
\tag{12}
\]

Prove it first as a raw-syntax identity by structural induction. The variable case is (11). For the coherence case,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\llbracket\operatorname{coh}_{P,A}[\tau][\sigma],y\rrbracket
&=C_{P,A,q}[\widehat{\tau\circ\sigma}_y]\\
&=C_{P,A,q}[\widehat\tau_y\circ T_n\sigma]\\
&=\llbracket\operatorname{coh}_{P,A}[\tau],y\rrbracket[T_n\sigma].
\end{aligned}
\]
The middle equality is the induction hypothesis on the entries of \(\tau\). The index \((P,A,q)\) of the chosen generic template is unchanged by outer substitution.

Now use (12) on the proper faces in (5). It proves that (11) has exactly the types required for a substitution
\[
T_nM'\vdash T_n\sigma:T_nM.
\]

Therefore
\[
T_n(\operatorname{id})=\operatorname{id},
\qquad
T_n(\tau\circ\sigma)=T_n\tau\circ T_n\sigma
\]
literally. For each fixed admissible template family, the construction is an actual functor on the CaTT substitution category. Its strictification is tensoring with \(D^n\).

The choices must remain confined to the universal templates. Choosing new fillers after each input substitution would not prove (12).

### Associativity for products of globe factors

Q1 defines tensoring by a globe. To make
\[
M\otimes(D^m\otimes D^n)
\]
a syntactic expression, an extension to this right-hand shape must also be specified.

Choose the compatible extension to finite ordered lists of globe factors:
\[
T_{(n_1,\ldots,n_k)}
=
T_{n_k}\circ\cdots\circ T_{n_1}.
\tag{13}
\]
Give its variables the flattened labels
\[
x\otimes(y_1\otimes\cdots\otimes y_k),
\]
and give them the iterated types, with those labels renamed.

For two factors, this gives substitutions
\[
(M\otimes D^m)\otimes D^n
\;\rightleftarrows\;
M\otimes(D^m\otimes D^n)
\]
defined by
\[
(x\otimes y)\otimes z
\longleftrightarrow
x\otimes(y\otimes z).
\]
They are well typed because the right-hand types were defined by this renaming, and their composites are literally identity substitutions. This is stronger than being inverse up to higher cells.

The strict presentation of the right side is correct by (10) and associativity of the strict Gray tensor. Thus this supplies Q3 for compatible choices of the requested presentations.

It does not assert that arbitrary, independently chosen CaTT lifts of the same strict presentation are weakly equivalent. Q1’s equality of strict boundary images alone would not establish that stronger statement.

The resulting repair consists of the two finite support conditions **(B)** and **(C)**, the connecting-type formula **(6)**, and the structural translation **(7)–(8)**. The permitted connection theorem is used only to prove that the universal template searches terminate.
