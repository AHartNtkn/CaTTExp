# Relative coherence in CaTT in every dimension: free weak ω-categories are strict exactly on homotopical computads

*A self-contained problem. It assumes familiarity with globular weak ω-categories, strict ω-categories, polygraphs (computads) and Steiner complexes, and nothing else. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked.*

**What we are asking for.** One theorem valid in every dimension: the exact class of finite computads on which the free weak ω-category is strict (in the sense of §1.2), ideally "exactly the homotopical ones". Individual new cases (one more context, one more dimension) are not useful to us. Bubbles obstruct strictness in every dimension (item 20), so the question is the comparison on bubble-free contexts. It has two halves. The first is the contraction, homotopical ⇒ strict. The second is necessity, which now reduces to one statement: the *coinductive* reconstruction map at free strict targets is an equivalence (items 22–23). The inductive reconstruction map is known to fail. Several exact reductions are known (items 9–14, 22–25).

## 1. Setting

### 1.1 CaTT

CaTT (Finster–Mimram, LICS 2017, arXiv 1706.02866) is a dependent type theory whose models are weak ω-categories (Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, arXiv 2106.04475: models are exactly Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis weak ω-categories).

```
Types          A ::= ⋆ | u →_A v
Terms          t ::= x | coh_{P, A}[γ]
Contexts       Γ ::= ∅ | Γ, x : A
Substitutions  γ ::= ⟨⟩ | ⟨γ, x ↦ t⟩
```

- `dim ⋆ = −1`, `dim(u →_A v) = dim A + 1`, and a term of type A has dimension `dim A + 1`.
- A **pasting context** `P ⊢ps` presents a globular pasting diagram. It has boundaries `∂⁻P`, `∂⁺P`.
- **The only term former.** If `P ⊢ps`, `P ⊢ u →_A v` and `Δ ⊢ γ : P`, then `Δ ⊢ coh_{P, u→_A v}[γ] : (u →_A v)[γ]`, provided either
  - (coherence) `Var(u : A) = Var(P) = Var(v : A)`, or
  - (composition) `Var(u : A) = Var(∂⁻P)` and `Var(v : A) = Var(∂⁺P)`.
- Definitional equality is syntactic equality. There are no other equations.
- Binary composites `u ∗_k v` and whiskerings are composition coherences over two discs glued along a k-disc.
- Contexts are exactly finite computads, and the terms of Γ are the cells of the free weak ω-category on Γ (Benjamin–Markakis–Sarti, arXiv 2405.00398). Coherence terms are invertible in the coinductive sense (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2406.12127).

### 1.2 Strict image and relative coherence

Every context Γ is a polygraph. Write `F(Γ)` for the free strict ω-category on it. Strict ω-categories are CaTT models, so every term t has a **strict image** `π(t) ∈ F(Γ)`: a coherence is sent to the unique strict composite of its arity.

> **RC(n) for Γ.** Any two parallel n-terms `T, T′` of Γ with `π(T) = π(T′)` are connected by an (n+1)-term `T → T′`.

Because coherence terms are invertible, RC(n) is symmetric. Write **RC(∞)(Γ)** for "RC(n) holds for Γ for every n ≥ 1".

> **RC_id(n) for Γ.** As RC(n), with the connecting term required to have identity image: `π(T → T′) = 1_{π T}`. Write RC_id(∞) as above.

RC_id implies RC. They agree when every endo-(n+1)-cell on an n-cell of F(Γ) is an identity, which holds in the loop-free class of Question 1. With loops, plain RC is too weak: in (x; f : x → x; α : f ⇒ f), the pair (f, f) is joined by α itself, whose image is not an identity. Wherever loops can occur, RC_id is the statement that matters.

*Equivalent form (proved for every finite context).* Every strict cell of F(Γ) is the image of some term. Given that, RC_id(∞)(Γ) is equivalent to: **π_Γ : Free(Γ) → F(Γ) is a trivial fibration of CaTT models**, and also to π_Γ being a weak equivalence (F(Γ) is gaunt: its only invertible cells are identities, since the multiset of top generators is additive). The trivial-fibration form says: for every pair of parallel (n−1)-terms u, v and every strict n-cell c : π(u) → π(v) there is an n-term t : u → v with π(t) = c. (Lift c with some boundary, then conjugate by RC_id connections, which have identity image.) 

### 1.3 Contexts we need

All of the following are finite. The full cylinders (Y = Var(Q)), the joins, the Gray products of globes and the orientals present strongly loop-free Steiner complexes (Steiner, *Omega-categories and chain complexes*, HHA 2004; Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches pour les ∞-catégories strictes*), so F(Γ) = ν(K) has cells determined by their supports. The partial cylinders (Y ≠ Var(Q)) also present strongly loop-free unital Steiner complexes (§3 item 9), and they are positive in every dimension (the source of x↑ has x⁻ as a factor and its target has x⁺).

- **Cylinders.** For a pasting context Q and an up-closed set `Y ⊆ Var(Q)` (if y ∈ Y occurs in the type of x then x ∈ Y), the context `Q↑Y` keeps each `x ∉ Y` and replaces each `x ∈ Y` by two copies `x⁻, x⁺` and a cell `x↑ : a_x → b_x`, where for x of dimension n

  ```
  a_x := ( … ((x⁻ ∗₀ (τⁿx)↑) ∗₁ (τⁿ⁻¹x)↑) … ) ∗_{n−1} (τx)↑
  b_x := (σx)↑ ∗_{n−1} ( … ((σⁿ⁻¹x)↑ ∗₁ ((σⁿx)↑ ∗₀ x⁺)) … )
  ```

  with every factor `w↑` for `w ∉ Y` omitted. For Y = Var(Q) the strict ω-category F(Q↑Y) is the Gray cylinder `D¹ ⊗ Q`. The smallest example is the **lax square** (x⁻, x⁺, y⁻, y⁺; x↑, y↑, f⁻, f⁺; F : f⁻ ∗₀ y↑ ⇒ x↑ ∗₀ f⁺).
- **Joins.** For a pasting context Q, `Cone(Q; c)` adds an object c and, for each variable t of Q of dimension m, a variable `p_t : p_{τt} → p_{σt} ∗_{m−1} (p_{σ²t} ∗_{m−2} ( … (p_{σᵐt} ∗₀ t)))` (with `p_x : c → x` for objects). F(Cone(Q; c)) is the Ara–Maltsiniotis join `c ⋆ Q`.
- **Gray molecules.** Gray products `D^{n₁} ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ D^{n_k}` of globes, and more generally pasting diagrams in Steiner's or Hadzihasanovic's sense, presented in CaTT by giving each cell the type `⌈∂⁻x⌉ → ⌈∂⁺x⌉` with chosen composite terms for its boundary molecules.
- **Orientals.** For example the **triangle** (x, y, z; f : x → y, g : y → z, h : x → z; α : f ∗₀ g ⇒ h).


## 2. Why it matters

RC is the single missing ingredient in several constructions in CaTT: lax naturality of every coherence with respect to every up-closed set of its variables (equivalently a lax cylinder Γ ⊗ D¹ for every context Γ); composites, unique up to higher cells, for non-globular pasting diagrams (Gray products of globes, joins); cones over every coherence; and the comparison of the free CaTT model on a computad with its strict free ω-category, which is the unit condition in comparing CaTT models with (∞,ω)-categories.

## 3. What is known

1. **RC(1) and RC(2) hold in every context.** For 2-terms: normalise to vertical composites of whiskered 2-variables; two composites with the same strict image are related by exchange moves (Ara–Burroni–Guiraud–Lafont–Métayer, *Polygraphs*, arXiv 2312.00429, §4.1), and each move is one coherence.
2. **Support lemma.** supp(π t) = supp(t) in every context, and if t has no variable of dimension dim t in its support then supp(t) = supp(σt) = supp(τt). Parallel terms whose image is an identity on a cell w are joined by one coherence over supp(w) whenever supp(w) is a pasting context. For a pasting context P, π_P is a trivial fibration (this is the coherence and composition rule).
3. **RC(3) is known** for the lax square, the triangle, every cylinder Q↑Y, the 2 × 2 and 2 × 3 grids, the Gray cube D¹ ⊗ D¹ ⊗ D¹, and joins over vertical chains of 2-discs. Each proof is a hand-built normalisation for one context; in each, RC(n+1) needed a separately constructed coherence of the normalisation one dimension up.
4. **Adjoining top generators in dimension 3.** If Λ has dimension ≤ 2 and an acyclic generating 1-graph, and Γ adds 3-generators between parallel 2-terms of Λ, then RC(3)(Γ) ⟺ RC(3)(Λ) (using depth-1 naturality, Benjamin–Markakis–Offord–Sarti–Vicary, arXiv 2501.11620, Thm 3.1). In dimension 4 only a conditional version (assuming RC(3) and RC(4) for Λ) is known.
5. **Exchange combinatorics in dimension 2 are settled.** For a 2-dimensional context with an acyclic generating 1-graph and non-empty boundary paths, the 2-paths representing a strict 2-cell c are the linear extensions of an event poset, and the graph of exchange moves with squares and hexagons as 2-cells is simply connected; squares and hexagons are single coherences.
6. **Loops obstruct.** In Γ = (x; a : 1_x ⇒ 1_x) the Eckmann–Hilton 3-cell a ∗₀ a ⇛ a ∗₀ a and the identity are parallel with equal strict image, and no 4-term joins them (the weak free object groupoidifies to S², the strict one B²ℕ to K(ℤ, 2), and π₃(S²) ≠ 0).
7. **Gaunt targets.** F(Γ) is gaunt for every finite Γ (generator counts), so an FHM ω-weak equivalence into F(Γ) is a trivial fibration. RC_id(∞)(Γ) ⟺ π_Γ is a trivial fibration ⟺ π_Γ is a weak equivalence.
8. **Homotopical computads.** Γ is *homotopical* when the comparison from the free (∞,ω)-category on Γ (generators attached by homotopy pushouts) to F(Γ) is an equivalence. Strongly loop-free Steiner complexes with unital basis, and regular directed complexes with frame-acyclic molecules, are homotopical (Campion, arXiv 2311.00200; Chanavat, arXiv 2603.22242, Thm 3.17). **A finite 2-polygraph is homotopical if and only if F(Γ) has no bubble** (no non-identity 2-cell 1_x ⇒ 1_x); in particular every 2-polygraph whose 2-generators all have non-empty target (or all non-empty source) is homotopical, loops and cycles in the 1-graph allowed. Bubble-freeness is undecidable. For a homotopical context, RC_id(∞)(Γ) is equivalent to the unit of the realization adjunction between CaTT models and (∞,ω)-categories being a weak equivalence at the free CaTT model on Γ.
9. **Partial cylinders are strong Steiner.** For Y ⊆ Var(Q) up-closed, the complex K_Y obtained from the Steiner tensor I ⊗ λQ by identifying c⁻ = c⁺ and killing Ic for c ∉ Y is strongly loop-free and unital, with ∂⁻-iterates (∂⁻)^j Ix = (σ^{j−1}x)⁻ + I(τ^j x), (∂⁺)^j Ix = (τ^{j−1}x)⁺ + I(σ^j x) (omitted terms zero); so F(Q↑Y) ≅ ν(K_Y).
10. **Cartesian strictification.** Call Γ *positive* if every generator of dimension ≥ 2 of its polygraph has non-identity source and target. For every generator-preserving map f : Δ → Γ of positive CaTT contexts (variables to variables, attaching terms preserved), the square Free(Δ) → Free(Γ) over F(Δ) → F(Γ) is a pullback in every dimension: a term t of Γ and a strict cell d of F(Δ) over π(t) have exactly one lift. (Proof: Henry's theorem that composition is cartesian on positive polygraphs, *Non-unital polygraphs form a presheaf category*, Cor. 2.3.13, 2.4.9, plus induction on terms.)
11. **Reduction to polyplexes.** For a strict cell c of F(Γ), Γ positive, let P_c → Γ be its generic (Henry) polyplex, with the uniquely lifted weak attaching terms. Then π_Γ is a trivial fibration iff every π_{P_c} is; the same holds for RC(n) and RC_id(n) for each n. Positive 2-polyplexes are strong Steiner, so **RC(n) for every finite positive context of dimension ≤ 2 is equivalent to RC(n) for every finite strong-Steiner context of dimension ≤ 2** (no hypothesis on the 1-graph).
12. **The fibre over one generator.** If Γ = (Λ, z : a → b) is a strong Steiner atom polygraph with z of top dimension m, then Free(Γ) ×_{F(Γ)} 𝔻^m is the free extension, by one m-cell, of Free(Λ) ×_{F(Λ)} ∂𝔻^m (𝔻^m the strict m-globe mapped onto π(z)). So the trivial-fibration property over the cell π(z) would follow from: *adjoining an m-cell to a trivial fibration B → ∂𝔻^m, along an attaching sphere over the two faces, gives a trivial fibration B[ẑ] → 𝔻^m.* That statement is unproved, and it does not cover strict composites of z with other generators.
13. **Arity lemma.** If a coherence coh_{P,A}[γ] has a substituted strict boundary using a top generator z exactly once, the variables of P labelled by terms involving z form one suspended subtree Σ^m Q of P's Batanin tree.
14. **Maximal variables.** If Y ⊆ Var(P) consists of variables occurring in no other variable's boundary, P↑Y is a pasting context, and every term over P (any dimension) has a naturality term over P↑Y with its full cylinder boundary. This does not give compatibility under substitutions that change a selected variable's boundary.
15. **Not counterexamples.** Chanavat's 4-dimensional regular directed complex (arXiv 2509.26563, Comment 2.67) has a polygraph Q whose strict free category differs from the "stricter" one, but the extra equations appear only in Q → r^>Q, so they do not give equal strict images in F(Q). Benjamin–Markakis's hom-computads (hom weak ω-categories of free computads are free) do not transport π: for (x), the hom computad has distinct 0-cells id_x and id_x ∗₀ id_x, while Hom_{F(x)}(x, x) has one.
16. **A related open conjecture.** Dean (*Globular multicategories with homomorphism types*, thesis, Thm 5.4.0.1) proves an all-dimensional acyclic-fibration strictification for fibrational homomorphism types with a strict computation law; the weak analogue is his Conj. 5.4.0.9. No equivalence with RC is known.

17. **Two-dimensional bubbles obstruct RC.** Let Λ be a finite context of dimension ≤ 2 such that F(Λ) has a non-identity 2-cell c : 1_x ⇒ 1_x. Then there are parallel 3-terms T, T′ with π(T) = π(T′) and no 4-term T → T′ at all, so RC(3)(Λ) fails. Proof:
    - Choose a 2-term U with π(U) = c, and correct its boundaries by coherences over the context (x) to get a : 1_x ⇒ 1_x literally, with π(a) = c.
    - Take T = eh(a, a), the Eckmann–Hilton 3-term a ∗₁ a → a ∗₁ a (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2402.01611, §6.1), and T′ = 1_{a∗₁a}.
    - Interpret Λ in the double delooping 𝒢 of the strict braided monoidal groupoid with objects ℕ, End(p) = ℤ, ⊗ = + and braiding β_{p,q} = pq. Every 1-cell goes to the unique 1-cell, and every 2-generator to 1.
    - 𝒢 is a Gray category, so it is an algebra of the Gray-category 3-operad. That operad is contractible with top-dimensional uniqueness (Batanin–Cisinski–Weber, arXiv 1209.2776, Ex. 3.6, §4.5, Ex. 5.7), and extended by identities it is a CaTT model with only identity 4-cells.
    - There, [[T]] = ±N² and [[T′]] = 0, where N > 0 is the number of 2-generators in c.
18. **Heredity.** If Δ ⊆ Γ is dependency-closed, RC_id(n)(Γ) ⇒ RC_id(n)(Δ): an identity-image filler H has supp(H) = supp(πH) ⊆ Var(Δ), by item 2. With item 17: if F(Γ_{≤2}) has a bubble, then RC_id(3)(Γ) fails, whatever the higher generators of Γ. For finite contexts of dimension ≤ 2: RC_id(∞) ⇒ RC(3) ⇒ bubble-free ⇔ homotopical.
19. **Higher endo-cells obstruct homotopicality.** If F(Γ) has a non-identity n-cell q : 1^{n−1−k}_c ⇒ 1^{n−1−k}_c (0 ≤ k ≤ n − 2, c a k-cell), then Γ is not homotopical. Proof: the n-skeleton inclusion is discrete Conduché, so homotopicality passes to Γ_{≤n}. Counting n-generators gives F(Γ_{≤n}) → Bⁿℤ, and q gives Σ^{n−2}B²ℕ → F(Γ_{≤n}). Steenrod powers then detect nonzero mod-p cohomology of the groupoidification in unboundedly many degrees, while the free cellular n-skeleton has dimension n. The converse ("no such endo-cell ⇒ homotopical") is proved in dimension 2 (item 8) and for positive-to-one polygraphs in every dimension, and is open in general.
20. **Higher bubbles obstruct RC_id(∞) in every dimension.** Suppose F(Γ) has an n-cell q : 1_b ⇒ 1_b, q ≠ 1, n ≥ 2, where b is an (n−2)-cell (possibly itself an iterated identity). Then π_Γ is not a trivial fibration, so RC_id(∞)(Γ) fails. Composite q of even weight and b of positive dimension are included. Proof:
    - *A list model.* Fix n and a set C of colours. Take one cell in each dimension below n, finite lists in C as n-cells, colour-preserving bijections of positions as (n+1)-cells, and identities above. This is a CaTT model, natural in recolourings. Every n-head concatenates its input lists, one block per n-generator occurrence of its generic strict cell, in a fixed order. Every (n+1)-head matches positions: give the positions in each *slot* distinct auxiliary colours, transport them along the labelled bijections, and match equal colours. A slot is a class of n-variables of the pasting context joined by (n+1)-variables. Two facts about generic pasting contexts make this well defined: each strict n-cell uses each slot at most once, and the two n-faces of a higher cell use the same slots. Both are proved by the decomposition of a Batanin tree into suspended subtrees, and checked by machine on all trees with at most 8 edges. (n+2)-heads are well defined because colour-preserving bijections between lists without repeated auxiliary colours are unique.
    - *The separation.* With distinct colours, the suspended Eckmann–Hilton term eh_{n−2}(a, b) : a ∗_{n−1} b → b ∗_{n−1} a (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2402.01611, §§6.1, 6.3) must be the block exchange, and by naturality it stays the block exchange after recolouring to one colour. For a = b of length m > 0 this is not the identity.
    - *The reduction.* By heredity (item 18), pass to the n-skeleton Λ. Boundary lifting gives a with literal type 1_d → 1_d and π(a) = q. Send each n-generator to a one-element list, so a has length m = (number of n-generators in q) > 0. Then eh(a, a) and 1_{a∗a} are parallel, have the same identity image, and are separated in the model.
21. **The unrestricted extension theorem is false.** Adjoin α : 1_f ⇒ 1_f (a 3-generator on the weak identity of f) to the 2-disc (x, y; f, g : x → y; β : f ⇒ g). The 2-disc satisfies RC_id(∞) (item 2), but the extended context fails RC(4) by item 20 with n = 3. The same works in each attachment dimension m ≥ 3. So adjoining generators can preserve RC_id(∞) at best for attachments that keep the context homotopical.
22. **Split criterion and the reduction of necessity to reconstruction.** Let A = Free(Γ), let C = F(Γ) as a CaTT model, and let q : KC → C be the computadic cofibrant replacement. KC has one generator for each parallel pair (u, v) of cells of KC and each strict cell c : q(u) → q(v) (Dean–Finster–Markakis–Reutter–Vicary, arXiv 2208.08719, §5; Markakis, arXiv 2303.11978, §5). Sending each generator x of Γ to the generator labelled by π(x) and its already-embedded boundary gives a generator inclusion i : A → KC over C. KC is A with the remaining generators freely attached.
    - **RC_id(∞)(Γ) holds iff there is a strict CaTT map r : KC → A with r i = id_A and π r = q.** (⇒) extends id_A across the remaining generators by boundary lifting. (⇐) lifts through q and applies r.
    - Let L be realization from CaTT models to (∞,ω)-categories, and N the nerve. Let e : LA → NC and d : LKC → NC be induced by π and q. Then e is the comparison defining homotopicality, and d is the *reconstruction map* of the strict target C. We have d ∘ Li = e. Under the split criterion we also have e ∘ Lr = d and Lr ∘ Li = id, so **e is a retract of d over NC**. Hence RC_id(∞)(Γ) together with "d is an equivalence for C = F(Γ)" implies that Γ is homotopical. More precisely, under RC_id(∞)(Γ), Γ is homotopical iff there is s : NC → LKC with d ∘ s ≃ id and s ∘ e ≃ Li.

23. **Inductive reconstruction fails; coinductive reconstruction suffices.** With the notation of item 22, the inductive reconstruction map d_C : L(KC) → NC is **not** an equivalence for any nonempty strict C. Proof:
    - Let D be the free strict ω-category with one object p, a loop ℓ, and in each dimension k ≥ 2 one generator for each ordered parallel pair of (k−1)-cells. D is gaunt, because top-generator counts are additive, and D → 1 has boundary lifting in every dimension.
    - K1 has one object v and a loop generator u. Sending u to ℓ and to 1_p gives two maps L(K1) → ND. They restrict to different points of the discrete space Map(D¹, ND), so they lie in different components, while Map(N1, ND) is a single point. So d₁ is not an equivalence.
    - For nonempty C, 1 → C → 1 exhibits d₁ as a retract of d_C.
    
    However, D → 1 is an FHM weak equivalence, so Q(ND) ≃ 1, where Q is coinductive completion. For Γ of generator dimension ≤ m, both ends of e lie in (∞,m)-categories. That subcategory is coreflective, with the m-core as right adjoint, so it is closed under the colimits building LA, and its objects are coinductively complete (Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde, arXiv 2603.11005, Lemmas 4.4, 5.22–5.23). Hence **e is an equivalence iff Qe is**, and by the retract of item 22, **RC_id(∞)(Γ) together with Q d_{F(Γ)} being an equivalence implies that Γ is homotopical**. Equivalently, with η the unit at A and W the FHM weak equivalences: given RC_id(∞), Γ is homotopical iff η ∈ W; given homotopicality, RC_id(∞) holds iff η ∈ W.
24. **Simultaneous algebraic lifts of strict simplices.** Suppose π : A → C has globular boundary lifting. Call a strict CaTT map h : K Oₙ → A with π h = σ q an *algebraic lift* of a strict simplex σ : Oₙ → C (Oₙ is Street's oriental). The pairs (σ, h) form a simplicial set, and forgetting h is a trivial fibration onto the Street nerve of C. Proof: the face suborientals satisfy O(F) ∩ O(G) = O(F ∩ G) (Steiner's tableaux), and the subcomputads K O(F) ⊆ K Oₙ satisfy the same formula. So compatible face lifts glue, and the remaining generators lift one at a time. This does not give the geometric section s of item 22, which needs a comparison from geometric simplices to the realizations of K Oₙ.
25. **The dimension-3 exchange step reduces to a wedge lemma.** Assume (H): Γ is finite, of dimension ≤ 2, has an acyclic generating 1-graph, and every 2-generator has nonempty source and target paths. Then the 2-paths representing a strict 2-cell are exactly the linear extensions of an event poset, and exchange moves are swaps of adjacent incomparable events (item 5).
    - Under (H), a contiguous antichain of events in a 2-path spans, in its generic polyplex (item 11), a wedge A₁ ∨₀ ⋯ ∨₀ A_k of single-generator contexts glued at cut vertices. So, by the polyplex reduction, the part of RC(3) that concerns permuting such a block is exactly **the wedge lemma**: RC(3)(A₁ ∨₀ ⋯ ∨₀ A_k) follows from RC(3)(A_i) for each i.
    - For k = 2 this is known: two independent letters with a cut vertex are handled by horizontal normal forms and one interchange coherence.
    - The rest of RC(3) under (H) consists of forced-order statements: letters joined by connectors that cross their boundary blocks, whose fibres have a single linear extension. This includes a fusion statement comparing normalisers along substitution. These are of the kind already proved for cylinders and grids.
    - The smallest wedge with k = 3 is the polyplex of the hexagon triple in the 3 × 3 grid: three lax squares glued at two cut vertices. Its key ingredient is the three-factor interchange (α ∗₀ β ∗₀ γ) · (α′ ∗₀ β′ ∗₀ γ′) ⇛ (α·α′) ∗₀ (β·β′) ∗₀ (γ·γ′).

26. **Both comparisons are 0-truncations.** In coinductively complete (∞,ω)-categories, Q d_C : QL(KC) → NC and Q e : QLA → NC both exhibit NC as the **0-truncation** of their source, in the mapping-space sense: every space of maps into a 0-truncated object is a 0-type, though such an object can have non-identity directed cells in every dimension.
    - *Algebraic descent.* For a globular trivial fibration p : X → C of CaTT models with C strict, and any strict D in which coinductively invertible positive-dimensional cells are identities, precomposition Hom_str(C, D) → Hom_CaTT(X, D) is a bijection. Two cells over the same strict cell are joined by a lifted globe cylinder whose mixed generators lie over identities. Those generators are therefore coinductively invertible, so they become identities in D.
    - The 0-truncated objects are exactly the discrete, coinductively complete Segal Θ-nerves (Berger; Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde). Precomposition with q (by descent) and with π (by freeness) is bijective on maps into them.
    - **Consequence: Γ is homotopical iff π_j Map(𝔻ⁿ, QLA) = 0 for all n ≥ 0 and j ≥ 1, at every base point.** Reconstruction at C is the same vanishing for QL(KC).

## 4. Questions

**Question 1 (main: the exact class).** Prove that for every finite CaTT context Γ,

> RC_id(∞)(Γ)  ⟺  Γ is homotopical,

equivalently (item 7) that π_Γ : Free(Γ) → F(Γ) is a trivial fibration of CaTT models exactly when the polygraph of Γ is homotopical. Or give a counterexample to either direction with a separating invariant.

- **(⇐, the contraction).** This is the main content. By item 11 the positive case reduces to generic positive polyplexes, and by item 12 the strong-Steiner case reduces to one attachment statement plus composites. Routes that would do:
  - (a) prove the attachment statement of item 12 for arbitrary trivial fibrations B → ∂𝔻^m of CaTT models (a homotopy-pushout property of free cell extensions of CaTT models), and extend it from a single generator to strict composites;
  - (b) describe the fibre of π_Γ over each strict cell as a CaTT-definable object and show it is contractible;
  - (c) a relative Guiraud–Malbos normalisation strategy towards the strict cells of F(Γ), producing all higher comparisons at once;
  - (d) an equivalence with Dean's Conj. 5.4.0.9 or another named open statement;
  - (e) by item 22, construct for every finite homotopical Γ a retraction r : KC → A over C with r i = id, for example by contracting the fibres of π over the cells of C, or by a homotopy-pushout property of attaching the generators of KC that are not in A. Item 24 gives compatible algebraic lifts over all strict simplices, which may help organise the choices;
  - (f) a **wedge theorem in every dimension**: RC_id(∞)(A₁ ∨₀ ⋯ ∨₀ A_k) from RC_id(∞)(A_i), for contexts glued at cut vertices (and its analogue for gluing along higher cells where the gluing is a cut), together with a forced-order normalisation for the remaining fibres, which have a single linear extension. In dimension 3 under (H) this is exactly item 25, and with the polyplex reduction (item 11) it would give the positive 2-dimensional case of Question 3.
- **(⇒, necessity on bubble-free contexts).** By item 20, RC_id(∞) already forces bubble-freeness in every dimension. Prove that a finite context with RC_id(∞) is homotopical, or give a bubble-free, non-homotopical context with RC_id(∞), together with a separating invariant. By item 26 this is a **vanishing statement**: under RC_id(∞), show that the globe mapping spaces Map(𝔻ⁿ, QLA) have no positive homotopy at any base point. By items 22–23 it also suffices to show the same vanishing for QL(KC) with C = F(Γ), for all free strict C, all gaunt C, or all C. The inductive d_C is never an equivalence (item 23). Possible routes: a description of Map(𝔻ⁿ, LA) for a free CaTT model A as a space of globe labellings up to coherence cells, so that RC_id(∞) contracts each component; or a direct computation for KC, whose generators are labelled by cells of C. A proof that bubble-freeness implies homotopicality in every dimension would also settle this direction.

**Question 2 (fallback for (⇐), still every dimension).** If Question 1(⇐) is out of reach, prove RC_id(∞) for every finite context that is positive in every dimension, or at least for every finite strong-Steiner context, and give the counterexample that separates the classes if one exists.

**Question 3 (fallback, every n).** Prove RC_id(n) for every n for every finite 2-dimensional context that is bubble-free (equivalently homotopical, item 8). By item 11, for positive contexts it suffices to treat finite strong-Steiner 2-dimensional contexts. Then prove an extension theorem: adjoining generators of any dimension ≥ 3 to a context with RC_id(∞), **such that the result is still homotopical** (or still bubble-free in every dimension), preserves RC_id(∞). Item 21 shows the condition is needed. Together these answer Question 1(⇐) for every homotopical context whose 2-skeleton is homotopical. For contexts satisfying (H), item 25 reduces the dimension-3 case to the wedge lemma plus forced-order statements. The useful answer is a proof of those for every k and in every dimension n, not a further single wedge.

## 5. What counts as a solution

A proof of either direction of Question 1 (for (⇒), coinductive reconstruction at free strict targets suffices), of Question 2, or of Question 3 with the restricted extension theorem; a counterexample in one of the named classes, with a proof that no connecting term exists (for example an invariant of a model); an equivalence with a named open problem; or a published reference. An existence proof of the connecting cells is enough, provided it is a proof about CaTT terms or CaTT models.

## Coinductive reconstruction gives the 0-truncation

Let $C=F(Γ)$ for a finite context, $A=\mathrm{Free}(Γ)$, and let $Q,L,N$ be as in items 22–23. In the ∞-category of coinductively complete $(∞,ω)$-categories, **both comparison maps exhibit their common target as the 0-truncation of their source**:

$$
QL(KC)\xrightarrow{Qd_C}NC,\qquad QLA\xrightarrow{Qe}NC.
$$

Here 0-truncation has its mapping-space meaning. An object $Z$ is 0-truncated if every space of maps into $Z$ is a 0-type. Such an object can have nonidentity directed cells in arbitrarily high dimensions. The assertion about $Qd_C$ also holds for any strict $C$ whose discrete Θ-nerve is coinductively complete.

**Algebraic descent.** Suppose $p:X\to C$ is a globular trivial fibration of CaTT models with strict codomain. Let $D$ be a strict ω-category in which every coinductively invertible positive-dimensional cell is an identity. Then precomposition is a bijection

$$
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{str}\,ω\mathrm{Cat}}(C,D)
\longrightarrow \mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{CaTT}}(X,D).
$$

To prove it, fix $h:X\to D$ and any two $n$-cells $u,v$ with $p(u)=p(v)=c$. Their boundaries in $X$ can differ. Use the CaTT presentation of the globe cylinder $I\otimes\mathbb D^n$ with its two endpoint globes. The endpoint variables have their globular types, and the cylinder is obtained from their disjoint union by freely adjoining the mixed generators in increasing dimension. Below, map the strict cylinder to $C$ by collapsing $I$ and selecting $c$. Above, prescribe $u$ and $v$ on the two endpoint globes. For each mixed generator its two attaching terms now evaluate to parallel cells in $X$ with the prescribed images in $C$. Boundary lifting for $p$ extends the assignment across that generator. This includes $n=0$, when the unique mixed generator is an edge. [Globe-cylinder presentation, item 6](../answered/canonical-gray-terms-r5-2026-11-19.md)

Every mixed generator maps under the collapse to an identity. Cells in $X$ over identities are coinductively invertible. Indeed a reverse cell over the same identity lifts against the reversed boundary. Both composites admit cancellation witnesses over identities by another application of boundary lifting, so the class satisfies the coinductive inverse condition. The strict map $h$ preserves this inverse data. Consequently the composite cylinder labelling in $D$ sends every mixed generator to an identity.

Since $D$ is strict, this labelling evaluates the strict Gray cylinder. For a face generator $x$ of the globe, its cylinder generator has source and target obtained by whiskering $x^-$ and $x^+$ with the mixed generators on their lower faces, as in §1.3. In $D$ these lower whiskerings are by identities. Starting with vertices and proceeding up the globe, the two faces of the mixed generator therefore evaluate to the two endpoint values of $x$. The mixed generator itself is an identity, so those values coincide. At the top this gives $h(u)=h(v)$.

Boundary lifting makes $p$ surjective on cells, by first lifting a labelling of the boundary of a globe and then its top cell. Thus $h$ descends uniquely as a globular map $\bar h:C\to D$. For each CaTT operation, lift its whole labelled globular pasting arity through $p$, one variable at a time. On this lift the equation expressing preservation of the operation by $\bar h$ is the corresponding equation for $h$. This proves that $\bar h$ preserves all operations, including identities and binary composites, as required.

**Truncation.** The 0-truncated objects of the coinductive theory are exactly its discrete complete Segal Θ-nerves. Indeed if $Z$ is 0-truncated, evaluating at any Θ-shape $P$ gives the 0-type $\mathrm{Map}(QP,Z)$. Taking components then gives an equivalent set-valued presheaf. Its Segal bijections are the strict ω-category nerve conditions. Conversely, mapping into a set-valued presheaf gives 0-types, also in the full subcategory of local objects. Completeness forces invertible cells in a discrete nerve to be identities, and coinductive completeness does so for coinductively invertible cells. The latter condition uses the coinductive inverse criterion, which in a discrete strict nerve evaluates on the cells and compositions of the strict category itself. For our finite $Γ$, $NC$ is discrete by gauntness and coinductively complete by the dimension bound in item 23. [Berger, Theorem 1.12](https://math.univ-cotedazur.fr/u/cberger/nerve.pdf) · [Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde, Propositions 5.29 and 5.42](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11005v2)

Test $Qd_C$ against any such target $ND$. Locality identifies maps out of $QL(KC)$ into $ND$ with maps out of $L(KC)$. These form a discrete space whose points, by the realization adjunction at a gaunt target, are the strict CaTT maps $KC\to D$. Maps $NC\to ND$ form the discrete space of strict ω-functors $C\to D$. On these sets precomposition with $Qd_C$ is precomposition with $q:KC\to C$, a bijection by algebraic descent. This proves the universal property of 0-truncation. For $Qe$ the same calculation gives precomposition with $π:A\to C$, a bijection already by freeness: an assignment of the generators of $Γ$ in a strict target evaluates every attaching term at its strict image and extends uniquely to a strict ω-functor from $F(Γ)$. [Realization at discrete gaunt targets, Known (II)](catt-models-unit-condition-r4.md)

**The remaining geometric test.** For $Z=QL(KC)$, reconstruction at $C$ is now equivalent to

$$
\pi_j\!\left(\mathrm{Map}(\mathbb D^n,Z),z\right)=0
\quad\text{for every }n\geq0,\ j\geq1,\ z\in\mathrm{Map}(\mathbb D^n,Z).
$$

Indeed these vanishings say precisely that the spaces of globe labellings are 0-types. The Segal condition computes the space of labellings of any pasting shape as a limit of those spaces. Hence the entire Θ-presheaf is 0-truncated, and its truncation map is an equivalence. The converse follows by evaluating a 0-truncated target on globes. The identical test with $Z=QLA$ characterizes homotopicality of $Γ$ by item 23. In particular, necessity in Question 1 would follow from vanishing for $QLA$ under $\mathrm{RC}_{\mathrm{id}}(∞)$. The base point and sphere in this test can be arbitrary geometric data. The cylinder descent proof identifies maps into discrete targets and leaves this positive-degree vanishing open.

## A geometric sphere can be tested on the support of its strict label

Write $X_Γ=L\mathrm{Free}(Γ)$ and $C_Γ=F(Γ)$. For a strict $n$-cell $c$, let $E_Γ(c)$ be the homotopy fibre over $c$ of the map from $\mathrm{Map}(\mathbb D^n,X_Γ)$ to the discrete set of strict $n$-cells of $C_Γ$. Let $Δ$ consist of the generators used by $c$ and their iterated boundary dependencies. Then the inclusion induces an equivalence

$$E_Δ(c)\ \simeq\ E_Γ(c).$$

In particular, if $Γ$ satisfies $\mathrm{RC}_{\mathrm{id}}(∞)$ and every generator in the support of $c$ has dimension at most two, **$E_Γ(c)$ is contractible**. This includes every strict label on the 0-, 1-, and 2-globes of a finite context of arbitrary dimension, as well as iterated identities on those labels.

**Proof.** More generally take any dependency-closed $Δ\subseteq Γ$. The support of an identity is the support of its base, and the support of a defined composite is the union of the supports of its factors, counting boundary dependencies throughout. These rules respect the strict unit, associativity and exchange equations. Membership in $C_Δ$ is equivalent to having support in $Δ$. Consequently a factorization of a cell of $C_Δ$ in $C_Γ$ has both factors in $C_Δ$. Identity factorizations lift as well, and the inclusion makes the lifts unique. Thus $C_Δ\hookrightarrow C_Γ$ is discrete Conduché, so pullback along it preserves colimits. [Loubaton, Theorem 2.2.2.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425)

Apply this pullback to the homotopy pushouts attaching the generators of $X_Γ$. A generator in $Δ$ retains its whole globe and attaching boundary. For a generator outside $Δ$, every cell of its globe outside the boundary is the top generator or an iterated identity on it. All of these have images outside $C_Δ$. Thus the pulled-back globe and its pulled-back boundary coincide, even if several boundary faces have the same image. It follows by induction over the attachments that $X_Δ\simeq X_Γ\times_{NC_Γ}NC_Δ$. For these finite contexts the realizations and strict targets are already coinductively complete by item 23. Applying $\mathrm{Map}(\mathbb D^n,-)$ preserves the pullback. Taking its fibre at any $c$ in $C_Δ$ gives the asserted equivalence. [Skeletal pullback calculation, §§3.1–3.2](../answered/fibration-presentations-all-dimensions-2026-11-19.md)

Now choose $Δ$ to be the support of $c$. Item 18 gives $\mathrm{RC}_{\mathrm{id}}(∞)(Δ)$. If $\dim Δ\le 2$, the bubble obstruction and the finite 2-polygraph theorem (items 8 and 18) give homotopicality of $Δ$. Its comparison is therefore an equivalence. Evaluation on any globe is still an equivalence, and its fibre over $c$ is contractible.

**Where the necessity test first remains open.** Suppose a counterexample to necessity has as few generators as possible, and let $m$ be its generator dimension. Then $m\ge3$, and some strict $m$-cell using every generator has a fibre with a nonzero positive homotopy group. Indeed Response 1 detects failure on a globe, over some strict label. Its support inherits RC and has the same fibre, hence minimality forces full support. A globe of dimension below $m$ cannot have such a label. A globe of dimension $n>m$ gives the same test as the $m$-globe: both targets of evaluation ($X_Γ$ and $NC_Γ$) are $(∞,m)$-categories, and intelligent $m$-truncation sends $\mathbb D^n$ to $\mathbb D^m$. In particular the fibre is unchanged on passing from the iterated identity label to its $m$-dimensional base. For a full-support $m$-cell the pullback above returns $Γ$ itself. Proving vanishing in these fibres, or finding a nonzero class in one, would decide necessity. [Loubaton, Construction 2.2.1.40 and Proposition 2.2.1.43](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425)

## A positive, bubble-free 3-computad with a circle as a full-support fibre

Positivity in Question 2 is insufficient. There is a finite positive context $Γ$ with acyclic generating 1-graph whose lower skeleton satisfies $\mathrm{RC}_{\mathrm{id}}(∞)$, but in which two parallel 4-terms of identical strict image have no connecting 5-term. It also disproves the bubble-free variant of the extension in Question 3. Its geometric comparison and its algebraic relative coherence both fail.

**The context.** On four objects in a line take edges $f_0,f_1:0\to1$, $g:1\to2$ and $h_0,h_1:2\to3$. Take parallel pairs
$\alpha_0,\alpha_1:f_0\Rightarrow f_1$ and $\beta_0,\beta_1:h_0\Rightarrow h_1$. Write juxtaposition with an edge for whiskering. Attach just
$$
 A:\alpha_0g\longrightarrow\alpha_1g,\qquad
 B:g\beta_0\longrightarrow g\beta_1 .
$$
Use the CaTT binary whiskerings for these attaching terms. Every generating face is nonidentity. A nonidentity 2-cell strictly increases the number of occurrences of $f_1,h_1$ in its boundary path. A nonidentity 3-cell strictly increases the number of occurrences of $\alpha_1,\beta_1$ in its boundary 2-cell. These counts are additive under contextual substitution, so there are no bubbles. Every strict cell in the lower skeleton has support in a globular pasting consisting of a horizontal string of edges and 2-discs. Indeed a 2-cell can use at most one of the two $\alpha$'s and at most one of the two $\beta$'s. By items 2 and 7 the lower skeleton has relative coherence in every dimension. Items 1 and 4 now give RC through degree three on $Γ$. All strict endomorphisms in positive dimensions are identities here, so the identity-image conditions hold in those degrees.

**The geometric calculation.** Write $E$ for the discrete pair, $J=[1]$, $Q$ for the walking parallel pair of arrows, and $D$ for the walking 2-cell, with inclusion $Q\hookrightarrow D$. Use cartesian iterated enrichment for $(∞,3)$-categories. All pushouts in this calculation are homotopy pushouts. The object sets in both enrichment steps are discrete: the only arrows between distinct objects go forward in a line or a square, and all endomorphism objects are terminal. Thus completion adds no object equivalences. [Haugseng, rectification and comparison with iterated enrichment](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.3881)

Put $X=L\mathrm{Free}(Γ)$. Its successive outer hom objects along the line are $Q,1,Q$. Each of the two hom objects spanning two edges is $D$. The remaining hom object is
$$
 X(0,3)=(D\times Q)\underset{Q\times Q}{\amalg}(Q\times D)
 \quad\text{in }\mathrm{Cat}_{(∞,2)} .
$$
To verify the formula, start with the free enriched line. Composition along it gives the products of the consecutive homs. Attaching $A$ extends the hom from 0 to 2 along $Q\to D$ and forces its right composites with $Q$. Attaching $B$ extends the hom from 1 to 3 in the same way and forces its left composites with $Q$. There is no path containing both of these length-two intervals. The only triple composition to specify is along the original three edges. Its associativity gives precisely the identification over $Q\times Q$ in the pushout. Equivalently, maps into any enriched target with the four objects prescribed specify the two compositors with their homotopy of agreement on the triple product. Strings of nonidentity edges have length at most three. This verifies the enriched universal property. The free inner cells represented by $Q\to D$ have exactly the two prescribed whiskered faces. Choices of their semantic identification with the CaTT whiskerings are contractible in the lower skeleton, which is homotopical by item 8.

The four objects of this inner pushout are $f_i g h_j$. Its four side homs are all $J$. Its diagonal hom $V$ from $f_0gh_0$ to $f_1gh_1$ is the following pushout in $\mathrm{Cat}_∞$:
$$
 V=(J\times J)\underset{P}{\amalg}(J\times J),\qquad
 P=(J\times E)\underset{E\times E}{\amalg}(E\times J).
$$
Here is the calculation of the diagonal, where the distinction between the two squares matters. In the three input objects $D\times Q$, $Q\times Q$ and $Q\times D$, the diagonal homs are respectively $J\times E$, $E\times E$ and $E\times J$. Their colimit is $P$. In each input there are two routes along sides between the opposite objects. The product of the side homs for *each* route is that input's diagonal hom, with its canonical product compositor. The colimit of the old route products is therefore $P\amalg P$, mapping by the fold to $P$. After taking colimits on sides, the two route products are $J\times J$ and $J\times J$. Extending the two compositors takes the pushout along $P\amalg P$, giving the formula. This is the universal property for the four-object enriched square: specifying its diagonal hom and a compositor from each route supplies all nonidentity compositions, since there are no length-three routes inside this square.

Present $V$ by two simplicial squares $\Delta^1\times\Delta^1$ glued along their four perimeter edges. This computes the pushout since the perimeter presents $P$ and its inclusions are monomorphisms in the Joyal model structure. Its vertices $u_{ij}$ are the strict 2-cells given by $\alpha_i$, $g$ and $\beta_j$ in horizontal order. There is one strict 3-cell $c:u_{00}\to u_{11}$: strict interchange identifies the two serial orders of $A,B$, and each order can be performed in either of the two simultaneous diagrams. Equivalently, the identical enriched pushout calculation with strict categories gives the ordinary commutative square for the diagonal. The label $c$ uses every generator, including $g$.

The mapping space of $V$ from $u_{00}$ to $u_{11}$ is a circle. In the necklace rigidification there are four vertices: the two perimeter routes and one diagonal edge from each square. Each of the four triangles joins its diagonal to its perimeter route, giving a four-cycle. There are no higher nondegenerate flags: on a route from these endpoints a necklace has room for at most one interior vertex. This also proves that each identity and each perimeter edge has a contractible mapping space over its label. The spaces of choices of the two lower globular boundaries are discrete up to equivalence in this calculation. Hence the circle is the actual geometric fibre over $c$. [Dugger–Spivak, §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.0814)

**Separating CaTT terms.** Fix two 1-terms $s,t$ lifting $f_0gh_0,f_1gh_1$ with common bracketing. There are two intermediate 1-paths $f_1gh_0,f_0gh_1$. Choose a weak representative of each. Each intermediate path supplies a map into $Γ$ from the globular pasting context of two 3-discs composable along a 1-disc. In the first map the rows are $A$ whiskered by $h_0$, then $B$ whiskered by $f_1$. In the second they are $B$ whiskered by $f_0$, then $A$ whiskered by $h_1$. For literal matching along the cut, choose the row's two 2-faces over their indicated strict labels with the prescribed 1-boundaries, then its 3-cell over the indicated whiskered generator. Boundary lifting in dimensions two and three follows from RC through degree two and strict surjectivity as in §1.2. In particular no assertion in degree four is needed to choose these maps.

In the generic two-disc context, compose a face of each disc along dimension one to obtain four corners. Composing one disc with one face gives their four side 3-terms. The two serial composites of sides are parallel 3-terms. The coherence head over the whole pasting context between them is a 4-term, since both composites have full support. Orient it from the route changing $\alpha$ first to the route changing $\beta$ first, reversing the generic orientation on the second copy as necessary. Applying the two substitutions gives tiles $H$ and $K$. Their images are the two square homotopies in $V$. Indeed the realization of the generic paste is its strict nerve by item 8. Composition of its two rows maps its square into $V$ by the compositor at the chosen intermediate object. The row labellings agree up to contractible choice with the given ones: their individual 3-cell fibres are perimeter-edge mapping spaces, and their common 1-cut has discrete fibre. The globe pushout for the generic paste therefore identifies the two maps up to homotopy over their labels. Within the generic square the homotopy between its perimeter routes is unique up to homotopy relative endpoints. Thus each tile goes through the diagonal of its own square in the four-cycle.

For clarity, the two tiles can be compared with *literal parallel endpoints* in CaTT. Write $U^H_p,U^K_p$ for the 2-terms at each corner $p$. They all have the fixed outer boundaries $s,t$. Choose $k_p:U^H_p\to U^K_p$ over the identity by RC in degree two. For each perimeter edge $p\to q$ with respective 3-terms $l_H,l_K$, choose a 4-term
$$
 k_q\circ l_H\ \Longrightarrow\ l_K\circ k_p .
$$
This uses RC in degree three, already proved for $Γ$. Here $\circ$ denotes composition of 3-cells over a 2-face. Let $H_1,H_2$ be the two serial 3-composites in the first tile and $K_1,K_2$ the corresponding ones in the second. From $k_{11}\circ H_1$ to $K_2\circ k_{00}$ form two 4-terms. The first uses the first tile whiskered by $k_{11}$ and then the edge comparisons on route 2. The second uses the edge comparisons on route 1 and then the second tile whiskered by $k_{00}$. Insert associators between the consecutive comparisons with the displayed common bracketing. These are ordinary bicategorical pastes in the twice-iterated hom, using CaTT composition, whiskering and associators. Both terms have strict image $1_c$.

Realization sends these pastes to the corresponding pastes of paths in the mapping space. One can check the operations separately: each binary composition, unit or associator comes from its generic globular paste, where the comparison with the semantic composite is unique up to homotopy over its label by homotopicality. Substitution transports those comparisons. The associativity and unit comparisons agree on iterated pastes by the same check in the generic paste of the inputs. ## The first two homotopy groups of a 3-cell fibre

Suppose $Γ$ has $\mathrm{RC}_{\mathrm{id}}(∞)$. For **every** strict 3-cell $c$, its geometric fibre $E(c)$ is connected and
$$
\pi_1E(c)=\pi_2E(c)=0.
$$
If $c$ has at most three occurrences of generating 3-cells, $E(c)$ is contractible. The occurrences can repeat and their whiskerings are arbitrary. In particular a failure of necessity detected in dimension three requires at least four occurrences and a homotopy group in degree at least three.

### A presentation of the geometric fibre

By Response 2 one can work in the 3-skeleton. Write $B$ for the 2-skeleton and $Z$ for the generating 3-cells. Form $B_k$ by adjoining to $B$ a fresh 2-generator $h(z,i)$, parallel to the two faces of $z$, for each $z\in Z$ and $1\le i\le k$. Here $B_0=B$. All these 2-polygraphs are homotopical. Indeed $B$ is bubble-free by items 18 and 20. At least one face of each $z$ is nonidentity, since otherwise $z$ would be a 3-bubble. Substituting such a face for every $h(z,i)$ sends every nonidentity 2-cell to a nonidentity by generator counts. Hence $B_k$ is bubble-free, and item 8 applies.

Fix parallel strict 1-boundaries $s,t$. Define a simplicial set $T$ by taking in degree $k$ the strict 2-cells $s\Rightarrow t$ of $F(B_k)$. Interpret $h(z,i)$ as changing from the source to the target of $z$ at step $i$. Restrict along an ordinal map by substituting the new change index, or the constant source or target when the index is never crossed. These substitutions compose strictly. In the infinity-category presented by $T$, the part of the mapping space from $u$ to $v$ labelled by $c:u\to v$ is $E(c)$.

Here are details of the last identification. Use the rectangular threefold Segal nerve, taking the innermost simplicial coordinate for the change index. Start with the presheaf homotopy pushout of the nerve of $F(B)$ and the 3-globes along their boundaries. Its complete Segal localisation is the defining cellular attachment for $X=L\mathrm{Free}(Γ)$, since $B$ is homotopical. At last index $k$ a 3-globe is horizontally the double suspension of the discrete set of monotone maps $[k]\to[1]$. The two constants give its boundary, and the $k$ changes give the fresh 2-globes above the common 1-boundary. Thus horizontal complete Segal localisation gives precisely $NF(B_k)$, naturally in $k$. [Haugseng on the rectangular and $\Theta$ models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.08480)

Only horizontal **Segal** localisation at fixed $k$ is needed. Here is a flag check using the uncompleted $\Theta_2$ presentation of the horizontal pushout. ### Cells of the mapping space

A single $d$-cube attaches one $(d-1)$-ball of morphisms from its initial to its final vertex. More precisely, in coherent rigidification the extreme hom of the cube is the nerve of the poset of vertex chains from $\varnothing$ to $[d]$ in its Boolean lattice, ordered by inclusion. The least chain is the diagonal. In the boundary cube the allowable flags of chains are those whose smallest chain has an interior cut: bead joints must divide the largest chain into beads contained in proper faces. Thus the boundary hom is the subdivision of the order complex of the nonempty proper subsets. The full hom is its cone. At every other endpoint pair the boundary already has the full hom. A facet with a specified cut $S$ is the product of the two smaller balls: chains containing $S$ are exactly pairs of chains below and above it, and the identification is the enriched composition. This describes the permutohedron with its subdivided faces, including the point for $d=1$. [Dugger–Spivak, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.0814)

Rigidification preserves the pushouts attaching cubes. Consequently a mapping space has cells indexed by composable strings of these balls, with dimension $\sum_i(d_i-1)$. This remains true when a cube has repeated vertices. Explicitly, freely compose the new morphism balls with arrows at their endpoints and impose their boundary maps; a word of interior points of balls has a unique such expression, and on a boundary facet its factor splits into the two factors at that cut. This is the free enriched-category pushout since in the individual cube no nonidentity arrow precedes its initial or follows its final vertex. Iteration gives the asserted CW description. Fibrantly replacing these homs preserves their weak homotopy types. Over weight $r>0$ every string has $\sum_i d_i=r$, so its dimension is at most $r-1$.

A vertex is a serial computation of generating 3-cells in one-hole lower contexts. An edge exchanges two consecutive occurrences in a specified two-hole context. Equality of strict labels of serial computations is generated by just these exchanges and equality of individual lower contexts, so every $E(c)$ is connected. Weight zero gives an identity point and every unary label gives a point. The only 2-cells are a square for two disjoint two-event blocks in a string, or a hexagon for a single three-event block, in each case possibly preceded, followed or separated by fixed steps. This description uses no positivity or width hypothesis on individual lower generators. [Ara et al., Appendix D.3, Propositions 6, 9 and 12](https://www.normalesup.org/~ara/files/polybook.pdf)

### Representing edges and faces by terms

Choose parallel weak lifts of the outer 1-boundaries, a weak 2-term for each intermediate strict 2-cell between them, and a 3-term for each individual rewriting step with those chosen boundaries. Use relative boundary lifting. Represent a serial computation by a fixed bracketing of its 3-terms. Its semantic identification with the corresponding vertex is made by composing the unary identifications. These choices are contractible within the unary factors.

For a simultaneous $d$-event diagram there is a globular pasting context that realises its cube. Serialise the **lower marked 2-diagram** into rows, each a whiskered ordinary lower generator or a whiskered marker. Use one 2-disc for each ordinary row and one 3-disc for each marker row, composing along 1-discs. Choose the weak 1-term at each cut, then the two faces and the whiskered 3-term of a marker row by boundary lifting. This labels the globular paste in $Γ$. Its generic extreme top hom category is the ordinary $d$-cube. Its map to $X$ realises the specified characteristic cube in $T$. In fact the canonical map in the transition presentation substitutes the whiskered marker at each time index and the original strict labels for the ordinary rows. The map from the chosen weak labelling agrees up to homotopy over the strict labels: maps out of the row paste are the pullback of the individual row maps at the cuts, all of whose label fibres are contractible by the unary and lower calculations. This also identifies the restrictions to fixed faces of the cube.

In a two-event paste the two serial paths are joined by a 4-term by absolute coherence on the paste. Its image represents the exchange edge, since the extreme hom in the generic cube is an interval with its indicated ends. To get the chosen global endpoints, connect the four corner 2-terms to their chosen representatives by identity-image 3-cells $k_p$. Along a side $p\to q$ compare $k_q l$ with $l' k_p$ by a 4-term, where $l$ is the paste side and $l'$ the chosen step. These comparisons have unary labels. Paste them to the generic exchange, cancelling the corner equivalences at the ends. This gives a 4-term between the two chosen serial paths, representing the edge with the specified vertex identifications. The corrections have unique relative path classes in the unary fibres. Choose this term once per two-hole diagram. To place it within a longer computation, whisker by the remaining factors and rebracket. Choose the rebracketing comparisons on the generic string of factors. Absolute coherence there makes their composites compatible with further regrouping.

Every edge loop in $E(c)$ is now represented by a 4-term loop at a fixed 3-term. For a backwards edge use a weak inverse, available over the identity label by boundary lifting. RC in degree four joins the resulting term to its identity by a 5-term, which realises a nullhomotopy. Thus $\pi_1E(c)=0$ for every $c$. In particular every weight-two fibre is a connected simply connected graph, hence contractible. This last conclusion controls the relative homotopy classes of the corrections needed one dimension higher.

For the square 2-cell, regard the two chosen local 4-terms as labels of two 4-discs composable along a 2-disc. The two boundary paths are the two ways to apply them consecutively. In this globular paste the interchange between those paths is a 5-term. Semantically it is the product square of the two paths under composition. This follows by evaluating in the paste: after passage to $(∞,3)$-categories the two independent 4-arrows give the two interval parameters, and in their product the two routes have a unique homotopy class of fillings relative to the sides. Constant intermediate or exterior factors are inserted by composition. Regrouping changes the boundary by the associativity comparisons on the generic string.

For the hexagon, use the row paste for the three marked occurrences. Inside it, use the two-event construction on each boundary facet with the third step fixed before or after it. The two three-edge routes from one serialization to its reverse give parallel 4-terms. Absolute coherence in the whole paste supplies a 5-term between them. In the three-cube its filling, with those facet identifications, represents the hexagon: the extreme hom is a disk, and the chosen facet paths are its six boundary edges up to homotopy relative to their endpoints.

The following boundary check is essential. Induced facets of the row paste may use different 4-terms from the ones chosen for the edges of the global complex. Choose a corner comparison $k_p$ at each of the eight cube corners, and a side comparison $k_q l\Rightarrow l' k_p$ at each of its twelve sides, just as for the two-event case. For each two-event facet from $p$ to $q$, there are now two parallel 4-terms going from $k_q$ composed with the first chart route to the second chosen route composed with $k_p$. One uses the chart exchange followed by the side comparisons. The other uses the side comparisons followed by the chosen exchange. RC supplies a 5-term between them. Its label has **two** occurrences. Its semantic homotopy class relative to these paths is therefore unique, by contractibility of the weight-two fibre. This is precisely the comparison of paths in the facet before composing with the fixed third step.

To see that these six corrections match, at each serial vertex slide $k$ along its three successive steps using the three chosen side comparisons in order. When its side comparison passes the facet exchange, use the interchange square for these two 4-terms, as in the preceding square construction. Reassociation of a string or a whiskered cell here is performed in the globular paste of that string with the varying cells as inputs. ### A contraction with no bound on the number of occurrences

There is also a family for which the preceding geometric presentation decides **all** homotopy groups. Give each generating 1-edge a positive integer width, additive on paths, and suppose every generating 2-cell preserves width and has positive width. In a path of width $w$ use the ordered unit positions $1,\ldots,w$. Each 2-generator in a diagram occupies a consecutive interval of these positions. For each 3-generator suppose the positions meeting a 2-generator are the same on both its faces, and this set is nonempty. The other positions pass unchanged through both faces.

For such a 3-computad the following criterion is exact. Check whether a generating 3-cell has a nonempty right whiskering path $g$ in common on its two faces and a generating 3-cell has exactly this $g$ as a left whiskering path on its two faces. Either whiskering may be part of a longer one, and the generators may coincide. If such a match exists, the computad fails homotopicality and relative coherence. If no match exists, **every geometric fibre is contractible**. In particular relative coherence implies contractibility throughout this family. This includes diagrams with different numbers of lower gates on the two faces, repeated top generators, partial overlaps and unchanged wires inside a replacement.

For the geometric calculation above, all the $B_k$ now have positive width on every 2-generator, so they are bubble-free without assuming RC. When a match exists, place the two rules so that just the path $g$ overlaps. Their order on this common path gives two different two-hole diagrams. They are distinct because the two marked lower boxes meet on an ordered wire. The boxes have different left endpoints since each has an active position outside the overlap. On substituting either face for one hole, the two diagrams coincide: the substituted face is the identity along the overlap. Thus the two characteristic squares have the same perimeter routes and different diagonals. They give a cycle in the weight-two graph, where there are no 2-cells to kill it. The fundamental-group result above excludes RC.

Suppose henceforth there is no match. Record a lower diagram by the types and intervals of its gate occurrences and their total orders on each position. These data determine the strict 2-cell. All serial listings of the gates are linear extensions of the orders, and adjacent incomparable gates occupy disjoint intervals. Swapping them preserves typing and is strict interchange. For the typing calculation one can spell an edge of width $d$ as the string of letters $(e,1),\ldots,(e,d)$. A gate replaces its designated subword by another of the same length. Labelling the occurrences is unambiguous up to unique renaming because every gate is fixed by an ordered position it meets.

Choose a serial computation of a strict 3-cell $c$. At each position follow the word of lower gate occurrences there. Distinguish its gaps as well as its letters, including the exterior gaps. In a top step an active position consumes a nonempty block of letters with its interior gaps and produces a new nonempty block with fresh interior gaps. Its two exterior gaps survive with the same edge letters since the two lower diagrams are parallel. At an unchanged position the step reads a particular gap and preserves it. Keep occurrences distinct, naming produced tokens by their producer, position and output port.

At each position order a token's producer before every reader or consumer and each reader before its eventual consumer. Any linear extension of this local partial order is a valid local execution. For two adjacent incomparable steps, the second uses none of the first's outputs and cannot consume a gap just read by the first. Since an active block and its replacement are both nonempty, the second block pulls back to a disjoint block or gap on the same side. Two reads may also coincide at one unchanged gap. Thus the two steps swap and yield the same named word. This proves the assertion by adjacent swaps. Two locally incomparable steps have a fixed spatial comparison whenever both are enabled. Execute the ideal consisting of their strict predecessors first. Both input positions are then exposed. Every other prefix exposing both can be reached from that ideal by steps that preserve their tokens, hence also their relative order or their common gap.

Take the union of the local partial orders. For two steps incomparable on each shared position, add their chronological comparison if two shared positions force opposite spatial orders. Take the transitive closure and call the resulting poset $H$. All these comparisons agree with the original serial computation. They are invariant under a genuine two-hole exchange, since lower boxes have the same relative order on every shared position. The occurrence identifications made under exchanges are canonical: each event consumes a letter on at least one active position, each such letter has only one consumer, and the ordered initial letters and output ports fix these identifications successively. The gap names then follow as well.

If two overlapping incomparable steps had only ties on their common positions, both would read identical unchanged gaps there. Neither interval could contain the other because each has an active position. Their intersection is therefore a suffix of one and prefix of the other, passing unchanged through both rules. The typed letters at those gaps agree. The ends of the intersection are boundaries of whole edges, since the letters include their edge offsets. This gives the excluded whiskering match. Thus every intersecting pair of incomparable steps has a uniquely forced spatial order on its intervals, including across any tied positions.

To prove that $H$ describes exactly the strict computation, take consecutive incomparable events in a valid execution. Revert the second event's matches past the first as in the local argument and collapse both source regions to marked boxes. Each outside gate keeps its comparisons with the first box from the state before the first step and with the second box from the state after the first step. Reverting a disjoint letter replacement preserves the latter comparisons. A gate of the second match is entirely unchanged: if the first had produced any of its letters there would be a producer dependence. On a lane the boxes inherit the order of the disjoint blocks and read gaps; only multiple reads of one gap can tie. Break those ties by the forced orders on intersecting intervals.

Here is why this simultaneous collapse is legal for any number of boxes with these pairwise comparisons. Regard outside gates as boxes too. All pair orders on intersecting intervals respect the weak spatial orders on their lanes. A shortest directed cycle of length at least four would have a chord, since the two neighbours of an interval with the smallest right endpoint both contain that endpoint. Either direction of the chord shortens the cycle. Three pairwise intersecting intervals have a common lane. A directed triangle would require all three positions to be tied on *every* lane shared by all three, by transitivity of the weak lane order. Each pair would then need an exclusive lane on which its strict comparison is witnessed. To the left of the triple intersection only the pair omitting the interval that starts last can overlap, and to the right only the pair omitting the interval that ends first can overlap. This supplies at most two of the necessary three pairs. Hence all precedence is acyclic.

A topological ordering gives a typed marked circuit. Indeed expanding the holes by their source diagrams reproduces exactly the original orders of all gates on their lanes, hence a valid serialization of the source. The input letters at an active lane agree with those at its first expanded gate. At a read gap the prescribed typed letter survives, including on gaps bordering the other replacements. Output types follow at the other end, so collapsing really gives the specified lower boxes. Their diagram is unique since all its lane orders are forced. For two events this proves the required exchange and hence shows that every linear extension of $H$ is in the strict class. For any ideal $I$, the named state after $I$ is independent of its listing by the swap property. Each minimal remaining event can execute there by choosing it first in an extension. A set of such events therefore has individually enabled boxes with the pairwise comparisons used above and admits a unique joint context. Conversely a joint context allows every ordering of its events, so they form an antichain.

Let $K$ be the subcomplex of the ideal nerve $N\mathcal I(H)$ consisting of chains whose total difference is an antichain. Send a chain to the simplex of $T$ given by the joint context with changes indexed by the successive differences. Uniqueness makes this compatible with faces and degeneracies. Every endpoint necklace in $T$ with label $c$ lifts uniquely to $K$ from the empty to the full ideal: serialise each bead, read off its successive cuts in $H$, and use its occurrences as the changes. The occurrence identifications and the cuts are unchanged by reordering simultaneous changes. Conversely a bead is recovered from its cuts by uniqueness of the joint context. This also verifies naturality for maps of necklaces, which compose steps or restrict their times. Thus $E(c)$ is the extreme mapping space of $K$.

The inclusion $K\subseteq N\mathcal I(H)$ is a categorical equivalence. Add the missing intervals $[I,J]$ in increasing order of the cardinality of $D=J\setminus I$. For a non-antichain $D$ the old part of this interval nerve is exactly the union of the faces omitting either endpoint. In rigidification all homs except the extreme one are unchanged. The old extreme hom is the nerve of nonempty chains of the open interval by the bead-joint calculation above. The open interval consists of the proper nonempty ideals $U$ of $D$ and is contractible: $U\cap\min D$ is nonempty and lies below both $U$ and the proper ideal $\min D$. The new extreme hom is also contractible, as its chain poset contains the least chain of just the endpoints. Hence each attachment is a pushout of a monomorphic categorical equivalence. The extreme hom of the full ideal nerve is a point. The lower-dimensional fibres are already contractible by the lower-skeleton calculation, proving the criterion.

The same criterion gives the implication under RC for every iterated suspension of a context in this family. The strict and geometric free comparisons of a suspension are suspensions of the corresponding comparisons: with the two poles fixed, prescribing the suspended generators and their attaching maps is precisely prescribing the original cellular diagram in the hom between the poles. Thus the positive case preserves all the contractions. In the overlap case the original four-cycle is still present in that iterated hom. If the suspended context satisfied RC, apply the two-event construction above using the suspended row paste, lifting corners and unary sides there by suspended boundary lifting. The unary fibres and the facet paths are the original ones in the iterated hom. The resulting loop would both traverse the four-cycle and admit an RC nullhomotopy. This proves failure in the suspended context without assuming that arbitrary terms can be desuspended.

## Positive 3-computads with no matching idle boundary paths

Let every generating 2-cell have nonempty input and output paths and every generating 3-cell have nonidentity 2-faces. A face has an *idle right suffix* $w$ if it factors horizontally as $a*₀1_w$ with $w$ a nonempty 1-path. Define an idle left prefix dually. In the following test choose the faces independently, including faces of the same generator.

**Theorem.** If no idle right suffix of a generating 3-face is an idle left prefix of a generating 3-face, every geometric fibre of the 3-computad is contractible. In particular this contracts full-support fibres at arbitrary weight. It needs neither a positive width grading on the generators nor equality of the active wires on opposite faces. For example on one loop $x$ take $r:xx⇒x$, $p:xxx⇒xx$ and $A:r*₀1_x⇒p$. The rule $r$ precludes every positive width grading. The two faces of $A$ act on different sets of wires, and the test applies. Horizontal powers of $A$ have full support and unbounded weight.

**Local widths.** Use the cubical presentation of the transition simplicial set $T$ of Response 4. That construction applies here before assuming relative coherence. Each lower marked polygraph still has nonempty inputs and outputs at every gate, hence is bubble-free and homotopical by item 8. Positivity also makes every marked circuit rigid. Fix a vertex of $T$, an ordinary planar 2-diagram $D$. Give each concrete wire segment the number of directed boundary-to-boundary graph paths containing it, allowing any incident input to continue to any output at a gate. Every wire has positive width, and the sum of input widths equals the sum of output widths at every gate, by counting the paths through it. At a serial cut partition a fixed interval of unit lanes in wire order with these widths. Executing a gate repartitions its interval. Swapping independent gates leaves their intervals fixed, so each gate and each wire of $D$ acquires a definite interval. This construction only uses one vertex $D$ at a time.

A signed edge germ is a one-marker circuit together with its endpoint choice. Expanding the marker by that face gives $D$. The isomorphism to $D$ fixes every gate and ordered port, since every component is boundary-anchored. In a serialization of the frame the wires entering the marker consequently give a definite consecutive interval of lanes, equal to the interval of its output wires after expansion. A different serialization exchanges disjoint gates and gives the same data. On each of these lanes the expanded face occupies a consecutive block of gates, or the gap on a through wire if that lane is idle. A germ includes that gap, even if its face has no gates on the wire. An ordinary gate of $D$ occupies one gate on each lane of its own interval.

**Links.** Take a finite family of pairwise adjacent germs at $D$. In a joint two-marker circuit, the expansions of the two markers have disjoint sets of gates of $D$. Their blocks on any common lane can be executed in the frame order. Regard a block as the interval between its first and last cut in that lane's gate list. One lies weakly before the other, and both orders are possible on that lane only for equal empty gaps. If two marker intervals overlap but *all* common lanes tie, neither interval can contain the other, since each face has a gate. Thus the common lanes occupy an end of each interval. On every one the same concrete wire passes through both faces untouched. The overlap consists of whole wires: its two endpoints are port boundaries of the two boxes, and a wire passing unchanged through a box cannot be cut by its port boundary. The ordered word of the common wires is an idle suffix of the left box's face and an idle prefix of the right box's face. The hypothesis excludes this. Consequently an intersecting pair has a unique order, forced on some common lane and valid on all of them.

Keep the proposed markers and the individual ordinary gates exterior to all of them. For a marker and an exterior gate the one-marker frame gives their comparisons, strict on an intersecting lane since the exterior gate occupies a gate there. Two ordinary gates retain their order in $D$. Orient the intersection graph of all these intervals by the resulting comparisons. This graph is acyclic. In a shortest directed cycle of length at least four the interval with smallest right endpoint gives a chord between its neighbours, shortening the directed cycle whichever way the chord points. Three pairwise intersecting intervals share a lane. A directed triangle forces three equal gaps on *every* triple-covered lane by transitivity of the lane order. Each pair needs a strictly witnessing lane outside the triple intersection. Only the pair omitting a last-starting interval can overlap to its left, and only the pair omitting a first-ending interval can overlap to its right. These can witness at most two distinct pairs.

Serialize the resulting acyclic graph. Expanding the marker blocks in this order preserves every original gate order on every lane. In particular every wire predecessor in $D$ precedes its successor. This is a serialization of $D$: linear extensions of the gate order differ by interchanging disjoint, enabled gates. When a block starts, its input port wires are the concrete wires at the prescribed lane cuts. On an idle gap this follows because every exterior gate at that lane has been placed on the prescribed side of the gap. Its whole input word, including the idle ports, is therefore present with the right types. The same holds at its output. Replacing each such block by its marker gives the joint circuit. Any two of the resulting orders differ by interchanging gates on disjoint intervals, so the joint circuit is unique. Conversely a cube corner gives pairwise adjacent germs. Its germs are distinct because their expanded faces contain disjoint nonempty sets of gates. The cubical links are consequently simplicial flag complexes, including the signed links at vertices with directed loops.

**From links to the actual fibre.** Take the universal cover of the cubical component of $T$ containing a serial representative of $c$, using the ordinary cubical realization before triangulation. Its links are flag. It is thus a simply connected nonpositively curved cube complex, with median 1-skeleton. Opposite edges of every square have the same transverse orientation, by the time coordinates of the marked circuit. Upstairs a hyperplane is two-sided, with its dual edge directions related by going across squares. The lifted directions select one side as positive on the entire hyperplane. A directed path can therefore cross each hyperplane at most once and is an edge geodesic. These are combinatorial facts and require no upper bound on the dimension of cubes over the whole component. [Genevois, §2 and Theorem 2.1](https://aif.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/aif.3430.pdf) · [Ardila–Owen–Sullivant, §2](https://fardila.com/Articles/cat0.pdf)

Lift the chosen serial representative from $v$ to $w$. In the interval between them the vertices are the order ideals of the finite poset of separating hyperplanes, ordered by the halfspaces containing $v$. More precisely use the order in which nested halfspaces must be exited when leaving $v$. Rooting at $v$, the ideal for $w$ is consistent, so all its subideals are consistent. The cubes in the interval are precisely independent additions of minimal remaining elements. Every edge geodesic between these endpoints is directed, and any two are related by swapping consecutive incomparable elements. Thus its projection has label $c$. Conversely every serial representative of $c$ lifts between those same endpoints, since strict equality is generated by exchanges in squares. This correspondence also lifts every cube necklace and its subdivisions uniquely: lift from the first vertex across the successive beads, each lying in a cube, and compose the lifts at their common vertices. Every bead lies in the interval since each of its vertex chains can be extended to a directed endpoint path. Projection is inverse and respects the face and composition operations. The mapping space over $c$ is therefore the extreme mapping space of the triangulated interval cubing. The order-ideal attachment argument at the end of Response 4 proves this space contractible. The lower fibres follow from the lower-skeleton calculation, and iterated identities give the same globe fibres. [Ardila–Owen–Sullivant, Theorem 2.5 and §3](https://fardila.com/Articles/cat0.pdf)

## Exactly what a two-event overlap can detect

For *any* positive 3-computad as above, without the no-overlap hypothesis, every weight-two fibre is a point, an interval with one or two exchange edges, or a circle with two exchange edges (using the unsubdivided permutohedra of Response 4). A circular component occurs somewhere if and only if there are two top generators, possibly the same one, and a nonempty path $w$ that is an idle suffix of **both** faces of one and an idle prefix of **both** faces of the other.

Indeed a vertex of a weight-two fibre is an ordered pair of serial steps. Its incident exchanges are exactly the joint two-marker frames at the intermediate state for the incoming and outgoing germs. The preceding lane argument gives at most two: the orders with every exterior gate are forced, and only the relative order of the two markers can vary. Each choice fixes all comparisons of intersecting intervals, hence at most one frame. If both orders occur, the shared lanes are equal idle gaps as above. In the order with those two markers left incomparable (retain only their forced orders with exterior gates), neither can reach the other by a directed path: both linear orders have occurred. They can consequently be placed consecutively with identical exterior prefix and suffix, in either order. Their common ports are a nonempty consecutive word of wires running directly between the two boxes. Reordering the two lower boxes across that word gives the second frame.

Suppose both serial endpoints of a given exchange have degree two. Work in its two-marker frame, with the consecutive lower serialization just described. At the other serial endpoint the markers are expanded by the opposite choices of face. Every wire port directly connecting the two boxes still gives a shared lane interval of positive width, using the path count at this new state. For ambiguity at that endpoint, *all* those wires must pass unchanged through the two newly chosen faces. Thus all four faces are idle on the same connecting word. Reordering the two lower boxes gives the same second frame at both endpoints: expanding either marker by either face preserves its through wires and hence gives identical one-marker circuits before and after the reordering. The two exchanges are parallel edges, and they exhaust the degrees of both vertices. An exchange cannot be a loop, since at its initial state in $T$ the two possible first steps use disjoint nonempty sets of ordinary gates. A path of three edges in any other component would have an edge with two degree-two endpoints. This proves the list, since the strict label indexes a connected component.

Conversely write the four faces as $a_i*₀1_w$ and $1_w*₀b_j$. Whisker and vertically compose their markers in either lower order across $w$, from the diagram $a_0*₀1_w*₀b_0$ to $a_1*₀1_w*₀b_1$ in transition time. The two frames are distinct by the order of the markers along a connecting wire. Their four one-marker sides agree in pairs by the displayed factorizations. Hence they give the circular component, which rules out relative coherence by the edge-realization argument of Response 4. If only one intermediate serial state is ambiguous, the two alternatives form an interval. Such an ambiguity alone cannot certify a violation of relative coherence. The all-weight case with these one-sided ambiguities, as well as the general higher-dimensional case, remains open here.

**Finite checks.** The [circuit enumerator](../../../tools/relative_coherence_local_links.py) compares cliques of signed one-marker germs with all simultaneous marked circuits independently enumerated from the lower generators. Faces in this check each expand to one gate, so bounding the number of gates introduces no missing corner at an included state. It also builds the actual exchange multigraph by expanding each two-marker circuit in its two serial orders. With four initial wires and at most four gates, the no-overlap example with additional splitting and unary gates has 25,007 states and 3,504 four-marker corners; every clique has exactly one corner. On five initial wires and at most three gates, allowing opposite one-sided idle faces gives 33 two-edge interval components. Adding two rules idle on both faces gives 33 circular components as well. These checks exercise the port and multiplicity distinctions in the proof.

# Researcher Response 6

## Four occurrences, including one-sided overlaps

Let $Γ$ be positive through dimension three and satisfy $\mathrm{RC}_{\mathrm{id}}(∞)$. **Every 3-cell fibre of weight at most four is contractible.** More generally, for every strict 3-cell $c$ of weight $r\ge2$,
$$
H_{r-1}(E(c);\mathbb Z)=0.
$$
The proof of this homology vanishing only needs the exclusion of the two-sided overlaps classified in Response 5. One-sided overlaps are allowed, and gates can change arity. By Response 2 it suffices that these hypotheses hold on the support of $c$.

**Occurrence names.** Fix the initial ordinary 2-diagram $D_0$ of a serial representative of $c$. Name its concrete gates by their occurrences. Name a rewriting event by its rule and the names of the gates it consumes, indexed by the gate occurrences in its source face. Give its output gates fresh names consisting of the event name and their positions in its target face. Every event consumes at least one gate. Inducting through a run, gate names are fresh: repeating the name of an earlier event would require consuming an already consumed gate again, and the initial names and the output names are disjoint. Rigidity of positive planar diagrams makes the identifications in a particular frame unique. A square swap changes neither event's inputs nor the names of the outputs. It follows that occurrence names are invariant throughout the strict class. In particular all full simultaneous $r$-marker frames over $c$ have the same named events, with the same pairwise disjoint, nonempty sets of source gates in $D_0$.

The only cells of dimension $r-1$ in Response 4 are the single permutohedra of full frames. If there are none the claim follows at once. Otherwise use the path-count widths of Response 5 on $D_0$, and choose an event $x$ whose leftmost active source lane is as far left as possible among all the events. This uses the gates in $D_0$ and hence the *same* choice works in all the full frames. For any such frame $C$, consider its two facets splitting off $x$ alone, first or last in rewriting time. These are distinct open cells, distinguishable by their ordered blocks of named events. Inside a full permutohedron each of these facets occurs once, also by the occurrence names.

**Changes across a facet.** Suppose $C'$ shares one of these two facets with $C$. At the intermediate ordinary vertex $M$ of the split, the facet supplies the signed germ of $x$ and the germs of all the others simultaneously. Compute widths afresh on $M$. The two frames compress the same expanded blocks and idle gaps there. Every comparison between two markers different from $x$ is fixed by the common factor in which they are still markers: expanding $x$ to its face retains their order on every shared lane. Every comparison with a gate outside the blocks is fixed by the corresponding germ. Therefore the only comparisons that can change are between $x$ and a marker $y$ tied with it at idle gaps on *all* common lanes.

More explicitly take a serialization of each frame on these blocks and exterior gates and pass between the two orders by adjacent inversions, crossing only pairs whose orders in these serializations differ. Disjoint intervals commute. For intersecting intervals an inversion can only cross $x$ with such a $y$. Both new blocks still expand to $M$, by the lane and port reconstruction in Response 5. The two factors at the split are preserved: replacing either of the consecutive boxes by its chosen face leaves the other free to pass its idle wires. Thus the successive orders give full frames on this very facet. A nontrivial crossing involves a nonempty word of directly connecting ports, an idle suffix of the spatially left box's chosen face and an idle prefix of the right box's chosen face.

At **every** such crossing $x$ is the spatially left box. To see this, expand the consecutive boxes and all other markers by their *source* faces, giving $D_0$. At least one of the two crossing faces used at $M$ was a source: at the first split it was $y$'s source, and at the last split it was $x$'s source. Thus in $D_0$ at least one of the two expanded boxes acts only outside the common port word. In the fixed widths of $D_0$, their intervals meet exactly along the suffix and prefix of the consecutive boxes at the cut between them. If the left box is idle there, all its source gates are left of the right box's interval. If the right box is idle there, all its source gates are right of the left box's interval. In either case *all* active source lanes of the spatially left box lie strictly to the left of those of the other. The choice of $x$ excludes $y$ being on the left. This argument never identifies the numerical widths at $D_0$ with those at $M$.

It follows that all the markers crossed during a change of frame at this facet meet the **rightmost lane of $x$** at $M$. On that lane their order amongst themselves is fixed. The slot of $x$ in this ordered list can only move in one direction from $C$ to $C'$ when crossing only inverted pairs. If the slot does not move, only disjoint intervals were interchanged and $C'=C$. Measure the direction intrinsically on the full marked circuit by the number of gates that are strict graph ancestors of $x$, counting other markers as gates. Each move of $x$ upward in the lower circuit across a consecutive $y$ strictly increases this number. Every former input ancestor still feeds $x$, either directly on the unchanged ports or through $y$ on the shared ports; $y$ itself becomes an ancestor. The reverse move strictly decreases the number. This count compares the two singleton facets using the same circuit $C$, even though their intermediate diagrams differ.

**A facet with nonzero boundary coefficient.** Suppose a nonzero integral top cycle exists. Among the full frames with nonzero coefficient choose one minimizing this ancestor count. Cancellation at each of its two singleton facets would require a different frame in the cycle sharing that facet. By minimality, at either facet the first nontrivial move toward such a frame must move $x$ upward. In both instances the first marker $y$ crossed is *the same gate of $C$*: it is the next gate on the rightmost output wire of $x$. Indeed immediately before that crossing the two boxes can be serialized consecutively and share that output port. Any preceding interchanges of disjoint intervals preserve the marked circuit and its wire incidences.

The word of direct connections from $x$ to $y$ in $C$ is likewise intrinsic. Crossing at the $x$-first facet requires the target face of $x$ and the source face of $y$ to pass this word untouched. Crossing at the $x$-last facet requires the source of $x$ and the target of $y$ to do so. The same nonempty word is now an idle suffix and prefix on both sides of the respective rules. Response 5 excludes this under relative coherence. At least one of the two facets consequently retains the nonzero coefficient of $C$, up to orientation. This contradicts the cycle condition.

For $r=4$ the CW model has dimension at most three. Response 4 gives connectedness and vanishing of $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$, while the top-cycle argument gives $H_3=0$. The space is simply connected and has trivial reduced integral homology in all degrees, hence is contractible by Hurewicz and Whitehead. Smaller weights were settled in Response 4. A positive 3-dimensional counterexample under relative coherence would therefore require weight at least five and a nonzero group in an intermediate degree: at its *first* nonzero positive homotopy group, Hurewicz and the top-degree vanishing force $3\le j\le r-2$.

The [circuit checker](../../../tools/relative_coherence_local_links.py) builds the facet incidence relation by independently expanding the two factors at every cut. On 5,970 four-marker frames with arity-changing rules it eliminates every full frame using facets that split off one event first or last. A separate four-event example has cyclic temporal preferences for the ambiguous pairs; the same test eliminates all 18 frames. For a chain with three two-sided overlaps the eight distinct full frames instead have even total incidence at **every** facet, giving a nonzero mod-two top cycle. This last calculation tests the use of the two-sided exclusion in the proof.

# Researcher Response 7

## Gluing temporal orders across cuts

There is an all-weight contraction allowing one-sided overlaps, insertions, deletions and several successive simultaneous blocks. Suppose the support of a strict 3-cell $c$ is positive and each generating 2-cell in it has one input edge and one output edge. These edges can have different labels. The positions in either outer 1-path are then a fixed line of **lanes**. A 2-diagram is a word of unary gates on each lane. Each top occurrence $v$ has a footprint $I_v$, the consecutive lanes on which its marker is placed, including any idle lanes in the marker.

Partition the line into consecutive strips so that each $I_v$ is a union of strips. On a strip $b$ keep just the events $V_b$ that cover it. Project one serial computation for $c$ there, keeping distinct occurrence names. In this projection an event replaces one subword on each lane of the strip; a subword on either face may be empty, even on every lane. An exchange specifies in which spatial order the two markers occur across the strip. Let $K_b$ be the component of the projected computation in the local complex of simultaneous rewrites, defined below.

**Cut hypothesis.** For every cut between successive strips, the events crossing it occur in one fixed, strict temporal order throughout each of its two local components, with the same order on both sides. In particular they cannot be simultaneous there. This holds automatically for cuts crossed by at most one occurrence. The hypothesis only concerns the events *crossing* the cut. Other events in either strip can exchange, or occur simultaneously with a crossing event.

Under this hypothesis the actual geometric fibre satisfies
$$
E(c)\simeq\prod_b K_b.
$$
Moreover **if $Γ$ has relative coherence, $E(c)$ is contractible**. No bound on the number of occurrences or on the number of lanes in a strip is needed. The assertion applies equally to full-support labels. In particular one can string together arbitrarily many overlapping events, with one event crossing each cut and arbitrarily many additional events inside the strips. The proof treats intermediate-dimensional cells as well as the single full frames of Response 6.

**Local complexes and occurrence names.** A serial step on a strip specifies the cut position of its source subword on each lane. A simultaneous tier specifies a marked word on every lane: expanding each marker by the corresponding source subword gives the incoming state, and expanding by the target gives the outgoing state. The marker names must occur in the same spatial order on every lane. Strict equality of lower diagrams imposes precisely this condition: the markers span the strip, and ordinary gates on distinct lanes commute. Thus these words determine the tier uniquely. For a sequence of tiers take the product of their temporal permutohedra as in Response 4. A face splits one or more tiers in time, expanding each marker before its turn by its source and afterwards by its target. Work with each prescribed event once. Distinct ordered refinements of a tier have distinct lists of names, so each closed cell is regular. With specified initial state and finitely many named events the complex is finite, since the word lengths and the number of temporal steps are bounded. $K_b$ consists of the component containing the given projection. An empty event list contributes a point.

Globally the names can be recovered in every serial computation by the input-gate and output-port construction of Response 6. Every event consumes a gate on at least one lane. Its name and the lane of that gate fix the whole footprint by its offset in the source. Initial gates have prescribed lanes, and gates produced by a named event retain their prescribed output lanes. Consequently these footprints and names agree across exchanges. Naming the events in this way does not change the fibre. In a strip we keep the global names even when the entire projected face is empty, so a particular projected component need not be the fibre of an *unlabelled* local computad.

**Proof of the product.** First take all global cube chains on these named events whose projections belong to the specified $K_b$, with the given initial and final states. Project a tier onto a strip by restricting its lane words; omit tiers with no marker on that strip. This commutes with taking faces. It gives a map of face posets, ordered by inclusion, to the product of the face posets of the $K_b$.

Fix a tuple $p$ of local cells. To compute the inverse image of its lower ideal, impose $v<w$ whenever $v,w$ belong to successively ordered (distinct) tiers in any one of these cells. These inequalities generate a partial order $H$. Indeed, adding strips from left to right, the names in the new strip that have already appeared are exactly those crossing the preceding cut, since their footprints are intervals. They form the same strict chain on both sides. Two acyclic orders glued along a common chain have acyclic union: any cycle visiting both sides breaks up into paths between common vertices, and each nonconstant such path moves strictly forward in the chain.

An ordered partition of all names respecting every strict inequality of $H$ prescribes, on each strip, a unique refinement of its cell in $p$. Conversely these are exactly the temporal partitions of the global chains over the lower ideal. For the existence assertion, reconstruct one global tier at a time from the prescribed refined words, keeping the current word where a strip has no step. On either side of a cut these diagrams have at most one marker of that tier in common by the cut hypothesis. Their gate orders can therefore be glued: the union of the orders along at most one common node is acyclic. Repeating along the line gives a serialization. It is well typed because on each lane it executes exactly the prescribed word with the specified ports. Two such serializations differ by interchanges on disjoint lanes; all pairs meeting on a lane already have their comparison fixed by that word. This proves uniqueness of the marked circuit. Its expansions at either end reproduce the corresponding strip states, so the constructed tiers compose. It also proves compatibility with further refinement.

The inverse image of the lower ideal is therefore the face poset of ordered partitions strictly respecting $H$. Its classifying space is contractible. Indeed it is the subdivision of the extreme mapping complex of the cubing of order ideals of $H$: a block adds an antichain of currently minimal remaining elements. Response 4 proves the contraction for this cubing by attaching the missing non-antichain intervals in its ideal nerve. Quillen's Theorem A applied to the projection now gives an equivalence with $\prod_b K_b$. This proves connectedness of the global complex just constructed as well. Its vertices are related by genuine two-event exchanges, so they all have the original strict label $c$. Conversely any computation of $c$ is reached by such exchanges and hence projects into the selected local components. All cube chains of that label were included. Response 4 thus identifies the domain of the equivalence with $E(c)$ itself.

**Why simple connectedness suffices on a strip.** This step allows local events idle on their whole strip, even when several read the same gap. Orient a local exchange toward the serial order that agrees with the spatial order of its two boxes. Count $+1$ on each oriented edge and $-1$ on its reverse. This integral cellular cochain is closed. On a square the two exchanges commute, and on a hexagon the spatial order of the three markers is a total order. In either case the edge counts in that cell are the differences of the number of correctly ordered pairs at its vertices. Expanding a third marker preserves the order of the two remaining markers because they span the entire strip. This last fact is essential for the cochain to agree on common edges.

Suppose $K_b$ is simply connected. The cochain is then a vertex-potential difference, and the finite oriented graph has no directed cycle. At a serial vertex let $a_i$ and $p_i$ on a given lane denote the length of the prefix before step $i$ and the length of its source subword. Positions are measured immediately before the respective step. The exchange of $i,i+1$ is outgoing exactly when $a_{i+1}+p_{i+1}\le a_i$ on every lane. Indeed this puts the later input wholly in the unchanged prefix of the earlier step and determines the simultaneous chart with the later marker on the left. If both markers occupy the same empty gap, this still selects just one of their two spatial orders.

On a consecutive run of outgoing exchanges those inequalities imply $a_j+p_j\le a_i$ for each earlier $i$ and later $j$ in the run, lane by lane. All input blocks can therefore be cut in the original words before the run, with coincident empty cuts taken in reverse temporal order. This produces one simultaneous chart, in that reverse order on every lane. Merge each maximal such run. The resulting product of permutohedra $F(v)$ has $v$ as unique source and contains every cell with source $v$ as a face: within each tier of such a cell the temporal listing at its source must be the reverse of its spatial listing. Every vertex of $F(v)$ is reachable from $v$ by oriented edges. In particular two outgoing moves have a common descendant. Finite termination and this local confluence give a unique sink in the connected component.

Remove vertices other than the sink in a topological order. At the removal of $v$ its remaining closed star is $F(v)$: a remaining cell containing $v$ whose source were different would have an earlier source, and all vertices of $F(v)$ survive up to this step. This polytope deformation retracts onto its union of faces missing $v$, the antistar of a vertex. This gives a deformation retraction at each step and contracts the component. This argument uses the [tier-collapse proof for planar diagrams](../answered/homotopy-polygraphs-2026-11-19.md) with the inequalities imposed on *every* lane; here termination follows from the closed cochain on the simply connected component.

Under relative coherence Response 4 gives $\pi_1 E(c)=0$. By the product equivalence every $K_b$ has trivial fundamental group, so each is contractible by the preceding argument. This proves the contraction.

**Checks and limit of the gluing argument.** The [strip checker](../../../tools/relative_coherence_strip_fibres.py) enumerates whole marked circuits independently of their strip projections, including all ordered cuts of each simultaneous tier. For five overlapping events whose faces delete and insert letters on their shared lanes, it finds 2,961 global cells. Every one of the 625 local cell tuples has an inverse lower ideal with the mod-two homology of a point. For four events with both faces idle on each shared lane the global Betti numbers are $(1,3,3,1)$, as predicted by the three circle factors. A separate test has two shared events forced into the same order by a consumed output on both sides. All 25 lower ideals pass there as well. A two-lane strip with unequal input lengths on its lanes passes all nine lower-ideal checks. If two shared events can exchange on both sides but require opposite spatial orders, the checker instead finds two isolated global serial paths. The two local exchanges cannot be glued. Thus a cut with unconstrained shared events cannot in general be treated as a product by this proof.

# Researcher Response 8

## Full-support spheres with arbitrarily late first homotopy group

For every $k\ge2$ there is a finite positive 3-dimensional context $Γ_k$ with an acyclic generating 1-graph and a strict full-support 3-cell $c_k$ such that
$$
E(c_k)\simeq S^{k-1}.
$$
**Every 3-cell fibre of $Γ_k$ is either contractible or homotopy equivalent to this sphere, and every proper-support fibre is contractible.** Thus for $k=4$ the entire context passes the geometric $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ tests, but a full-support fibre has $\pi_3=\mathbb Z$. All weight-two fibres are contractible for every $k$. The result supplies a geometric obstruction in arbitrary degree at a fixed generator dimension. The algebraic relative-coherence hypothesis on $Γ_k$ for $k\ge4$ is a separate question.

**Presentation.** Along a line of $2k+2$ objects, name its $2k+1$ successive positions
$$
s_0,\ p_1,\ s_1,\ p_2,\ldots,\ p_k,\ s_k.
$$
All arrows at a given position go from its left object to its right object. The following are the 1- and 2-generators; letters named at different positions are distinct.

* At each $s_i$ take two edges $u_i,v_i$ and three parallel 2-generators $L_i,M_i,R_i:u_i⇒v_i$. At each *internal* $s_i$ also take an edge $q$ and a 2-generator $t:q⇒u_i$.
* At $p_i$ take edges $q,p$, a 2-generator $t:q⇒p$ and two 2-generators $d_i,e_i:p⇒p$. Subscripts on $q,p,t$ are suppressed.

A 3-generator replaces a specified word of 2-gates on each lane of its interval by another word. Write $1$ for the identity on the edge specified in the table.

| Generator | Lanes | Source and target by lane |
|---|---|---|
| $a_i$ | $s_{i-1},p_i$ | $M_{i-1}⇒R_{i-1}$ on $s_{i-1}$, $d_i⇒1_p$ on $p_i$ |
| $b_i$ | $p_i,s_i$ | $1_p⇒e_i$ on $p_i$, $L_i⇒M_i$ on $s_i$ |
| $z$ | The entire line | $L_0⇒M_0$ on $s_0$, $M_k⇒R_k$ on $s_k$, $1_q⇒1_q$ on each interior lane |

To form the actual CaTT declarations, take an explicit identity 2-term for an empty subword and the horizontal composite of the indicated 2-terms, with the same bracketing on the two faces of each generator. The 1-boundaries match literally with this choice. Each 3-generator has a nonidentity face on both sides. A marked lower diagram is recorded by its words on the lanes: markers common to two lanes denote the same box. It is a strict circuit exactly when the lane orders of the gates and markers admit a common serialization. When they do, that circuit is unique since linear extensions differ by interchanges on disjoint lanes. Response 4 applies here without assuming relative coherence: the lower 2-polygraph and all its marked versions have nonempty 1-inputs and 1-outputs at every gate.

**The strict label.** Initially put $L_i$ on each signal lane $s_i$ and $d_i$ on each seam $p_i$, prefixed by $t$ at every interior position of the line. Finally use $R_i$ and $e_i$ respectively with the same prefixes. Use every 3-generator once. The signal gates impose the strict temporal inequalities
$$
H:\qquad b_i<a_{i+1}\quad(1\le i<k),\qquad b_k<z<a_1 .
$$
In particular a serial run exists: execute all the $b_i$, then $z$, then all the $a_i$, choosing to insert $e_i$ immediately before $d_i$ at each seam. Take its label as $c_k$. Every generator belongs to its support, including the $t$ gates, which are used in the outer boundaries. In every computation the marker for $z$ reads the $q$ wire *before* $t$ on each interior lane, whereas the two short markers on a seam act on $p$ *after* $t$. The internal signal markers likewise act after their $t$. The edge types enforce this separation throughout, including in simultaneous tiers.

**A cover of the fibre.** Choose one of the two spatial orders of $a_i,b_i$ at each seam, independently. Denote the list of choices by $\epsilon\in\{-,+\}^k$. A simultaneous local two-marker word has the prescribed order. If $b_i$ comes first in time, expanding its two serial sides records which side of $d_i$ receives $e_i$. These are distinct serial diagrams for the two spatial choices. If $a_i$ comes strictly first, it deletes $d_i$; the later insertion into the empty gap is identical for either choice. This describes all possibilities at a seam.

For fixed $\epsilon$, every ordered temporal partition strictly respecting $H$ gives exactly one global cell with those choices. On each signal lane at most one marker of a tier can act on its gate, since successive replacements there are strictly ordered in $H$. On a seam the preceding two-marker calculation prescribes the word, including its intermediate words when the two events occur at different times. If $z$ is present, place it before each interior $t$. These lane words admit a common serialization of each tier: $z$ can be placed before the short markers, and among simultaneous short markers only the disjoint pairs $a_i,b_i$ at their respective seams can require a comparison. Ordinary unary gates have exactly their positions in the respective words. In particular inserting all the unary gates into such a marker order is possible: on a single lane they need only be inserted between its successively ordered markers. Uniqueness follows from the lane description. All refinements give precisely the corresponding faces. Thus this subcomplex $K_\epsilon$ is the ordered-partition complex for $H$ and is contractible by the ideal-cubing argument of Response 4.

Every global cell with these endpoints and occurrences lies in one of the $K_\epsilon$: read its choice on each seam where the choice is visible and extend arbitrarily on the others. If a family of sign lists varies exactly in coordinates $D\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}$, its intersection is the ordered-partition complex obtained by imposing the additional **strict** temporal inequalities $a_i<b_i$ for $i\in D$. Indeed this is exactly where the two distinct local charts give the same cell. The intersection is empty if $D$ is the full set, and contractible otherwise. To see the dichotomy, the added edges, together with $H$, lie on the one directed cycle
$$
a_1\to b_1\to a_2\to\cdots\to a_k\to b_k\to z\to a_1 .
$$
The full cycle prevents a temporal order; deleting any of its edges leaves a poset. In particular lists differing in one sign have nonempty intersection since $k\ge2$. Hence the union is connected. Its vertices are related by genuine exchanges, so the entire union has the single strict label $c_k$. The same endpoints and generator counts are preserved by every exchange, proving that the union is the actual fibre complex.

By the finite nerve lemma for subcomplexes, $E(c_k)$ has the homotopy type of the nerve on the sign lists. A subset is a simplex precisely when at least one coordinate is constant on that subset. This nerve is itself covered by the $2k$ simplices prescribing one signed coordinate. Their nonempty intersections are simplices, and their nerve has one vertex for each signed coordinate with just the opposite signs of a single coordinate prohibited together. This is the join of $k$ pairs of points, hence $S^{k-1}$.

**All other labels.** Fix any representative of a strict 3-cell. Its outer 1-path visits each lane at most once. Its 2-diagram on a signal lane contains at most one of $L_i,M_i,R_i$, because their distinct 1-source and 1-target never occur in reverse order on that lane. The only changes of this gate are $L_i$ to $M_i$ to $R_i$. Consequently each generating 3-cell occurs at most once in the entire history. The inequalities in $H$ apply whenever both entries of the respective replacement pair occur, with $b_k<z$ and $z<a_1$ treated separately. Take the transitive closure of these surviving edges. A step with a pre-existing $M_i$ needs no earlier producer. Other occupied positions of the outer diagram remain in their lane words.

At a seam where both $a_i,b_i$ occur, name the particular initial $d_i$ consumed and the particular final $e_i$ produced, by their word positions. They are fixed under square exchanges, since the two-marker expansion retains the same consumed letter and output port, and the final word has ordered positions. Delete these two named occurrences from the initial and final seam words respectively. The surviving word is the same on both sides with the same order. The two positions are now gaps of this word. Distinct gaps force a unique simultaneous chart and allow both temporal orders. A common gap gives exactly the two charts and the common serial order calculated above. If only one event occurs at the seam, its location is already fixed. At a lane shared with $z$ the $q$ read has a unique place whenever a short marker also acts: the lane must have its $t$ and the read is before it. If only $z$ acts on an interior lane it also has a unique $q$ gap: no ordinary gate there has $q$ as output. These facts account for all positions, including repeated ordinary seam letters and identity labels.

Repeat the cover argument on the actual set of occurrences, taking signs only at the common-gap seams. The surviving temporal constraints are a subgraph of the displayed cycle, and each one still has exactly its indicated role. If all occurrences and all $k$ common gaps are present, the same nerve gives $S^{k-1}$. In every other case even the intersection of *all* sign choices respects an acyclic temporal order. The nerve is then a simplex, so the fibre is contractible. This also proves connectedness of the constructed cover in each case and identifies it with the chosen strict label by exchanges as above. A spherical case needs all the markers, and their simultaneous use in one history requires the $t$ on every interior lane by typing. This proves the proper-support assertion. The lower-dimensional comparison is already an equivalence by the positive 2-polygraph theorem; evaluations on higher globes in this 3-dimensional context reduce to those on 3-globes as in Response 2.

# Researcher Response 10

## Detecting cycles of seams in full-support fibres

The sphere in Response 8 is one instance of a cubical classification. For the unary presentations described below the fibre has the homotopy type of an explicit subcomplex of a cube. Its missing faces are determined by directed cycles in a graph on the seams. **Under relative coherence the fibre is contractible whenever every induced directed cycle has length at most five.** There is no bound on the number of events, the number of seams, or the number of overlapping cycles. In particular the five-seam context $Γ_5$ of Response 8 fails $\mathrm{RC}_{\mathrm{id}}(n)$ for some $n\le7$. The five-dimensional parameter used here is provided by [canonical Gray terms, Researcher Response 6](canonical-gray-terms-r6.md#researcher-response-6).

**Hypotheses on the presentation and label.** Work on the support of a positive unary 3-dimensional presentation as in Response 7. Fix the initial and final lane words and a list of distinct named top generators, each used once in the label. Use the following lane data; they include idle portions of a generator's interval.

* On a signal lane the changing part of the word is one gate. Successive distinct gate states $g_0,\ldots,g_l:u⇒v$, with $u\ne v$, are traversed by a prescribed list of distinct events. Each replaces $g_{j-1}$ by $g_j$.
* On seam $i$ the changing part starts at $d_i:p_i⇒p_i$ and ends at $e_i:p_i⇒p_i$. Event $a_i$ deletes $d_i$ and $b_i$ inserts $e_i$. The gates are distinct. The two events in a pair are distinct.
* Fixed unary words may flank the changing part. An idle occurrence on a lane reads a specified gap in the fixed exterior, on an edge type identifying that gap uniquely throughout the computation. Each such gap has at most one idle reader on the lane. On every lane in an event's footprint it either acts as specified or performs such a read. Use fixed, equally bracketed horizontal composites for the two attaching terms, with explicit identities for empty subwords.

Let $H$ be the transitive closure of the strict orders of the signal lists and assume it is acyclic. There is a finite spatial-order test. For each antichain of $H$, form a graph on its markers. On each lane impose the order of the idle reads and their positions before or after the changing part. At a seam where both active markers are present, choose either spatial order. Require the resulting graph to be acyclic for every such choice. This tests whether the lane words of a simultaneous tier have a common serialization. Finally suppose $H\cup\{a_i<b_i\}$ is acyclic for each individual $i$. This last hypothesis permits both signs at each seam in the same strict label.

**The cubical model.** Write $K$ for the simplicial complex of subsets $D$ of seams such that
$$
H_D=H\cup\{a_i<b_i:i\in D\}
$$
is acyclic. In the signed cube $I^k$, let $Z_K$ consist of the closed faces whose set of free coordinates belongs to $K$. Then the indicated histories constitute one strict label $c$, and
$$
                         E(c)\simeq Z_K .
$$
To prove this, fix a spatial sign at each seam. Every ordered partition of the events strictly respecting $H$ now has a unique chart with those signs. At a signal the earlier tiers determine the gate state. On a seam, simultaneous markers and an insertion before deletion retain the sign, whereas a strict deletion before insertion hides it, as in Response 8. The fixed exterior determines every read. A topological sort of the spatial graph gives the common marker serialization in each tier. The ordinary unary gates can be inserted at their specified positions on their own lanes; they impose no further cross-lane relations. Conversely a typed chart gives just these lane words and temporal constraints. For unary lanes the words determine the diagram: linear extensions of the lane orders differ by swaps on disjoint lanes. Splitting a tier has exactly the facet maps of Response 4.

For fixed signs this is the ordered-partition complex of $H$. Several sign sheets intersect in the ordered-partition complex of $H_D$, with $D$ the coordinates where their signs vary. Indeed all those seams must hide their sign, and the signs on the other coordinates are prescribed. The intersection is empty if $H_D$ has a cycle and contractible otherwise by the ideal-cubing argument of Response 4. Each sheet is connected and changing a single sign gives intersecting sheets. All the histories therefore have one strict label by exchanges. Any history of that label has the same endpoints and generator counts, so the chart description exhausts its fibre.

Send a cell $x$ to the face $P(x)$ that fixes precisely its visible signs. Its hidden set belongs to $K$ by the actual tier order. This map reverses face order. For a face $F\in Z_K$ with free set $D$, the cells with $P(x)\supseteq F$ constitute the intersection of all sheets extending $F$. This is exactly the contractible complex for $H_D$. Applying the poset fibre lemma to the map into the opposite face poset of $Z_K$ gives the stated equivalence on subdivisions. In particular this is the visible-sign map itself, including on any coordinate projection used below.

**Which obstructions survive relative coherence?** Define a directed graph on the seams by putting $i\to j$ when $b_i\le_H a_j$, with equality allowed when two seams use the same event. A subset is in $K$ precisely when its induced directed graph is acyclic. In one direction concatenate the new edges $a_i\to b_i$ with the indicated old paths. In the other direction split a cycle in $H_D$ at successive new edges; all intervening segments lie in $H$, including possible zero-length segments. Thus the minimal nonfaces are precisely the vertex sets of chordless directed cycles. Any additional directed chord of a directed cycle gives a cycle on fewer vertices. The singleton assumption excludes loops.

For a minimal nonface $M$ of size $m$, projection onto those coordinates gives $Z_K\to\partial I^M$. It has a section fixing all other coordinates at minus, since every proper subset of $M$ is allowed. If $m$ is the smallest nonface size, $Z_K$ contains the entire $(m-1)$-skeleton of $I^k$. Cellular approximation in the cube shows that $Z_K$ is $(m-2)$-connected, and the section detects a copy of $\mathbb Z$ in $H_{m-1}$. In the absence of any directed cycle $Z_K=I^k$. Thus, even without relative coherence, the fibre is contractible if and only if the seam graph is acyclic.

For $2\le m\le5$ we can also detect the obstruction by CaTT terms. On a face $F$ of the parameter $I^M$ put the fresh gate $h_i:p_i⇒p_i$ of Response 9 at each fixed coordinate of $M$. Leave all seams outside $M$ uncoloured. The map to the original context sending $h_i$ to $d_i$ transfers relative boundary lifting by positivity and §3 item 10. The face maps erasing colours to explicit weak identities preserve both weak endpoints literally: use the two slots on either side of the changing part as in Response 9 and keep the fixed exterior in the same bracketing. The coloured fibre maps into exactly the union of the sheets compatible with $F$. The persistent gate separates the two active gaps, and the exterior reads retain their unique positions. No spatial order is added between markers beyond the fixed seam sign. Adjacent compatible sheets still intersect by the singleton condition.

Compose $P$ with the projection to $\partial I^M$. At least one sign in $M$ is visible on every cell because $M$ is a nonface. On the carrier of proper $F$ its projected face lies in a closed facet meeting $F$. The union of these facets is contractible by the cone-nerve argument of Response 9. It follows that a boundary map through the indicated carriers has projected degree one. This step works even when a coloured carrier has other cycles of its own.

Take one CaTT presentation of the Gray $m$-cube and restrict to its face subcontexts. Presentations for $m\le4$ are cited in Response 9, and the five-cube theorem cited above supplies $m=5$. Each face has contractible geometric groupoidification by the same strict connection argument. Label the threefold suspensions of the proper faces in their coloured contexts. The initial vertices lift with their prescribed poles, and at each later face all the previously chosen boundary labels are transported by the literal erasures. They give parallel attaching terms with the required common strict value. Assuming $\mathrm{RC}_{\mathrm{id}}$ through degree $m+1$ supplies all proper-face labels. The resulting boundary diagram realizes a sphere of projected degree one, by the objectwise contractible source-face diagram and the facet carriers. A term filling the top generator would extend this sphere through the contractible whole cube. Thus either an earlier condition fails or the two parallel $(m+2)$-terms on the top boundary have no filler. This proves the contraction assertion: under relative coherence a cyclic graph in the stated class would have a minimal nonface of size at most five, which is impossible. The proof also applies if the presentation is the dependency-closed support of a label in a larger relatively coherent context, by §3 item 18.

The same test applies in generator dimension $3+s$ to any $s$-fold suspension of these presentations. Free geometric suspension shifts the fixed-pole hom back to the original one: on a globe this is the suspension–hom adjunction, and taking the attaching pushouts gives it for the full presentation. Strict suspension shifts the label in the same way. Suspend the coloured contexts, their two kinds of maps and the chosen poles. The colour-to-variable maps remain generator-preserving in positive contexts. Use the $(3+s)$-fold suspensions of the parameter faces in those contexts. The same degree argument gives failure in some degree at most $m+2+s$, without assuming that relative coherence reflects along a desuspension of arbitrary terms.

**Two overlapping cycles.** Here is a full-support example where the fibre is neither a point nor a single sphere. Take seven lanes, alternating four signals with three seams. Call the seam pairs $(A,B),(C,D),(E,F)$ in deletion–insertion order. Each deletion also acts on the signal immediately to its left, each insertion on the signal immediately to its right. Use two extra events $Z$ spanning signals 0 to 2 and $Y$ spanning signals 1 to 3, inclusive. The successive active signal lists from left to right are
$$
                          ZA,\quad BYC,\quad DZE,\quad FY .
$$
At each signal use distinct parallel gates for its successive states; at each seam use the two loops $d,e$. Flank each changing part by one prefix and one suffix gate with fresh exterior edge types. In the interior of its span $Z$ reads before the prefix and $Y$ after the suffix. Every simultaneous tier has a serialization with $Z$ first and $Y$ last when present, and the three seam choices among the short markers are independent. Use every event once and include the flanking letters in both endpoints. With just these declarations, every generator occurs in the label or its boundaries.

The seam graph is $1\rightleftarrows2\rightleftarrows3$. The cubical model consists of the two opposite squares on coordinates $1,3$ and the four edges joining corresponding vertices in coordinate 2. Contracting each square to a vertex gives a graph with two vertices and four edges, hence a wedge of three circles. The [typed-history checker](../../../tools/relative_coherence_seam_cycles.py) independently enumerates the 14,198 cells of the original fibre, checks every facet of the visible-sign map, and computes mod-two Betti numbers $(1,3)$. # Researcher Response 11

## A product formula for substituting higher presentations at top generators

Let $P$ be a finite positive presentation of dimension $d\ge1$. For each generating $d$-cell $g$ choose a finite CaTT presentation $K_g$. Replace $g$ by the $d$-fold suspension of $K_g$ at the original attaching poles of $g$. Thus each object of $K_g$ gives a parallel $d$-generator of the old type, and its higher declarations give generators in the correspondingly shifted types. Call the result $D$. The lower faces of $g$ may be composites. No positivity or homotopicality assumption on $K_g$ is needed for the formula.

For a strict $d$-cell $w$ of $P$, let $O(w)$ be the set of top-generator occurrences in its generic positive polyplex, with $g(o)$ the label of occurrence $o$. A strict $(d+j)$-cell of $D$ is uniquely specified by $w$ and a strict $j$-cell $u_o$ of $K_{g(o)}$ for every $o\in O(w)$. Identification of occurrences here and below uses the unique identification of generic polyplexes over their universal arrows. For every $j\ge0$ there is an equivalence of actual globe fibres
$$
 E_D\bigl(w,(u_o)_o\bigr)\ \simeq\ E_P(w)\ \times\!
          \prod_{o\in O(w)}E_{K_{g(o)}}(u_o).
$$
Labels of dimension below $d$ have their old fibres. In particular homotopical presentations remain homotopical under this substitution if all the inserted presentations are homotopical. The formula also retains the homotopy type of a noncontractible factor and distinguishes repeated occurrences of one generator.

**Colouring occurrences.** First replace each $g$ by a discrete set $S_g$ of parallel $d$-generators, obtaining $P[S]$. Above $w$ its strict cells are precisely the choices of an element of $S_{g(o)}$ at every $o$. Indeed the lower skeleton is unchanged, and a lift of the map from the generic polyplex of $w$ consists exactly of these choices on its top generators. In a composite at a cut below $d$ the two top occurrence sets form a disjoint union, since the common boundary has lower dimension. These identifications respect associativity, exchange and units by the polyplex pushout formula and rigidity. In particular a specified factorization of the projection of a coloured cell lifts uniquely by restriction of its colours. Identities lift uniquely as well. This is the discrete Conduché property for $F(P[S])\to F(P)$. [Henry, §§2.2–2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00744)

Writing $X=L\mathrm{Free}$, we have
$$
             X_{P[S]}\simeq X_P\times_{NF(P)} NF(P[S]).
$$
Pull back the cellular construction of $X_P$. The lower skeleton is unaffected since a strict cell over a lower-dimensional identity has no top occurrences. For the characteristic $d$-globe of $g$, the pullback has exactly its old boundary and one parallel $d$-cell for each $s\in S_g$. This is $\Sigma^d S_g$: in the coordinate of the characteristic globe there are no pairs of composable nonidentity generators. It is the homotopy pushout attaching these parallel disks along the original boundary, by the directed suspension–hom adjunction with the poles fixed. The attaching maps to the lower skeleton are the original ones. Pullback preserves these colimits by the discrete Conduché theorem. The argument allows empty colour sets, and arbitrary small sets follow also by filtered unions of finite sets. [Loubaton, Definition 2.2.2.2 and Theorem 2.2.2.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425)

The pullback argument also gives a fact for unrestricted **positive** presentations. For any generator-preserving $f:Λ\to P$, the square of geometric comparisons over $NF(Λ)\to NF(P)$ is cartesian. Indeed restriction of a polyplex labelling along the two pieces of a specified composite gives the same discrete Conduché lifting, now in every dimension. For a characteristic $n$-globe let $Z=F(Λ)\times_{F(P)}\mathbb D^n$. Its nonidentity cells project to nonidentity cells in the globe in their least dimension, by unique lifting of identities. Hence there are no compositions of two nonunits in $Z$: in the globe any pair composable at cut $i$ has a unit at that cut, and a unit projects in $F(P)$ to a unit so also lifts as one in $F(Λ)$. Thus $Z$ is freely generated by its nonidentity cells with their globular faces. This basis is strong Steiner: all incidence inequalities are strict inequalities in the strong ordering of the globe under projection, and iterated signed boundaries are single basis elements of augmentation one at rank zero. The same holds over the globe boundary. By item 8, passing from that pullback boundary to $NZ$ is a geometric attachment of one $n$-globe per top lift. Each lift of the unwhiskered generator is itself a generator by the representing plex of a generator. In rank zero this just attaches the lifted objects. Induction on the attaching pushouts, using that $f$ preserves the actual weak attaching terms, proves the square is cartesian. In particular $E_Λ(v)\simeq E_P(fv)$ for any lifted strict label $v$, even when $f$ identifies generators. This applies to the full generic polyplex of a positive label.

**Computing the free geometric substitution.** Put $Y_g=X_{K_g}$. The defining cellular pushouts for $D$ give the pushout of $X_{P_{<d}}$ and the $\Sigma^dY_g$ over their copies of $\partial\mathbb D^d$. This follows first for each inserted globe by suspension and then for all its attachments by the same adjunction with poles fixed. Represent it in iterated Segal spaces, with $d$ outer simplicial directions and enough inner directions for all the $K_g$. Begin with the presheaf homotopy pushout of the displayed diagram of nerves. At a fixed inner rectangle $R$, the suspended summand in the horizontal diagram is the family of parallel $d$-cells parametrized by the **space** $S_g=Y_g(R)$. Indeed one directed suspension has two objects, only identity endohoms and the given object as its forward hom. Iterating gives the assertion at every rectangle, including the degeneracies. The boundary and the lower skeleton are constant in $R$.

We give the flag check for localizing this horizontal diagram. It matters here because at an arbitrary inner rectangle the universal property only permits horizontal Segal localization. Restriction from uncompleted $d$-fold Segal objects to the first $d-1$ globular dimensions preserves colimits. To see this in the equivalent $\Theta_d$ model, use Haugseng's monad $T_d$ on globular spaces. Restriction $r$ commutes with the free monad in those dimensions: the formula at the $i$-globe sums over trees of dimension at most $i$. Its right adjoint on globular spaces therefore lifts to algebras: the structure on $r_*A$ is adjoint to $rT_dr_*A\simeq T_{d-1}rr_*A\to A$. The unit and multiplication follow from the same truncation of trees. This gives the claimed colimit preservation. Equivalently, restriction commutes with Segal localization of presheaves: the two colimit-preserving functors agree on representables, whose restrictions are already Segal nerves of the corresponding truncated strict shapes. In rectangular coordinates one may also apply colimit preservation directly to the diagrams of Segal objects used here. [Haugseng, Proposition 3.3, Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.08480)

For discrete $S_g$, horizontal *completion* of the pushout is the coloured object just calculated. Before completion, restriction of the horizontal Segal pushout through dimension $d-1$ gives exactly $X_{P_{<d}}$: in that restriction each suspended family is its boundary. The flags through dimension $d-1$ of its completion are the same, by the support pullback of Response 2 for these lower globe labels. Completion is fully faithful on top hom spaces between fixed lower flags, successively in the iterated Segal model. Since the lower flag maps are equivalences, the horizontal comparison is already an equivalence before completion. In particular, at a horizontal rectangular tree $T$ its value is a coproduct over strict labellings $\psi:T\to F(P)$. The summand is
$$
 X_P(T)_\psi\ \times\!
 \prod_{b\text{ a generating }d\text{-cell of }T}\;
 \prod_{o\in O(\psi b)} S_{g(o)} .
$$
The first factor is the fibre of the evaluation at $T$ over the labelling $\psi$. This computation is natural in colour maps.

For spaces $S_g$, resolve them simultaneously by simplicial sets viewed as simplicial diagrams of discrete spaces. The inputs to the horizontal pushout commute with geometric realization of these diagrams: at a rectangle a parallel family consists of the unchanged lower diagrams and the choices of colours for its displayed slots. These are finite products, and geometric realization commutes with finite products in spaces. The same observation applies to the last displayed formula, with the factors $X_P(T)_\psi$ held constant. Its objectwise realization is horizontally Segal. In a spine decomposition the top input slots partition among the pieces; their overlaps have lower dimension, and the remaining pullbacks are precisely the Segal pullbacks for $X_P$. Thus this objectwise realization computes the colimit in horizontal Segal objects as well. This proves the formula for space-valued colours without discretizing $Y_g(R)$.

Substitute $Y_g(R)$ for $S_g$ in that formula. In the inner directions each summand is a product of complete Segal objects and a constant space. Their coproduct is complete Segal because all inner face and degeneracy maps preserve $\psi$. The outer globularity conditions hold because a lower-dimensional horizontal rectangle has no top slots. For outer completeness it suffices to test the bottom inner layer. Its comparison to $X_P$ has exactly the old equivalences: in the gaunt strict target a horizontal equivalence projects to an identity, and over such an identity in dimension $d$ the colour product has no factors and is a point. Below $d$ all the flag spaces are unchanged. Hence the bottom layer is horizontally complete as $X_P$ is. These are the iterated completeness tests. The horizontal localization of the presheaf pushout is therefore already local for the full model. Every map to a full local object factors through that localization, which proves it computes $X_D$.

For clarity, on the strict side apply the same occurrence formula with $NF(K_g)$ as inner diagrams and $NF(P)$ outside. Horizontal composition joins occurrence lists by the polyplex pushout, and inner composition is pointwise. This is the free strict category on $D$. To check its universal property, evaluate a tuple on an expression for $w$ by replacing each top occurrence with its labelled $(d+j)$-cell and lower inputs with iterated identities. A different expression gives the same value: the strict relations at cuts below $d$ act on these labelled inputs and use precisely the canonical identifications of occurrences above. Equivalently evaluate the generic polyplex in the strict $d$-category having the receiving category's $(d+j)$-cells as its $d$-cells with cuts below $d$, and its original cells below $d$. This evaluation preserves inner composition by strict interchange. Conversely each slot label is generated by its inserted presentation and the slots compose to the chosen tuple, forcing the evaluation in any strict target. The resulting comparison from $X_D$ acts by the original comparisons on each factor. Evaluation at the $(d+j)$-globe has one horizontal $d$-input, and the inner test is the $j$-globe, with all its boundaries allowed to vary. Taking the fibre at the strict tuple proves the product formula.

**An all-dimensional consequence under relative coherence.** Start with finite presentations of dimension at most two. Iterate the substitution above any finite number of times, requiring the outer presentation at each substitution to be positive and each inserted presentation to have an object. If the resulting context satisfies $\mathrm{RC}_{\mathrm{id}}(∞)$, **every geometric fibre is contractible**, including full-support labels in its top dimension. There is no bound on generator dimension or occurrence count. To prove it, a nonidentity 2-bubble in any starting presentation would survive as a higher bubble in the result. When carrying an outer shape forward, select one object per inserted presentation. This gives a strict section of the projection onto the shape (sending higher decorations to identities), so preserves nonidentity. When carrying an inserted factor forward, suspend its bubble at the attaching poles. Over the single occurrence of $g$ the strict tuple formula distinguishes every cell of that factor, and suspension preserves the iterated-identity form of the bubble boundary. Repeating these two steps along the construction proves survival. Item 20 excludes the resulting bubble under relative coherence. Thus every starting 2-presentation is bubble-free and is homotopical by item 8; the lower-dimensional starting cases are homotopical as well. Induction with the product formula now proves the claim. This argument does not require desuspending an arbitrary CaTT filler.

A concrete unconditional case is every finite positive context in which both faces of each generator of dimension at least three are variables. In the top rank $d+1$, group the $d$-variables by their literal type and keep one representative per group. This is a positive $d$-dimensional outer shape. Inside each group the original $d$-variables are the vertices of a graph and the $(d+1)$-generators its arrows. Substitution of these graphs reconstructs the presentation. Induction on dimension terminates at a positive 2-presentation, which is homotopical by item 8. A graph presents its free category homotopically, including with loops and parallel arrows. Hence all the fibres in this case are contractible even without assuming relative coherence. Composite paths in the boundaries of 2-generators, repeated uses of the same generator, and cycles of top changes are allowed.

For example, take one object, a loop $f$, and $p:f⇒f$. Replace $p$ by a triangle with two paths $hk,l$ and a 2-cell $t:hk⇒l$. Above $(p*_1p)*_0p$, applying the suspended $t$ in all three slots gives a full-support 4-term. Its fibre is contractible. The [substitution checker](../../../tools/relative_coherence_substitution.py) enumerates the 222 serial histories from the three initial to the three final colours, obtaining eight path tuples. Its Agda output checks the actual 4-term with both lower cuts in the repository's certified CaTT checker. As a test with nontrivial fibres, substitute the presentation of Response 3 at a generating loop in dimension one and use the loop twice. At the pair of circle labels the formula gives a torus. Independently synchronizing the two copies of the two-event charts gives 24 vertices, 48 edges, 28 two-cells and four three-cells, with mod-two Betti numbers $(1,2,1)$.

This covers substitutions in which each inserted operation acts within one occurrence of an outer generator. The cyclic seam changes of Responses 8–10 can act across several occurrences. Their general algebraic obstruction, and the full-support necessity statement for unrestricted presentations, remain open here.

# Researcher Response 12

## The target map computes the whole fibre for many-to-one attachments

Let $B$ be a finite positive CaTT context of dimension $d\ge1$. Adjoin finitely many $(d+1)$-generators $z:s_z\to g_z$, where $g_z$ is a single generating $d$-variable and $s_z$ is any parallel term with nonidentity strict image. Denote the extension by $D$. For every strict $(d+1)$-cell $c:u\to v$, restriction to its target induces an equivalence
$$
                         E_D(c)\ \xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ }\ E_B(v).
$$
Here the lower boundaries vary in both globe mapping spaces. The formula allows repeated occurrences, multiple rules with the same target, and arbitrary noncontractible fibres in $B$. If all the sources are variables instead, restriction to the source gives $E_D(c)\simeq E_B(u)$.

In particular such an extension is homotopical exactly when its base is. This applies when only the **top rank** is many-to-one, even if generators of lower ranks have composites on both sides. The opposite face of a top generator can involve several interacting occurrences. The all-ranks positive-to-one case was already known (§3 item 19).

**The simplicial diagram of changes.** Use $d$ horizontal nerve directions and one last direction for time. At time index $j$ write $B_j$ for $B$ with fresh $d$-generators $h(z,i)$ of the type of $g_z$ for $1\le i\le j$. Let $b_j:B_j\to B$ fold each marker to $g_z$. In a simplicial restriction the marker records the single change of a nonconstant map $[j]\to[1]$. A restriction on which the map is constant substitutes the corresponding face of $z$. In particular evaluation at the last vertex is exactly $b_j$.

Start with the geometric attaching pushout for $D$, formed before complete Segal localization. At time $j$ its horizontal localization is $X_{B_j}$. Indeed the $(d+1)$-globe at this index is a family of parallel $d$-globes indexed by the maps $[j]\to[1]$. Its boundary contains the two constants and the common lower boundary. The horizontal pushout therefore appends just the indicated markers. The flag check of Response 11 applies to this pushout. Through rank $d-1$, restriction of the uncompleted Segal pushout preserves colimits and each new disk contributes just its boundary. Its lower flags are those of $X_{B_{<d}}$, which also give the lower flags after completion by Response 2. Full faithfulness of completion on top homs then identifies the uncompleted horizontal localization with $X_{B_j}$. The identification comes from the attaching maps and is natural under time restrictions, including substitution of the composite source.

Fix a horizontal rectangle $R$. Let $T_j=NF(B_j)(R)$ and $V_j=X_{B_j}(R)$, so $T$ is a simplicial set and $V$ is a simplicial space over it. Since $b_j$ is a colouring projection in the sense of Response 11, its geometric comparison square is cartesian. Evaluation at the last vertex thus gives
$$
                         V_j\simeq T_j\times_{T_0}V_0.                 \tag{1}
$$
This is a statement about last vertices. Restriction to a first vertex can act nontrivially on the factor $V_0$.

**The category presented by $T$.** First take $R$ to select a single $d$-slot with its lower strict poles fixed. The vertices are strict $d$-cells, and the changes in a simplex form a simultaneous marked lower diagram with specified times. Read a one-step change backwards from its target $v$. It consists precisely of an occurrence of $g_z$ in $v$ and a choice of $z$. This follows from the colouring bijection of Response 11 applied to a diagram with exactly one marker. More generally a simultaneous diagram ending at $v$ is a set of *distinct* top occurrences in $v$, each marked by its applicable rule. Equality of labels at two different occurrences leaves those occurrences distinct.

On expanding a marked occurrence backwards by $s_z$, the new occurrence set consists of the surviving occurrences and the top occurrences of $\pi s_z$. To see the identification, give each surviving occurrence, and each occurrence of each inserted source, its own fresh colour over its generator. Evaluate the resulting coloured source in the marked lower diagram by strict substitution. Each fresh colour occurs once, since composing at a cut below $d$ takes the disjoint union of the top occurrence sets. The colouring bijection now locates those occurrences canonically in the uncoloured result. Evaluation respects the strict equations. It also shows that expanding several distinct slots successively produces the same labelled occurrence set as simultaneous expansion, in any order. This argument only uses rigidity and composition for positive polyplexes. [Henry, §§2.2–2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00744)

A serial history read backwards from $v$ therefore specifies a rooted forest. Its root slots are the occurrences of $v$. An expansion at a slot puts a node labelled by $z$ there, with child slots indexed by the occurrences of $\pi s_z$. Unexpanded slots carry no node. Every linear extension of the ancestor order is a legal reverse history: after any ideal has been expanded, precisely its exposed slots are available, with their canonical addresses. Incomparable exposed nodes have a unique simultaneous diagram by the colouring bijection. Conversely any simultaneous diagram can be split in either order at any partition of its marked occurrences. Thus two serial histories have the same strict $(d+1)$-label exactly when they have the same forest. In detail, equality in a free strict extension is generated by exchanges in two-hole lower contexts and equality of the elementary contexts. Each such exchange preserves the forest by simultaneous expansion. Two linear extensions of a single forest are connected by adjacent swaps of incomparable nodes, and the unique two-marker context realizes each swap. [Ara et al., Appendix D.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00429)

For a fixed label $c$ the mapping space of the categorification of $T$ is contractible. A necklace representing $c$, read backwards bead by bead, lifts uniquely to a chain of ideals in its forest from the empty ideal to the full ideal, the difference across each bead being an antichain. To recover a simplex of a bead, mark the exposed slots and assign the recorded times. Conversely, serializing the bead and then forgetting the ordering within each simultaneous change recovers the same slots and times. These constructions respect splitting beads, restricting times and inserting degeneracies, so they identify the rigidified mapping space with the extreme hom of the antichain cubing in Response 4, with time reversed. Its inclusion in the full ideal nerve is a categorical equivalence by the interval argument there. The identity label has the empty forest and a point as its hom. Consequently the categorification of $T$ is the ordinary category of strict $(d+1)$-arrows at these poles. For any rectangle, horizontal spine decomposition first fixes the discrete lower labelling and then takes products over its $d$-slots. Categorification preserves coproducts and finite products, giving the same conclusion at $R$: the vertical categorification of $T$ is the vertical nerve of $F(D)$ there. This calculation uses the strict marked diagrams regardless of whether $B$ is homotopical. [Dugger–Spivak on necklace rigidification](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.0814)

**Carrying the lower fibre through categorification.** We give the argument because a calculation of the strict forests alone would lose the homotopy already present in $B$. For a simplicial space $V$ over a simplicial set $T$ satisfying (1), its categorification over the categorification $C$ of $T$ is a right fibration, with fibre over a vertex $v$ equal to $(V_0)_v$. Indeed take the presheaf of spaces on the simplex category of $T$ that assigns to $\sigma$ its fibre in $V_{\dim\sigma}$. By (1) it inverts maps preserving the last vertex. Localization of the simplex category at those maps presents $C$, so the presheaf classifies a right fibration over $C$. On each simplex $[j]\to C$ its pullback has as its complete Segal nerve exactly $V\times_T\Delta^j$. On an $i$-simplex of $\Delta^j$ the corresponding factor is the value of the presheaf on that restricted simplex, equivalently its value at the last vertex, with transition maps given by the presheaf. Finally $C$ is the colimit of its presenting simplices indexed by the simplex category of $T$. Pullback along a right fibration preserves this colimit by exponentiability. On the input side the analogous simplex decomposition is the presheaf colimit for $V$ since pullback of spaces preserves colimits. Categorification preserves that colimit. This proves the assertion including its naturality in maps of such diagrams. [Last-vertex localization](https://kerodon.net/tag/02MC) · [Ayala–Francis on exponentiable fibrations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.02681)

Apply this argument at every horizontal rectangle. Write $W_{R,k}$ for the vertical complete Segal nerve of the categorized $V$, and $S_{R,k}=NF(D)(R,[k])$ for the strict nerve. As $F(D)$ is gaunt, the right-fibration description gives, by last-vertex evaluation,
$$
                  W_{R,k}\simeq S_{R,k}\times_{S_{R,0}} X_B(R).     \tag{2}
$$
This also proves that the successive localizations compute the fully localized geometric attachment. At fixed $k$ the right side is a pullback of horizontal Segal objects, natural in $R$, so the horizontal Segal conditions hold. On lower rectangles the time direction is constant by (2) and globularity of $S$. Vertical completeness holds by construction. Vertical equivalences project to identities in the gaunt base, and over each vertex the right fibration is a space. Its core is therefore exactly its vertex space up to equivalence. The remaining horizontal completeness tests are those of the bottom layer $X_B$, which is complete. The result is local for all the defining conditions and has the universal property of the original attachment, since each of the two localization steps is required of every fully local target. Thus $W$ presents $X_D$.

Evaluating (2) on the $d$ outer single-edge directions and one time edge gives the claimed equivalence by taking the fibre at $c$. For atomic sources the first-vertex version uses forward expansion and a left fibration, reversing only the time coordinate. Lower labels retain their fibres by Response 2, and higher identity labels have the fibre of their $(d+1)$-base. This proves the assertion about homotopicality.

**Consequences under relative coherence.** A smallest finite positive counterexample to necessity has neither a uniform generating target nor a uniform generating source among its top declarations. Otherwise its lower skeleton inherits relative coherence, is homotopical by minimality, and (2) gives contractibility. More generally one can start with any positive base already proved homotopical under relative coherence, including the interacting three-dimensional families of Responses 4–10 under their stated hypotheses. Iterate the extensions above with either uniform choice at each new rank. If the final context is relatively coherent, heredity supplies the hypothesis on the base, and every full-support fibre in every new rank is contractible. The formula also transports an obstruction when relative coherence fails. In the circle example of Response 3, adjoin a 4-generator $a\to a$ on one of the two generating 3-events. Apply it at that event in a strict serialization of the circle label. The resulting full-support 4-label has the same circle as its fibre by target restriction.

**Checks.** The [forest checker](../../../tools/relative_coherence_forests.py) enumerates simultaneous adjacent binary and ternary contractions on a seven-letter word. Independently it generates plane forests by partitioning leaf intervals. It matches every chart string with a schedule for exactly one forest and compares every facet obtained by splitting a tier, including the shift of word positions. For one output there are 654 forests, 4,122 strings and 4,710 facet incidences. For two outputs the counts are 444, 3,036 and 3,588. With $a:p\to p$ a 3-generator and $z:(a*_2 a)\to a$ a 4-generator, the checker also emits the full-support term $(z*_2 z)*_3 z$. Agda checks its type in safe mode using the repository's certified CaTT checker. The categorical equivalence in (2) is proved above. The general full-support case, including top generators with composite faces on both sides, remains open.

# Researcher Response 13

## A reflection detects full-support spheres in arbitrarily high degree

There is an all-dimensional obstruction using only globes. Suppose a symmetry of the CaTT presentation fixes a strict $m$-label $c$ and a choice of its weak poles, and reverses the two faces in every dimension above $m$. It may permute lower poles and reverse lower dimensions, provided it preserves the indicated lifting problem. If there is an equivariant map from the geometric fibre at these poles to the antipodal $S^{k-1}$, then $\mathrm{RC}_{\mathrm{id}}(n)$ fails for some $n\le m+k-1$. Here and below the symmetry is an involution of the presentation with the indicated opposites. This gives a test for **every** cycle length in the visible-sign model of Response 10 when a symmetry reverses all signs on a minimal cycle. In particular there are positive three-dimensional seam presentations with full-support fibre $S^{k-1}$ for which relative coherence fails, for every $k\ge2$, including $k=6$. We calculate separately what the reflection of the original one-bridge ring detects.

**Proof of the obstruction.** Choose a weak $m$-term $x_0$ over $c$ at the prescribed poles by relative boundary lifting. Let $y_0=x_0^\dagger$. They have the same type and image. Given $x_{j-1},y_{j-1}$, use relative coherence to choose
$$
                  x_j:x_{j-1}\longrightarrow y_{j-1},
                  \qquad y_j=x_j^\dagger .
$$
Choose the identity strict image at each step. Reversal in dimension $m+j$ and $\dagger^2=1$ give $y_j:x_{j-1}\to y_{j-1}$ with the *same literal endpoints*. Thus the condition through degree $m+k-2$ constructs the terms up to $j=k-1$, and a further filler between that pair would give the stage $j=k$ by reflection. The first lift of $c$ uses at most the conditions through degree $m-1$.

For the topology, take the globular set with two generators $p_j,q_j$ in each rank $0\le j\le r$, both higher generators going from $p_{j-1}$ to $q_{j-1}$. Its free geometric groupoidification is $S^r$. In rank zero it is two points, and in each successive rank it attaches two disks along the entire preceding sphere. With all positive parameter dimensions reversed, interchanging $p_j,q_j$ induces the antipodal action: it interchanges the two cones on the previous sphere and acts by its previous involution on their equator. Give its $m$-fold suspension the indicated action on its outer poles. The displayed terms are an equivariant substitution out of this suspension. By the suspension–hom correspondence they induce a homotopy-coherent equivariant map from $S^r$ into the geometric fixed-pole fibre over $c$. Here “fixed-pole” means that the poles are prescribed. The points of the fibre may move under the involution. [Opposites of computads and their compatibility with homs, §§5.2–5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01611v2)

We spell out the equivariance in passing to geometry. Reversal relabels the globular pasting trees, including their generating globes and their inclusions. On a free pasting tree the semantic interpretation of a CaTT operation is the designated strict composite or identity in the gaunt tree. The space of choices over that value, relative to its already interpreted boundary, is contractible: both the globe and its boundary have discrete mapping spaces into this tree, and the specified extending map exists. Consequently the comparison with reversed interpretation can be chosen coherently. More explicitly, work first on the free heads of the coherator in order of attachment. With the previous boundary fixed, the choices over any orbit of heads form a product of these contractible spaces. Taking homotopy fixed points still gives a contractible space, since its equivariant map to the point is an objectwise equivalence. Freeness extends the choices over substitution; taking the limit of the stages gives the coherent comparison. Realizing a computad then attaches the generating globes by colimits, naturally for these operations. On the parameter above its groupoidification can be taken to be the explicit pair-of-cones model at each rank. Equivalently the resulting map is one over $BC_2$ on Borel constructions, which suffices for the obstruction. [Realization on the coherator, Known (I)–(II)](catt-models-unit-condition-r4.md)

An equivariant map from the resulting $S^{k-1}$ to the antipodal $S^{k-1}$ has odd degree. It cannot extend over one hemisphere of $S^k$. Equivalently an equivariant map $S^k\to S^{k-1}$ would induce a map of projective spaces preserving the double-cover class $w$. Its $k$th power is nonzero in $H^k(\mathbb{RP}^k;\mathbb F_2)$ and zero on $\mathbb{RP}^{k-1}$. The Borel constructions give the same argument for coherent maps. This proves the bound and shows precisely which two terms witness failure if the earlier lifts exist. More generally any finite-dimensional regular fibre complex acted on cellularly in this way must have an invariant cell under relative coherence. Otherwise choose opposite signs on each pair of cells and take each signed vertex of its barycentric subdivision to the corresponding signed coordinate vector indexed by cell dimension. A chain uses a dimension at most once, giving an equivariant map to a finite antipodal sphere. In the seam test, projection to the visible signs on a minimal cycle supplies the equivariant map directly. A cyclic label with this symmetry is therefore impossible under relative coherence, and a label with acyclic seam graph has contractible fibre by Response 10.

**A ring with two reflected bridges.** Keep the lane positions $s_0,p_1,s_1,\ldots,p_k,s_k$ of Response 8. All 1-arrows on one lane go between its consecutive objects. Distinct edge types below indicate distinct 1-generators on that lane.

* At a seam use edges $q^-,p,q^+$, gates $t^-:q^-\Rightarrow p$, $t^+:p\Rightarrow q^+$, and loops $d,e:p\Rightarrow p$. The initial and final words are $t^-dt^+$ and $t^-et^+$.
* An internal signal has edges $q^-,u,v,q^+$, gates $t^-:q^-\Rightarrow u$, $t^+:v\Rightarrow q^+$, and three gates $L,M,R:u\Rightarrow v$. Its endpoints are $t^-Lt^+$ and $t^-Rt^+$.
* An end signal has edges $u,p,v$. For $G=L,M,R$ use $G^-:u\Rightarrow p$ and $G^+:p\Rightarrow v$. Write $G$ for the word $G^-G^+$ there. Use $L$ initially and $R$ finally.

The short 3-generators $a_i,b_i$ have the same two-lane rules as in Response 8, with the displayed two-gate words at end signals. The bridge $z^-$ changes $L^-\to M^-$ at $s_0$ and $M^-\to R^-$ at $s_k$, reading the identity on $q^-$ on every interior lane. The bridge $z^+$ uses the plus gates and $q^+$ instead. For the literal weak terms take the unbiased vertical composite of the letters of each word, an explicit identity for each empty word, and the unbiased horizontal composite over the lanes. This choice matters for the three-letter words. Denote the whole weak endpoints by $U,V$ and the strict label of all insertions, both bridges and then all deletions by $c$.

Take the opposite in dimension two and all dimensions at least four. At the ends exchange $u,v$ and the two signs of each gate. In the interior exchange $q^-,q^+$ and $t^-,t^+$, exchange $u,v$ at internal signals, and fix the changing gates. Exchange the two bridges and fix all short events. Dimension-two reversal reverses the order of a vertical word and preserves horizontal lane order. The opposite of an unbiased composite has the reflected tree with the reflected list of inputs, by recursive restriction to its two boundary trees. Thus both weak poles $U,V$ are literally fixed, and each declaration has the type of its specified reflected declaration. This defines the required involution on all terms.

The temporal orders are the transitive consequences of
$$
           b_i<a_{i+1}\ (i<k),\qquad b_k<z^-<a_1,\qquad b_k<z^+<a_1 .
$$
The positive lower and marked 2-presentations satisfy the hypotheses of the chart computation in Response 4. For fixed seam signs, every ordered partition strictly respecting these orders gives exactly one chart string. On an end signal the two bridges act on their separate typed gates, and a short event reads or changes both gates together. On interior lanes the bridge reads are forced to opposite exterior gaps. In a simultaneous tier place $z^-$ first and $z^+$ last when present; the other required spatial comparisons are the disjoint seam-pair comparisons. Insert unary ordinary gates in their prescribed lane positions. This proves existence and uniqueness by the lane orders, including for a tier containing both bridges. Conversely the types and the signal changes force these same orders. Splitting a tier gives the corresponding faces. The histories use every generator, and all choices constitute a single strict label: within a sheet the orders are connected by exchanges, and sheets differing at a single coordinate meet.

As in Response 8 an intersection of sign sheets with differing coordinates $D$ imposes $a_i<b_i$ at every $i\in D$. Either bridge completes a cycle when all coordinates are imposed. Omitting one breaks both cycles. All nonempty intersections are therefore contractible ideal-cubing complexes, so $E(c)\simeq S^{k-1}$. The lower strict labels have contractible fibres, so the same calculation applies at the chosen poles. The reflection of a chart reverses every spatial word on a lane and exchanges the bridge names, with time order unchanged. It fixes $c$ and reverses every visible sign, including the sign read from the intermediate word when insertion occurs first. The map taking a cell to the face spanned by its hidden coordinates consequently gives an equivariant map to $\partial I^k$ with its central reflection. The obstruction above proves failure in some degree at most $k+2$ for **every** $k$. It also applies after any number of directed suspensions by shifting the poles and the opposite dimensions. In particular this excludes relative coherence for the six-seam two-bridge context in degree at most eight.

**What the original symmetry does.** The original $Γ_k$ has a different involution: reverse the line and reverse time (opposites in dimensions one and three), exchange $a_i$ with $b_{k+1-i}$, exchange $L,R$ and $d,e$ on reflected lanes, and keep the bridge. Its exterior reads stay before the prefixes. On the visible-sign cube the action is
$$
                         (x_1,\ldots,x_k)\longmapsto(-x_k,\ldots,-x_1).
$$
In fact the fixed locus of the original chart involution has homotopy type $S^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor-1}$. To see this, restrict the visible-sign face map of Response 10 to the fixed face posets. An invariant simplex in a barycentric subdivision is a chain of individually invariant cells. For an invariant cube face with free set $D$, the poset fibre test is the fixed part of the intersection of its extending sheets. It consists of the temporally ordered partitions of $H_D$ unchanged by reversing time and relabelling the events. This poset has contractible nerve. Assign real times $t_e$ with $t_e<t_f$ for each strict order and $t_{\rho e}=-t_e$ for the event involution $\rho$. The open convex region is nonempty: average any feasible vector with its reflected negative. Its braid fan strata are exactly the invariant feasible ordered partitions, since the relative interior of every invariant stratum contains its own averaged point. Their closure order is refinement up to reversal. Intersect the fan with the unit sphere in the antisymmetric time subspace. There is a strict order, so the region misses the origin and its spherical section is contractible by radial normalization of straight-line contraction to any of its points. In the barycentric subdivision of the fan this open region retracts to the order complex of the feasible strata: on each simplex drop the weights of excluded faces and normalize the remaining weights. At least one remains and the straight-line homotopy stays in the region. Thus all the required inverse order ideals are contractible. The poset fibre lemma identifies the fixed locus with the fixed boundary of the sign cube, the boundary of a cube of dimension $\lfloor k/2\rfloor$. In particular the original six-seam reflection fixes a space of type $S^2$; the antipodal obstruction above cannot be applied to that action.
