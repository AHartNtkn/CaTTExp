## 0. Summary

Without universes, the rule list for "ordinary mathematics" has grown one rule at a time: R-U, EX, Trunc_n, T-coprod (disjointness), G, DJ, R-A⁺, G⁺, R-cot⁺, and the function-set rows GB-shape and L-×. This note sorts the list by **what job a universe would have done** and finds that the universe-shaped rules do only two jobs, each of which one general schema covers.

| Job a universe does | Rules on the list doing that job | General schema proposed here |
|---|---|---|
| **Exactness**: say what maps *into* a colimit, or pullbacks of a colimit, look like ("encode–decode") | EX, T-coprod and disjointness, G (fibres and transport of lax colimits), G⁺, the fibre clauses of R-U | **(DESC)** descent for cocartesian fibrations along colimits, lax descent (DESC-or) along oriented colimits, plus descent for all maps along groupoid-indexed colimits |
| **Construction**: build an object by a universal property *out of* it, including object-valued recursion | Trunc_n, R-A, R-A⁺, ℕ (route I), lists, W-types, R-U, Θ as an object | **(INIT)** every positive finite context (targets limit-preserving, sources arbitrary accessible) has an initial model for strong model morphisms; read impredicatively, this is a System-F quantifier `∀Δ. X` with naturality |
| Representability (Yoneda) | DJ | none new: DJ is a theorem of the limit rules, following Riehl–Verity's proof in an abstract homotopy 2-category, **[proved here, sketch]**, §5 |
| Not universe-related | GB-shape, L-× | already instances of one schema, the chapter 8 row table (`spec/08`, F.7.4); out of F10's scope, §6 |

With DESC and INIT, every rule in F10's list is either an instance of one of the two schemata or a candidate theorem; §7 gives the status of each. Two schemata are a **fixed, finite** list: a new inductive object, quotient, truncation or recursively defined family is a new *instance*, never a new rule. The semantic questions went to a researcher and are answered (`conjectures/answered/universe-free-descent-and-initial-models-2026-11-21.md`, verified): DESC holds for every small colimit, DESC-or holds along oriented colimits, and INIT holds exactly for limit-preserving targets with strong model morphisms (§3.2, §4.2). What stays open is listed in §8.

Candidate (b) of the ledger, a W-types-over-a-base schema, is the special case of INIT for polynomial contexts. It cannot be derived from colimits alone (§4.1): building the Adámek tower already needs object-valued recursion. So INIT is the most general form of (b) (standard 4).

## 1. What a universe does, and why the list kept growing

A universe 𝒰 gives three things at once:
1. **Families by recursion.** A map `B → 𝒰` is a family over B, and recursion into 𝒰 defines families whose fibres are computed by a formula (U_T = ⟦T⟧ in R-U; `x ↦ R(x, –)` in the HoTT proof of effectiveness).
2. **Descent.** Univalence makes families over a colimit the same as compatible families over its pieces (Rezk: descent is equivalent to the existence of univalent object classifiers for bounded classes; Rijke's thesis gives the type-theoretic version for pushouts) **[recall; Rezk's theorem number not re-checked, see §2]**.
3. **Impredicative constructions** (with an impredicative 𝒰): truncations, quotients and inductive types as System-F encodings (Awodey–Frey–Speight).

Each rule on F10's list replaced one *use* of one of these, at one place. That is why the list grows. Replacing the three capabilities themselves, without the object 𝒰, gives (1)+(3) as INIT and (2) as DESC.

## 2. Literature (standard 2)

- **Descent and local classes.** Rezk, *Toposes and homotopy toposes* (v0.15): descent (§6.5, properties P1, P2), with the equivalent forms "homotopy colimits commute with homotopy pullback", "coproducts are disjoint" and "groupoid objects are effective" (§6.8) **[paper, partly via summary]**. The equivalence with local classes and object classifiers is in the later sections, not re-read here **[recall]**. Lurie HTT 6.1.3.9 is the ∞-topos version **[recall]**.
- **van Kampen colimits.** A colimit is van Kampen when the self-indexing `x ↦ 𝒞/x` sends it to a limit (Sobociński–Heindel; nLab *van Kampen colimit*) **[paper]**. Lextensive = coproducts van Kampen; no statement there about which colimits in Cat are van Kampen.
- **Cat is not a topos.** Ayala–Francis, *Fibrations of ∞-categories* (Higher Structures 4, 2020), Example 1.6: `{0<2} → [2]` is not exponentiable, because base change along it does not preserve the pushout `{0<1} ⊔_{1} {1<2} ≃ [2]` **[paper]**. Their Main Theorem 1 (Thm 0.8): the flagged ∞-category Corr classifies exponentiable fibrations; Thm 0.9: the classifying map factors through Cat exactly for locally cocartesian fibrations **[paper]**. Exponentiable = base change is a left adjoint (Def. 0.7), the homotopy-invariant form of Conduché's condition (Lemma 1.11) **[paper, via summary for Lemma 1.11]**.
- **Cocartesian fibrations descend.** Sattler–Wärn, *A synthetic construction of universal cocartesian fibrations* (arXiv:2603.28688): "cocartesian fibrations descend along cocommas, sequential colimits, and localisations" (Thm 5.7, Lemmas 6.1–6.2); from this they construct directed univalent cocartesian fibrations (Thm 7.7) and *derive* straightening (Thm 8.1). They assume that left fibrations descend along pushouts (§2.10) **[paper, intro and statements]**. This is the (∞,1) version of exactly the move this note proposes: straightening (our G) as a consequence of descent.
- **Cocartesian = exponentiable + locally cocartesian.** Gepner–Heine, *Colimits in oriented category theory* (arXiv:2608.02592), Thm 1.8.11 (= 4.7.18), for (∞,∞)-categories **[paper]**. So base change along a cocartesian fibration has a right adjoint and preserves colimits. Loubaton (arXiv:2406.05425): Thm 2.2.2.10 (base change along a discrete Conduché functor preserves colimits), Thm 3.2.3.15 (the same for classified left cartesian fibrations), Thm 4.1.4.2 (lax Grothendieck construction) **[paper]**.
- **Impredicative encodings need naturality.** Awodey–Frey–Speight (arXiv:1802.02820): System-F encodings lack η; restricting to the subtype cut out by a naturality condition gives the correct universal property for ℕ, sums, ‖A‖₀, W-types, and (§5) 1-types such as S¹; no univalence is used, one impredicative universe is **[paper; §5–6 via summary]**. Paré–Román, *Dinatural numbers* (JPAA 128, 1998): ordinary dinaturals `X^X → X^X` on Set are **not** the numerals (Johnstone's and Freyd's examples; a proper class), but Barr (strong) dinaturals are exactly the iterates `f ↦ fⁿ`, Prop. 1 **[paper]**. Piróg–Sieczkowski (arXiv:2510.06777): ends of strong dinaturals give generalised codensity monads, which exist only under conditions (Def. 3.1, Thm 4.2, Thm 6.4) **[paper]**.
- **Elementary higher toposes.** Rasekh (arXiv:1805.03805) takes universes as axioms; elementary toposes satisfy weak descent without universes (Prop. 1.49, citing Mac Lane–Moerdijk) **[paper]**. Nothing there constructs truncations or ℕ without universes.
- **Yoneda from limits.** Riehl–Verity, *Fibrations and Yoneda's lemma in an ∞-cosmos* (arXiv:1506.05500), Thm 6.0.1, proved in an arbitrary ∞-cosmos, whose axioms have no colimits; the intro says most results hold in an abstract homotopy 2-category with weak comma objects **[paper for the statement and axioms; the proof in §6.2 was not read]**.

## 3. Candidate A: descent (DESC)

### 3.1 The schema [proposal]

All pieces are finite syntax over objects, maps, pullbacks and cocartesian-fibration structure (`ordinary-math.md` §4.6: a left adjoint ℓ to `[⟦D¹⟧, E] → p ↓ B` with invertible unit). Let `D` be a finite diagram (a substitution into a finite context, as for every colimit rule) with colimit `X = colim D` and legs `ι_k : D_k → X`.

```
(DESC-pb)   p : E → X cocartesian,  g : Y → X   ⟹   g*p : Y ×_X E → Y cocartesian
(DESC-univ) p : E → X cocartesian   ⟹   the comparison  colim_k (D_k ×_X E) → E  is invertible
(DESC-glue) cocartesian p_k : E_k → D_k for each k, and for each arrow u : k → k' of the
            diagram an invertible cell  E_k ≃ D_k ×_{D_k'} E_k'  over D_k (with the
            higher compatibility cells the diagram's shape requires)
            ⟹  p : E → X cocartesian  with invertible  ι_k*p ≃ p_k  compatible with the data
(DESC-uni)  two such gluings are connected by an invertible cell, at every level (uni-style)
(DESC-grpd) D indexed by a groupoid (in particular a coproduct, D indexed by a finite set):
            the same four clauses with "cocartesian" deleted, for every map p
(DESC-or)   S the partially lax (oriented) colimit of D, lax along the unmarked arrows of
            the shape.  Lax gluing data: cocartesian p_k : E_k → D_k, and for each arrow
            u : k → k' a map  E_k → D_k ×_{D_k'} E_k'  over D_k (any map, not required
            cocartesian-preserving; invertible when u is marked), with the higher cells
            the shape requires
            ⟹  p : E → S cocartesian with  ι_k*p ≃ p_k  and the gluing maps recovered as
            transport;  plus uniqueness at every level.  No universality clause.
```

DESC-pb is a theorem of the definition (Riehl–Verity prove pullback stability of cartesian fibrations in any ∞-cosmos, FYL §5.2) **[paper, statement]**, listed for completeness. DESC-univ is "base change along a cocartesian fibration preserves colimits". DESC-glue and DESC-uni are effectiveness. DESC-grpd is extensivity generalised from coproducts to groupoid-indexed colimits. DESC-or is the directed form (standard 4): gluing maps need not be invertible, at the price of using the oriented colimit and dropping universality, which fails for oriented colimits (§3.2).

Because the localisation `E[W⁻¹]` (§6.1 of `ordinary-math.md`) and the sequential colimit (§6.2) are finite pushouts, DESC for finite diagrams already covers object-indexed colimits.

### 3.2 Where descent holds in Cat_ω, and where it fails

- **Fails for arbitrary maps and pushouts.** Pull the pushout `[2] = {a0<a1} ⊔_{a1} {a1<a2}` back along `f : [1] → [2]`, `b0 ↦ a0, b1 ↦ a2`. The pieces pull back to `{b0}`, `{b1}` and `∅`, whose pushout is the discrete `{b0, b1}`; the pullback of `[2]` is `[1]`. **[agda]** `CaTTExp.Descent.PushoutNotUniversal`: the pullbacks of the pieces, that the two-point coproduct has no order isomorphism to `[1]` (so the comparison is not invertible), and that f fails Conduché factorisation lifting (`a0 ≤ a1 ≤ a2` has no lift). The fact that `[2]` is that pushout in Cat is cited (Ayala–Francis Ex. 1.6), not encoded. So "all colimits are van Kampen" is false in Cat_ω, and DESC must be restricted.
- **Holds for cocartesian fibrations, along every small colimit.** `Cocart(X)` is the underlying category of `[X, ω]` (natural straightening, Loubaton Thm 4.1.4.2, Cor. 4.1.4.3), and `[–, ω]` sends colimits to limits, so DESC-glue and DESC-uni hold, and maps over the base between glued fibrations (not only cocartesian-preserving ones) are glued from their restrictions. DESC-univ: base change along a classified fibration preserves all slice colimits (Loubaton Thm 3.2.3.15, Cor. 3.2.3.16) **[researcher, verified 2026-11-21]**. Sattler–Wärn prove the (∞,1) case along cocommas, sequential colimits and localisations **[paper]**.
- **Not for exponentiable maps in general.** A family of exponentiable maps over `* ← {0<2} → [3] ← {1<3} → *` (colimit `*`) need not descend: fibre the walking retraction's full subcategory on x at even vertices and the walking retraction at odd ones. Cores preserving the colimit is sufficient (Ayala–Francis), not necessary **[researcher, verified]**. So DESC is stated for cocartesian fibrations, not exponentiable maps.
- **Holds for all maps along groupoid-indexed colimits.** For a groupoid K, `colim_K F` is the Grothendieck construction `∫F` itself (cocartesian lifts of invertible arrows are invertible, so nothing is localised), and every map into an object over a groupoid is a cocartesian fibration up to equivalence. So `Cat/(colim_K F) ≃ lim_k Cat/F(k)` **[proved here, sketch; confirmed by the researcher, verified 2026-11-21: space-indexed colimits are "special" (computed in presheaves, Loubaton Prop. 2.2.1.27) and presheaf descent transfers]**. DESC-tr holds with the shift by one, which is optimal (`* → K(ℤ/2, n+1)`) **[researcher, verified]**. Coproducts are the case K discrete: this is the extensivity `ordinary-math.md` §2.3 already uses for T-coprod.
- **Lax gluing: only along oriented colimits (DESC-or).** At an ordinary colimit lax gluing is false: the constant point over the walking arrow has colimit a point, and the lax datum `∅ → *` cannot descend. At the *oriented* colimit (the walking arrow itself) the Gray mapping object into the classifier is the oriented limit of the pieces' mapping objects; this is the definition of oriented colimit (Gepner–Heine, arXiv:2608.02592, Def. 3.3.6) evaluated at the classifier, combined with oriented straightening (Thm 1.8.1–1.8.2, Cor. 1.8.4: lax transformations correspond to arbitrary maps over the base). That is DESC-or. Universality fails for oriented colimits: for `E = [1] → *`, pulling back the oriented colimit of the point over [1] gives `E × [1]`, while the oriented colimit in the slice gives the Gray `E ⊠ [1]` **[researcher, verified 2026-11-21]**. So DESC-or has gluing and uniqueness only.

### 3.3 What DESC derives

**T-coprod's disjointness [proved here].** The summand inclusion `i_A : A → A ⊔ B` is a cocartesian fibration (no arrow leaves A). DESC-grpd for the coproduct, applied to the family that is `id_A` over A and `∅ → B` over B, gives a map `E → A ⊔ B` with `E ×_{A⊔B} A ≃ A` and `E ×_{A⊔B} B ≃ ∅`; by DESC-grpd's universality `E ≃ A ⊔ ∅ ≃ A` and the map is `i_A`. So `A ×_{A⊔B} B ≃ ∅`. ∎

**T-coprod itself [proved here, from DESC-grpd].** `ordinary-math.md` §2.3 proves `A ⊔ B trunc n` from "coproducts are universal and disjoint". DESC-grpd's universality applied to any `c → A ⊔ B` gives `c ≃ c_A ⊔ c_B`, and disjointness is above. So T-coprod is a theorem of DESC-grpd. ∎

**EX, effectiveness of quotients [proved here, sketch, relative to DESC and the truncation clause below].** Let X be a set, `R ↪ X × X` an equivalence relation, `C := coeq(r₀, r₁)` (pseudo) and `Q := ‖C‖₀`, `q : X → Q`. The HoTT proof (encode–decode against the family `x ↦ R(x, –)`) uses univalence in exactly one place: to define a family over Q. DESC replaces it.
1. *A family over X × C.* `X × –` preserves colimits (exponential law (E), which `ordinary-math.md` assumes), so `X × C = coeq(1 × r₀, 1 × r₁)`. Give the family `R → X × X` over the vertex `X × X` and, over `X × R`, the descent cell `R(x, r₀ρ) ≃ R(x, r₁ρ)`, composition with ρ and with its symmetric (maps between propositions in both directions, so invertible by T-fill). The bases are sets, so DESC-grpd applies with no cocartesian condition. It glues to `P → X × C` with fibre `R(x, y)` over `(x, c y)`.
2. *Down to X × Q.* The fibres of P are propositions. **Clause (DESC-tr):** for a groupoid Y, families over Y with n-truncated fibres descend uniquely along `Y → ‖Y‖_{n+1}`. In ∞-topos theory this is the statement that n-types are classified by an (n+1)-type **[recall]**; semantically in Cat_ω it is the same statement in spaces, since C is a groupoid (colimits of groupoids are computed in spaces, the inclusion having a right adjoint, the core). It is a descent statement, so it belongs in DESC. It gives `P' → X × Q`.
3. *Encode–decode.* `refl` gives a point of `P'` over `(x, q x)`. For `(x, y)` with `q x ≅ q y` (a point of `X ×_Q X`), transport (P' is a family over a set, so transport along the cell `q x ≅ q y` is part of the descended family) gives a point of `R(x, y)`. This is a map `X ×_Q X → R` over `X × X`; both are subobjects of `X × X` (Q is a set, R is a mono), so it is inverse to the canonical `R → X ×_Q X`. ∎

Step 3, from the output of steps 1–2 taken as a hypothesis (propositional descent along q), is **[agda, 1-truncated]** `CaTTExp.Descent.EncodeDecode` (`ex`). That hypothesis is equivalent to EX itself, so the check confirms only that step 3 reduces EX to descent. The hypothesis is satisfiable and does not follow from q merely respecting R. Steps 1–2, the coequaliser and ‖–‖₀ are not encoded. So EX moves from "a genuine rule" (`ordinary-math.md` §5.3, with a countermodel in posets) to an instance of DESC. The poset countermodel fails DESC; that is consistent, since it was a countermodel to EX from limits, colimits, R-A and Trunc, without descent.

**G, the Grothendieck principle [proved here, sketch, following Sattler–Wärn].** For a diagram γ over a graph shape ⟦Γ⟧, `⟦Γ⟧` is the pushout of its vertices and edges. Over the edge `⟦D¹⟧`, the collage `cyl(γ_f) := (γ_x ⊗ ⟦D¹⟧) ⊔_{γ_x ⊗ {1}} γ_y → ⟦D¹⟧` is a cocartesian fibration with fibres γ_x, γ_y and transport γ_f (**base clause G₁**). DESC-glue glues the collages to `π : E → ⟦Γ⟧`; DESC-univ (along π) shows `E` is the colimit of the collages, which is the lax colimit of γ: G-fib and G-fibre. G-transport is G₁'s transport, and G-nat is DESC-uni applied to the glued map. So G reduces to its **edge case G₁**, plus DESC. **G₁ from DESC-or [proved here, sketch].** `⟦D¹⟧` is the lax colimit of the point over `D¹` (`conjectures/lax-colimit-of-point.md`, semantically Loubaton Ex. 4.2.3.12). Lax gluing data over it are two objects and a map `f : A → B`, so DESC-or gives a cocartesian fibration over `⟦D¹⟧` with fibres A, B and transport f, unique at every level: G₁'s existence, transport and uniqueness. DESC-or alone does not give the *formula* for its total space, that it is the collage `cyl(f) = (A ⊗ ⟦D¹⟧) ⊔_{A ⊗ {1}} B` (G-fibre in the one-edge case). Call that step **G₁-tot**.

**G₁-tot from DESC-or and DJ [proved here, sketch; following Sattler–Wärn §2.16 and Lemma 6.1].** It suffices to show that `cyl(f) → ⟦D¹⟧` is cocartesian, i.e. to compute the homs of the collage out of `k(a)`, where `k : A → cyl(f)` and `l : B → cyl(f)` are the inclusions.
1. *Left fibrations over the collage.* `cyl(f)` is the cocomma of `A ←id– A –f→ B`, a partially lax colimit (the first leg strict, the second lax). By DESC-or for left fibrations (cocartesian with groupoid fibres), a left fibration over `cyl(f)` is the same as a triple `(L_A, L_B, φ : L_A → f*L_B)` of left fibrations over A and B and a map over A, and maps of left fibrations are the same as maps of triples.
2. *The triple for a point a of A.* Take `(a ↓ A, fa ↓ B, φ_a)`, where `φ_a` sends `u : a → x` to `fu : fa → fx`. A map of triples from it into `(M_A, M_B, ψ)` is a pair of maps `a ↓ A → M_A` and `fa ↓ B → M_B` with a compatibility square. By DJ, the first is a point `m ∈ M_A(a)` and the second a point `n ∈ M_B(fa)`, and the square forces `n ≅ ψ(m)` (DJ-uni). So these maps are exactly the points of `M_A(a) = (k*M)(a)`: the triple corepresents evaluation at `k(a)`.
3. *Comparing with DJ in the collage.* By DJ in `cyl(f)`, `k(a) ↓ cyl(f)` also corepresents evaluation at `k(a)`. So under step 1 it is the triple of step 2. Its fibres are therefore `Hom_cyl(k a, k x) ≃ Hom_A(a, x)` and `Hom_cyl(k a, l b) ≃ Hom_B(fa, b)`, the second by composing with the arrow `k a → l(fa)` of the collage.
4. *Cocartesian lifts.* The arrow `k a → l(fa)` over `0 → 1` is cocartesian by step 3. Over identities the lifts are identities, and the full inclusions are fully faithful by step 3 and its analogue for `l(b)` (the triple `(∅, b ↓ B, !)`). So `cyl(f) → ⟦D¹⟧` is a cocartesian fibration with fibres A, B and transport f, and by DESC-or's uniqueness it is the fibration of the previous paragraph. ∎

Its semantic content (the collage with these homs is a cocartesian fibration with fibres A, B and transport f) is **[agda, 1-truncated]** `CaTTExp.Descent.Collage`; steps 1–3 themselves are not encoded. So **G₁ is a theorem of DESC-or and DJ**, and G reduces to DESC (with DESC-or) and DJ. Sattler–Wärn take straightening for left fibrations as an axiom (their §2.10) and prove the analogue of step 1 from it; here DESC-or plays that role. **Relative G₁ [proved here, sketch], no slices needed.** Over a base `B_e`, the Gray cylinder `B_e ⊗ ⟦D¹⟧` is itself the cocomma of `B_e ←id– B_e –id→ B_e`. Absolute DESC-or along that cocomma says that cocartesian fibrations over `B_e ⊗ ⟦D¹⟧` are triples `(E_0 → B_e, E_1 → B_e, F : E_0 → E_1 over B_e)`, with F any map over `B_e`. The candidate total space is the cocomma W of `E_0 ←id– E_0 –F→ E_1`. Steps 2–3 above, run for this cocomma (the first leg is again an identity), give `Hom_W(k x, l y) ≃ Hom_{E_1}(F x, y)`. With the cocartesian lifts in E_1 this makes `W → B_e ⊗ ⟦D¹⟧` cocartesian, as in Sattler–Wärn Lemma 6.1. So relative G₁ follows from absolute DESC-or and DJ. An earlier draft asked for DESC-or in slices; that is not needed. Its strict form is **[agda, 1-truncated]** `CaTTExp.Descent.RelCollage`: over the strict cylinder `B_e × I`, the collage of F (over `B_e` up to a natural iso) is an opfibration exactly when (given p0, p1 opfibrations) F sends p0's chosen lifts to p1-cocartesian arrows (`opfibQ`, `opfibQ⁻¹`). The lax form, any F, is a cocartesian fibration over the Gray cylinder (a 2-category); its 1-categorical shadow (W → `B_e × I` locally cocartesian) is expressible but not yet encoded, so the lax form stays [sketch]. **This matters for G⁺:** edges whose map `B_e × C_v → C_w` is constant in `B_e` (`B_e × g`) preserve cocartesian arrows, and that case is checked (`gplus-opfib`). More generally the condition is that γ_e(β, id) is invertible for every β. A general edge map γ_e that varies with b need not preserve them when `B_e` is directed (`B_e = [1]`, `C_v = ⊤`, `γ_e(b, ∗) = b`; argued, not machine-checked), so general G⁺ rests on the lax form of relative G₁ and stays [sketch].

**G⁺ [proposal; proved here, sketch].** Object-labelled edges are suspensions `ΣB_e = ⊤ ⊔ ⊤ ⊔_{B_e ⊗ ∂⟦D¹⟧} B_e ⊗ ⟦D¹⟧`. DESC-glue over that pushout reduces G⁺ to the collage over `B_e ⊗ ⟦D¹⟧` of the map `B_e × C_v → B_e × C_w` over `B_e`. That is relative G₁ for `E_0 = B_e × C_v`, `E_1 = B_e × C_w`, which is a theorem (above). So G⁺ reduces to DESC and DJ **[proved here, sketch]**.

**R-U's fibre clauses [proved here, relative to INIT].** If U is built by INIT (§4.3), its fibre over `node(ts)` is computed by Lambek's lemma plus DESC-grpd over the set Θ₀ (pullback along a point of a set preserves the colimits in Φ). No separate fibre clause is needed.

## 4. Candidate C: initial models of positive contexts (INIT), the impredicative quantifier

### 4.1 Why candidate (b) cannot come from colimits alone

The ledger's candidate (b) is "initial algebras of polynomial functors on fibrations (W-types over a base)". The obvious derivation is Adámek's: `μΦ = colim_k Φᵏ(∅)`. But the chain `k ↦ Φᵏ(∅)` is an ℕ-indexed family of objects with `E_{k+1} = Φ(E_k)`. Building it is object-valued recursion over ℕ, the very thing (b) is meant to provide. A sequential colimit needs its family as input (`ordinary-math.md` §6.2: a total object `X → N` with `t : X → X`), and for `Φᵏ(∅)` the total object is `∐_k Φᵏ(∅)`, which is again `μ` of a related functor. So (b) is a construction principle and has to be a rule. The question is only which rule is most general. **[proved here, as an observation about the construction]** It is also a **non-derivability theorem** for the descent fragment (researcher, verified 2026-11-22, `conjectures/answered/universe-free-initial-models-r2-2026-11-22.md`). Take sequences of arrows of spaces that are eventually k-connected for every k. This model has finite limits, finite colimits, full finite descent, descent for cocartesian fibrations and groupoid-indexed colimits, and DESC-tr. In it the positive context `(X, a : A → X, l : X ×_{X×X} X → X)` (target X, so inside the corrected class of §4.2) has no initial model, because the pointwise carrier `∐_r A^{(S¹)^r}` falls outside the model. So INIT is not a consequence of the limit, colimit and descent rules. Conversely, **ℕ is**: finite descent alone gives a natural number object, via `ΩS¹` and Sattler–Wärn's sign construction (arXiv:2405.18388), and the project's DESC is strong enough (every map into the groupoid `S¹` is cocartesian). The boundary is undecidable: `(X, A → X, X^K → X)` for a connected finite K follows from descent iff K is contractible. So the derivable part cannot be cut out by a syntactic side condition, and INIT stays one rule.

### 4.2 The schema [proposal; sound in Cat_ω, researcher, verified 2026-11-21]

A **positive context** is a finite context Δ (the usual context grammar of chapters 1–9: object variables, map and cell variables, marks such as `⋆^{≤n}` and `inv`) together with a distinguished object variable X, such that:
- in the **target** of every operation, X appears only under **limit-preserving** operations: X itself, ⊤, `Hom_X(x, y)` for points x, y given by earlier variables, cotensors `[A, X]` and lax cotensors by objects A not mentioning X, and finite limits of these (pullbacks over fixed objects or over earlier data). **Not** coproducts, pushouts, ⊗, Σ, or a constant other than ⊤;
- in the **source** of every operation, X appears under any accessible covariant operation (finite limits including pullbacks over fixed objects, colimits, ⊗ and × with fixed objects, cotensors `[A, X]` by fixed A);
- a **cell** variable is a cell (of any dimension, invertible or not) between composites of earlier operations; its filler is an operation out of the source tensored with a globe, restricted on the boundary, and its target obeys the target condition;
- **marks** restrict X to a reflective class closed under limits and filtered colimits (`⋆^{≤n}`), or mark an operation invertible (`inv`, presentable by bi-invertibility data);
- X does not appear contravariantly anywhere (no `[X, –]`, no `Hom` with X in the first argument).

The check is syntax-directed and decidable, like strict positivity for inductive types. **Why targets must preserve limits** (researcher, verified): with one operation `⊤ → 2` (2 = ⊤ ⊔ ⊤ fixed) the models are `Cat_ω ⊔ Cat_ω` and there is no initial model; likewise `⊤ → X ⊔ X`. The earlier draft of this grammar allowed colimit-preserving targets and was wrong. For a constant source A, a model is an object of `(A ↓ T)`, so initial models for every A is exactly a left adjoint to T: **limit-preserving accessible targets are the largest class that can be paired with every source**. Sufficiency is the presentability argument (accessible sources, limit-creating forgetful functors, cells as restricted operations). For single signatures outside the uniform class no decidable syntax can be exact (with fixed `BG` for a finitely presented G, `⊤ → BG` has an initial model iff G is trivial, Adian–Rabin).

```
(INIT-form)  Δ positive in X   ⟹   μΔ : ⋆   and a model  η : Δ[μΔ/X]          (the generic model)
(INIT-J1)    Γ ⊢ σ : Δ[A/X]   (a model with carrier A)
             ⟹  rec_σ : μΔ → A  and an invertible cell  rec_σ ∘ η ≅ σ          (a model morphism)
(INIT-J2)    two (strong) model morphisms h, h' : μΔ → A from η to σ
             ⟹  an invertible cell  h ≅ h'  of model morphisms
(higher)     derived, as for Trunc (`ordinary-math.md` Prop. 5.3(2)) and the recursor
             (Prop. 4.2): a level-k cell between model morphisms is a model morphism into
             the cotensor [⟦Dᵏ⟧, A] with the pointwise model.
```

"Model morphism" is itself a finite context (the arrow context `Δ^{→}`: two copies of Δ, a map between the carriers, and an **invertible** cell per variable of Δ: model morphisms are strong). So the whole schema is a substitution-stable rule of the shape of R-A's `uni`, quantified over contexts, as R-A is quantified over pasting contexts.

**The impredicative reading (Anthony's System-F suggestion).** Write `∀Δ. X` for μΔ. Given INIT, a map `W → ∀Δ. X` is the same as a family of maps `W → A`, one for each model `(A, σ)`, natural along model morphisms: the initial model is coinitial, so the limit of the forgetful functor from models to the ambient, i.e. the end of the codensity form `∫_{(A,σ)} A`, is its carrier. **The converse fails**, so the rule is stated as initiality and the end is a consequence: for `⊤ → 2` the limit of the forgetful functor exists (it is ∅) but there is no initial model (researcher, verified). The rest of this reading: and System F's `∀X. (ΦX → X) → X` is the case `Δ = (X : ⋆, a : ΦX → X)`. Two points from the literature fix how it must be stated:
- **Naturality is essential.** The plain System-F encoding, i.e. a product over all A, lacks η (AFS). The plain end over *dinatural* families is wrong already for ℕ in Set: the dinaturals `X^X → X^X` form a proper class (Paré–Román §2.2). The *strong* (Barr) dinaturals are exactly the numerals (Paré–Román Prop. 1). The model-morphism naturality in INIT-J2 is the strong-dinaturality condition; it is built into the rule, not left to a meta-level parametricity theorem.
- **No universe is introduced.** INIT binds an object variable inside the *rule* (as `coh` binds a pasting context), not in a term or type: no term has type ⋆, nothing ranges over "all objects" as an object, and `∀Δ. X` is an ordinary object. Spec §0.2, as reworded under S15 (PR #397), allows exactly this: a binding former in Lawvere's style that binds an object variable in a premise, states its universal property by cells, and makes no judgment a term. INIT fits it as written.
- **Strong model morphisms; lax cells inside models.** Standard 4 asked for lax model morphisms first. That version is **false**: for pointed objects with a non-invertible comparison `f(i) → y`, every candidate initial object has two inequivalent maps to `([1], 1)`, the constants at 0 and at 1 (researcher, verified). So model morphisms preserve every operation and cell up to invertible cells. The cells *declared in* Δ may still be non-invertible, so directed structure is available inside models.

**Why "initial model" and not a general end.** Ends `∫_A G(A, A)` for arbitrary mixed-variance G are large limits that need not exist (Piróg–Sieczkowski Def. 3.1: "may or may not exist"). The codensity form over models of a positive context is the case where the end is the carrier of an initial object, and there existence is a theorem about accessible functors on a presentable category (Adámek–Rosický; Kerodon 9.4.9, 9.5.1–9.5.2), extended to finite contexts with cells by the researcher (verified 2026-11-21). The dual, **final models** of copositive contexts (coends, `∃Δ. X`), would give final coalgebras and right adjoints such as R-cot⁺ and the cartesian `[A, B]`. It is **unsound in general**: the right adjoint to base change along the non-exponentiable `{0<2} → [2]` (§3.2) does not exist, so the final model of `(W, p : W → [2], e : W ×_{[2]} [1] → B)` does not exist. A final-model schema would need an exponentiability side condition. Recorded, not proposed.

## 5. DJ is a theorem of the limit rules (F10b) [proved here, sketch, relative to Riehl–Verity's abstract results]

*Source, read this session from the arXiv PDF:* Riehl–Verity, *Fibrations and Yoneda's lemma in an ∞-cosmos* (arXiv:1506.05500), §4–§6. §4 opens: "For this section we shall assume that we are working in an abstract homotopy 2-category C ... nothing we say here will depend on that being the case [that C is the homotopy 2-category of an ∞-cosmos]". An abstract homotopy 2-category is a strict 2-category with comma and iso-comma objects whose comparison functors are smothering (surjective on objects, full, conservative), Def. 3.2.5 **[paper]**. §6.1 is also stated for an abstract homotopy 2-category. Only §6.2 uses the ∞-cosmos, and only to assemble the 1- and 2-cell statements into an equivalence of hom quasi-categories (Observation 6.2.5 and "pointwise isomorphisms in qCat₂ are isomorphisms", Obs. I.3.2.3) **[paper]**.

**What the project's rules provide.** Under P, the homotopy 2-category of the ambient (objects, maps, cells up to invertible 3-cells) has comma and iso-comma objects with a universal property at every level, which is stronger than smothering. A biterminal object is ⊤. The project's definition of a cocartesian fibration (an adjunction `ℓ ⊣ r` with invertible unit, `ordinary-math.md` §4.6) is RV's Thm 4.1.10(ii), dualised, which RV prove equivalent to cocartesian 2-cells with induction and conservativity (Def. 4.1.1) in any abstract homotopy 2-category (Appendix §7) **[paper, statement and the setting of §7]**.

**The proof, dualised to cocartesian fibrations** (RV §6.2, with the hom-quasi-category step replaced by generalised elements):
1. *DJ-cocart* **[agda, 1-truncated]** (`CaTTExp.Descent.CocartLemmas`, which also checks the lemmas on cocartesian arrows used in steps 2–3). `π_a : a ↓ C → C` is a groupoidal cocartesian fibration: RV Ex. 4.1.16/4.1.18 and 4.2.11 (domain projection of a comma), using Prop. 5.2.1 (pullback stability), all in an abstract homotopy 2-category. By Prop. 4.2.5, every 2-cell with codomain `a ↓ C` is π_a-cocartesian **[paper]**.
2. *DJ-sec* **[agda, 1-truncated]** (`CaTTExp.Descent.DJOneTruncated`: `dj-sec`, for e lying over a on the nose). Let `φ : a ∘ ! ⇒ π_a` be the universal comma cell and `e : ⊤ → E` with `q e ≅ a`. Take the q-cocartesian lift `χ_φ : e ∘ ! ⇒ h` of φ (apply ℓ to the map `(e∘!, φ) : a ↓ C → q ↓ C`), so `q h = π_a`. Then h is a CF-morphism: for any cell τ into `a ↓ C`, τ is π_a-cocartesian (step 1), and middle-four interchange gives `hτ · χ_φ = χ_φ · e!τ`; the right side is cocartesian (χ_φ composed with an invertible cell), so `hτ` is cocartesian by left cancellation (RV Lemma 5.1.9, dualised). This is RV Lemma 6.2.9. Restricting along `ι = (a, 1_a)`, φι is an identity, so `χ_φ ι` is a cocartesian lift of an identity, hence invertible (RV Obs. 4.1.3): `h ∘ ι ≅ e`.
3. *DJ-uni* **[agda, 1-truncated]** (`CaTTExp.Descent.DJOneTruncated`: `dj-ext` gives a natural transformation over the base from any map ρ : h(ι) → h'(ι) over a, invertible or not, `dj-uni` its invertibility for invertible ρ, `dj-ext-uni` its uniqueness). Let `h : a ↓ C → E` be any CF-morphism. The comma's 2-cell induction gives `η : ι ∘ ! ⇒ id` with `π_a η = φ` (RV Obs. 6.2.3). By step 1 η is cocartesian, so `hη : h ι ! ⇒ h` is a q-cocartesian lift of φ from `hι!`. Two cocartesian lifts of the same cell with the same source are connected by an invertible cell over the identity (RV Obs. 4.1.3). So h is isomorphic to the map built in step 2 from `e := hι`. Given two CF-morphisms h, h' and `θ : hι ≅ h'ι`, both are isomorphic to the construction of step 2 applied to `hι ≅ h'ι`, which is functorial in invertible cells (the lift of φ from an isomorphic source is the isomorphic lift, RV Obs. 4.1.2). So `h ≅ h'`, restricting to θ. This is RV Lemma 6.2.12.
4. *Higher levels.* As in `ordinary-math.md` §4.6, a cell `h ⇒ h'` over C is a map into the fibrewise cotensor `E^{D¹}_C`. That is a cocartesian fibration: cotensoring by ⟦D¹⟧ is a 2-functor, so it sends the adjunction `ℓ ⊣ r` with invertible unit to one, and pulling back along the diagonal preserves cocartesian fibrations (RV Prop. 5.2.1). Steps 2–3 applied to `E^{D¹}_C` give level 2, and so on, coinductively. **[proved here, sketch]**

**Generalised elements replace the hom quasi-categories.** RV need §6.2's quasi-categorical step because they state an equivalence of mapping quasi-categories. DJ is stated as existence and uniqueness of maps and cells, at every level, which steps 2–4 give directly, so the ∞-cosmos-specific facts are not needed. Steps 2–3 also work with e a generalised element `W → E` over `a ∘ !`, by the same argument in the slice over W.

**Conclusion.** DJ adds nothing beyond the limit rules (commas, cotensors by ⟦D¹⟧, pullbacks, ⊤) and the definition of cocartesian fibration. It moves from "rule" to "theorem", relative to RV §4–§5 (abstract homotopy 2-category results, cited, not re-proved here). *Consequence for `ordinary-math.md` §4.5's countermodel* (all limits and R-A, route III fails): DJ holds there, so G fails there, and therefore so does DESC + G₁. **[proved here, relative to the above]** Which of DESC and G₁ fails is not needed and is not checked.

## 6a. T-graph and T-loc by encode–decode (F10d) [proved here, sketch]

HoTT computes π₁(S¹) = ℤ by encode–decode, with univalence defining the covering family. Here DESC defines the family, DJ (now a theorem, §5) gives "encode", and INIT gives "decode" and the uniqueness step.

**Setting.** Γ is a finite graph with vertex set V, ⟦Γ⟧ its free object (an INIT instance, §4.3), and `a : ⊤ → ⟦Γ⟧` a vertex.

1. *Path sets.* Let Π be the initial model of the multi-sorted positive context `(X_v : ⋆ for v ∈ V, nil : ⊤ → X_a, snoc_e : X_v → X_w for e : v → w)`. INIT is stated with one sort, but a finite family of sorts is the one-sort case over the finite set V (`X → V`, fibres X_v; DESC-grpd over the coproduct V). Π_v is "paths from a to v".
2. *The covering family.* DESC-glue over the pushout presentation of ⟦Γ⟧ (vertices and edges), with fibre Π_v over v and, over each edge e, the collage of `snoc_e` (base clause G₁), gives a cocartesian fibration `E → ⟦Γ⟧` with fibres Π_v and transport snoc_e. nil is a point of E over a.
3. *Encode.* DJ-sec gives a CF-morphism `enc : a ↓ ⟦Γ⟧ → E` over ⟦Γ⟧ with `enc ∘ ι ≅ nil`.
4. *Decode.* The family `v ↦ Hom_{⟦Γ⟧}(a, v)` with `1_a` and `– ; e` is a model of the context in step 1, so INIT-J1 gives `dec_v : Π_v → Hom(a, v)`, a model morphism. Its cells `dec ∘ snoc_e ≅ (dec – ) ; e` say that the fibrewise maps commute with transport, so DESC-uni (maps between glued fibrations are glued from compatible fibrewise maps) assembles them into a CF-morphism `dec : E → a ↓ ⟦Γ⟧` over ⟦Γ⟧.
5. *dec ∘ enc ≅ 1.* Both are CF-morphisms `a ↓ ⟦Γ⟧ → a ↓ ⟦Γ⟧` over ⟦Γ⟧, and both send ι to `(a, 1_a)`. DJ-uni.
6. *enc ∘ dec ≅ 1.* Restricted to fibres, both are model endomorphisms of the initial model Π: they preserve nil, and they commute with snoc_e because CF-morphisms commute with transport. INIT-J2.

So `Hom_{⟦Γ⟧}(a, b) ≃ Π_b`. A 1-truncated shadow of steps 3–6 is **[agda]** `CaTTExp.Descent.EncodeDecode` (`hom≅Π`, for any pointwise-initial Π). It takes Hom_{⟦Γ⟧}(a, –) to be the inductive path type, so it amounts to uniqueness of initial models. There, encode is the fold along paths and step 5 is path induction (initiality of the path model, not DJ). No covering family, free category or DESC-uni assembly is encoded there. The substantive 1-truncated version is **[agda]** `CaTTExp.Descent.EncodeDecodeDJ`: FΓ is the path category, the covering family is the category of elements of Π (an opfibration over FΓ), encode is read off the CF-morphism that DJ-sec (`DJOneTruncated.dj-sec`) gives at the point Π.m, decode is initiality assembled into a functor over FΓ (the 1-categorical form of DESC-uni), step 5 is `dj-ext` applied to Dec ∘ k and the identity CF-morphism, and step 6 is uniqueness. Here dj-sec's section is the chosen lift, so encode is definitionally the fold, and step 5 also has a direct proof; the file shows that the DJ route type-checks from k's interface, not that it is needed. It does not glue the covering family from collages by DESC-glue, and does not prove FΓ's universal property (R-A). The same argument with the context `(X, z : ⊤ → X, s : X → X marked inv)` (an initial "object with an automorphism", i.e. ℤ; `inv` is presented by bi-invertibility data `l, r : X → X`, `l ; s ≅ 1`, `s ; r ≅ 1`, whose space of witnesses is a proposition) and the localised loop gives `Hom_{⟦L⟧[s⁻¹]}(x, x) ≃ ℤ_init`. **The presentation matters.** With one inverse `s⁻¹` and the two cells `s ; s⁻¹ ≅ 1`, `s⁻¹ ; s ≅ 1` (quasi-inverse data, not a proposition) the initial model is *not* a set: this is the HIT `Z_h` without its coherence constructor (Basold–Geuvers–van der Weide 2017, cited in Altenkirch–Scoccola, arXiv:2007.00167, §1.2). Step 6 would then fail, since the fibre maps need not preserve the chosen cells. With bi-invertibility data, maps out of the initial model are determined by z and s (Altenkirch–Scoccola Thm 2.4), so step 6 goes through. An earlier draft used the quasi-inverse form. With M edges inverted in a general Γ, the transports of inverted edges are equivalences, as in HoTT, and the fibres are the initial models of the context with inverse data on M.

**What remains to reach T-graph's statement.** Two steps, both general clauses, not graph-specific rules:
- *(T-init) [proposal; semantically sound, sketch].* If every operation of a positive context has target X and source built from coproducts, finite products and parameters that are trunc n (n ≥ 0), and the context has **no cell variables** except `inv` marks (presented by bi-invertibility data), then `μΔ trunc n`. Semantically, the transfinite Adámek tower stays inside n-truncated objects, which are closed under coproducts (n ≥ 0), finite products and filtered colimits in Cat_ω (n-truncation is tested on the compact generators) **[recall]**; an `inv` mark is a reflective condition closed under these. The earlier draft allowed arbitrary invertible cells, which is **false**: the quasi-inverse context above has a non-set initial model. This is a closure clause of the same kind as T-lim and T-coprod. It gives `Π_b trunc 0`, so `Hom_{⟦Γ⟧}(a, b) trunc 0`.
- *From homs to the object.* T-graph says `⟦Γ⟧ trunc 1`, about cells into ⟦Γ⟧ from arbitrary c, while encode–decode gives homs between points. With generalised points `a : W → ⟦Γ⟧` the argument runs in the slice over W (DJ holds in the slice 2-category, RV's §4 being 2-categorical), giving the homs over W. The step "all relative homs are trunc n ⟹ the object is trunc (n+1)" is the converse of T-hom. **Semantically it holds already for homs between points [proved here, sketch]:** n-truncation of X is tested on the globes, `Map(⟦Dᵏ⟧, X) → X^≃ × X^≃` has fibres `Map(⟦D^{k−1}⟧, Hom_X(x, y))`, which are n-truncated, and the loop space of `X^≃` at x is the space of invertible points of `Hom_X(x, x)`, a union of components of an n-truncated space; so `X^≃` and every `Map(⟦Dᵏ⟧, X)` are (n+1)-truncated, and cells above dimension n+1 of X are cells above n of its homs, hence invertible. So the converse of T-hom is a sound closure clause; whether it is *derivable* from the existing rules (rather than added as a clause like T-lim) is the open part, F10g.

So T-graph and T-loc, two rules about specific objects, are replaced by the general closure clause T-init plus the converse of T-hom.

**Status of the two clauses (F10g, settled 2026-11-22).** `C trunc n` is a judgment whose introduction rules are syntax-directed by object former (T-lim for limits, T-coprod for ⊔, T-trunc for ‖–‖_n, T-exp for exponentials, `ordinary-math.md` §2). Neither clause is an instance of that list. INIT introduces a new object former μΔ, which needs its own introduction rule for `trunc`, exactly as ⊔ needed T-coprod: that is T-init. The converse of T-hom is a structural introduction rule, stated with the relative truncation of the boundary map: `(s, t) : [⟦D¹⟧, C] → C × C relatively trunc (n − 1) ⟹ C trunc n`. That premise covers homs over every generalised point, and the semantic argument above (testing on globes) proves the rule sound. So both are **closure clauses of the trunc judgment, sound [sketch]**. For T-init, non-derivability is open (below). *T-hom⁻* (superseded, see the next paragraph): an earlier version argued by inspecting rule conclusions for an unmarked variable. In `CaTTExp.Trunc` marks are inputs and no rule concludes `trunc` at all, so that inspection was vacuous.

**T-hom⁻ is not derivable [proved here, sketch; 1-truncated shadow agda].** *Countermodel:* read `C trunc n` as `trunc′ C n` := "C is an n-truncated ω-groupoid", with declared marks `C : ⋆^{≤n}` and INIT's carrier marks read the same way. Everything else keeps its Cat_ω meaning. Since absolute truncation is relative truncation over ⊤ (`ordinary-math.md` §2.4), the relative judgment is reread too: `p : E → B relatively trunc′ n` := p is relatively n-truncated and conservative (every cell of E whose image in B is invertible is invertible). This is stable under pullback and closed under limits, and over B = ⊤ it is trunc′. The relativised versions of T-graph, T-loc and T-hom⁻ are excluded along with the absolute ones. Every rule other than T-hom⁻ stays sound:
- *Rules that use `trunc` (T-fill, T-inv, T-ctr, DESC-tr):* trunc′ implies trunc, so they stay sound.
- *T-var and T-cum:* immediate.
- *T-unit:* ⊤ and ∅ are groupoids.
- *T-lim:* groupoids are closed under all limits, since the inclusion Gpd ⊂ Cat_ω has a left adjoint. A lax/weighted limit is a limit of cotensors `[W_j, G_j]`, and those are groupoids when G_j is.
- *T-exp:* the same argument as T-lim.
- *T-hom:* homs of a groupoid are groupoids.
- *T-init:* the core `Y ↦ Y^≃` carries every model to a model. The sources are built from ⊤, ⊔, × and groupoid parameters, which commute with the core, and the bi-invertibility cells become invertible. So μΔ is a retract of μΔ^≃ and hence a groupoid (as in step 1 of the T-init attempt below). The truncation level is T-init's ordinary soundness.
- *T-coprod:* coproducts of groupoids are groupoids.
- *T-trunc:* INIT's mark ⋆^{≤n}, read as "n-truncated groupoid", is still a reflective class closed under limits and filtered colimits. So INIT applies, and ‖X‖_n becomes the n-truncation of the groupoid reflection |X|.

T-hom⁻ fails at n = 1. For `[1] = ⟦D¹⟧`, the boundary map `Fun(D¹, [1]) → [1] × [1]` has discrete fibres and is conservative (it is a full, injective-on-objects map of posets), so it is relatively trunc′ 0. But `[1]` is not a groupoid.

So T-hom⁻ is not derivable from the other rules. Neither is T-graph's one-edge instance `⟦D¹⟧ trunc 1`. More sharply, every rule except T-hom⁻ preserves "is a groupoid", so without T-hom⁻ (or T-graph/T-loc as primitives) no directed object is ever `trunc n`. That is why T-hom⁻ is needed.

This is a non-derivability result for T-hom⁻. Non-admissibility would need T-hom⁻'s premise to be derivable, without T-hom⁻, for some non-groupoid C. That is not settled, and it is not claimed. The consequence that matters is not affected: T-graph's instance has no premise, and it is underivable without T-hom⁻.

The 1-truncated shadow is **[agda]** `CaTTExp.Descent.TruncGrpdModel`. It shows trunc′ is closed under T-cum, T-unit, products, comma objects, T-hom and T-coprod, and that `[1]` is not trunc′ ≥ 1 (the relative premise is not encoded). T-init and general weighted limits are argued, not encoded.

**Soundness checks, 1-truncated [agda].** `CaTTExp.Descent.TruncHomInv` covers T-hom⁻ on global points: sound at n = −1, the counterexample at n = 0, sound at n = 0 with the groupoid premise, and vacuous at n ≥ 1. `CaTTExp.Descent.TInit` covers T-init at n = 0 for finitary signatures without cells: any 2-categorically initial algebra with trunc 0 parameters is trunc 0, and the tree category is such an algebra. Inv marks and the content at n ≥ 1 stay argued.

**Status of T-init's derivability [conj].** The syntax-directed rules never conclude `μΔ trunc n` directly. But for some contexts, μΔ may be shown n-truncated by encode–decode, with a code family built by INIT over `μΔ × μΔ` and then T-hom⁻, as HoTT does for W-types. So T-init may be derivable from T-hom⁻ + INIT + DESC; this is open **[conj]**, F10g. Two general clauses replace two rules about specific objects (T-graph, T-loc), and every future INIT instance gets its truncation level from T-init with no new rule.

**Correction: T-hom⁻ is false at n = 0 [proved here].** The walking arrow `[1]` has every hom a proposition (`Hom(0,1) = ⊤`, `Hom(1,0) = ∅`, endo-homs ⊤), so all homs are trunc −1, but `[1]` is not trunc 0, because its arrow is not invertible. The semantic argument above has a gap at this level. A k-cell of C is a (k−1)-cell of a hom, and invertibility of the homs' cells above dimension n − 1 says something only about their cells of dimension ≥ 1. So it makes C's k-cells invertible only for k > n *and* k ≥ 2. For n ≥ 1 that is every k > n. For n = 0 it misses k = 1. For n = −1 the rule is sound: contractible homs everywhere force C ≃ ⊤ or ∅. So T-hom⁻ must be stated for **n = −1 or n ≥ 1**, or at n = 0 with the extra premise that C is a groupoid. Every use in F10 (T-graph, T-loc: n = 1) is at n = 1. Reported to the spec thread, since spec §9 states the rule for n ≥ −1.

**Attempted derivation of T-init from T-hom⁻ (F10g, 2026-11-22) [partial].** This follows the HoTT proof that W-types of sets are sets.
1. *Groupoid parameters first.* Suppose every parameter is a groupoid. Then μΔ is the initial model of Δ with the mark "X is a groupoid", provided the groupoid core `Y ↦ Y^≃` is available. Each model Y gives a groupoid model `Y^≃`: Δ's sources, built from coproducts, finite products and groupoid parameters, commute with the core. Model maps out of a groupoid into Y factor uniquely through `Y^≃`, so the groupoid-marked initial model is initial among all models, and μΔ is a groupoid. **The rules have no core former**, so step 1 is not derivable as they stand. It would need either a core rule or a clause "μΔ is a groupoid when its parameters are" (call it T-init-grpd).
2. *Encode–decode over a groupoid.* If μΔ is a groupoid, every map into `μΔ × μΔ` is a cocartesian fibration (DESC-grpd), so a code family `Code → μΔ × μΔ` can be built by INIT over that base. It is the "same constructor, related arguments" family, using Lambek and disjointness of coproducts (DESC-grpd) to put ∅ on mixed constructors. Encode is DJ (path induction), decode is INIT-J1, and the round trips are INIT-J2 and DJ-uni. So `Hom_{μΔ}(x, y) ≃ Code(x, y)`.
3. *Induction on n.* Code is again an initial model, over a base, of a context whose parameters are homs of the original parameters (trunc n − 1 by T-hom). So "Code trunc n − 1 relatively" is T-init one level down, relative to a base, and T-hom⁻ (n ≥ 1, or n = 0 using step 1's groupoid property) closes the step. The base case n = −2 (contractible parameters) still needs its own argument, which is not yet given.
4. *Non-groupoid parameters (n ≥ 1, e.g. a 1-category parameter A).* μΔ is not a groupoid. The code family would have to be a two-sided directed fibration whose transport is not automatic, so step 2 does not apply.

**Verdict.** T-init is derivable from T-hom⁻ + INIT + DESC + DJ **only** for groupoid parameters, and only given T-init-grpd (or a core rule) and the n = −2 base case. For directed parameters no derivation is known. So T-init stays a clause; at best it could be split into T-init-grpd plus encode–decode. Consequences for the Agda queue (F10f part 6): check soundness of T-init itself, and of T-hom⁻ in its corrected range (n = −1, n ≥ 1), including the `[1]` counterexample at n = 0. A countermodel to the derivability of T-init would have to break T-init-grpd, which stays **[conj]**.

## 6b. R-cot⁺ over a set base from the closed structure and DESC (F10c) [proved here, sketch]

R-cot⁺ (`ordinary-math.md` §6.6(iii), (v)) asks, for a set I and `q : U → I`, for an object `[q, B] → I` such that maps `W → [q, B]` over I are maps `W ×_I U → B`, with the tensor the single-shape cotensor uses. It needs no rule of its own, by a partial-map construction:
1. Let `U\(B ⊔ ⊤)` be the closed-structure hom (chapter 8, adopted), so maps `W → U\(B ⊔ ⊤)` are maps `U ⊗ W → B ⊔ ⊤`.
2. I has decidable equality (it is a set built by INIT, like Θ₀, with equality by recursion into Bool). Let `[q, B] ↪ I × U\(B ⊔ ⊤)` be the equaliser saying that `h` sends `U_i ⊗ –` into the summand B and every `U_j ⊗ –` with j ≠ i into ⊤. This is an equation between two maps into the set-valued `U\Bool` (compose with `B ⊔ ⊤ → ⊤ ⊔ ⊤`), so it is a finite limit.
3. Universal property. Given `W → I`, DESC-grpd splits `W ≃ ∐_i W_i`, and ⊗ preserves coproducts in each variable, so `U ⊗ W ≃ ∐_{i,j} U_j ⊗ W_i`. A map from W into the equaliser over I is then a family of maps `U_i ⊗ W_i → B` (the j ≠ i parts are forced to the unique map to ⊤, and the B-part lands in the summand B by disjointness). That is a map `W ×_I U → B` in R-cot⁺'s sense. Cells at every level are the same argument applied to cotensors.

So R-cot⁺ over a set base is a construction from the closed structure, DESC-grpd and INIT (for decidable equality on I). Over a non-discrete base it would be a right adjoint to base change along q, which exists only for exponentiable q (§4.2, the final-model counterexample), so the general R-cot⁺ stays unproposed, as before.
