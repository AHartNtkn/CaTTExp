# A mark that recovers two-tier L from uniform L

*2026-11-21, thread MKL. Ledger S16(b). Builds on `uniform-marks.md` §8 (the E13 answer), spec §2.2–2.3 (uniform and two-tier side conditions, the projection π), and spec §3.6 (marks over V + S). Agda: `src/CaTTExp/Uniform/OrdCoh.agda`.*

*Markers: **[proved]** proved here; **[Agda]** machine-checked; **[cited]** a verified answer or a requirement of the admissible theories, not re-proved; **[open]**.*

## 0. Summary

- **The problem.** The E13 answer showed that an `ord` mark meaning only "every inner letter into C is invertible" does not recover two-tier L's coherences, in any setting. Its witnesses are the loop H against 1_w, and the outer W-strip. So spec §0.4's "each level is the previous one plus marks" was false for the step from uniform L to two-tier L.
- **The fix.** The mark must change the side condition, not only invert letters. Read `C ord` as "the inner letters into C are **trivial**" (identities in strict models) rather than merely invertible. Add one coherence rule:

  > **(COH-ord)** If every object variable of the pasting context Γ is sent by γ to an object marked `ord`, then coh_{Γ, t → u}[γ] is accepted when the **two-tier** side condition SC_𝔉(Γ, t → u) holds.

- **What this gives.**
  - With every object marked `ord`, uniform L + (COH-ord) accepts exactly the two-tier heads, and with no `ord` mark exactly the uniform ones **[proved]**. Only the Boolean rule form is **[Agda]** (`recovers`, `conservative`), on abstract side conditions and assuming S2's inclusion, which holds under the S1 reading. Corollary 3.2 is on paper.
  - The rule is sound in every strict model in which the `ord` objects have trivial inner letters **[proved]**.
  - The two-tier mark rules apply verbatim to the heads it accepts **[proved]**.
  - It is decidable, since its side condition is two-tier's.
- **The rule class.** It is a `coh` rule over an annotated pasting context, the "every object `ord`" variant of `⊢ps`. Its side condition is computed on that context alone. This is within spec §0.3's classes.
- So §0.4 stays true with a precise meaning: **two-tier L = uniform L + `ord` on every object**, where `ord` carries (COH-ord). The invertibility-only reading of `ord` is a strictly weaker mark that recovers only the invertibility judgments.

## 1. The rule

**Annotated pasting contexts.** Γ^ord is a pasting context Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ with every object variable a₀, …, aₙ marked `ord`. Γ^ord ⊢ps holds iff Γ ⊢ps. A substitution γ : Δ → Γ^ord must send each aᵢ to an object variable of Δ marked `ord`. This is the existing discipline for `ord` (spec §3.6): an `ord` mark is sent only to an `ord` variable.

```
(COH)       Γ ⊢ps,  SC_{V+S}(Γ, t → u)                     ⊢  coh_{Γ, t → u}[γ]
(COH-ord)   Γ^ord ⊢ps,  SC_𝔉(Γ, t → u),  γ : Δ → Γ^ord    ⊢  coh^ord_{Γ, t → u}[γ]
```

SC_𝔉 is the two-tier side condition of spec §2.2: either ⟦t⟧ = ⟦u⟧ in 𝔉(Γ) (the coherence case), or 𝔉(Γ) has a cell ⟦t⟧ → ⟦u⟧ of full support (the operation case). Terms are interpreted in 𝔉(Γ) by π. The designated witness of a (COH-ord) head is a cell of 𝔉(Γ).

**The marks on (COH-ord) heads.** Use the two-tier rules (spec §3.3) on the head as it stands. A (COH-ord) head in the coherence case is `inv` by (COH-inv). In the operation case its top letters are whiskered Gray generators on generators of Γ, so (OP-inv_S′) case (i) and the two-tier (OP-inv) coincide.

## 2. Semantics and soundness

**Models.** A strict model is an algebra 𝒦 of an admissible theory E ⊇ V + S, with the marks read as in spec §3.6. The new reading of `ord`:

> **C ord** iff for every finite pasting diagram Γ and every σ : F_{V+S}(Γ) → 𝒦 (through F_E(Γ)), every inner letter ℓ of F_{V+S}(Γ) lying in a hom whose target object σ sends to C has σ(ℓ) an identity.

This implies the invertible reading of `uniform-marks.md` §2.2, so (ORD) stays sound.

**Lemma 2.1 (quotient) [proved; the cited content is that the kernel of π₁ is generated by the interchangers along k ≥ 1 (requirement 4)].** The congruence on F_{V+S}(Γ) generated by ℓ = 1 for all inner letters ℓ has quotient 𝔉(Γ), and the quotient map is π₁.

*Proof.*
- Requirement 4 (cited): π₁ is the quotient by the interchangers along k ≥ 1. Equivalently, it is the free algebra on Γ of V + S + "those interchangers are identities".
- Those interchangers are inner letters, so the congruence generated by the inner letters contains the kernel of π₁.
- Conversely, π₁ sends every inner letter to an identity, by the definition of an inner letter (letters that π₁ sends to identities). So the kernel of π₁ contains every instance ℓ = 1, and hence the congruence they generate.
- The two congruences are therefore equal. ∎

**Theorem 2.2 (soundness of (COH-ord)) [proved].** Let 𝒦 be a strict model and ρ an interpretation of Δ under which the `ord` marks hold. Then every (COH-ord) head has an interpretation with the stated boundary.

*Proof.*
- Let σ = ⟦γ⟧_ρ : F_{V+S}(Γ) → 𝒦. Every inner letter of F_{V+S}(Γ) lies in a hom F(Γ)(aᵢ, aⱼ), and σ(aⱼ) = ρ(γ(aⱼ)) is `ord`. So σ sends it to an identity.
- By Lemma 2.1, σ factors uniquely as σ̄ ∘ π₁ with σ̄ : 𝔉(Γ) → 𝒦 (the universal property of the quotient algebra).
- Interpret coh^ord by σ̄(w), where w is the designated two-tier witness. Its boundary is σ̄(π₁⟦t⟧) = σ(⟦t⟧) = ⟦t[γ]⟧_ρ, and likewise for u.
- Uniform subterms of t and u are interpreted through their designated lifts c, and σ(c) = σ̄(π₁ c). So the two interpretations of a term agree. ∎

The path-level core of this argument is formalized: a map of letter paths into a monoid that kills the inner letters factors through erasing them (`OrdCoh.Erase.factor`). Two paths with the same projection then have the same value (`OrdCoh.Erase.coherent`). The algebra-level statement is Lemma 2.1 together with the universal property of a quotient. That step is on paper.

**Theorem 2.3 (the two-tier marks on (COH-ord) heads) [proved].** The two-tier mark rules (spec §3.3: (COH-inv), (OP-inv), (OP-ps), (INV-fac), (INV-obj), (PS-fac)) are sound for (COH-ord) heads in every strict model.

*Proof.* The two-tier proofs (`invertibility-markings.md` §2, Lemmas 2.1–2.3) are equational arguments in 𝔉 of auxiliary pasting contexts Γ′. They use Γ with a factor x split as x ∗ x′, where x′ is to be sent to the inverse of x[γ]. Γ′ has the same objects as Γ, all sent to `ord` objects. So the map F_{V+S}(Γ′) → 𝒦 factors through 𝔉(Γ′) by the argument of Theorem 2.2, and the two-tier equations hold in its image. ∎

**The intended model.** 𝒦 = ∞-Cat_lax is a model of two-tier L (spec §2.6). Since `ord` everywhere gives exactly two-tier L (Corollary 3.2), we expect every object to be `ord` there. Theorems 2.2 and 2.3 are proved for strict models only, and the weak reading of `ord` is not written out (§5), so this remains a claim, not a theorem. The models that uniform L adds, those whose objects interchange directedly inside themselves (spec §2.6, "what uniform L adds"), are exactly those in which some object is not `ord`.

## 3. Exactness: `ord` everywhere is two-tier L

**Theorem 3.1 [proved; Agda `OrdCoh.Rules.recovers`, `conservative`, `within-two-tier`, `mark-mono`, for the Boolean rule form on abstract side conditions].** Write accept_b(h) = SC_{V+S}(h) ∨ (b ∧ SC_𝔉(h)), where b says that every object of the head's context is sent to an `ord` object.
1. accept_true = SC_𝔉. With every object `ord`, the coherence rules accept exactly the two-tier heads.
2. accept_false = SC_{V+S}. With no `ord` objects in a head, nothing changes.
3. accept_b ⊆ SC_𝔉 always.
4. accept_false ⊆ accept_true.

*Proof.* Uses only the inclusion SC_{V+S} ⇒ SC_𝔉 (spec §2.3, ledger S2: a uniform witness c projects to π(c), which has full support). That inclusion is closed under the S1 reading of the side condition, and the Agda takes it as the field `incl`. ∎

**Corollary 3.2.** Let Δ have every object variable marked `ord`. Then the terms, types and `inv`/`ps` judgments derivable over Δ in uniform L + (COH-ord) are exactly those of two-tier L. Terms correspond to terms, with every `coh` read as `coh^ord`.

*Proof.* By induction on derivations.
- **Coherences.** A two-tier coherence over Γ with γ into Δ is a (COH-ord) head, since all objects are `ord`. Conversely, a (COH) head is also a two-tier head (Theorem 3.1(3)).
- **Marks.** On (COH-ord) heads the marks are the two-tier rules, by Theorem 2.3. A (COH) head is also a (COH-ord) head here. The uniform rules on such a head derive no more than two-tier: this is `uniform-⊆-two-tier` in `Uniform/Marks.agda`, and the repaired (OP-inv_S′) only strengthens premises.
- The remaining rules (substitution, context and type formation) are shared. ∎

So E7's question has a positive answer with this mark: "marking every object `ord` restores two-tier L". The E13 countermodels do not apply. In them the inner letters are invertible but not identities, so their objects are not `ord` in the new sense.

## 4. Why the invertible reading cannot do this, and why the side condition is the right place

- **No mark that only adds invertibility can work** [proved from the E13 answer]. Take any mark whose semantics is a property of the inner letters' images that holds in the E13 loop model: H ↦ (4, 1), 1_w ↦ (4, 0), only identities above degree 4, every inner letter invertible. Every rule sound in that model derives no term of type H → 1_w, because the model has no such cell. Two-tier L derives one. So a recovering mark must rule out that model. That means its semantics must force inner letters to satisfy equations, not only to have inverses.
- **The strongest such equations are the quotient.** "Every inner letter into C is an identity" is exactly what makes the homs into C two-tier, by Lemma 2.1. Anything weaker leaves a model like the loop model, as long as the weaker condition still holds there.
- **Equations need the side condition.** Spec §0.3 forbids definitional equality. The only place where a mark can make equations available is the oracle of a `coh` rule. Declaring cells instead (an L3-style hypothesis) runs into the regress of `design-decisions.md` §1.4(c).

## 5. Mixed marks and what is open

- **Mixed heads.** If only some objects of Γ are `ord`, the natural side condition computes in F_m(Γ). This is F_{V+S}(Γ) with the inner letters in homs into the marked objects killed. Whiskering forward then kills their images in later homs too. Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 2.2 go through verbatim for F_m, so the rule would be sound.
  - **[open]** Is SC in F_m(Γ) decidable for mixed m? It holds for m = ∅ (V + S) and for m = all (𝔉).
  - **[open]** Is marking the target object alone enough, i.e. is F_{aₙ}(Γ)(a₀, aₙ) = 𝔉(Γ)(a₀, aₙ)? It would be enough if every outer letter whose operand contains an inner letter of an earlier hom decomposes, by the unary subdivisions, into an inner letter of the last hom and an operand-pure outer letter. The E13 example E(Z, b) is the test case.
  - **[proved] The source object's mark is irrelevant.** In Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ no hom of F_{V+S}(Γ) has target a₀, so no inner letter lands in a hom into a₀. Hence F_m(Γ) = F_{m ∪ {a₀}}(Γ), and (COH-ord) needs only a₁, …, aₙ to be `ord`. This relaxation is adopted in spec §3.6.
  - **(b), partial analysis (2026-11-23).** Try marking only aₙ. Take the congruence generated by the instances C[ℓ] = C[1], with ℓ an inner letter of an earlier hom and C a context landing in hom(a₀, aₙ). There are two cases.
    - *ℓ enters C[ℓ] by whiskering, or as the moving operand of a cylinder.* Then C[ℓ] = C[1] modulo inner letters of hom(a₀, aₙ). The whiskered ℓ is itself an inner letter there. For a cylinder, the unary subdivisions of B split E(u ∗ ℓ ∗ v, b) into pieces, and the piece E(ℓ, b) has π₁ = 1, so it is killed. The E13 letter E(Z, b) is of this kind and causes no problem.
    - *ℓ sits inside an operand of a binary outer letter such as W(A₁ ∗ ℓ, B).* Splitting that operand needs a binary subdivision, which V has only in guarded form (`uniform-marks.md` (F6)). Then W(A₁ ∗ ℓ, B) is an outer letter of hom(a₀, aₙ) with Gray image W(A₁, B), and nothing in the target-only quotient identifies it with W(A₁, B).
    - So the expected answer to (b) is **no**: marking the target alone does not give the two-tier hom when a binary outer letter takes an operand carrying inner content from an earlier hom. A countermodel is not built yet. The guarded subdivision tests of r8 Response 1 suggest a tag model, as in the outer W-strip table of the E13 answer.
  - Until these are settled the kernel takes (COH-ord) only with every object a₁, …, aₙ of Γ marked. That already gives Corollary 3.2. Both questions are ledger E15.
- **Two marks or one.** Decided in §6: keep both. `iord` is the invertible reading, and `ord` is the trivial reading with (COH-ord).
- **Weak models.** The soundness proofs are for strict models. For the intended model, see §2. A uniform-L reading in oriented (∞,ω)-categories with some objects not `ord` would interpret (COH-ord) heads through the two-tier interpretation of the full subcategory on the `ord` objects. It is not written out.

## 6. Decision: keep both marks, `ord` and the weaker `iord` (2026-11-23)

Project rule 3 makes this a design question to be settled from the evidence, not a choice to defer. Rule 4 says to prefer the more general design. This section compares the options and records the decision.

**The candidates.**
- **(A) One mark `ord`** in the trivial reading, with (COH-ord). This is the state after S16(b).
- **(B) Two marks.** `ord` as in (A), plus **`iord`**, which says "every inner letter into C is invertible". `iord` is the first reading of `ord`, from `uniform-marks.md` §2.2.
- **(C) One mark `iord`** with no (COH-ord). This is the state before S16(b).

**What each derives, by example.**

| Example | unmarked | `iord` on C | `ord` on C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Directed EH in C (spec §2.3 Ex. (c)): EH₁ : b∗a ⇛ a∗b, EH₂ : a∗b ⇛ b∗a | two directed cells, neither invertible | both invertible by (ORD); no term EH₂ → EH₁⁻¹ | one coherence each way, inverse to each other up to coherence (two-tier coherence case) |
| The loop H : w ⇛ w on Σ[2] ∨ Σ[2] inside C | H directed | H invertible; no term H → 1_w (E13 loop model) | coh(H → 1_w) accepted by (COH-ord) |
| A composite of invertible cells of C composable along a 0-cell (spec §3.6 Ex. (b)) | not `inv` | `inv` by (OP-inv_S′)(b) | `inv` (two-tier) |
| Outer W-strip inside C | distinct, unconnected | distinct, unconnected | equal up to an invertible coherence |

**Models.**
- `ord` ⇒ `iord`, since identities are invertible.
- The converse fails in strict models. The verified E13 loop model (`conjectures/answered/invertibility-in-directed-interchange-2026-11-21.md`) is one witness: every inner letter is invertible, with integer labels, while H ↦ (4, 1) ≠ (4, 0) ↤ 1_w. Its long target object is therefore `iord` but not `ord`.
  - Every theorem proved with `iord` holds there.
  - A theorem proved with `ord` need not hold there: coh(H → 1_w) has no interpretation.
  - The same holds for any strict model whose inner interchange is invertible but not an identity. These are the "Gray-category-like" objects, with interchange data up to invertible cells and no higher coherence identification. That description is a heuristic; only the E13 model is a checked instance.
- In the (∞,ω) reading the two marks plausibly have the same models up to equivalence. The step down one level of M7 says that a Gray-enriched structure whose composition inverts mixed cells is Cat_ω-enriched. But that statement concerns the homotopy-coherent setting, which uniform L's strict side condition does not see. So the gain from `iord` lies in strict models and in derivability, not in the intended ∞-model. This is [conj] at the ∞-level and is not needed for the decision.

**Comparison.**
- (C) fails the purpose of the mark. It cannot recover two-tier L (§4), and §0.4 would be false again. **Rejected.**
- (A) is simpler, but its only lighter-weight option forces all inner coherences. A development that only needs inner interchangers to be invertible, such as one that uses invertible EH or composites of invertible cells, would then be valid only in models where the inner letters are trivial. That excludes the `iord`-not-`ord` models above for no reason.
- (B) costs one extra mark and no new rule. (ORD), (OP-inv_S′)(b) and case (iii) already have exactly the `iord` reading. Its rules are syntax-directed and decidable like the rest.
  - It fits the lax / pseudo / strict pattern of spec §0.4, applied to the interchange *inside* an object: unmarked (directed), `iord` (invertible), `ord` (trivial). This is the same pattern as lax / pseudo / strict transformations (unmarked, `ps`, coherence case).
  - A theorem proved at the `iord` level holds in strictly more models than one proved at `ord` (rule 4).

**Decision: (B).**

```
(C : ⋆)^iord   every inner letter whose hom lands in 𝒦(X, C) is invertible
(C : ⋆)^ord    every such inner letter is trivial;  ord ⇒ iord
(ORD), (OP-inv_S′)(b), (OP-inv_S′)(iii):  premise "target iord" (an ord target satisfies it)
(COH-ord):     every object of Γ sent to an ord object
substitution:  an iord variable may be sent to an iord or ord variable; an ord variable only to an ord variable
```

- **Soundness.** As before. (ORD) and the (OP-inv_S′) premises are proved under the invertible reading (`uniform-marks.md` Theorems 4.3 and 8.1). (COH-ord) is proved under the trivial reading (Theorem 2.2). `ord` ⇒ `iord` makes every `iord` rule sound for `ord` objects.
- **Surface.** Which of the two marks a person writes by default, and with what keyword, is a surface question. It belongs with Q3.3 (scope of `ord`) in spec §3.7.
