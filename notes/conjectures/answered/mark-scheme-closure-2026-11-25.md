# Which coinductively declared cell structures are closed under the operations of a Gray-enriched ω-category, and which are properties?

*A self-contained problem. Background: weak and strict ω-categories, the Gray tensor, coinductive invertibility.*

## 1. Setting

**Ambient.** Work in either of two settings, and say which results hold in which:
- (strict) categories enriched in strict ω-categories for the lax Gray tensor of Ara–Maltsiniotis;
- (weak) oriented (∞,ω)-categories, i.e. categories enriched in Cat_ω for the lax Gray tensor (Gepner–Heine, arXiv 2510.10504; Campion, arXiv 2311.00205), with coinductive equivalences.

Inside a hom 𝒦(a, b), cells compose as in an ω-category. Across objects, composition 𝒦(a, b) ⊗ 𝒦(b, c) → 𝒦(a, c) is Gray, so for cells x, y of positive dimension in consecutive homs there is a directed interchange cell x ⊗ y, the **Gray cell**. Plain weak ω-categories are the one-object, no-crossing special case.

**Schemes.** A *scheme* P of dimension n is a finite list of clauses over a generic n-cell t : u → v (a cell of some hom). There are two kinds of clause.
- An *∃-clause* declares a new cell cᵢ : Aᵢ. Here Aᵢ is a type whose endpoints are built from t, its iterated boundaries and earlier clause cells, using only the ω-category operations of the hom (composites, identities, whiskerings, coherence cells). The clause may require cᵢ to carry a scheme again, P itself or another scheme of dimension n + 1.
- A *∀-clause* states that a cell s(t, y), built from t and a cell y ranging over all cells of some boundary shape, carries a scheme Q.

A *P-structure* on t is a choice of all ∃-clause cells, recursively, so an infinite tower, interpreted as a greatest fixed point (as for coinductive invertibility). Equivalently, a P-structure is a map out of the *walking P-cell* W_P, the colimit of the finite stages of the unfolded tower, extending t.

**Examples.**
- **Inv.** s, s′ : v → u; l : s ∗ t → 1_v [Inv]; r : t ∗ s′ → 1_u [Inv]. This is coinductive invertibility. W_Inv is a coinductive walking equivalence.
- **RInv.** s : v → u; r : t ∗ s → 1_u [RInv]. Coinductive right inverses.
- **LAdj.** s : v → u; η : 1_u → t ∗ s [LAdj]; ε : s ∗ t → 1_v [LAdj]; two triangle cells [Inv]. Adjoints at every level.
- **Idem** (t an endomorphism). c : t ∗ t → t [Inv].
- **Ps** (a ∀-scheme). For every y of positive dimension composed before t, the Gray cell y ⊗ t carries Inv. With the same clause on the boundaries of t, these are the pseudo transformations and modifications among lax ones.

**Known.**
1. *Inv is closed under everything.* Full coherence cells carry Inv. So do composites (and more generally pasting composites) of Inv-cells. This holds in weak ω-categories (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2406.12127, Prop. 16 and Thm 33), and there the inverse of a composite is built syntactically by a meta-operation. In the Gray setting:
   - whiskerings of Inv-cells carry Inv;
   - a Gray cell x ⊗ y carries Inv when x or y does;
   - a Gray cell carries Inv when its later factor carries Ps.

   These are proved semantically, via Dᵐ ⊗ Eᵏ ≃ Dᵐ ⊗ Dᵏ⁻¹ for E the walking invertible cell.
2. *Not every scheme is closed under composition.* In the 1-category Set, Idem-structures are idempotents, and a composite of idempotents need not be idempotent.
3. *Inv is a property.* The space of Inv-structures on a cell is empty or contractible in the coinductive weak setting. W_Inv → Dⁿ⁻¹ is an equivalence there. It is not an equivalence in the raw inductive setting (Hadzihasanovic–Loubaton–Ozornova–Rovelli, arXiv 2404.14509).
4. *RInv is not a property.* Right inverses are not unique.
5. *∀-clauses cannot be certified from data.* Freely attaching a generic earlier cell y at the source of t makes "y ⊗ t invertible" equivalent to "t invertible". So a Ps-structure on t that is a consequence of data alone is just invertibility.

## 2. Questions

**Q1 (main: closure criterion).** Give a criterion on schemes, as decidable as possible from the clause list, that determines for each of the following operations whether P-structured cells are closed under it, in every model of each setting. The criterion should be a general theorem with explicit countermodels for the negative direction:
- (a) full coherence cells carry P;
- (b) whiskering a P-cell by any cell of lower dimension gives a P-cell;
- (c) a vertical composite of P-cells is a P-cell;
- (d) a pasting composite whose top-dimensional inputs are P-cells is a P-cell;
- (e) a Gray cell x ⊗ y is a P-cell when one factor, or a specified factor, is a P-cell.

We expect something like the following, and would like it proved or refuted.
- If every ∃-clause type is built from t and earlier clause cells only, then (a) holds.
- If, in addition, the clause types are "functorial in t", then (b) and (e) hold. Functorial means that the endpoints of each clause type are images of fixed shapes under the operations, so that a functor or a Gray whiskering transports clause cells.
- (c) and (d) hold when the clause types are additionally "multiplicative". This is the condition Inv, RInv and LAdj satisfy and Idem fails: the clause data for t₁ ∗ t₂ is assembled from the clause data of t₁ and t₂.

Please state the precise conditions, and whether they are also necessary.

**Q2 (syntactic admissibility).** In the cases where Q1 gives closure, is the P-structure on the composite (or Gray cell, or coherence) produced by a *uniform construction*? That is, a finite recipe from the P-structures of the inputs and coherence cells, applied at every level of the tower, as Benjamin–Markakis do for Inv in weak ω-categories. The question is most important for Inv itself in the strict and weak Gray settings, and for the Gray-cell rules of item 1 above. Are those rules admissible from the Inv clauses plus coherence cells? Or is there a Gray cell x ⊗ y with x Inv-structured whose Inv-structure cannot be given by any uniform recipe of this kind? A negative answer should come with the obstruction.

**Q3 (properties).** Characterize the schemes P for which the space of P-structures on any cell is empty or contractible, in the coinductive weak setting. Equivalently, for which W_P → Dⁿ⁻¹ is a monomorphism onto its image (the inclusion of a full sub-object)? Is LAdj such a scheme in the ω case, or only after truncation? Is there a syntactic sufficient condition, of the kind "every clause cell is uniquely determined up to an Inv-cell by the earlier ones"?

**What would help most.** One theorem covering Q1 for all schemes and all five operations, with the uniform-construction statement of Q2 as part of its positive direction, and the property criterion of Q3. Inv, RInv, LAdj, Idem and Ps should come out as instances.

# Researcher Response 1

A walking-cell test gives a necessary and sufficient condition for each of (a)–(e) for existential schemes. Its negative instance is the free arity of the operation with the specified input towers attached. Composition closure is already undecidable in this fragment. Allowing quantification over the ambient model changes what can be constructed. Even a scheme supported on **every** cell can fail to admit a natural whiskering rule. For Inv in either Gray slot a finite search produces every requested layer. The space of structures is controlled by the codiagonal of the input inclusion when the scheme is representable. For the LAdj clauses in the question it can be disconnected, already in a 3-groupoid.

## 1. The free test for closure

Use left-to-right composition of cells, written $fg$. Here a whisker uses lower-dimensional composition, and crossing two positive-dimensional cells means (e). A scheme used in different dimensions must specify its dimension-indexed instances. Include all restrictions on the input shape, such as equal endpoints for Idem. Write $D$ for the free shape of the cell with these restrictions. In the weak setting take derived cell spaces, colimits and fibres, and use the coinductively complete localization for the stated facts about equivalences. [Campion, Definition 1.33 and Example 1.34](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00205)

Let $A$ be the walking arity of a **specified** operation. It has input maps $d_i:D_i\to A$ and an output $o:D_o\to A$. Use the free coherence context with its full coherence for (a), the whiskering context for (b), the two composable globes for (c), and the specified pasting arity and its composite for (d). For (e) use the free enriched chain with the two hom disks in consecutive homs. Its long hom is their lax Gray tensor and $o$ selects the tensor's top cell. If a rule is conditional on a separately supplied output boundary coincidence, start with the unrestricted disk $G$ and replace the arity by $A\amalg_G D_o$. This classifies the extra guard as part of the instance. When the operation already acts on endomorphisms, as ordinary whiskering does, use its induced endomorphism typing. An unguarded rule into an endomorphism scheme must first establish the boundary coincidence. Only the designated inputs carry marks. In particular the choice of slot in (e) changes the arity **with its marked inputs**. One can equally describe all these diagrams as enriched contexts by suspending a single hom. Test every indicated arity if the rule ranges over arities.

**Free-arity theorem.** Suppose the input and output structures are represented by $i_j:D_j\to W_j$. Form the pushout

$$
       U=A \amalg_{\coprod_i D_i} \coprod_i W_i .
$$

The rule holds in every model if and only if the composite $D_o\to A\to U$ extends along $i_o:D_o\to W_o$. Indeed $U$ itself realizes the generic structured inputs. Applying a universally valid rule there gives the extension. Conversely, every instance with chosen input towers gives a map $U\to X$. Composing with the chosen extension supplies the output tower, naturally in this instance. In the weak case the extension includes its identification over $D_o$, and the same argument uses the mapping-space pushout formula. On failure take **$U$ with its tautological inputs**. This specifies a countermodel using just the operation and clause data.

For existential clauses $W$ is obtained by successively attaching a globe for each witness, with its prescribed boundary, then doing the same for its children. Mapping out of this colimit gives exactly a compatible tower. Thus the extension test applies without any independence condition on later clauses. It gives precise meanings to transport and multiplicativity through extensions in the arities for transport and multiplication, respectively. This condition is necessary and sufficient for representable structures. One need not assume any associativity or unit law for the chosen *witness operation* merely to prove closure of its support. A list of correctly typed term operations that constructs all the extension cells is sufficient. For an effective cellular term presentation the exact extra requirement for a finite metaprogram is a computable compatible family of generic terms (including boundary derivations), uniform also in the arity when the rule varies. Evaluation proves sufficiency. Running a proposed metaprogram on the generic formal inputs proves necessity. A point of the inverse limit of extension spaces alone supplies no such program.

Ordinary functoriality is much weaker. For $n\ge1$ let $P_n(t)$ choose an arrow from the ultimate target of $t$ to its ultimate source. This is one existential clause formed from boundaries and preserved by functors. In the walking arrow $f:0\to1$, the full identity coherence $1_f$ has no such structure. It is also the whiskering of $1_{1_0}$, which has the structure, by $f$. Regard $f$ as the other input of a tensor with the identity 1-cell on the point of the tensor unit. The latter has $P_1$, and their crossing is $1_f$. Both tensor orders give the same obstruction. Thus the proposed syntactic conditions in (a), (b) and (e) fail in the stated grammar.

**Ambient quantification.** Universal tests need not be represented by a map $D\to W$ in the original model category. In the free enriched arrow with hom $[1]$, its nonidentity cell $t$ satisfies Ps. Every earlier positive-dimensional test is degenerate. Freely attach a fresh object and an earlier hom $[1]$ with generator $y$. The top $y\otimes t$ in the long hom is the directed comparison in the walking lax square. It has no reverse. The inclusion carries the original cell to a cell failing Ps. A witness given by a map $W\to X$ would persist by postcomposition. The free lax square is also the corresponding weak Gray tensor of the intervals. [Campion, Introduction and Theorem A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00205)

**A set-sized test including ambient quantifiers.** Here a universal clause has the pointwise meaning in the question. Each legal instantiation must carry the indicated scheme. Use ordinary classical set theory with choice. There is also a countermodel test for this whole language, although one pays for ambient quantifiers by inspecting model tables. The following construction bounds the size of a countermodel.

* In a fixed model make positions of all instantiated clauses and their finite witness prefixes. An existential move selects a well-typed next witness. A challenge may select a conjunct, a legal value of a universal variable, or any recursive obligation. Witnesses shared by several obligations are chosen before the challenge branches. Equivalently the one-round operator on sets of marked cells evaluates the finite dependent clause list, using the candidate sets for recursive marks. Empty universal ranges pass. Start with all positions, apply this monotone operator at successors and intersect at limits. On a set of positions of size μ this stabilizes before μ⁺ (use an infinite bound). The survivors admit strategies for continuing forever by choosing a witness inside the fixed point. Conversely, the positions reached under such a strategy form a postfixed set. In the corresponding game with each move written separately, the first stage of loss supplies ranks. At a losing witness move every legal choice has smaller rank. At a losing challenge move some challenge has smaller rank. A rank can strictly decrease only finitely often along a play. In particular, passing each finite depth is insufficient.
* For strict enrichment it suffices to inspect tables on a countable set. In a countermodel keep the input instance, winning strategies for its premises and a ranked losing strategy for its conclusion. Close its cells under the structure operations and under the prescribed choices on every retained finite history. Repeat countably often. This is a strict submodel since the countable globular signature has finite arities (the Gray composition can equally be given on generator pairs with its equations). The premise strategies still work against every retained challenge. Every retained response to the refuter was already a response in the large model, so its original ranks still descend. The smaller table is itself a countermodel.
* For weak enrichment one can use the following deliberately generous bound, which avoids assuming finite arity of coinductive completeness. Fix a presheaf-of-spaces presentation of the oriented categories. Use its simplicial tables, restriction maps, and a set of local fibrancy tests, including the tests at the coinductive equivalence in each hom. The oriented Segal presentation is a presheaf localization. Homwise localization adds the suspended maps of the monoidal coinductive localization. Locality at a map is expressed, using cofibrant representatives and a mapping cylinder, by lifts against its pushout-products with simplex boundaries, in addition to ordinary fibrancy. Include shapes representing the operations, boundary tests and their coherent gluings. Take an infinite cardinal κ at least the size of the site (arrows included), the set of tests, and every simplicial diagram in those tests and clause arities. Test tables of cardinal at most $\lambda=2^\kappa$. To shrink a bad fibrant table, do the preceding strategy closure, also closing under restrictions and one chosen filler for each retained local fibrancy problem. Perform κ⁺ stages. At any stage there are at most $\lambda^\kappa=\lambda$ diagrams to treat. Each chosen filler adds at most κ entries. Every diagram of size at most κ in the union appears at a bounded stage, so the resulting table is fibrant and local. When the clauses use composites chosen up to coherence, keep the corresponding evaluations and boundary homotopies with the histories. They have the same size bound. Inclusion preserves these operations, and the same two strategy arguments apply. [Gepner–Heine, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10504), [Campion, Definition 1.33 and Corollary 3.15](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00205)

For each operation arity enumerate set-theoretically all labelled tables up to the bound, all legal instances in them, and perform the fixed-point test above. Failure produces the offending table, the actual inputs, and the ranks refuting the output. Conversely a failed universal law has such a certificate by the submodel construction. This is an exact criterion derived from the clauses even when a walking object fails to exist. It is a bounded test. Its space of countable tables alone can already be uncountable. It tests the pointwise quantifier as written. Requiring a specified coherent section over a varying parameter space would be additional clause syntax.

**Semantic closure need not give a uniform construction.** The obstruction persists if access to the input towers is allowed. Consider the scheme on arrows $t:a\to b$ that first chooses $z:a\to b$ and $c:z\Rightarrow z$. For every $y:b\to a$, require a comparison $pqr\Rightarrow z$, with $p,r:a\to b$ and $q:b\to a$. To express this in the clause grammar, mark $c$ by the auxiliary scheme that, for each $y$, requires the scheme on $1_c$ choosing $p,q,r$ and an unmarked comparison $pqr\Rightarrow z$. Here $z$ and its endpoints are boundaries of $1_c$. The tested cell can be constant in $y$. Every arrow supports this scheme. If the reverse cell space is empty, choose $z=t$. Otherwise choose one reverse $q$ and take $z=tqt$. In both cases use $c=1_z$, and in the inhabited case use the same factorization for every test. This is an existence argument, also for space-valued test domains.

Use a three-object enriched chain whose first hom is $[1]$, second hom the point, and long hom initially $[1]$. Whiskering by the second hom's object is the identity on the first hom. Enlarge only the long hom to the free category on $t:a\rightleftarrows b:q$, extending composition by the inclusion. The first hom, its structured input and the whiskering parameter agree in both instances. Any output choice in the smaller long hom must have $z=t$. In the larger long hom $t$ has a reverse arrow available for the test, but the word $t$ cannot equal $pqr$ because all three factors must be nonempty words between distinct objects. There are only identity comparison cells. Hence no substitution-natural choice of output exists. For (b) read entirely *inside* an individual hom, use these three-object categories as strict 2-categories and take them as a single enriched hom. The first hom's arrows are now 2-cells, and the whisker is a 1-cell. Shift the displayed scheme to that dimension in the local hom between the parallel 1-cells. This also works in the weak setting since the discrete word lengths obstruct equivalences. The obstruction even survives the finite quotient that identifies paths of length at least three with the same endpoints. The length-one arrow still differs from every three-factor composite. Every finite recipe using the listed cell operations commutes with these substitutions. Consequently Q2 has a negative answer for general schemes, even for a rule that always holds semantically.

## 2. No decision procedure for vertical composition

Fix a finitely presented group $G$ with unsolvable word problem. Given a word $w$ put $K=G*\langle z\rangle$, $u=[w,z]$, and form the finite presentation

$$
 H=\langle K,a,h\mid a^2=1,\ h^{-1}uh=ua\rangle .
$$

If $w=1$, then $a=1$ in $H$. Otherwise the reduced free-product word $u$ has infinite order in $K$, and $ua$ has infinite order in $K*C_2$. The presentation is an HNN extension of $K*C_2$ identifying the cyclic groups generated by $u$ and $ua$. The base embeds by Britton's lemma. Thus $a\ne1$ exactly in this second case. [Britton, *The Word Problem*](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970200)

Make an existential scheme for an endo-1-cell $t$. Use $t$ for $a$, choose loops for the other generators, choose inverse loops for all generators and choose invertible 2-cells witnessing the inverse equations and every relator. In any model this is inhabited exactly when there is a homomorphism from $H$ to the group of units of the endomorphism monoid in the homotopy 1-category that sends $a$ to $[t]$. In one direction pass to classes. In the other choose representative loops, then lift the finite list of equalities to invertible 2-cells. Equality of parallel arrow classes means they are joined by an invertible 2-cell, since those cells compose and admit inverses up to equivalence.

If $a=1$, precisely the endomorphisms in the identity class carry the scheme, by the trivial representation, and they compose. If $a\ne1$, use the one-object ordinary category with endomorphism group $H*H$. The two copies of $a$ each have the scheme, and their product has infinite order by reduced normal form. Its square cannot be the identity, as the scheme requires. A one-hom suspension places this example in either enriched setting. A decider for (c) on finite clause lists would decide $w=1$.

## 3. The five examples

The entries concern **existence**. For Idem test only endomorphic outputs. For Ps write $\mathrm{Ps}_0$ for the displayed test on the top cell and $\mathrm{Ps}_*$ for that test also on every positive-dimensional iterated boundary. Column (d) allows lower-dimensional whiskers, as in the question. The two entries in (e) refer to the earlier and later slot individually. An identity of positive dimension is a legal input. Finite-dimensional strict examples below also give weak models. On the strong Steiner site, tensoring two strict test maps induces the lax monoidal comparison from the Day tensor of their nerves to the nerve of their strict tensor. Composing with strict enriched composition and taking the monoidal localization gives the weak interpretation. In particular a strict 2-category interpreted weakly still has its original sets of parallel 2-cells over fixed boundaries. This argument does not require arbitrary strict tensors to compute derived tensors. [Campion, §§1.2 and 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00205)

| Scheme | (a) full coherence | (b) whisker | (c) vertical | (d) pasting | (e) earlier / later | Property in weak setting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inv | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes / yes | yes |
| RInv | yes | yes | yes | yes | no / no | no |
| LAdj | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes / yes | no |
| Idem | strict yes, weak no | yes | no | no | no / no | no |
| $\mathrm{Ps}_0$ | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes / yes | yes |
| $\mathrm{Ps}_*$ | no | no | yes | no | no / no | yes |

**Inv and RInv.** The Inv support entries use the given closure results. For RInv, let $S$ be all finite top-dimensional pastes of RInv cells and invertible cells, with arbitrary lower whiskers and identities. In every dimension this includes the basic RInv cells and full coherence cells. To get a right contraction of a paste, decompose it into binary compositions and whiskerings. For a generating $f$ take its specified reverse and contraction (an invertible contraction in the invertible case). For a vertical composite $fg$ use $g'f'$ as reverse. Whisker the contraction of $gg'$ by $f,f'$, then apply the contraction of $ff'$. For a lower whiskering apply the same whisker to the reverse and contraction, inserting the invertible composition and unit comparisons; lower compositions of top cells decompose into such whiskerings and vertical compositions. Other bracketings or a chosen weak pasting composite are related by invertible pasting coherence. All the contractions produced are pastes in $S$ one dimension higher. Thus $S$ is postfixed for RInv, proving (a)–(d) and giving a finite recursive construction on such pastes. For the weak Inv pasting algorithm see [Benjamin–Markakis, Proposition 16 and Theorem 33](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12127).

For (e), let $S$ now denote the category generated by $f:A\to B,r:B\to A$ with $fr=1_A$. Send $A$ to the terminal category, $B$ to $[1]$, $f$ to selection of $0$ and $r$ to the unique functor. A lax transformation from this diagram to itself has component the identity at $A$, constant $1$ at $B$, and comparison $0\to1$ at $f$. Give $r$ the identity comparison. Pasting respects the single relation since $r$ sends the comparison to an identity. For the opposite comparison convention select $1$ and use constant $0$. By the lax/oplax Gray cylinder description, this supplies the detecting cubical functor in either tensor order. The image of the crossing has no reverse, although $f$ is split in the required direction. Equivalently take the free enriched chain on these two factors and apply the detecting functor to its long hom. [Campion, Introduction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00205)

**LAdj has the same support as Inv.** Collect all left adjoints occurring recursively in LAdj towers and their supplied right adjoints. For each pair $f,g$, the right adjoint of its unit gives $fg\to1$ and its counit gives $gf\to1$. Both contractions belong to the collection. They serve for either member of the pair, so coinduction gives Inv. Conversely an Inv cell, viewed as a 1-arrow in the relevant iterated hom, is an equivalence in its homotopy bicategory. This bicategory identifies parallel 2-cells related by an invertible 3-cell. Equip the equivalence there with an adjoint equivalence. Choose representatives of its unit and counit. They are Inv, since lifting their inverse equations supplies contractions by invertible 3-cells. Lift the two triangle equalities to invertible 3-cells. This displays Inv as a postfixed class for LAdj, so the choices can be iterated. All the LAdj closure entries follow. [Gurski, Theorem 1.9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.0979)

**Idem.** Strict full structural coherences are identities. Whiskering preserves top composition and invertible comparisons, giving the multiplication witness for the whiskered cell. For (c) use the endomorphisms of $\{0,1,2\}$ given by $p(1)=0$ and $q(2)=1$, each fixing other elements. Both are idempotent, whereas $pq$ has values $(0,0,1)$ and $(pq)^2$ has values $(0,0,0)$ in left-to-right order. This also disproves (d). In the weak case the full associator can fail (a) even with endomorphic typing. Use the one-object bicategory associated to the skeletal monoidal groupoid with object group $C_2$, automorphism group $C_2$ at each object, and addition as tensor on arrows. Set its normalized associator to $\alpha(g,h,k)=ghk$ in $\mathbb F_2$. The pentagon is the identity $hkl+(g+h)kl+g(h+k)l+gh(k+l)+ghk=0$. Normalization gives the unit axioms. The value at $(1,1,1)$ is a nonidentity endomorphism whose square is the identity. There is no comparing 3-cell.

For (e) take the walking ordinary idempotent $e^2=e$. Its one-object diagram in Cat has value the group category $BC_2$ and $e$ acts by the trivial group homomorphism $p$. Choose a lax transformation from this diagram to itself with identity component and comparison $\alpha:p\Rightarrow p$ the nonzero element of $C_2$. Naturality holds since $p$ kills all arrows. The two whiskered contributions to the comparison for $e^2$ are $p\alpha=0$ and $\alpha p=\alpha$, hence their sum is $\alpha$ as required. Give the identity its identity comparison. The same calculation with the opposite orientation gives the other tensor order. The endomorphic crossing has image $\alpha$, whose square is $0\ne\alpha$, in a target with only identity 3-cells. Thus even an endomorphic crossing need not carry Idem.

**Ps.** Full coherences are invertible, hence have $\mathrm{Ps}_0$. To test a whiskering or a pasting of dimension $n$, tensor its globular pasting arity with the earlier test disk $D^m$. In the strict tensor's cellular presentation its generators are pairs of generators. A generator in dimension $m+n$ therefore consists of the top of $D^m$ paired with a top $n$-generator of the paste. Every such generator evaluates to an invertible cell by the input tests. Their paste with lower whiskers and identities is invertible. The same globular pasting arity and its tensor present the weak composites: the globular sum is a finite loop-free complex, tensor products with the disk have the pair-of-generators presentation, and the weak pasting and Gray theorems identify these arities with their strict nerves. Any choice of weak composite differs by invertible coherence. Whiskering across objects by a hom 0-cell also follows by enriched associativity. For (e), if $y$ is later and has $\mathrm{Ps}_0$, the test evaluated at $x$ gives Inv of $x\otimes y$, hence $\mathrm{Ps}_0$. If $x$ is earlier and has $\mathrm{Ps}_0$, a test by $z$ gives $(z\otimes x)\otimes y$ by associativity. Its earlier factor is Inv. [Campion, pasting theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00200), [Campion, Gray tensor theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00205)

For $\mathrm{Ps}_*$, vertical composition retains the exposed boundaries of the end factors, and the top test follows from $\mathrm{Ps}_0$. Let $h$ be a nonpseudo cell obtained by the free earlier-arrow attachment above. Then $1_h$ fails the test on its face $h$. It is a full identity coherence, a whisker of the hereditarily pseudo identity on an identity, and a paste with only that identity as top input. Its occurrence as the crossing of the identity 1-cell on the enrichment unit with $h$, in either order, also disproves the unilateral rules in (e). If a pasting rule prescribes hereditary Ps on all its positive-dimensional generators, every face enjoys the same top-generator proof as the paste itself.

## 4. A finite recipe for the two Gray factor rules for Inv

A finite recipe here means one metaprogram which returns the next finite layer on demand. The bound is on the description of the program. Its running time can depend on the node. The weak ambient category is specified semantically in the question, so we give the weak program in an explicit presentation by finite shapes of that category.

Let the structured input have dimension $n>0$, the other input dimension $m>0$, and put $d=n+m$. In the generic long hom take $T=E^n\otimes D^m$, where $E^n$ is generated by the actual Inv tower. Reverse the tensor order for the other rule. In either order the collapse has target $V=D^{n-1}\otimes D^m$ (with the same reversal). This is a gaunt complex of dimension $d-1$ because its generators are pairs of disk generators, with dimensions added. In particular there are only identity cells in degrees $\ge d$.

**The algorithm.** Start at the tensor of the two top generators. At a requested $k$-cell $z$, dovetail finite typed derivations in the generic presentation for **one whole layer** consisting of two reverses and two cancellation cells with the specified endpoints. Include the boundary identifications required by the presentation. Return the first complete derivation in a fixed enumeration. Use exactly the same instruction on either cancellation when that child is requested. Evaluate the finite derivations by substituting the supplied tower and disk and applying enriched composition. All searches take place in the generic presentation, so testing equality or searching for fillers in the user's model is unnecessary.

The critical assertion for termination is that **every** $k$-cell encountered, including every possible choice of cancellation, admits such a layer. Existence of one infinite tower at the root alone would leave open the possibility of choosing a dead branch.

*Strict homs.* Each generator of $E^n$ of degree at least $n$ is Inv. The marked nodes are Inv by construction and the bare inverse candidates are Inv by cancellation against an equivalence. A single step preserves shallower object spaces, is surjective on objects at the completed depth, and is fully faithful on the fixed-endpoint homs there. ## 5. The property criterion and the two triangle witnesses

For any cell context $D$ and representable structure $i:D\to W$, put $p_X:\operatorname{Map}(W,X)\to\operatorname{Map}(D,X)$. The structure space on $t$ is its homotopy fibre at $t$. A map of spaces has empty or contractible fibres exactly when its diagonal is an equivalence. The diagonal of $p_X$ is mapping out of the fold

$$
                     W\amalg_D^{h}W\longrightarrow W .
$$

Varying $X$ and applying Yoneda proves that **the fold must be an equivalence**, and this is sufficient. It is the forgetful map on *mapping spaces* that is a monomorphism. For instance the scheme with no clauses has $W=D^n$ and a terminal structure space on every cell, including noninvertible cells. For an ambient-dependent scheme one uses the diagonal of its actual family of structure spaces over the cells of each model. One can test it directly on the clauses. Form the spaces of finite prefixes by dependent sums for witnesses and restrictions to the locus where assertion-only universal tests hold. For a universal clause asserting a recursive property, evaluate that property by the greatest fixed point above. Passing finite approximations is insufficient. Take the homotopy inverse limit of the prefix spaces. Over a fixed cell, subterminality is equivalent to the following extension test for every $j\ge0$: every map from the sphere $S^j$ to that limit extends over the ball. Equivalently use a map of $S^j$ into the prefix spaces compatible through the entire tower and ask for a compatible filling. This follows since mapping out of the sphere or the ball preserves limits in the target. The $j=0$ test gives path connectedness when inhabited, and the higher tests force all higher homotopy groups to vanish. If the language specifies coherent witness data at a universal clause, use the dependent section space over its parameter space. A product over the vertices of an arbitrarily chosen presentation would depend on that presentation. For Ps the fibres are subterminal, so pointwise truth already implies that the section space is contractible.

A sufficient proof by clauses can group dependent choices into blocks. Exhibit a contraction, when inhabited, of the **whole space** of extensions of each prefix by the next block, uniformly in the prefix. The restriction maps then have proposition-valued fibres. Compositions and inverse limits of these subobjects are subobjects, so the limit tower has the property. A syntactic instance is a block given by a variable endpoint and a path from a specified endpoint to it in a cell space. Path induction contracts the pair, including its higher identifications. A single invertible comparison between every two candidates only tests connectedness. For example, over the fixed identity 1-cell in $B^2C_2$, a clause choosing one extra endo-1-cell has witness space $BC_2$. It has one object and a nontrivial automorphism group.

Inv has the property by the coinductive walking equivalence in the question. A Ps structure in a fixed ambient model is a dependent product of Inv-structure spaces indexed by the tests, with the further boundary factors for $\mathrm{Ps}_*$. Products of subterminal spaces are subterminal. For RInv choose an inclusion of a two-element set into a three-element set. Its retractions can send the extra element to either included point. These give distinct components over the fixed inclusion in the ordinary categorical hom. For Idem take $t=1$ in the strict 2-groupoid $B^2 C_2$, with one object and one 1-cell. The chosen invertible comparison $t^2\Rightarrow t$ ranges over the discrete set $C_2$ in the fibre at fixed $t$. Each value has Inv structure. [Hadzihasanovic–Loubaton–Ozornova–Rovelli, coherent walking equivalence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.14509)

**The two free triangle witnesses in LAdj survive the tower.** Take the strict 3-groupoid $B^3 C_2$, with one cell in each dimension below 3 and 3-automorphism group $C_2$. Take the identity for $t,s,\eta,\varepsilon$, complete both unit/counit adjunction subtrees with identities, and choose the two triangle witnesses to be $(0,0)$ or $(1,0)$. Equip these witnesses with strict inverses and identity higher contractions. These are complete structures.

To distinguish them in the *space* of structures, restrict to the free fragment on $t,s,\eta,\varepsilon$ and the two triangle disks $T_t,T_s$. Groupoid completion takes its successive free disk attachments to homotopy cell attachments in spaces. In the cellular chains with coefficients in $\mathbb F_2$, each triangle attaching map traverses the unit and the counit once, whiskered by 1-cells. Hence

$$
             \partial T_t=\eta+\varepsilon=\partial T_s .
$$

Evaluation of the fundamental degree-three cohomology class of $B^3 C_2=K(C_2,3)$ on the cycle $T_t+T_s$ is the sum of the two triangle values because the lower-dimensional data of our maps are constant. The values are respectively zero and one. Even the restrictions of the two structures are not homotopic, so the full fibre is disconnected. In dimension at most two with only identities above it, recursion on the unit and counit forces them to be isomorphisms and the two triangles become equations. The usual unique isomorphism between right adjoints compatible with unit and counit makes the groupoid of adjoint-equivalence data over an equivalence contractible. This dimension bound is relative to the cell. For an arrow it means a hom genuinely truncated at dimension two. An $(\infty,2)$-category can itself be the space $K(C_2,3)$, so invertibility of all 3-cells alone does not give uniqueness. The same obstruction suspends for higher-dimensional roots. Coherent adjunction presentations explicitly impose a swallowtail relation involving the two triangle witnesses. [Araújo, presentation and Theorem 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09897)
