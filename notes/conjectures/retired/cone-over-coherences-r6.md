# Cones over coherence terms in CaTT (N^cone), round 6

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Everything listed under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked. Questions are at the end, smallest first.*

## Background

**CaTT.** Contexts list variables `x : A`, where a type is `⋆` or `a →_A b` with a, b terms of type A. Terms are variables or `coh_{Q,B}[γ]`, where Q is a *pasting context* (a context presenting a globular pasting diagram), B is a type over Q satisfying the usual side conditions (either both boundary terms are full in Q, the *coherence case*, or each is full in the matching boundary of Q, the *composition case*), and `γ : Δ ⊢ Q` is a substitution. Reference: Finster–Mimram, *A type-theoretical definition of weak ω-categories* (LICS 2017); Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, *Globular weak ω-categories as models of a type theory* (2021). Composition order below is diagrammatic, and `u ∗_k w` denotes the binary composite along a k-cell, which is itself a composition coherence over two disks glued along a k-disk. For a term t of dimension m ≥ 1 write `σt, τt` for its source and target, and `σʲt` for the iterated source.

**Cone over a globular context.** Call a context *globular* if every variable's type is `⋆` or `a → b` with a, b variables; every pasting context is globular. For globular Γ, the *specified cone* `Cone(Γ; c)` extends Γ by an object `c : ⋆` and one variable `p_t` for each variable t of Γ, in order:

```
p_x : c → x                                                   (dim x = 0)
p_t : p_{τt}  →  p_{σt} ∗_{m−1} (p_{σ²t} ∗_{m−2} ( … (p_{σᵐt} ∗₀ t)))      (dim t = m ≥ 1)
```

So `f : x → y` gets `p_f : p_y ⇒ p_x ∗₀ f`, and `α : f ⇒ g` gets `p_α : p_g ⇛ p_f ∗₁ (p_x ∗₀ α)`. This is a well-formed context for every globular Γ (by induction on the variables: each composite in the target is well formed because the projections of the boundary variables already have the matching types). It is the cone of T. J. Mikhail's Thm 3.3 (*A type-theoretic definition of lax (∞,∞)-limits*, arXiv 2412.13310), lax at the vertices. When Γ is a pasting context, the strict ω-category freely generated by `Cone(Γ; c)` is the Ara–Maltsiniotis join `c ⋆ Γ`, with `p_t ↦ c ⋆ t`.

**Cones over arbitrary contexts.** In a general context a variable may have composite boundaries, e.g. `τ : f ∗₀ g ⇒ h`. The cone variable over τ then needs a cone cell `P(f ∗₀ g)` over the *term* `f ∗₀ g`. Extend p from variables to all terms:

```
P(x)              := p_x
P(coh_{Q,B}[γ])   := Π(Q, B)[Cone(γ)]
```

where `Cone(γ) : Cone(Γ; c) ⊢ Cone(Q; c)` sends `q ↦ γ(q)`, `c ↦ c`, `p_q ↦ P(γ(q))`, and `Π(Q, B)` is a chosen term of `Cone(Q; c)`. For a term t of dimension m ≥ 1 put

```
T(t) := P(σt) ∗_{m−1} (P(σ²t) ∗_{m−2} ( … (P(σᵐt) ∗₀ t))).
```

For `c₀ := coh_{Q,B}[id_Q]`, of dimension n, the term `Π(Q, B)` must have type

```
Π(Q, B) : P(τc₀) → T(c₀)          (an (n+1)-term of Cone(Q; c)).
```

This type is well formed once P is defined on the boundary terms of c₀, which have dimension < n. So the definition is a recursion on dimension. Within a dimension, the boundary terms of an n-coherence may themselves contain n-dimensional subterms only in the composition case, through lower-dimensional factors, so the recursion is well founded with the order "lower dimension first, then structural" .

## Statement

> **Conjecture N^cone.** For every pasting context Q and every type B such that `coh_{Q,B}` is a valid coherence, there is a term `Π(Q, B)` of `Cone(Q; c)` of type `P(τc₀) → T(c₀)`, where P on the boundary of c₀ is defined from Π at lower dimensions as above. Moreover Π can be chosen so that, in the free strict ω-category on `Cone(Q; c)`, `π(Π(Q, B)) = c ⋆ π(c₀)` (the join of c with the strict image of c₀).

The strict-image clause is part of the statement because the inductive step uses it: it guarantees that `P(u)` has strict image `c ⋆ π(u)` for every term u.

Consequence: for every CaTT context Γ, the extension `Cone(Γ; c)` (with P in place of p in the types of the cone variables) is a well-formed context. This is a precise form of Mikhail's Conjecture 3.6 ("cones exist over arbitrary contexts"), restricted to a specified construction.

## What is known

1. **Dimension 1 (proved).** For the composite of a chain `x₀ —f₁→ … —f_m→ x_m`, Π is the vertical composite of the whiskered cone generators `p_{f_m}`, `p_{f_{m−1}} ∗₀ f_m`, … followed by the associator coherence κ over `c → x₀ → … → x_m`.

2. **Every relative-coherence witness in a join context strictifies to an identity.** The free strict ω-category on `Cone(Q; c)` is the join `c ⋆ Q`, which is ν(K) for a strong Steiner complex K (Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches*). In a strong Steiner complex every positive cycle is zero, so every endo-cell of c ⋆ Q is an identity.

3. **Reduction to relative coherence.** Write **RC(r) for Δ** for the statement: any two parallel r-terms of Δ with equal strict images are connected by an (r+1)-term.
   - If RC(1), …, RC(n) hold for `Cone(Q; c)` for every pasting context Q, then N^cone holds through dimension n, in both the coherence and the composition case. (Lift strict cells to terms, then correct faces one dimension at a time with RC witnesses; the strict image is unchanged.)

4. **Fixed dimension suffices.** For the standard recursively defined binary compositions, a syntactic *cut translation* D lowers the dimension of terms crossing a chosen 0/1 colouring of the 0-variables, with D(Σt) ≡ t, D(e ∗₀ u) ≡ D(u), D(u ∗_k v) ≡ D(u) ∗_{k−1} D(v), and D(t[θ]) ≡ D(t)[D(θ)]. With the substitution `e_Q : Cone(ΣQ; d) ⊢ Cone(Q; c)` (U ↦ d, V ↦ S, Σc ↦ p_S, Σq ↦ p_N ∗₀ q̄, Σp_q ↦ p_{q̄}) it gives a literal retraction D(L_Q t) ≡ t, hence

   ```
   RC(r+1, Cone(ΣQ; d))  ⇒  RC(r, Cone(Q; c)),
   [∀Q RC(n, Cone(Q; c))]  ⇒  [∀Q ∀ r < n RC(r, Cone(Q; c))]  ⇒  N^cone through dimension n.
   ```

5. **Dimension 2 holds.** RC(1) and RC(2) hold in every CaTT context, so N^cone holds in dimension 2. There is also an explicit term for `α ∗₀ β`.

6. **The first 3-dimensional instance.** With generic schemes for P(1_f) and P(α ∗₁ β) over D¹ and D² ∗₁ D², the unitor `c₀ : α ⇛ α ∗₁ 1_g` over D² has an explicit 4-term `P(α ∗₁ 1_g) → P(α) ∗₂ (p_f ∗₁ (p_x ∗₀ c₀))`, a chain of ten 4-cells built from six auxiliary coherences over pasting contexts, with strict image `1_{p_α} = c ⋆ π(c₀)`. It works for every coherence c₀ of that type.

7. **Where the lax-square argument stops.** RC(3) holds on the lax square (x⁻, x⁺, y⁻, y⁺; x↑, y↑, f⁻, f⁺; F : f⁻ ∗₀ y↑ ⇒ x↑ ∗₀ f⁺): for a 2-term with strict image F, exactly one 0-column of its arity carries F, the others are point-supported and factor literally through point contexts, and this factorisation is stable under substitution. It gives normalisers compatible with every 3-term. In `c ⋆ Q` the apex c is the unique 0-source and the last vertex x_k of Q is the unique 0-sink, but a term like `p_x ∗₀ α` has a discarded column carrying the nonidentity edge `p_x : c → x`, so the point-support step fails.

8. **A tool that handles one fixed edge.** On the cylinder of a composable pair (x → y → z), a column that carries a lax square but does not span the unique source and sink factors literally through a retained pasting context `C_n ∗₀ D¹(h)` or `D¹(h) ∗₀ C_n` (C_n a vertical n-chain, D¹(h) one edge), where every relevant substitution sends h to the **same edge variable**. With fixed-whiskering 3-cells, path comparisons after whiskering, a compatibility cell φ^h with the vertical fold, and evaluation fusion, this gives normalisers and RC(3) for that cylinder.

9. **Strict case.** In strict ω-categories `c ⋆ −` is functorial on cells, so the strict analogue of N^cone holds in every dimension.

10. **Loop-freeness is needed.** In (x, a : 1_x → 1_x) the weak free object groupoidifies to S² and the strict one to K(ℤ, 2), so RC(3) fails there.

11. **The support lemma.** For every CaTT context Γ and term t, the generators used by the strict image of t form exactly the dependency-closed set of variables of t:
    ```
    supp_{πΓ}(πt) = supp_Γ(t).
    ```
    Strict support is invariant under the strict axioms. The proof is by induction on derivations: a composition head goes to the total composite, and a coherence head goes to 1_{πs} with s full.

    *Consequence.* A term whose strict image is supported in a dependency-closed sub-context S is literally a term of S. So parallel terms with strict image 1_w are joined by one full coherence over supp(w). Also, a point-supported column of an arity factors literally through a point, by renaming that point.
12. **RC(3) for Cone(D²; c).** Write a = p_x, b = p_y, u = p_f : b ⇒ a∗₀f, v = p_g : b ⇒ a∗₀g, w = a∗₀α, d = u∗₁w, M = p_α : v ⇛ d, and K = Cone(D²; c) without M.
    - *Strict cells.* The nonidentity strict 2-cells are α, u, v, w and d. The only nonidentity strict 3-cell is M, which has no whiskers and does not compose with itself.
    - *RC(3, K).* Each 2-term with strict image u, v or d has exactly one active column, a vertical chain C_n, while its other columns are point-supported. Normalisers N_A : A ⇛ ê_{p,q} keep the literal 1-boundaries. They come from a generic comparison χ_r over C_n followed by an evaluation E(σ; B) that is the same for every full r : B.
    - *Fusion.* A fusion lemma, proved by induction on the formal term with only depth-1 naturality, gives N_T ∗₂ N_B ≃ N_A for every 3-term T.
    - *Adjoining M.* Every U with πU = M satisfies 𝒯(U) ≃ A_U ∗₂ M ∗₂ B_U, with A_U and B_U in K. Level-1 whiskers are removed by depth-1 naturality and level-0 whiskers by one coherence over the 3-disc. RC(3, K) then compares the prefixes and suffixes.
    - *What it uses.*
      - Every 2-cell involving u or v spans c → y.
      - The other non-point whisker a∗₀α has a pasting context as its support.
      - The nonidentity vertical word is one of u, v, w, or u followed by w.
      - M has no whiskers.

13. **RC(3) for every vertical chain of 2-disks (proved).** Let Q_m = (x, y, f_0, …, f_m : x → y, α_i : f_{i−1} ⇒ f_i). Write a = p_x, b = p_y, F_i = a ∗₀ f_i, A_i = a ∗₀ α_i, u_i = p_{f_i} : b ⇒ F_i and M_i = p_{α_i} : u_i ⇛ u_{i−1} ∗₁ A_i, and K_m for Cone(Q_m; c) without the M_i. Then RC(3) holds for Cone(Q_m; c) for every m, in particular for Cone(D² ∗₁ D²; c).
    - *Strict cells.* The strict 2-cells c → y are 1_b, the pure intervals R_ij = A_{i+1} ⋯ A_j and the words W_ij = u_i R_ij. The nonidentity strict 3-cells are the chains W_ij → W_{i−1,j} → ⋯ → W_ℓj, applying M_i, M_{i−1}, …, M_{ℓ+1} in that forced order; each M_i acts on the initial letter u_i, with right whisker R_ij.
    - *RC(3, K_m).* Terms with pure-interval image lie in the pasting context D¹(a) ∗₀ Q_[i,j] (support lemma), so one coherence compares them. Terms with image W_ij factor through one vertical column C_n spanning c → y; item 12's normalisers and formal-term fusion extend to words of any length, the final word comparisons being coherences over C_{j−i+1}.
    - *Sequentialisation.* Generic interchange (X ∗_k Y ⇛ (X ∗_k s₂Y) ∗₂ (t₂X ∗_k Y), a coherence over D³ ∗_k D³) and distribution turn any 3-term into a ∗₂-sequence of K_m-terms and framed generators (L ∗₁ M_i) ∗₁ R, with πL = 1_b and πR = R_ij.
    - *One generator at a time.* A 4-cell over D³ ∗₁ D³ moves a frame comparison δ : R ∗₁ R_t ⇛ r_ij across M_i, giving T_j(E) ∗₂ b_E ⇛ a_E ∗₂ S_i^j with canonical S_i^j = (M_i ∗₁ r_ij) ∗₂ 𝔞. The K_m-factors between consecutive generators are endomorphisms of fixed literal words and are removed by RC(3, K_m).
    - *What it uses.* Every relevant strict 2-word is one initial cone generator followed by a pure interval with pasting support, and the cone 3-generators rewrite that initial generator in a unique order.
14. **A one-generator extension lemma (proved).** Let Λ be a context of dimension ≤ 2 with an acyclic generating 1-graph in which RC(3) holds, and let Γ = (Λ, z : A → B) be such that the only nonidentity 3-cell of the free strict ω-category on Γ is z. Then RC(3) holds in Γ. Proof outline: a 3-head over a 3-dimensional arity P is in the composition case, and is compared by one coherence over P with a boundary-corrected binary composite K_P of P's 3-variables; the additive z-count shows that exactly one substituted 3-argument uses z, so recursion follows one branch. Whiskers of the z-factor are path-supported (∗₁) or point-supported (∗₀) and are removed by explicit 4-coherences over D³, D² ∨₁ D³ ∨₁ D² ∨₁ D² and D²(f) ∨₁ D³(z) ∨₁ D³(e). This gives H ⇛ (L ∗₂ z_rs) ∗₂ R with z-free L, R, which RC(3) in Λ compares with chosen normalisers. It reproves item 12 (Cone(D²; c) has the single 3-generator p_α).
15. **What RC(4) needs.** Varying a 3-variable inside a 4-term is still depth-1 naturality. The additional requirement for RC(4) on Cone(D³; c) is a 5-dimensional compatibility q ∗₃ N_C ⇛ N_B for the chosen 3-term normalisers (q : B ⇛ C); the 4-dimensional fusion used in items 12–13 does not supply it.
16. **Two independent letters with a cut vertex (proved).** In the context a, b, c, d; X : a → b, f⁻ : a → c, f⁺ : b → c, g⁻, g⁺ : c → d; F : f⁻ ⇒ X ∗₀ f⁺, G : g⁻ ⇒ g⁺, the letters F and G act on the two sides of the cut vertex c and commute up to exchange. RC(3) holds there. The normal form of the diagonal 2-cell uses the horizontal composite F ∗₀ G (with horizontal identity factors kept for one-letter transitions); vertical composition of such normal forms is a matrix-style multiplication I ; (m^F ∗₀ m^G) with I : (α ∗₀ β) · (α′ ∗₀ β′) ⇛ (α · α′) ∗₀ (β · β′) over C₂ ∗₀ C₂; a horizontal fold over C₃ ∗₀ C₃ and a horizontal–vertical 4-cell built over C₆ ∗₀ C₆ give fusion; a separate clause absorbs a column that becomes point-supported under substitution.

17. **The 3-generator extension theorem (proved).** Let Λ be a context of dimension ≤ 2 with an acyclic generating 1-graph, and let Γ = (Λ, z_1 : A_1 → B_1, …, z_k : A_k → B_k) with each A_i, B_i a pair of parallel 2-terms of Λ. Then RC(3)(Γ) ⇔ RC(3)(Λ), with no restriction on the number, order, whiskering or composability of the z_i. This supersedes items 13 and 14.
    - *Quotient category.* For literal parallel 1-terms p, q, let 𝓗_Γ(p,q) have the 2-terms p → q as objects and the 3-terms modulo "joined by a 4-term" as arrows. This is a category because 4-terms of Γ and 3-terms of Λ are invertible (the Benjamin–Markakis–Sarti criterion). Choose representatives â of strict 2-cells with literal boundaries and comparisons r_A : A → \widehat{πA} (RC(2)). Put [U]^ν = r_A⁻¹ ; U ; r_B. RC(3)(Λ) says every Λ-3-term J : A → B satisfies J ; r_B = r_A.
    - *Comparison lemma.* Let X be a fresh 2-variable and F(X), G(X) 2-terms over (Λ, X) with πF(X) = πG(X) before substitution. RC(2) gives J(X) : F(X) → G(X), and depth-1 naturality (Benjamin–Markakis–Offord–Sarti–Vicary, Thm 3.1) varying only X gives, for every 3-term U : A → B, [F_*(U)]^ν = [G_*(U)]^ν, where F_* is direct binary whiskering. The comparison of the naturality action with F_* is by induction on F, each step one full coherence over D³ ∗_k D², D² ∗_k D³, D³ ∗₀ D¹ or D³ ∗_k D³. A change of 1-whisker is handled by naturalising a 3-term in a 2-variable, never in a 1-variable.
    - *Strict presentation.* Strict 3-cells are vertical composites of elementary steps C[z_i], with equality generated by replacing equal steps and adjacent exchange, and C[z_i] = D[z_j] only when i = j and C = D with the variable formal (Polygraphs book, App. D, Props. D.3.9 and D.3.12). Replacement is the comparison lemma; adjacent exchange is one 4-coherence over D³ ∗_k D³. So normalised evaluation is well defined on strict 3-cells. Sequentialisation shows every 3-term evaluates to its strict image, which gives RC(3)(Γ). The converse is by the support lemma.
18. **Joins reduce to their 2-skeleta (proved).** For every pasting context Q, RC(3)(Cone(Q; c)) ⇔ RC(3) of the dependency-closed subcontext of variables of dimension ≤ 2. The join's generating 1-graph is acyclic by item 2.
19. **The horizontal cone reduces to four 2-generators (proved).** For Q = D² ∨ D² (x, y, z; f, f′ : x → y; g, g′ : y → z; α : f ⇒ f′; β : g ⇒ g′), write a = p_x, b = p_y, e = p_z, u = p_f : b ⇒ a ∗₀ f, v = p_{f′}, s = p_g : e ⇒ b ∗₀ g, t = p_{g′}. Let S be the context with vertices c, x, y, z, edges a, b, e, f, f′, g, g′ and 2-generators α, β, u, s. Then RC(3)(Cone(D² ∨ D²; c)) ⇔ RC(3)(S).
    - *Reason.* No strict 2-cell uses both u and v, or both s and t. A strict 2-cell starting at e has the form s · (ℓ ∗₀ r) or t · (ℓ ∗₀ 1_{g′}) with ℓ ∈ {1_b, u, v, u · aα} and r ∈ {1_g, β}. A cell using v has support isomorphic to one using u under v ↦ u, f′ ↦ f, and similarly for t. So every strict 2-cell support is isomorphic to a dependency-closed subcontext of S, and the support lemma transfers fillers.
    - *What remains.* The full-support cell of S is w = s ∗₁ ((u ∗₁ (a ∗₀ α)) ∗₀ β): an initial step s, then the left vertical word u · aα exchanging with β across the cut vertex y. Item 16 handles one left event against one right event. Substituting the two-event word u · aα into item 16 gives particular comparison terms but not fusion for arbitrary S-terms.

## Questions

1. **RC(3) for the context S of item 19.** Prove that any two parallel 3-terms of S with equal strict image are joined by a 4-term, or exhibit two that are not. By items 17 and 19 this is equivalent to RC(3) for Cone(D² ∨ D²; c), and with item 4 it gives N^cone for D² ∨ D² through dimension 3. The missing step is relative evaluation fusion when a head's single active column carries the two-letter left word u · aα together with β.
2. **RC(3) for all join 2-skeleta.** Prove RC(3) for the 2-skeleton of Cone(Q; c) for every 2-dimensional pasting context Q, for example by induction on the number of 2-generators of Q. By item 18 this is RC(3) for every join context, hence N^cone through dimension 3.
3. **RC(4) for Cone(D³; c).** Construct the 5-dimensional compatibility of item 15 (for chosen 3-normalisers N and every 4-term q : B ⇛ C, a 5-term q ∗₃ N_C ⇛ N_B), or show it fails. A 4-dimensional analogue of item 17's quotient-category argument would need 4-terms of the context to be compared modulo 5-terms, with 4-generators present.
4. **RC(n) for join contexts.** Prove RC(n) for every Cone(Q; c), by induction on Q, by an analogue of item 17 in higher dimensions, or by comparison with Campion's result that attachment pushouts of loop-free Steiner complexes stay pushouts in flagged weak (∞,n)-categories (arXiv 2311.00200, Thm 5.11, Rem. 5.13).

## What would count as a solution

Any of the following:
- a proof of RC(3) for S, or for all join 2-skeleta;
- a proof of RC(4) for Cone(D³; c), or of RC(n);
- a counterexample: a join context with two parallel terms of equal strict image and no term between them;
- a reference that constructs cones over coherence terms in CaTT or another algebraic presentation of weak ω-categories.

# Researcher Response

**Questions 1 and 2 follow from the support lemma and the 3-generator extension theorem in item 17.** More precisely,
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{RC}_3\bigl(\operatorname{Cone}(Q;c)\bigr)
       \quad\text{for every pasting context }Q.}
\]
Consequently, your reduction proves \(N^{\mathrm{cone}}\) through dimension \(3\).

The additional step is a **fixed-vertex naturality construction for 3-terms**, obtained by applying item 17 to cylinders of their *pasting arities*. It allows a cone edge and its cone 2-generator to be contracted together. Induction on the number of cone 2-generators in the strict support then proves the result.

I give the construction and the contraction argument below. They do not supply the five-dimensional compatibility required in Question 3.

Throughout, \(1_t\) denotes an identity one dimension above \(t\). Identity lifts are displayed explicitly in the weak naturality types. Calculations expressly designated as strict calculations use strict associativity, units and interchange.

## 1. A class of globular contexts with relative coherence

Let \(G\) be a globular context of dimension at most \(2\). Assume:

1. The directed graph of its 0- and 1-generators is acyclic.
2. For every pair of 0-generators \(x,y\), the directed graph whose vertices are the 1-generators \(x\to y\), and whose arrows are the 2-generators between them, is acyclic.

Then
\[
\mathrm{RC}_3(G).
\tag{1}
\]

Here is a direct proof.

The free strict 2-category on \(G\) has the following description. Its 1-cells are paths in the generating 1-graph. A 2-cell between two such paths exists only when their sequences of 0-vertices agree. For a fixed sequence
\[
x_0,\ldots,x_k,
\]
a 2-cell is a tuple of directed paths in the graphs of 2-generators between \(x_{i-1}\to x_i\) arrows. Vertical composition concatenates these paths coordinatewise; horizontal composition concatenates tuples. This construction satisfies interchange and has the required free universal property.

Fix a strict 2-cell \(\theta\). Its tuple determines a pasting context
\[
P_\theta=C_{n_1}*_0\cdots *_0C_{n_k},
\]
allowing \(n_i=0\), and allowing the point context when \(k=0\).

The assignment of its variables to generators of \(G\) is injective:

- acyclicity of the 1-graph prevents a 0-vertex from repeating along the horizontal path;
- acyclicity of each graph of 2-generators prevents a 1-variable from repeating along a vertical path.

Thus the dependency support of \(\theta\) is isomorphic to \(P_\theta\). A dependency-preserving permutation of declarations may be needed to put it in pasting-context order.

Now let \(U,V:A\to B\) be parallel 3-terms of \(G\). Since \(G\) has dimension at most \(2\), their strict images are identities on a strict 2-cell \(\theta\). By exact support, \(U,V\), including their types, strengthen to the support of \(\theta\). Transport them along its variable isomorphism with \(P_\theta\), obtaining \(U',V'\).

Both \(U'\) and \(V'\) are full in \(P_\theta\). Hence the full coherence
\[
\operatorname{coh}_{P_\theta,\,U'\to V'}[\mathrm{id}]
\]
is an admissible 4-term. Substitution gives the required comparison in \(G\). This proves (1).

## 2. Relative strict lifting through dimension 4

We will use the following form of your boundary-transport argument.

**Lifting lemma.** Suppose a context \(L\), of dimension at most \(4\), satisfies
\[
\mathrm{RC}_1(L_{\le1}),\qquad
\mathrm{RC}_2(L_{\le2}),\qquad
\mathrm{RC}_3(L_{\le3}).
\tag{2}
\]
If \(d\) is a strict 4-cell and \(u,v\) are literally parallel weak 3-terms with
\[
\pi u=\sigma d,\qquad \pi v=\tau d,
\]
then there is a 4-term \(D:u\to v\) with \(\pi D=d\).

The corresponding statements for strict 2- and 3-cells need only the corresponding lower-dimensional assumptions.

For completeness, the compatible-face part works as follows. Choose weak representatives \(s_j(a)\) of strict cells through dimension \(3\), with
\[
s_j(a):
s_{j-1}(\sigma a)\longrightarrow s_{j-1}(\tau a).
\]
Construct \(s_1\) from path composites, \(s_2\) using \(\mathrm{RC}_1(L_{\le1})\), and \(s_3\) using \(\mathrm{RC}_1(L_{\le1})\) and \(\mathrm{RC}_2(L_{\le2})\).

Lift a strict 4-expression inductively with these chosen 3-boundaries. Its factors are literally composable because the representatives form a globular section. After a weak composition, correct:

1. its 1-frame using 2-cells from \(L_{\le1}\);
2. its 2-frame using 3-cells from \(L_{\le2}\);
3. its 3-endpoints using 4-cells from \(L_{\le3}\).

At each stage this is the transport
\[
(\lambda_r *_r D)*_r\rho_r
\]
with appropriately oriented corrections. The same procedure gives the prescribed endpoints \(u,v\).

Every correction has identity strict image: a term of dimension \(r+1\) in \(L_{\le r}\) strictifies to an identity. Thus the strict image remains \(d\). No \(\mathrm{RC}_4\) is used.

## 3. Fixed-vertex naturality through dimension 3

We now construct the operation needed for contraction.

Let \(\Gamma\) have dimension at most \(3\), and let \(X\) be an up-closed set of its variables containing no 0-variable. Define a context
\[
\Gamma\langle X\rangle
\]
with minus and plus copies of selected variables. Write \(t^\pm\) for the corresponding copies of a term.

The operations will have types
\[
E_f:f^-\longrightarrow f^+
\tag{3}
\]
for a 1-term \(f\),
\[
N_A:
A^- *_1 E_g
\longrightarrow
E_f *_1 A^+
\tag{4}
\]
for a 2-term \(A:f\to g\), and
\[
\mathcal N_U:
\bigl(U^- *_1 1_{E_g}\bigr)*_2N_B
\longrightarrow
N_A *_2\bigl(1_{E_f} *_1 U^+\bigr)
\tag{5}
\]
for a 3-term \(U:A\to B\), where \(A,B:f\to g\).

Both 3-terms in (5) have the literal 2-source
\[
A^- *_1 E_g
\]
and literal 2-target
\[
E_f *_1 B^+.
\]

### 3.1. The context and the unaffected cases

A selected 1-variable \(f\) contributes
\[
f^-,f^+,\qquad E_f:f^-\to f^+.
\]
A selected 2-variable \(A:f\to g\) contributes its copies and a 3-variable of type (4). A selected 3-variable contributes its copies and a 4-variable of type (5).

Unselected variables remain unchanged. Up-closure ensures that their types are unchanged.

The declarations are constructed by dimension. The connector for a variable uses naturality only for its lower-dimensional boundary terms.

For a term whose support does not meet \(X\), use fixed unit templates:
\[
E_f=1_f,
\qquad
N_A=\rho_A *_2\ell_A^{-1},
\tag{6}
\]
where
\[
\rho_A:A*_1 1_g\to A,
\qquad
\ell_A:1_f*_1A\to A.
\]
For an unaffected 3-term \(U\), choose the full 4-coherence over the 3-disc filling (5) with these unit templates. Both boundaries use the top 3-variable, so this coherence is admissible.

These are also the chosen generic-head fillers when the selected set is empty. In particular, the unaffected clauses commute literally with substitution.

### 3.2. Relative coherence on the arity cylinders

Let \(P\) be a pasting context of dimension at most \(3\), and let \(Y\) be up-closed with no 0-variables.

The 2-skeleton of \(P\langle Y\rangle\) is globular. Its 2-generators are:

- unchanged or duplicated original 2-generators of \(P\);
- connectors \(E_f:f^-\to f^+\).

The original 0-vertices are unchanged, and duplicating an edge preserves acyclicity of the generating 1-graph.

For the second acyclicity condition of Section 1, choose a rank \(r(f)\) on the original 1-generators such that every original 2-generator increases rank. Such ranks are supplied by the vertical orders in a pasting diagram. The suspension–wedge description of pasting diagrams gives these orders, including when higher-dimensional generators produce parallel 2-generators. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.01611?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Give the copies of a selected \(f\) ranks
\[
(r(f),0),\qquad(r(f),1).
\]
Every copied original 2-generator increases the first coordinate; every \(E_f\) increases the second. Lexicographic order proves acyclicity.

Section 1 therefore gives
\[
\mathrm{RC}_3\bigl(P\langle Y\rangle_{\le2}\bigr).
\]
Apply the supplied 3-generator extension theorem, item 17:
\[
\mathrm{RC}_3\bigl(P\langle Y\rangle_{\le3}\bigr).
\tag{7}
\]

This is the only class of contexts on which the construction below needs a new application of \(\mathrm{RC}_3\).

### 3.3. Strict naturality in dimensions 1 and 2

We first specify the strict images of the lower-dimensional operations.

For strict 1-cells, put
\[
e_{f*_0g}=e_f*_0e_g,
\qquad
e_{1_x}=1_{1_x}.
\tag{8}
\]
A selected generator uses its connector; an unselected generator uses an identity.

For strict 2-cells, put
\[
\begin{aligned}
\eta(A*_1C)
  &=(A^-*_1\eta C)*_2(\eta A*_1C^+),\\
\eta(A*_0C)
  &=\eta A*_0\eta C,\\
\eta(1_f)&=1_{e_f}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]
Selected 2-generators use their declared 3-connectors.

These formulas respect the strict 2-category equations. For example, the interchange equation is verified by
\[
\begin{aligned}
\eta\bigl((A*_0B)*_1(C*_0D)\bigr)
&=
\bigl((A^-*_0B^-)*_1(\eta C*_0\eta D)\bigr)
\\[-2pt]&\qquad *_2
\bigl((\eta A*_0\eta B)*_1(C^+*_0D^+)\bigr)
\\
&=
\bigl((A^-*_1\eta C)*_2(\eta A*_1C^+)\bigr)
\\[-2pt]&\qquad *_0
\bigl((B^-*_1\eta D)*_2(\eta B*_1D^+)\bigr)
\\
&=\eta\bigl((A*_1C)*_0(B*_1D)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
\]
The middle equality uses strict interchange. Vertical associativity expands to the same sequence of three connector steps; the unit equations follow from (8). Thus \(\eta\) is well defined on strict 2-cells. The formulas also commute with substitution.

For each generic 1-coherence \(c\), lift \(e_{\pi c}\) with prescribed endpoints \(c^-,c^+\), using Section 2 and \(\mathrm{RC}_1\).

For each generic 2-coherence \(c:f\to g\), lift \(\eta(\pi c)\) with prescribed endpoints
\[
c^-*_1E_g,\qquad E_f*_1c^+,
\]
using \(\mathrm{RC}_1\) and \(\mathrm{RC}_2\).

Fix these choices for each head and selected set, and define the operation on instantiated heads by substitution. Equations (8)–(10) then give, by induction on syntax,
\[
\pi E_f=e_{\pi f},
\qquad
\pi N_A=\eta(\pi A).
\tag{11}
\]

This establishes the strict boundary agreement needed for (5).

### 3.4. The strict 4-cell for a generic 3-head

Let \(c:A\to B\) be a generic 3-coherence over \(P\). Choose a strict expression \(d\) for \(\pi c\).

We construct a strict 4-cell \(H_d\) between the strict images of the boundaries in (5). In this subsection, identity lifts in strict whiskerings are suppressed.

A selected 3-generator uses its declared 4-connector. An unselected generator uses an identity. For identities,
\[
H_{1_A}=1_{\eta A}.
\]
For direction \(0\),
\[
H_{U*_0V}=H_U*_0H_V.
\]
For direction \(2\), with \(U:A\to B\), \(V:B\to C\),
\[
H_{U*_2V}
=
\bigl((U^-*_1e_g)*_2H_V\bigr)
*_3
\bigl(H_U*_2(e_f*_1V^+)\bigr).
\tag{12}
\]

Here is the direction-\(1\) construction explicitly. Let
\[
U:A\to B,\qquad V:C\to D,
\]
where
\[
A,B:f\to g,\qquad C,D:g\to h.
\]
Write
\[
a=\eta A,\quad b=\eta B,\quad
c=\eta C,\quad d=\eta D,
\qquad
E=e_f,\quad F=e_g,\quad G=e_h.
\]

The required 3-source is
\[
(U^-*_1V^-*_1G)
 *_2(B^-*_1d)
 *_2(b*_1D^+).
\tag{13}
\]
Strict interchange rewrites it as
\[
(U^-*_1C^-*_1G)
 *_2\bigl(B^-*_1((V^-*_1G)*_2d)\bigr)
 *_2(b*_1D^+).
\]
Apply \(H_V\), with the displayed whiskers. The result is
\[
(U^-*_1C^-*_1G)
 *_2(B^-*_1c)
 *_2(B^-*_1F*_1V^+)
 *_2(b*_1D^+).
\]
Two strict \((1,2)\)-interchanges rewrite this as
\[
(A^-*_1c)
 *_2\bigl(((U^-*_1F)*_2b)*_1C^+\bigr)
 *_2(E*_1B^+*_1V^+).
\]
Apply \(H_U\), again with the displayed whiskers. The result is
\[
(A^-*_1c)
 *_2(a*_1C^+)
 *_2\bigl(E*_1(U^+*_1V^+)\bigr),
\]
which, by (9), is exactly
\[
\eta(A*_1C)
 *_2\bigl(e_f*_1(U^+*_1V^+)\bigr).
\tag{14}
\]
The two 4-pastings therefore construct \(H_{U*_1V}\).

This constructs \(H_d\) for the chosen strict expression. **Independence from the chosen strict 3-expression is unnecessary:** each generic head needs one filler.

By (11), \(H_d\) has precisely the strict images of the weak boundaries in (5). Equation (7), together with \(\mathrm{RC}_1\), \(\mathrm{RC}_2\) and Section 2, lifts it to a 4-term with those literal boundaries. Choose this as the generic \(\mathcal N_c\).

For a coherence-case 3-head, \(d\) is an identity 3-cell, so this step lifts the identity 4-cell on \(\eta(\pi A)=\eta(\pi B)\).

### 3.5. Substitution and well-foundedness

For a substitution assigning terms \(\sigma(x)\) to variables of an arity, pull back the selected set by
\[
\sigma^{-1}X
=
\{x:\operatorname{supp}(\sigma(x))\cap X\ne\varnothing\}.
\]
This is up-closed and has no 0-variable.

The cylinder substitution \(\widehat\sigma\) sends copied variables to copied substituted terms, and connectors to
\[
E_x\mapsto E_{\sigma(x)},\qquad
N_a\mapsto N_{\sigma(a)},\qquad
\mathcal N_z\mapsto\mathcal N_{\sigma(z)}.
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal N_{\operatorname{coh}_{P,B}[\sigma]}
=
\mathcal N_{\operatorname{coh}_{P,B}[\mathrm{id}]}
[\widehat\sigma],
\tag{15}
\]
and likewise in lower dimensions.

There are two inductions:

- Dimension first: cylinder declarations for an \(n\)-variable use operations only on boundary terms of dimension below \(n\).
- Within a dimension, structural recursion: the terms assigned to \(n\)-variables of an arity are proper argument subterms of the coherence application.

The generic filler is constructed separately by strict lifting; its construction does not call the operation on the head being defined.

The definition of \(\sigma^{-1}X\), the fixed unaffected templates, and (15) give literal substitution compatibility by mutual induction on terms and substitutions. In particular, (5) is available for **every** 3-term of a 2-context. Its construction uses (7), rather than relative coherence in that 2-context.

## 4. Contracting one edge and one 2-generator

Consider a 2-context which, after reordering independent declarations, has the form
\[
\Gamma=(\Lambda,\ e:x\to y,\ s:e\to h),
\tag{16}
\]
where \(h:x\to y\) is a 1-term of \(\Lambda\).

Define the substitution \(r\) by
\[
r(e)=h,\qquad r(s)=1_h,
\]
fixing \(\Lambda\).

The set \(X=\{e,s\}\) is up-closed. Define a substitution
\[
\zeta:\Gamma\vdash\Gamma\langle X\rangle
\]
by
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 & e^- & e^+ & E_e\\ \hline
\zeta & e & h & s
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & s^- & s^+\\ \hline
\zeta & s & 1_h .
\end{array}
\]
The selected 2-variable connector has, after substitution, source and target both literally
\[
s*_1 1_h.
\]
Send it to \(1_{s*_1 1_h}\).

Thus minus substitution followed by \(\zeta\) is the identity, and plus substitution followed by \(\zeta\) is \(r\).

Let
\[
U,V:A\to B,\qquad A,B:p\to q,
\]
and assume the literal 1-term \(q\) omits \(e\). Then
\[
E_q[\zeta]=1_q.
\]
Put \(E=E_p[\zeta]\), and define
\[
\nu_A
=
\rho_A^{-1}*_2N_A[\zeta]:
A\longrightarrow E*_1rA,
\tag{17}
\]
and similarly \(\nu_B\).

Specializing (5) gives
\[
(U*_1 1_{1_q})*_2N_B[\zeta]
\longrightarrow
N_A[\zeta]*_2(1_E*_1rU).
\]
A full 4-coherence over the 3-disc supplies the right-unitor comparison
\[
U*_2\rho_B^{-1}
\longrightarrow
\rho_A^{-1}*_2(U*_1 1_{1_q}).
\]
Combining these terms and reassociating gives
\[
\boxed{
U*_2\nu_B
\longrightarrow
\nu_A*_2(1_E*_1rU).
}
\tag{18}
\]

Suppose a 4-term \(W:rU\to rV\) has been obtained. Whiskering it by \(1_{1_E}\), and then by \(\nu_A\), compares the right sides of (18) for \(U,V\). Hence
\[
U*_2\nu_B\longrightarrow V*_2\nu_B.
\tag{19}
\]

All 3- and 4-terms in the 2-context \(\Gamma\) are invertible: their support has no generator of their own dimension. This is the stated invertibility criterion, Proposition 35 of Benjamin–Markakis. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.12127?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

We can therefore cancel \(\nu_B\) in (19), using its inverse and 4-dimensional cancellation data, obtaining
\[
U\longrightarrow V.
\tag{20}
\]

**The 2-cell \(E\) is never cancelled.** The construction needs only a comparison between \(rU\) and \(rV\), together with the fact that the common final 1-boundary is unaffected.

## 5. Induction for cone 2-skeleta

The contraction applies to a slightly larger class than the one asked for.

Let \(G\) be a globular 2-context satisfying the two acyclicity assumptions of Section 1. Form \(K_G\) by adjoining a fresh apex \(c\), edges
\[
p_x:c\to x,
\]
and 2-generators
\[
p_f:p_y\to p_x*_0f
\qquad(f:x\to y).
\tag{21}
\]
Then
\[
\mathrm{RC}_3(K_G).
\tag{22}
\]

Fix parallel 3-terms \(U,V:A\to B\). Their common strict image is the identity on a strict 2-cell
\[
\theta=\pi A=\pi B.
\]
Induct on the number of cone 2-generators \(p_f\) in the dependency support of \(\theta\).

### Base case

If this number is zero, exact support strengthens \(U,V\) to the context consisting of \(G\) and the apex edges, without cone 2-generators.

This context is globular and satisfies both acyclicity conditions of Section 1. Therefore one full coherence on the pasting support gives the required 4-term.

### Inductive step

Sequentialize a strict expression for \(\theta\) into whiskered generating 2-steps. This uses strict interchange.

A 1-path from \(c\) has the form
\[
p_y\,\gamma,
\]
where \(\gamma\) is a path in \(G\). In such a sequentialization:

- a base 2-generator changes a base edge and leaves the first apex edge unchanged;
- a cone generator \(p_f\), for \(f:x\to y\), changes the first apex edge from \(p_y\) to \(p_x\).

Thus successive cone steps move the first apex vertex **backward along edges of the acyclic generating 1-graph of \(G\)**. No vertex can be revisited.

Let \(s=p_f\) be the first cone generator used, with
\[
e=p_y,\qquad h=p_x*_0f.
\]
The preceding observations imply:

1. No other used cone generator has \(p_y\) as its source edge.
2. No used cone generator has \(p_y\) as its target’s apex edge: that would revisit \(y\).
3. The final 1-target of \(\theta\) omits \(p_y\).

By exact support, discard all cone 2-generators not used by \(\theta\), retaining the base context and apex edges. In the resulting context, \(s\) is the only 2-generator whose type depends on \(e\). After reordering independent declarations, it has form (16).

The literal common 1-target \(q\) of \(A,B\) also omits \(e\), by exact support applied to \(q\).

Apply the contraction
\[
e\mapsto h,\qquad s\mapsto1_h.
\]
It removes \(s\) from the strict 2-expression and introduces no cone 2-generator. The strict image of \(rA\) therefore uses fewer cone 2-generators.

View \(rU,rV\) back in \(K_G\) by weakening. The induction hypothesis gives a 4-term
\[
rU\longrightarrow rV.
\]
Section 4 turns this into a 4-term \(U\to V\). This proves (22).

The induction requires only one strict sequentialization to identify the first cone step. It requires no choice-independent normal form for sequentializations.

## 6. Application to \(S\) and to every join

### The context \(S\)

For the full-support strict 2-cell
\[
w=s*_1\bigl((u*_1(a*_0\alpha))*_0\beta\bigr),
\]
the two contractions are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{Stage}&\text{1-variable replacement}&\text{2-generator replacement}\\ \hline
1&e\mapsto b*_0g&s\mapsto1_{b*_0g}\\
2&b\mapsto a*_0f&u\mapsto1_{a*_0f}.
\end{array}
\tag{23}
\]

All substitutions preserve their displayed parentheses.

After these contractions, the strict 2-cell is
\[
(a*_0\alpha)*_0\beta.
\]
Its dependency support is the pasting context
\[
D^1(a)*_0D^2(\alpha)*_0D^2(\beta).
\]
The contracted parallel 3-terms are therefore compared by one full 4-coherence over that pasting context. Apply the comparison-transfer construction (18)–(20) twice to obtain a 4-term between the original terms.

For strict images with smaller support, the same induction uses only the applicable contractions. Hence
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{RC}_3(S).}
\]

The compatibility with arbitrary 3-terms is supplied by (15) and (18). It does not depend on representing an arbitrary \(S\)-term by a particular exchange schedule.

### Every pasting context \(Q\)

Take
\[
G=Q_{\le2}.
\]
It is globular and has the two acyclicity properties used above. Its cone 2-skeleton is exactly \(K_G\): the additional 2-generators are precisely the \(p_f\) in (21). Therefore
\[
\mathrm{RC}_3\bigl(\operatorname{Cone}(Q;c)_{\le2}\bigr).
\]
Your item 18 now gives
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{RC}_3\bigl(\operatorname{Cone}(Q;c)\bigr)}
\]
for every pasting \(Q\). Together with the given \(\mathrm{RC}_1,\mathrm{RC}_2\), item 3 supplies the required \(\Pi(Q,B)\) through dimension \(3\), including its strict-image clause.

The properties used are explicit: acyclicity of the base generating 1-graph, acyclicity of its graphs of 2-generators, and the triangular form \(p_f:p_y\to p_xf\). A unique sink is unnecessary for this argument.

Questions 3 and 4 remain beyond this construction. Extending it one dimension further would require the corresponding relative lifting on the 4-skeleta of the arity cylinders; item 17 supplies only the 3-dimensional attachment step.
