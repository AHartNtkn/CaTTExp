# Canonical CaTT terms for the cells of Gray tensors of pasting diagrams: general pairs and iterated tensors

*A self-contained problem. It assumes familiarity with the type theory CaTT, strict ω-categories and Steiner's directed complexes, and nothing else. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked.*

**What we are asking for.** The most general theorem: a construction for **all** pairs and all finite families of globular pasting diagrams, compatible with associativity, or a proof that none exists for some family together with the equations such a counterexample forces. The cylinders I ⊗ P (items 6–8) are settled. Simultaneous suspension is now settled too: CT(P ⊗ I ⊗ Q) ⇒ CT(ΣP ⊗ ΣQ) for all P and Q (items 10–12). So CT for all pairs reduces to CT for the **three-factor** tensors P ⊗ I ⊗ Q, and what is asked is the iterated case. The question is purely syntactic: whether chosen terms can match their boundaries on the nose in a theory with no definitional equalities.

## 1. Setting

**CaTT** (Finster–Mimram, LICS 2017, arXiv 1706.02866). Types are `⋆` or `u →_A v`. Terms are variables or `coh_{P, u →_A v}[σ]`, where P is a context presenting a globular pasting diagram, σ is a substitution into P, and either (coherence) `Var(u : A) = Var(P) = Var(v : A)`, or (composition) `Var(u : A) = Var(∂⁻P)` and `Var(v : A) = Var(∂⁺P)`. The type of `coh_{P, u→_A v}[σ]` is `u[σ] →_{A[σ]} v[σ]`. **Definitional equality is syntactic identity.** A context is well formed when, for each variable `x : u →_A v`, the terms u and v have type A syntactically.

**Gray tensors.** For globular pasting diagrams Θ, Δ, let λΘ, λΔ be their Steiner complexes and ν(λΘ ⊗ λΔ) the strict ω-category of the tensor (Steiner, *Omega-categories and chain complexes*, HHA 2004). Its generators are the pairs y ⊗ x of cells, of dimension dim y + dim x, with boundary given by the Leibniz formula. Its cells are molecules (Hadzihasanovic, *Combinatorics of higher-categorical diagrams*, arXiv 2404.07273) and are determined by their sets of generators.

**The family context.** Fam(Θ ⊗ Δ) has one variable v_g per generator g, typed `T(∂⁻g) → T(∂⁺g)`, where T(c) is a CaTT term over the earlier variables denoting the cell c.

## 2. Statement

> **Conjecture CT.** For all finite globular pasting diagrams Θ, Δ there is an assignment T from cells c of ν(λΘ ⊗ λΔ) of dimension ≥ 1 to CaTT terms over Fam(Θ ⊗ Δ) such that
> - (a) T(g) = v_g for every generator g;
> - (b) T(c) is built from the variables by coh terms, and its strict interpretation (v_g ↦ g) is c;
> - (c) `T(c) : T(∂⁻c) → T(∂⁺c)` holds **syntactically** for every cell c. In particular Fam(Θ ⊗ Δ) is well formed.

The same question for iterated tensors λΘ₁ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ λΘ_k.

## 3. What is known

1. **Low dimensions.** For Θ, Δ of dimension ≤ 1, binary composites can be chosen consistently by hand. CT holds for D² ⊗ D¹.
2. **Layered terms fail for D¹ ⊗ D¹ ⊗ D¹.** "Layered" means T(c) is the vertical composite, along a layering of the molecule c, of whiskered generators. The source hexagon of the top cell has a unique layering whose first two layers are a square whiskered on the right and a square whiskered on the left, meeting at a 3-edge path. The first layer's target contains a subterm denoting the 2-edge path bc, and no subterm of the second layer's source denotes bc (in CaTT every term uses exactly the variables of its strict image). So the two sides bracket the 3-edge path differently and (c) fails. Non-layered terms are not ruled out.
3. **The weak form holds through dimension 4.** With (c′) — T(c) has a type u → v with u, v connected to T(∂⁻c), T(∂⁺c) by invertible coherence cells, inserted into the variables' types — CT holds for generators of dimension ≤ 4.
4. **Single coherence heads do not avoid the problem directly.** Taking T(c) to be one composition coherence coh_{P, u→v}[σ] requires T(∂^±c) to be literally u[σ], v[σ]; this fails when the boundary is a composite of lower cells that are not among σ's arguments.
5. **Semantics is settled.** The (∞,ω) Gray tensor of pasting diagrams is the free (∞,ω)-category on the Steiner tensor (Campion, arXiv 2311.00205, Cor. 3.5; Chanavat, arXiv 2603.22242, Thm 3.29). CT is only about syntax.
6. **Suspension closure (all dimensions).** With I = D¹ and P a finite globular pasting diagram, CT(I ⊗ P) ⇒ CT(I ⊗ ΣP). Proof: the hom (I ⊗ ΣP)(A, D) between the two extreme vertices is strictly isomorphic to I ⊗ P (checked on chains, f_r bijective on bases for r > 0, degree 0 giving the two kinds of A → D paths); every other hom is globular. Whiskerings R_r, L_r over D^r ∨₀ D¹ and D¹ ∨₀ D^r with recursively chosen types satisfy the composition-case support condition. In a globular pasting diagram every positive-dimensional generator has generators as source and target, so the endpoint declarations of Fam(I ⊗ P) have variable boundaries; a context Γ′ with a substitution σ : Γ′ → Σ_{A,D} Fam(I ⊗ P) then declares the mixed variables by σ-substituted suspended types, and T′(c) = (ΣT(c̄))[σ] satisfies (c) by stability of typing under suspension and substitution. Consequences: CT holds for I ⊗ Dⁿ, Dⁿ ⊗ I (via the odd-dimensional opposite) and I ⊗ Σ^r[m], for every n, r, m.
7. **Wedge closure for cylinders (all dimensions).** For finite globular P, Q: CT(I ⊗ P) ∧ CT(I ⊗ Q) ⇒ CT(I ⊗ (P ∨ Q)). Write z for the wedge point, u : z₀ → z₁ for the seam, a ∈ P₀∖{z}, b ∈ Q₀∖{z}, U = P(a,z), V = Q(z,b), L = (I⊗P)(a₀,z₁), R = (I⊗Q)(z₀,b₁).
   - *Strict part.* i : U → L (p ↦ p₀ # u) is a full sieve and j : V → R (q ↦ u # q₁) a full cosieve. This follows from the sign test: a nonzero positive chain of positive degree in a strongly loop-free unital basis has a differential with both signs.
   - The crossing hom (I⊗(P∨Q))(a₀,b₁) is the full sub-ω-category of L × R on the vertices (l, r) with l ∈ iU₀ or r ∈ jV₀. On Steiner tableaux the inverse is l + r − E_u, with E_u the tableau of u.
   - Every crossing cell from (ip, r) to (l, jq) factors strictly as (1_p, β) # (α, 1_q) through d = (p, q). Crucially, d depends only on the cell's 0-endpoints, so it is unchanged under higher faces.
   - *Terms.* Horizontal composites of the given terms on X = L × V and Y = U × R. One 2-coherence γ_d : S_Y(d) → S_X(d) per vertex d of U × V, over the generic path context, with identity image. Composition heads A_m (∗₀), V_m (∗₁) and W_m (∗₁ with a 2-term) whose types are prescribed recursively, so that ∂A_m(a,b) = A_{m−1}(∂a, ∂b) etc. literally.
   - The four endpoint cases are assigned S_X, S_Y, W_m(S_Y(h), γ_d) and V_m(W_m(S_Y(h_Y), γ_d), S_X(h_X)). Literal boundary agreement then holds in every dimension.
   With item 6 and the Batanin-tree decomposition P = ⋁ ΣP_j, this gives **CT(I ⊗ P) and CT(P ⊗ I) for every finite globular P**.
8. **The three-interval cube.** CT holds for I ⊗ I ⊗ I, the same terms serving both parenthesizations. Choose right-associated composites for paths. Declare each square with its two path terms. For a 2-cell (a sequence of adjacent swaps), whisker each square and pre- and post-compose with path coherences (identity image) so that consecutive steps share the same path term, then compose vertically. Declare the top cell between the two corrected full routes 321 → … → 123. All nonidentity cells (30 paths, 18 2-cells, 1 top cell) have been machine-checked against a certified CaTT checker. So the failure of *layered* terms (item 2) is a failure of that particular choice only.
9. **Where the cylinder argument stops.** For a general first factor Θ in place of I, reconstructing a cell across a wedge of the second factor subtracts a varying cell of the common copy of Θ, not the fixed seam tableau E_u. Positivity conditions then occur in degrees above one, and the intermediate vertex d of item 7 is no longer determined by the 0-endpoints, so a single 2-coherence per vertex no longer suffices.

10. **A strict comparison for the diagonal hom of two suspensions (all dimensions).** For finite globular P and Q, write ΣP and ΣQ for their suspensions, with vertices 0 and 1, and Sp for the suspended basis element of p. Let ε be the augmentation, extended by 0 in positive degree. The degree-+1 chain map
    - F(p ⊗ e ⊗ q) = Sp ⊗ Sq,
    - F(p ⊗ v ⊗ q) = ε(q) Sp ⊗ (1 − v) + ε(p) v ⊗ Sq (v = 0, 1),
    
    has nonnegative coefficients and commutes with ∂ in positive degree. Here e is the arrow of I, and at total degree 0 the image is one of the two directed paths from (0,0) to (1,1). Prefixing the row ((0,0),(1,1)) to the image of each tableau row gives a strict functor **Φ : P ⊗ I ⊗ Q → (ΣP ⊗ ΣQ)((0,0),(1,1))**. Φ sends the atom p ⊗ e ⊗ q to the atom Sp ⊗ Sq, so every mixed atom of ΣP ⊗ ΣQ has a source of one lower dimension. Φ is not injective.

11. **Φ has a pinned globular section for all finite globular P and Q.** There is a map s of underlying globular sets, commuting with both faces and preserving identities, with Φ s = id and s(Sp ⊗ Sq) = p ⊗ e ⊗ q on every mixed atom. Proof outline:
    - For two paths J = [r] and K = [s], cells of the diagonal hom are lifted directly. Arrows become sorted words (P-moves before Q-moves on each route, with the middle move at the crossing). 2-cells become sequences of oriented square swaps, whose extra squares lie in the endpoint panels and have identity image. 3-cells become whiskered oriented cubes, after strict exchange of disjoint swap blocks.
    - For general P and Q, a diagonal cell y is cut into local data over pairs (u, w) of nodes of the Batanin trees, by restriction to node paths and augmentation. Each local triple (U on J ⊗ I, V on I ⊗ K, W on J ⊗ K one degree lower), subject to three compatibility equations, is lifted by the path case. The bottom rows of the local lifts are then assembled into a tableau of P ⊗ I ⊗ Q. A coefficient bound (each prospective bottom row has mass ≤ 1, by positive chain maps to globes) makes each local bottom row a single vertex triple.
    - Restricted to the faces of the mixed atoms, Φ is injective, and the face-closure of the mixed atoms is recovered row by row from the image.
    
    The section is checked by machine on every diagonal cell of D⁴ ⊗ D⁴ and of a pair of branching trees.
12. **Transfer theorem: CT(P ⊗ I ⊗ Q) ⇒ CT(ΣP ⊗ ΣQ) for all finite globular P, Q.** Let U be a CT assignment for B = P ⊗ I ⊗ Q, and s the section of item 11. Build a substitution ρ from the family context of ΣP ⊗ ΣQ into Σ Fam(B), in order of dimension. Each endpoint-panel generator of B is sent to a boundary lift, inside the route pasting context R_v (R₀ = ΣQ ∨₀ ΣP, R₁ = ΣP ∨₀ ΣQ), of its strict image with the literally prescribed substituted endpoints. This is possible because strict interpretation on a pasting context is a trivial fibration, and the support of a term is that of its strict image. Each mixed generator Sp ⊗ Sq is declared with the ρ-substituted suspended type of p ⊗ e ⊗ q. Then T(c) = (Σ U(s c̄))[ρ] on the diagonal hom, and boundary lifting on the pure sides, satisfies (a)–(c). Composition preservation of s is not needed. For P = Q = I the panel lifts can be taken to be single exchange coherences over D² ∨₀ D²; this case was also checked by a certified CaTT checker.
13. **CT through dimension 3, and in dimension 4 when the top cells are atoms.** For any family context, choose terms degree by degree. Take the variable on a generator and the identity on an identity. For any other cell, compose the terms of a minimal strict expression, then correct its j-faces for j = 1, …, d−1 by composing at cut j with identity-image (j+1)-terms. Those terms live in the ≤ j-dimensional subcontext and exist by RC(j) there. RC(1) and RC(2) hold in every CaTT context, so this gives (a)–(c) for every cell of dimension ≤ 3. In degree 4 only declarations are needed when every non-identity 4-cell is an atom, which holds for all iterated tensors of globes of total dimension 4 (checked exhaustively). Making the choices on flattened basis tuples gives the same terms for every parenthesization. **The same argument works in every dimension given RC(j) for the j-skeleta of the family context.** These skeleta are strongly loop-free Steiner, so relative coherence on finite strong-Steiner contexts (any two parallel n-terms with the same strict image are joined by an (n+1)-term) would give CT for every tensor of globular pasting diagrams.
14. **Consequences.** CT holds for: every iterated tensor of globes of total dimension ≤ 4, including the four-cube I^{⊗4} compatibly with every parenthesization; D² ⊗ D² (with its identity towers), D³ ⊗ D² and D² ⊗ D³; and ΣJ ⊗ ΣK for all paths J = [r], K = [s] (each factor a vertical string of 2-generators of any length), since [r] ⊗ I ⊗ [s] has dimension ≤ 3.

15. **Wedge closure with a finite path factor (all dimensions).** For every finite path J = [r] and finite globular P, Q: CT(J ⊗ P) ∧ CT(J ⊗ Q) ⇒ CT(J ⊗ (P ∨₀ Q)). Write z for the common vertex. For a vertex l (a path) on the P side, let A(l) be the time of its last P-edge; for a vertex q on the Q side, let B(q) be the time of its first Q-edge.
    - The crossing hom is the full subcategory of L × R on the pairs with A(l) ≤ B(q), with inverse x + y − E_ij. Both A and B are nondecreasing along arrows.
    - Every crossing cell h factors as (1_{l₋}, β) #₀ (α, 1_{q₊}) through d = (l₋, q₊). The two factors split horizontally at the times A(l₋) and B(q₊), and all faces of h share the same d and the same two times.
    - The terms are cut-1 composition heads of horizontal composition heads, padded by path comparisons over the path contexts.
16. **A single seam path is impossible for three factors.** In I_A ⊗ I_B ⊗ [2] (spatial edges C, D), take the strict 2-cells CDBA → CDAB → CADB → ACDB → ACBD → ABCD and CDBA → CBDA → BCDA → BCAD → BACD → ABCD.
    - They are the two faces of one 3-cell: the image of the generating cube under the map that sends the last edge of I to CD.
    - The first can only factor through the seam path CABD, and the second only through CBAD, because the position in [2] of the BA → AB square is invariant.
    - The same happens in P ⊗ I ⊗ (Q ∨₀ Q′) whenever P, Q and Q′ each have an edge, via a strict retraction.

    So wedge closure with an interval in the middle cannot use one intermediate vertex per cell, as items 7 and 15 do.
17. **Cylinder factorization of crossing cells.** Let K be any finite strong-Steiner complex (for example P ⊗ I), W = U ∨_z V, and X, Y endpoints strictly on opposite sides of z. Each n-cell h of (K ⊗ W)(X, Y) factors uniquely as a normalized strict map D^n ⊗ [3] → K ⊗ W, with constant ends at X and Y and three cylinders in K ⊗ U, K ⊗ {z} and K ⊗ V.
    - The columns are L(g), Z(g), R(g): the parts of each signed row off the seam in U, at z, and off the seam in V. The moving vertices A(g) and B(g) are the incoming and outgoing edge sums at z.
    - The factorization commutes with faces, identities and hom composition.
    - Two projected cells l and r glue (as c = l + r − θ_z) **iff the middle column is a directed cylinder** D^n ⊗ I → K. Every positive degree can be the first degree in which this fails, so no finite set of low rows decides it.
18. **Suspension in any slot is a pullback.** (A ⊗ ΣP ⊗ B)((x,0,b), (y,1,b′)) ≅ (A ⊗ I ⊗ B)(X, Y) ×_{A ⊗ B′} (A ⊗ P ⊗ B′), where B′ is B with its differential negated in positive degrees, the right map augments the middle factor, and the left map takes the interval-edge column.

## 4. Questions

**Q1 (main).** Decide CT for **all** pairs Θ, Δ of finite globular pasting diagrams and for all iterated tensors Θ₁ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ Θ_k, compatibly with associativity (the assignments for (A ⊗ B) ⊗ C and A ⊗ (B ⊗ C) agree up to renaming). By item 12, the pairs follow from the three-factor tensors P ⊗ I ⊗ Q. A positive answer may use any terms (single coherence heads over chosen pasting diagrams, boundary lifts in pasting subcontexts, correction coherences as in items 7, 8 and 13, simultaneous inductive choices). A negative answer should give a family with a proof that no assignment satisfies (a)–(c).

Natural routes:
- (i) **Transfer for k suspended factors.** Compare the diagonal hom of ΣP₁ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ ΣP_k, from the bottom corner to the top corner, with a tensor of P₁, …, P_k and a (k−1)-dimensional shape recording the order in which the factors are traversed (for k = 2 this is the interval I of item 10). Prove a strict comparison with a pinned section and a transfer theorem as in items 11–12. Together with closure under wedges and under interval factors, this would give an induction on total dimension that covers every iterated tensor.
- (ii) **Interval factors in any position: the cylinder-lifting problem.** CT(P ⊗ I ⊗ Q) for all P and Q, by suspension and wedge closure in each outer factor. Suspension is strictly a pullback (item 18), and a crossing cell of a wedge factors uniquely through three strict cylinders with moving endpoints (item 17). The remaining step is about CaTT terms. **Lift these normalized strict cylinders D^n ⊗ [3] → K ⊗ W to CaTT substitutions (weak cylinders) whose shared caps use the same chosen terms, and whose two constant ends give the selected globular boundaries.** Then compose them by weak cylinder stacking, which requires typed cylinders with matching caps. A CT section on each piece gives terms for individual strict cells, but its type for the image of a cylinder generator may use a different term for a composite boundary from the one the cylinder substitution needs. Item 16 shows that the middle cylinder genuinely moves, so the choice of terms must be compatible along the moving seam in every dimension. The smallest uncovered three-factor tensors have total dimension 5 (for example I ⊗ I ⊗ D³ and D² ⊗ I ⊗ D², the antecedent for D³ ⊗ D³). A uniform argument is wanted, not these cases one at a time.
- (iii) **Through relative coherence.** Prove RC(n) for all n on the skeleta of the family contexts of Gray tensors (strongly loop-free Steiner complexes), which by item 13 gives CT outright. Or prove the weaker statement that item 13's correction step needs, namely the existence of identity-image (j+1)-terms between parallel j-terms with equal image in those skeleta.

**Q2 (fallback, still every dimension).** If Q1 is out of reach, prove CT for every Gray cube I^{⊗n} (all n, compatibly with every parenthesization), or for every pair Dᵐ ⊗ Dⁿ of globes, by a construction uniform in the dimensions. By item 12, the globe pairs follow from CT(D^{m−1} ⊗ I ⊗ D^{n−1}) for all m and n.

**Q3 (if CT fails).** If CT fails for some pair or family, prove that no assignment satisfies (a)–(c) and identify the minimal extra rule that makes the family contexts well formed (for example a typing rule for generators whose boundaries are molecules, or a definitional equation between bracketings). A precise description of the forced equations is the useful answer.

## 5. What counts as a solution

An explicit construction of T for all pairs and iterated tensors; CT for all three-factor tensors P ⊗ I ⊗ Q; a transfer theorem for k suspended factors; relative coherence on the strong-Steiner skeleta; a uniform construction for all cubes or all pairs of globes; an impossibility proof with the forced equations; or a published reference.

# Researcher Response 1

**CT holds for $[r]\otimes P$ and $P\otimes[r]$ for every finite path and every finite globular pasting diagram $P$.** In particular the height of $P$ and the number of consecutive path edges are independent. The construction also solves the constant-end part of Q1(ii) for arbitrary $K$. Once compatible typed cylinders have been stacked, their filler can be turned into an ordinary cell with the selected globular faces.

### Extracting a globular cell from a cylinder

Use the cylinder convention $I\otimes D^n$. Its filler has dimension $n+1$. The two $n$-cells at the ends of the interval are its *caps*. Its two side cylinders are obtained by restricting the disk. We index the sides by their signs in the global tensor tableau. In particular the global negative side uses the positive disk face, because $\partial(e\otimes g)=(1-0)\otimes g-e\otimes\partial g$. The opposite convention follows by duality.

Suppose an $(k+1)$-dimensional cylinder filler has caps which are literal identities. By the cylinder type formula, each endpoint of the filler is obtained from a raw $k$-dimensional side filler $v$ by composing at cut $k-1$ with a $k$-cell $W$. In the negative endpoint $W$ precedes $v$, and in the positive endpoint it follows $v$. Starting with the cap, $W$ successively whisks it by side data of dimensions $1,\ldots,k-1$ at cuts $0,\ldots,k-2$. For $k=1$, $W$ is just the cap. There are CaTT comparisons **in both directions** between each padded endpoint and its raw side $v$, all with strict identity image. [Cylinder type formula, Fig. 11 and Proposition 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.11620)

Here is the construction of those comparisons. A whiskered identity $1_u *_j b$ can be compared in either direction with $1_{u *_j b}$ when $\dim b\le\dim u$ and $j<\min(\dim u,\dim b)$. Both expressions are parallel over the generic pasting context $D^{\dim u}\vee_j D^{\dim b}$. They use its entire support and have the same strict value, so use a coherence there and substitute $u,b$. The left-handed version works in the reverse arity order. Start with the actual identity cap. After each whisker, whisk the comparison obtained so far and apply this generic comparison at the new step. This yields $W\leftrightarrows 1_w$, where $w$ is the same sequence of whiskerings on the base of the cap. Thus $W$ is literally an endomorphism of $w$. Whisk the last comparison with $v$ at cut $k-1$, then use the top unitor or its reverse over the single globe on $v$. All generic comparisons evaluate to identities by globular boundary lifting, or directly because their arities have no generators in the dimension of the comparison. No comparison between arbitrary parallel terms in the ambient context is needed.

Two applications will be used.

1. **Cylinder identities.** For any typed cylinder $v$ with caps $a,b$, use $1_a,1_b$ as the next caps and $v$ on both sides. The comparison from the padded negative side to $v$, followed by the comparison from $v$ to the padded positive side, has exactly the new filler type. Its strict value is the identity on $v$. This gives a degeneracy for the disk coordinate with the same two literal side cylinders.
2. **Constant ends.** Let $h$ be a cylinder of global dimension $m$ whose cap data are the ordinary iterated identities on two objects $X,Y$. Write $v_j^\pm$ for its signed raw side fillers of global dimension $j$. Set $N_1(h)=h$. For general $m$, start with the raw filler and process cuts $j=m-1,m-2,\ldots,1$. At cut $j$ precompose with the comparison from $v_j^-$ to its padded endpoint and postcompose with the comparison from the padded endpoint to $v_j^+$. Use the binary heads on the two globes glued at cut $j$, with a fixed bracketing. At the first step the endpoints match by the cylinder type formula. After that step the immediate faces are the raw sides. Their own boundaries are given by precisely the same cylinder formula one dimension down. More generally, in a composite at cut $i>j$ the lower $j$-faces are inherited, so the same argument applies at each cut. Above cut $j+1$ the face of a whiskering is literally the whiskering of the face, with the lower-dimensional comparison held fixed. It follows that
   $$
     N_m(h): N_{m-1}(v_{m-1}^-)\longrightarrow N_{m-1}(v_{m-1}^+)
   $$
   for $m>1$, and its strict value is that of the raw filler. The signed sides on the right mean the whole restricted cylinders, including their caps. Every operation in $N$ commutes with substitution. Thus the construction applies even when the ambient computad has composite generator boundaries.

### Compatible choices on one interval

For a single tile $I\otimes P$ we need CT **with the usual cylinder declarations** on every generator $e\otimes g$. Items 6–7 give this refinement if all binary heads are the unbiased heads. For a point there is only the spatial arrow. At a wedge the one-interval gluing proof retains the cylinder declarations of each piece. At a suspension the hom transfer sends the suspended cap variable on the initial copy to the right whisker of the new cap by the target spatial edge. On the terminal copy it uses the left whisker by the source spatial edge. It sends the suspended mixed variable to the new mixed variable. Suspension sends a binary head at cut $j$ to the head at cut $j+1$, since the arity and its two recursive boundary heads are all suspended. Consequently transferring a cylinder declaration inserts exactly the innermost cut-zero whisker of the cap and increments all the old cuts. This is precisely the recurrence of Fig. 11: the negative boundary whisks the initial cap on the right at successive cuts, and the positive boundary whisks the terminal cap on the left. The signed side variables remain variables. This proves the refinement by the globular tree decomposition. [Single-tile transfer](../answered/canonical-gray-terms-2026-11-19.md) · [Single-tile wedge construction](../answered/canonical-gray-terms-r2-2026-11-19.md)

On the edge between layers $i-1$ and $i$ we now choose, for **every** strict $d$-cell $u$ of $P$, a typed cylinder representing the strict map $I\otimes D^d\to I\otimes P$ induced by $u$. Its disk faces must be the choices for the faces of $u$. Give each generator its cylinder variable. For composites use the sidewise cylinder compositions of Theorem 4.2, and for repeated identities on objects use the cylinder identities above. The cap terms in either layer follow the same composition recipe on $P$ and can thus be chosen identically in both adjacent tiles. [Cylinder composition and cap equations, Theorem 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.11620)

For clarity, the needed bracketing of cells of $P$ can be fixed without making any exchange choices. In a globular pasting diagram written as a wedge of suspensions, a positive cell from object $a$ to object $b>a$ consists of one desuspended cell in each root segment between them. This follows by restricting its positive tableau rows to the disjoint suspended bases. Compose these components horizontally in order, with a fixed binary bracketing, omitting the composition on a single segment. Choose the component expressions recursively inside each subtree, increasing their composition cuts by one. For $a=b$ use identity towers. Equivalently this recursion takes place in the hom of the chosen cells at the current endpoints, where all cuts are shifted by one. Faces above the horizontal cut use the same list of segments and the same bracketing. At the cut they give the outer endpoints. This proves the face property, including when some components are identities. Every generator follows a single segment at each descent and retains its variable. In the cylinder interpretation a cut $j$ on disk cells uses the cut $j+1$ operation on their cylinders. Its literal cap and side equations are exactly the equations used in this recursion.

The strict interpretation of the cylinder composite is the induced cylinder of the strict composite. One can check this directly from the cited side and cap equations, inducting on dimension. In the generic domain $I\otimes(D^d\vee_j D^d)$ the two top basis elements have distinct cap generators on the initial copy. Thus the differential is injective on top chains. Once the side and cap images are known, the strict filler agrees with the one induced by composition of the two globes. Likewise for stacking in $[2]\otimes D^d$, the two top generators have independent differentials, detected at the two exterior cap copies. The strict filler of a stack is therefore the image under the composite interval edge. In degree one this is ordinary path composition. Substitution proves these assertions for arbitrary arguments, and iterating gives the same assertion for a bracketed stack of any finite length.

### The terms across several path edges

Glue the single-tile contexts on their copies of $P$. On each such copy use a single globular section. Its generator boundaries are variables. In the path coordinate a cell with both object endpoints in one layer stays there, and any positive-dimensional face keeps its object endpoints. So choosing the common section on the layers preserves every mixed declaration. Keep the single-tile sections on all cells with object endpoints one layer apart. This includes every generator.

Fix outer endpoints $(i,a),(j,b)$ with $j-i=s>1$. For a global $(n+1)$-cell $h$ between them there is an ordered stack with $2s+1$ strict cylinders. The first is in layer $i$, the next crosses the first path edge, the third is in layer $i+1$, and so on, ending in layer $j$. To see the precise data, write each global signed $(k+1)$-row as the sum of $v_t\otimes z_t(g)$ over spatial vertices and $e_l\otimes u_l(g)$ over spatial edges, where $g$ is the corresponding $k$-generator of $D^n$. A vertex path uses each of the $s$ spatial edges exactly once. Each $u_l$ is a cell diagram $(D^n)^{\mathrm{op}_{\rm all}}\to P$. Here the total opposite has differential $-d$ in positive degrees and is again a globe by relabeling the two faces in each lower degree. The signs follow from the term $-e_l\otimes\partial u_l$ in the differential. Set $u_i$ and $u_{j+1}$ to the constant diagrams at $a$ and $b$, indexing $e_l$ by its target $l$. The vertex equation is
$$
 \partial z_t(g)=z_t(dg)+u_{t+1}(g)-u_t(g).
$$
In degree zero the constant diagrams supply the outer endpoints. Hence $z_t$ gives the edge column of a strict cylinder $I\otimes(D^n)^{\mathrm{op}_{\rm all}}\to P$ from $u_t$ to $u_{t+1}$, placed in layer $t$. Between successive layers insert the cylinder $I\otimes u_l$ on the corresponding spatial edge. Stacking inserts each vertex column and each edge column exactly once, giving back the entire tableau of $h$. Restricting the disk restricts each column. The reversal in the disk parameter is precisely the reversal of the side signs in $I\otimes D^n$; thus the global signed sides of the stack agree with those of $h$.

Use the chosen connector cylinder of $u_l$ on each spatial edge. Lift each layer cylinder in the globular pasting context of that layer as follows. Prescribe its cap terms from the connectors, using ordinary iterated identities for the exterior caps. In increasing disk dimension prescribe the two already chosen side cylinders and fill the cylinder type with the strict cell given by the layer map. Its two literal endpoints evaluate to the required strict boundaries by the cylinder formula. Boundary lifting holds on this globular context. Make the choice once for each strict layer diagram with these caps and side data, so restrictions of all the stacks use the same lifts. At a common layer the cap recipe on $P$ agrees from the two sides. Theorem 4.3 now stacks these **typed** cylinders, with exactly the chosen side stacks by its face equations. Apply $N_{n+1}$ to the result. The resulting term has value $h$ and both literal boundaries are the terms assigned to its signed global faces. For $n=0$ the stack itself is the chosen path term. The argument applies to identity towers as well. [Boundary lifting in globular contexts, item 2](../answered/relative-coherence-all-dimensions-r5-2026-11-19.md) · [Stacking, Theorem 4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.11620)

There are no further paths with decreasing layer coordinate. For $r=0$ the whole diagram is the globular layer. This proves all of CT(a)–(c) for $[r]\otimes P$. Applying the odd-dimensional opposite, which exchanges the two tensor factors and preserves globular pasting diagrams and CaTT typing, gives $P\otimes[r]$.

# Researcher Response 2

**Wedge closure holds with a seam of dimension at most two.** Let $K$ be a finite strong-Steiner complex of dimension at most two and $U,V$ finite globular pasting diagrams. Then
$$
  CT(K\otimes U)\ \land\ CT(K\otimes V)
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad CT(K\otimes(U\vee_0V)).
$$
There is no dimension bound on $U$ or $V$. In particular this proves the wedge step of Q1(ii) with $K=[r]\otimes I$, including the moving square seam in item 16. The proof uses the two-dimensional bound only for the caps. The strict cylinders in the two pieces can have arbitrarily high nonidentity fillers.

### Lifting a family of cylinders with bounded cap dimension

Fix a CT section $T$ in one piece. Use the convention for signs in Response 1, or its dual for a right interval. Suppose a face-closed family of strict disk cylinders is given with compatible typed lifts of its cap diagrams. Assume each cap in disk degree $n>d$ is the **literal identity** on its preceding cap, for a fixed $d\ge1$. Suppose identity-image comparisons of parallel $j$-terms with equal strict value exist in the piece for $1\le j\le d$. Then the cylinders have typed lifts with the specified caps, compatible under disk faces. In this statement disk degree $n$ means that the raw filler has global dimension $n+1$.

For $n\le d$, induct on disk degree. The cap terms and previously chosen side cylinders prescribe a raw filler type $a\to b$ by the cylinder type formula. Start with $T(f)$ for the strict filler $f$. Process cuts $j=1,\dots,n$ in increasing order. At cut $j$, precompose with a comparison from the prescribed $j$-source to the current $j$-source, and postcompose with one from the current $j$-target to the prescribed $j$-target. The object endpoints agree because objects are variables. At subsequent cuts the terms being compared are parallel because all lower boundaries have already been fixed. Their strict images agree because $f$ has the cylinder boundary and the corrections have identity image. This proves that the comparisons exist and that the corrected term has the prescribed type. Cut composition here and below uses heads whose faces above the composition cut are the corresponding lower heads, as in item 13. Fix the choice of comparison once for each **ordered pair of terms**. In particular, two disk-degree-$d$ cylinders with the same prescribed type and parallel strict fillers use precisely the same sequence of left and right corrections. Indeed $T$ sends their strict immediate boundaries to the same two terms, and this remains true through cuts below their immediate boundaries. In degree zero the unmodified path $T(f)$ already has its prescribed endpoints.

Now take $n>d$. Above disk degree $d$ the strict globular faces of the filler are the fillers of the signed side cylinders. This follows by applying the cylinder boundary formula with an identity cap, or by interpreting the two comparisons in Response 1 strictly. The iterated disk-degree-$d$ sides have identical cap labels because the cap diagram above them consists of identities. They have identical proper side data by globularity. Thus they prescribe the same raw filler type, and their strict fillers are parallel. Use their common sequence of cuts $1,\dots,d$ on $T(f)$, with the same comparison terms held fixed. The cut heads whisk these comparisons when $f$ is higher dimensional. By the face equations of the heads and of $T$, the induced terms on both disk-degree-$d$ sides are exactly their chosen lifts. Restricting to a side of disk degree greater than $d$ restricts this same construction.

It remains to restore the actual cylinder types above disk degree $d$. Continue in **ascending** order at cuts $j=d+1,\dots,n$. Immediately before cut $j$ its two $j$-faces are the chosen raw fillers of the two disk-degree-$(j-1)$ sides. For the first such cut this is the previous paragraph. For a later cut it follows by taking the faces of all the preceding whiskerings. The prescribed endpoints at this stage are the padded forms of these sides for the given identity cap. Response 1 supplies a comparison from the padded negative endpoint to the negative side and one from the positive side to the padded positive endpoint. Use them as the pre- and postcomposition at cut $j$. Their construction uses the actual cap base and the array of lower side terms. In particular the cap base at the first new cut may be a nonidentity $d$-term. All these comparisons have identity image. The last cut gives the required type; restricting the earlier cuts gives exactly the terms already used for its side cylinders. This proves the lemma in all dimensions.
