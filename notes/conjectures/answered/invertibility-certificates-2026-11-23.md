# Finite certificates for invertible cells in free Gray-enriched ω-categories

## Setting

**Gray-enriched ω-categories.** Let ω-Cat be the category of strict ω-categories, with the (lax) Gray tensor product ⊗ of Ara–Maltsiniotis (*Joint et tranches pour les ∞-catégories strictes*, Mém. SMF 165, 2020; arXiv:1607.00668). A *Gray ω-category* is a category enriched in (ω-Cat, ⊗). In such a K:
- inside each hom K(a, b), cells compose as in a strict ω-category;
- composition across objects, K(a, b) ⊗ K(b, c) → K(a, c), sends the top cell of x ⊗ y (for cells x, y of positive dimension) to a *directed interchanger*, which in general is not invertible.

The weak analogue is an oriented (∞,ω)-category in the sense of Gepner–Heine (arXiv:2510.10504), enriched in Cat_ω for the Gray tensor.

**Invertibility (coinductive).** A cell f : x → y of dimension n ≥ 1 is *invertible* if there are cells g, g′ : y → x and invertible (n+1)-cells l : g ; f → 1_y and r : f ; g′ → 1_x. Here f ; g is composition along the (n−1)-boundary in *diagrammatic* order (first f, then g), used throughout. The definition is coinductive: the invertibility of l and r is again of this kind (Cheng; Rice; Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara; Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv:2406.12127).

**Marked computads.** A *marked computad* is a finite polygraph (computad) C together with a set M of its generators of positive dimension, called *marked*. Let F(C, M) be the Gray ω-category freely generated by C in which every marked generator is invertible. Concretely: adjoin, for each marked generator m, generators m⁻ˡ, m⁻ʳ, λ_m : m⁻ˡ ; m → 1, ρ_m : m ; m⁻ʳ → 1, then mark λ_m and ρ_m and repeat. This is the Gray analogue of the computads with invertible generators of Benjamin–Champin–Markakis (arXiv:2606.30254). The weak version of F(C, M) is defined the same way in oriented (∞,ω)-categories.

**Certified cells.** Cert(C, M) is the *smallest* set of cells of F(C, M) that is closed under the following rules:
1. **Marked generators.** Every marked generator m is in Cert, and so are m⁻ˡ, m⁻ʳ, λ_m and ρ_m.
2. **Identities and coherences.** Every identity is in Cert. In the weak setting, every coherence cell whose source and target have the same image in the strict quotient is in Cert.
3. **Composites.** A composite (pasting) of cells inside a hom is in Cert if each of its top-dimensional constituents is in Cert.
4. **Gray cells.** Take an elementary Gray cell: the image of the top cell of x₁ ⊗ … ⊗ x_k under iterated composition across objects. It is in Cert if some xᵢ of positive dimension (as a cell of its hom) is in Cert, or if every xᵢ has dimension 0 in its hom (is a 1-cell of K) and all of them are in Cert.
5. **Inverses.** If f is in Cert, so is any chosen inverse g of f together with the cells witnessing it.
6. **inv-intro.** If f : x → y and there are cells g, g′ : y → x and l : g ; f → 1_y, r : f ; g′ → 1_x with l and r in Cert, then f is in Cert.

Every certified cell is invertible.

**What is known.**
- *Ordinary (non-Gray) case, no marks.* A cell of the free weak ω-category on a computad is invertible iff its top-dimensional support consists of invertible cells, and coherence cells of dimension above the computad's dimension are invertible (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv:2406.12127, Thm 33 and Prop 37).
- *Ordinary case, with marks: finite certificates suffice.* Every cell of dimension greater than dim C is in Cert: by induction on the term, its top constituents have the same dimension, and identities, coherences and inverses are certified. So if f is invertible, the coinductive tower (l, r, then their witnesses, …) reaches certified cells after at most dim C − dim f + 1 levels, and rule 6 applied finitely often certifies f.
- *Gray case.* That argument fails. Directed interchangers of every dimension above dim C exist and are not invertible: in the free Gray ω-category on two composable non-invertible 2-cells across an object, the 3-cell interchanger has no inverse.
- *Decidability.* Membership in Cert is not decidable uniformly in (C, M). It contains the unit problem of finitely presented monoids: one object, the generators as 1-cells, each relation u = v as a marked 2-cell u ⇒ v. That problem is undecidable (Narendran–Ó'Dúnlaing–Otto, Discrete Math. 98 (1991)).

## Questions

1. **(Main question.)** For every finite marked computad (C, M), is every invertible cell of F(C, M), the free Gray ω-category, in Cert(C, M)? Answer for the strict Gray ω-categories above, and for the weak (oriented (∞,ω)) version. If the answer differs between the two, say where.

2. **(If not.)** Give a finite marked computad and an invertible cell of F(C, M) outside Cert(C, M). Then give the smallest extension of rules 1–6 that is complete, for example a rule that takes a single term built over a generic "walking invertible cell" context as a finite description of an infinite tower, in the style of a recursor.

3. **(Characterization.)** Characterize the invertible cells of F(C, M) in terms of supports and marks, in the spirit of the Benjamin–Markakis criterion: for example, by a condition on the top-dimensional constituents of a normal form, relative to the congruence generated by M. Is there a characterization that separates the decidable part (rules 1–5) from the undecidable part (rule 6)?

4. **(Pseudo cells.)** Call a cell t of a hom K(a, b), of dimension ≥ 1 as a cell of that hom, *pseudo* if, for every cell y of positive dimension in a hom K(c, a), the Gray cell of y ⊗ t is invertible. Is t pseudo as soon as the Gray cells y ⊗ t are invertible for the *generic* such y, that is, for y the top cell of a disk Dᵏ attached at a, for every k ≥ 1? Is k = 1 enough when t has dimension 2 (a transformation)? Answer for the free Gray ω-categories F(C, M) and in general.

## Researcher Response 1

**Rules 1–6 certify every invertible cell in both settings.** The quantifier “any chosen inverse … together with the cells witnessing it” in rule 5 is essential. It allows us to choose the inverse data of an *identity*. The resulting derivation has finite depth, although checking that a proposed choice is inverse data is a semantic condition. In fact, membership using rules 1–5 alone is already undecidable. For the pseudo-cell question, one freely attached incoming interval suffices in every positive dimension of the outgoing cell.

### 1–2. Cancellations are witnesses for identities

Write juxtaposition for composition in diagrammatic order. Suppose $c:z\to z$ is an endocell and $w:c\to 1_z$ is invertible. With strict units, choose $c$ as both inverses of $1_z$. The two required witnesses have sources $c1_z=c$ and $1_zc=c$, so we can choose $w$ for both. Rules 2 and 5 certify $w$.

With weak units, write $u:c1_z\to c$ and $v:1_zc\to c$ for the invertible unitors. The choices $uw$ and $vw$ now witness $c$ as an inverse of $1_z$, so rule 5 certifies $uw$. Rule 2 certifies $u$, and rule 5 gives a certified inverse $u^*:c\to c1_z$. Rule 3 therefore certifies the contraction $u^*(uw):c\to1_z$. Its dimension is at least two in the enriched category, so this paste lies inside a hom even when $c$ is a global 1-cell. Consequently, **whenever an invertible contraction of $c$ exists, a certified contraction with those same endpoints exists**. This uses closure of equivalences under vertical composition. In a globular model this is the pasting theorem for weakly invertible cells. [Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara, *Weakly invertible cells in a weak ω-category*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14907)

Now take any invertible $f:x\to y$ and its defining data $g,g',l,r$. Apply the contraction result first to $gf\to1_y$, then to $fg'\to1_x$. Rule 6 certifies $f$ using the two resulting contractions. The reverse implication is the stated soundness of the rules. The proof works in arbitrary dimension and uses neither freeness nor a bound on the generators. The finiteness assertion concerns derivation depth. Choosing the inverse data of the two identities in rule 5 uses the coinductive invertibility of the original contractions. A rule projecting a single predetermined inverse package from a previously constructed certificate would have a different premise.

### 3. A quotient criterion and the limit of support tests

There is an exact characterization on the terms of the marked presentation. It needs their boundaries and composition, as well as which generators the marks supply. Expand the marks into the inverse and witness generators specified in the question, and use the resulting terms modulo the equations of the chosen model. For each $n\ge1$, fix a parallel lower boundary and take the local category whose objects are the $(n-1)$-cells on that boundary and whose arrows are the $n$-terms along it. For $n=1$ take all objects. More precisely, choose at each dimension a relation $R_n$ on parallel terms, an equivalence relation for each pair of endpoints and a congruence for top composition. Form the quotient $H_n(R)$ with arrow classes modulo $R_n$. In the weak case require $R_n$ to identify the two ends of the unitors and associators for this composition, so the quotient is an ordinary category. Use a separate copy at each lower boundary, keeping its objects fixed.

Call the family admissible if whenever $p\,R_n\,q$, there is an actual $(n+1)$-term $h:p\to q$ whose class in the corresponding $H_{n+1}(R)$ is an isomorphism. This condition mentions only term incidence and isomorphisms of ordinary quotients. Then

$$
 f\text{ is invertible}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 [f]\text{ is an isomorphism in }H_n(R)
 \text{ for some admissible }R .
$$

Indeed, for any admissible family, collect the cells whose arrow class is an isomorphism in these quotients. For each such $f$, represent its inverse class by a term $g$. Both products $gf,fg$ are related to their respective identities. Admissibility gives actual contractions in the same collection. The collection is thus closed under supplying the witnesses required by coinduction, and all its members are invertible.

In the other direction, let $p\,E_n\,q$ mean that there is an invertible $(n+1)$-cell from $p$ to $q$. Identities, inverses, composition and whiskering of equivalences make this an equivalence relation compatible with composition. For global 1-cells, paste the two whiskered equivalences in sequence. Weak unitors and associators give the required identifications. Every invertible cell represents an isomorphism modulo $E$ by its cancellations, since a left inverse and a right inverse agree in an ordinary category. Hence $E$ is admissible, and the preceding coinduction shows that every admissible $R$ is contained in $E$. In particular the largest admissible family $E$ identifies the two boundary arrows of each marked generator in dimension at least two. The quotient criterion uses the full ordered pasting and its incidences. It supplies no algorithm for finding this largest family.

For a one-object monoid presentation with every defining relation marked as a 2-generator and no other generators above degree one, the criterion specializes to the usual unit test. A word is invertible exactly when it represents a unit of the presented monoid. Each application of a relation, in either direction and in any word context, lifts to an invertible 2-cell by marking and whiskering. Words representing mutually inverse monoid elements therefore have both required contractions. Conversely, interpretation in the one-object category of the monoid, with identity higher cells, takes an invertible word to a unit.

Even a convergent presentation defeats a test that examines only the support and multiplicities of the normal form. Take two letters $a,b$ and one marked relation $abab\Rightarrow1$. The reduction $abab\to\varepsilon$ decreases length. Disjoint reductions commute and the only proper overlap is $ababab$, whose two reducts are both $ab$. Termination and local confluence give unique normal forms. Both $ab$ and $ba$ are normal and contain each generator once. The word $ab$ is its own inverse by the marked relation. Interpret $a$ as successor and $b$ as truncated predecessor on the natural numbers, in diagrammatic order. Then $ab=1$, while $ba$ identifies 0 and 1. This interpretation respects $abab=1$ and detects the failure of invertibility of $ba$.

Finally take a fixed finitely presented group with undecidable word problem. Write it as a finite monoid presentation by including formal inverse letters and their two cancellation relations. Again mark its relations in dimension two and mark no 1-generator. **Membership of a word under rules 1–5 is equivalent to its value being the group identity.** If its value is the identity, a finite sequence of contextual relations lifts to an invertible 2-cell from the word to the identity. The word is then a permissible choice of inverse of the identity in rule 5 (using unitors in the weak setting). Conversely, interpret the presentation in the locally discrete group. Induction on a rules 1–5 derivation shows that every certified 1-cell has identity image. Rules 1 and 2 supply only identities or their value in degree one, a degree-one product in rule 4 has only certified degree-one factors, and rule 5 takes the inverse of the identity value. Pastes inside homs and higher cancellation witnesses supply no additional group values. Thus the proposed decidable/undecidable cut at rule 6 already fails on a single finite computad. [Novikov, *On the algorithmic unsolvability of the word problem in group theory*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/tm1180)

### 4. The freely attached interval detects every generic disk

Let $H=K(a,b)$, and write $d_k$ for the principal cell of the globe $D^k$. Freely attach a fresh source object $s$ and a map $D^k\to K'(s,a)$. The new hom to each old object $z$ is $D^k\otimes K(a,z)$. Compose on the right using the old composition, take the endohom of $s$ to be the tensor unit, and the homs from old objects to $s$ to be empty. The enriched unit at $a$ gives the generating disk. These formulas have the free attachment property because a functor out is determined by the old functor, the image of $s$, and the chosen incoming disk. Composition gives its extension on each new hom. In the weak case these are equivalences of mapping spaces by the free right-module property. Thus the generic test is invertibility of $d_k\otimes t$ in $D^k\otimes H$. Every actual incoming $k$-cell specializes that test by its disk classifier followed by enriched composition. The same argument works after mapping $K$ to any other enriched category. Conversely, a condition required after *every* such map includes the free attachment itself.

Suppose $u=d_1\otimes t$ is invertible. For $k>1$, $d_{k-1}\otimes u$ is invertible as well. The Gray tensor of cells is invertible when either positive-dimensional factor is invertible. There is a functor

$$
 D^{k-1}\otimes D^1\longrightarrow D^k
$$

that collapses each end $D^{k-1}\otimes\{i\}$ to its endpoint and takes the principal cell to $d_k$. Tensoring this functor with $H$ carries $d_{k-1}\otimes u$ to $d_k\otimes t$ by associativity of the Gray tensor. This proves sufficiency of the single generic interval for **every** positive dimension of $t$, including dimension two. Both results hold for the strict Gray tensor. [Ara–Lucas, §4.7 and Proposition 4.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.13564#page=26)

In the weak setting the same fold on disks is available because the weak tensor agrees with the strict tensor on these Steiner shapes. To obtain absorption for arbitrary operands from the given hypotheses, take the free enriched chain on two consecutive globes and mark the positive-dimensional generator representing the invertible operand. Rules 1 and 4 certify the mixed cell there. Choosing the inverse data of the given operand defines its evaluation in the chain with the desired homs and their tensor as long hom. Soundness and functoriality give invertibility of the evaluated cell. This uses the stated weak soundness only in that free marked chain. [Gepner–Heine, §3.8, especially Theorem 3.8.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10504v1#S3.SS8)

In fact the freely attached interval tests invertibility of $t$ itself. Collapse the two endpoint copies of $H$ in $D^1\otimes H$ separately to points. The quotient is the suspension with forward hom $H^{\mathrm{coop}}$ (total dual, for this order of the two factors). On $H=D^m$ the cylinder quotient sends the mixed principal cell to the principal cell of the suspended globe. Naturality along the disk classifying $t$ therefore sends $d_1\otimes t$ to the suspended dual of $t$. For $m>0$ its entire inverse problem lies in the forward hom. Total duality preserves and reflects invertibility, so invertibility of the generic cell implies invertibility of $t$. The converse is tensor absorption. For the weak quotient, apply the duality reversing the tensor factors to the right-cylinder suspension formula. This gives the left-cylinder antisuspension. [Ara–Maltsiniotis, B.6.5–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.00668#page=199) · [Gepner–Heine, Theorems 3.11.2 and 4.6.8(3)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10504v1#S3.SS11)

Two finite free examples distinguish the internal quantifier in the definition of pseudo from free attachment.

* Take just the suspension of $D^2$, with $t=d_2$ in its sole nontrivial hom $K(a,b)$. All incoming positive-dimensional cells at $a$ are identities (or equivalent to them), so $t$ is internally pseudo. The top of $D^2$ has no reverse 2-cell. The cylinder quotient therefore detects failure of the generic test in both settings.
* Even checking every *existing* incoming 1-cell can miss an existing 2-cell. Use a three-object free chain with first hom $A=D^2\amalg_{\partial D^2} *$, second hom $D^2$, and no marks. Write $y$ for the 2-generator of $A$, whose source and target are the identity on its sole object, and again take $t=d_2$. In the strict first hom every 1-cell is an identity. In the weak first hom take the subcategory with all objects, only equivalences in degree one, and all higher cells between the retained boundaries. The attaching diagram factors through it because both boundaries of $y$ are identities. By freeness the identity of $A$ factors through this inclusion up to equivalence. Thus all 1-cells of $A$ are equivalences. Together with the unit endohom this accounts for every degree-one input.

  The long hom is $A\otimes D^2$. To detect $y\otimes t$, use the target with unique cells below dimension four, additive 4-endomorphisms $\mathbb N$, and identity higher cells. The free Steiner shape $D^2\otimes D^2$ maps to this target by sending its sole top generator to 1 and its lower generators to identities. Its restriction to $\partial D^2\otimes D^2$ is constant, since this boundary tensor has generating cells only through degree three. It therefore agrees there with the constant map from $*\otimes D^2$. Tensor preserves the attaching pushout, so the induced map sends $y\otimes t$ to 1. In the weak version use the same maps on the disk shapes and their constant agreement in the weak pushout. Hence the existing 2-cell fails. The weak tensor preserves these colimits by biclosedness. [Gepner–Heine, §3.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10504v1#S3.SS8)

Thus the single generic test is sufficient for internal pseudo-ness and is necessary and sufficient for pseudo-ness after arbitrary base functors. Both distinctions already occur on finite free presentations.

### Validation

Lean kernel-checked the cancellation argument at an arbitrary level with typed left and right unitors, a certified reverse unitor, composition, and the indicated instances of rules 2, 3, 5 and 6 as hypotheses. The two inverse candidates for the original cell were independent. The quotient criterion and the universal properties in question 4 were verified by reviewing the mathematical arguments.

Using the repository’s globular chain differential and atom tableaux, a separate calculation verified the disk fold on all 864 basis generators in degrees $2\le k\le17$ and compared both faces in every row of each principal cell. In $D^2\otimes D^2$ the degree-four detector picked out one of the 25 basis generators and vanished on the attaching boundary. Exhaustive reduction of the 32,767 binary words of length at most 14 gave a unique normal form for each and preserved its shift-map values at inputs $0,1,|w|,|w|+1$. The unbounded claims use the disk-fold theorem and the overlap proof, respectively.

<summary>Lean proof of the cancellation argument</summary>

Run with Lean 4.35.0. The final command reports the axioms of the theorem.

```lean
set_option autoImplicit false
universe u v w

structure Level where
  Obj : Type u
  Arr : Obj → Obj → Type v
  Cell : {x y : Obj} → Arr x y → Arr x y → Type w
  id : (x : Obj) → Arr x x
  comp : {x y z : Obj} → Arr x y → Arr y z → Arr x z
  paste : {x y : Obj} → {a b c : Arr x y} → Cell a b → Cell b c → Cell a c
  rightU : {x y : Obj} → (a : Arr x y) → Cell (comp a (id y)) a
  leftU : {x y : Obj} → (a : Arr x y) → Cell (comp (id x) a) a
  rightUBack : {x y : Obj} → (a : Arr x y) → Cell a (comp a (id y))

structure Rules (L : Level) where
  cert : {x y : L.Obj} → L.Arr x y → Prop
  higherCert : {x y : L.Obj} → {a b : L.Arr x y} → L.Cell a b → Prop
  higherInv : {x y : L.Obj} → {a b : L.Arr x y} → L.Cell a b → Prop
  idCert : ∀ x, cert (L.id x)
  rightUInv : ∀ {x y} (a : L.Arr x y), higherInv (L.rightU a)
  leftUInv : ∀ {x y} (a : L.Arr x y), higherInv (L.leftU a)
  backCert : ∀ {x y} (a : L.Arr x y), higherCert (L.rightUBack a)
  pasteInv : ∀ {x y} {a b c : L.Arr x y} (p : L.Cell a b) (q : L.Cell b c),
    higherInv p → higherInv q → higherInv (L.paste p q)
  pasteCert : ∀ {x y} {a b c : L.Arr x y} (p : L.Cell a b) (q : L.Cell b c),
    higherCert p → higherCert q → higherCert (L.paste p q)
  witnessOfChoice : ∀ {x y} (a : L.Arr x y) (b : L.Arr y x)
    (p : L.Cell (L.comp b a) (L.id y)) (q : L.Cell (L.comp a b) (L.id x)),
    cert a → higherInv p → higherInv q → higherCert p
  introCert : ∀ {x y} (a : L.Arr x y) (b b' : L.Arr y x)
    (p : L.Cell (L.comp b a) (L.id y)) (q : L.Cell (L.comp a b') (L.id x)),
    higherCert p → higherCert q → cert a

theorem completeness (L : Level) (R : Rules L) {x y : L.Obj} (f : L.Arr x y)
    (g g' : L.Arr y x) (l : L.Cell (L.comp g f) (L.id y))
    (r : L.Cell (L.comp f g') (L.id x)) (hl : R.higherInv l) (hr : R.higherInv r) :
    R.cert f := by
  have cancellation {z : L.Obj} (a : L.Arr z z) (w : L.Cell a (L.id z))
      (hw : R.higherInv w) : ∃ w' : L.Cell a (L.id z), R.higherCert w' := by
    let p := L.paste (L.rightU a) w
    let q := L.paste (L.leftU a) w
    have hp := R.witnessOfChoice (L.id z) a p q (R.idCert z)
      (R.pasteInv _ _ (R.rightUInv a) hw) (R.pasteInv _ _ (R.leftUInv a) hw)
    exact ⟨L.paste (L.rightUBack a) p, R.pasteCert _ _ (R.backCert a) hp⟩
  obtain ⟨l', hl'⟩ := cancellation _ l hl
  obtain ⟨r', hr'⟩ := cancellation _ r hr
  exact R.introCert f g g' l' r' hl' hr'
```

**Q3 (quotient criterion): verified by reading.**
- Admissible families of relations, and the largest one E (connected by an invertible cell), give f invertible ⇔ [f] an isomorphism in H_n(E). The two directions are the coinduction shown, and closure of equivalences under composition, inverses and whiskering (Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara for the weak case).
- The criterion is not algorithmic. It changes no rule.
- The counterexample to support tests (`abab ⇒ 1`: ab invertible, ba not) was checked by the script and by hand. Shift map: a = +1, b = truncated −1, so ab = id and ba sends 0 and 1 to 1.

**Q4 (pseudo cells): verified by reading and the fold check, with citations taken on trust where marked.**
- Freely attaching D^k at the source object a makes the new hom into z equal to D^k ⊗ K(a, z) (free right module). So the generic test is invertibility of d_k ⊗ t in D^k ⊗ H.
- The fold D^{k−1} ⊗ D^1 → D^k carries d_{k−1} ⊗ (d_1 ⊗ t) to d_k ⊗ t. Its chain-level compatibility was checked by the script. Strictly, that it is an ω-functor rests on Ara–Lucas Prop. 4.9 (taken on trust).
- **Main fact:** collapsing the two end copies of H in D^1 ⊗ H gives the suspension of H^coop, and d_1 ⊗ t maps to the suspended dual of t (Ara–Maltsiniotis B.6.5–6, taken on trust; Gepner–Heine 3.11.2 and 4.6.8 for the weak case). So the generic test holds **iff t is invertible**. The small case H = D^1 was checked by hand: collapsing the rows of the lax square leaves a 2-globe whose cell is the image of d_1 ⊗ t.
- The two finite examples, showing that the internal quantifier in the definition of pseudo is weaker than free attachment, were checked by reading. The D² ⊗ D² detector was checked by the script.
- **Consequence for the project.** No sound rule can conclude `ps` from inverse data alone, unless t is invertible. Pseudo-ness must be declared (see `marking-alternatives.md` §8.2).
