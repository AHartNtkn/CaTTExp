# Free higher categories with directed interchange: the least pasting profile and the laws visible on pasting diagrams

*A self-contained problem. No background beyond standard higher category theory is assumed. Everything in §2 is proved and is not re-asked. The constructions summarized in (K43)–(K53) are defined in full in the round-5 answers, which are reproduced in the repository file `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r5-2026-11-20.md` (Responses 1–34), with Responses 8–9 of `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r4-2026-11-19.md`. (K52)–(K53) are from `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r6-2026-11-20.md`, (K54)–(K57) from `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r7-2026-11-20.md`, and (K58)–(K64) from `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r8-2026-11-20.md` (Responses 1–2). The summaries below fix notation.*

**What we are asking for.** There is a theory **T₁₂** with H ≠ 1 satisfying all five requirements of §3 in every dimension ((K47)–(K50)), with normal forms in every degree ((K52)). There is no initial or weakly initial theory ((K54), (K57)), and on the fixed signature there is not even a minimal admissible law set ((K60)). But on free objects of finite pasting diagrams there **is** a least theory: the admissible theory V of (K58), whose free objects map to those of every admissible theory, with decidable equality and reachability. Free objects on pasting diagrams are what a type checker computes with, so V and the theories above it are now the main object. We ask for an **explicit description of V's free objects, and a classification of what can be added to V on pasting diagrams**: which laws are visible there, which pasting profiles occur, and when adding a visible law keeps admissibility. Results for one more shape or one more degree are not what we want; a uniform argument is.

## 1. Setting

**Globular sets and composition.** Work with reflexive globular sets with source and target maps s, t, identities, and for k < n a partial composition ∗ₖ of n-cells along k-cells. Composition is written in **diagrammatic order** (u ∗ₖ v means "first u, then v"), and it is strictly associative and unital. The boundary formulas hold: for k < m and (m+1)-cells u, v,

```
s(u ∗ₖ v) = s(u) ∗ₖ s(v),     t(u ∗ₖ v) = t(u) ∗ₖ t(v).        (B)
```

For k < l < n and n-cells in interchange position put `L(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₗ b) ∗ₖ (c ∗ₗ d)` and `R(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₖ c) ∗ₗ (b ∗ₖ d)`. A strict ω-category requires L = R.

**The Gray case (directed interchange along 0-cells only).** In a category enriched in strict ω-categories with the lax Gray tensor product (Ara–Maltsiniotis, arXiv 1607.00668), for 2-cells α : f ⇒ f′ : x → y and β : g ⇒ g′ : y → z there is a 3-cell

```
χ_{α,β} : (f ◁ β) ∗₁ (α ▷ g′)  ⇛  (α ▷ g) ∗₁ (f′ ◁ β)
```

and nothing in the reverse direction. Composition along k-cells with k ≥ 1 is strict. For a finite globular pasting diagram Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ, the free such structure has homs ν(λΘᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘⱼ). Here λ is Steiner's directed complex, ⊗ is Steiner's tensor with `∂(x ⊗ y) = ∂x ⊗ y + (−1)^{|x|} x ⊗ ∂y`, and ν is Steiner's functor to strict ω-categories (Steiner, HHA 2004). Each hom is generated by tuples of cells, and a cell is determined by the set of generators it uses.

**The aim** is the same for directed interchange along k-cells for **every** k, including inside homs.

## 2. What is already known (all proved)

**(K1) Raw higher interchangers are impossible.** If every pair of n-cells in interchange position had an (n+1)-cell L → R, then applying this to identities and using (B) forces L = R one dimension down. That contradicts directedness.

**(K2) A single comparison for each simultaneous variation is impossible.** The obvious repair of (K1) adds, for 3-cells A : a ⇛ a′, …, D : d ⇛ d′ in interchange position, a 4-cell between the parallel 3-cells

```
L(A,B,C,D) ∗₂ χ_{a′}     and     χ_a ∗₂ R(A,B,C,D).        (N)
```

This cannot be required for every instance. Take Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², with two 3-cells A : α ⇛ α′ and B : β ⇛ β′ side by side along a 0-cell, and the instance (A, 1, 1, B). Its two sides map to the hom 2-cells

```
U = (v₀ ∘₀ u₁) ∘₁ c₁₁,     V = c₀₀ ∘₁ (u₀ ∘₀ v₁)
```

of ν(λD² ⊗ λD²), where `u_j = A ⊗ g_j`, `v_i = f_i ⊗ B` and `c_ij = α_i ⊗ β_j`. There is no 4-cell between U and V in either direction: the degree-3 chain would need a positive coefficient at u₀ (or at v₀), but every basis element of degree 3 has a non-positive coefficient there.

**(K3) The two variables have opposite naturality directions.** The degree-3 basis elements are `r_j = A ⊗ β_j` and `t_i = α_i ⊗ B`. Their differentials are

```
∂r_j = c_1j + u₁ − c_0j − u₀,     ∂t_i = c_i0 + v₁ − c_i1 − v₀.
```

So when only A varies, the Gray structure has a naturality 4-cell from the "χ first" side to the "variation first" side (V ⇛ U). When only B varies, it goes the other way (U ⇛ V). The simultaneous variation is the 5-cell A ⊗ B. It goes between two routes of 4-cells, `S → V → T` ⇛ `S → U → T`, where S and T are the composites through c₀₁ and c₁₀. In short, the higher structure is **tensor-shaped**: one Leibniz-signed naturality cell per variable, and higher cells between routes.

**(K4) Supports must use an expanded basis.** If every composite's support were the union of the supports of its parts, L and R would always have equal support. Then "support determines the cell" would force strict interchange.

**(K5) One finite basis forces finitely many cells.** If one finite set B admits injective support maps in every dimension, the structure has finitely many non-identity cells in total.

**(K6) No single directed complex presents the answer, and neither does a hom-wise family of them.** In ν(K), the two composites defining χ have the same chain table, so they are equal. A description must therefore be an iterated tower: objects, then homs presented again by objects and homs, and so on.

**(K7) The first uniform case through hom-dimension 3.** For Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², directing interchange inside the hom 𝒦(x, z) adds exactly two generators of hom-dimension 3 beyond the 25 tensor generators. They are the interchangers χ₁ of (A⊗g, f′⊗B) and χ₂ of (f⊗B, A⊗g′), the only pairs of hom-2-cells composable along a hom-0-cell. Their two sides have different supports. Neither occurs in the boundary of A ⊗ B.

**(K8) The (K7) 3-skeleton cannot be extended by 4-generators alone.** Write h_i^j = α_i ⊗ g_j, k_j^i = f_i ⊗ β_j, P_ij = k_j^0 h_i^1, Q_ij = h_i^0 k_j^1, so c_ij : P_ij ⇒ Q_ij. The tensor 3-generators are r_j : c_0j ; a_j^+ ⇛ a_j^- ; c_1j and t_i : b_i^- ; c_i1 ⇛ c_i0 ; b_i^+ (a_j^±, b_i^± the whiskered edge cells u_j, v_i), and the interchangers are χ₁ : b_0^+ ; a_1^+ ⇛ a_0^+ ; b_1^+ and χ₂ : a_0^- ; b_1^- ⇛ b_0^- ; a_1^-. The six composite hom-2-cells P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ are S = b_0^- ; c_01 ; a_1^+, V₀ = c_00 ; b_0^+ ; a_1^+, V₁ = c_00 ; a_0^+ ; b_1^+, T = a_0^- ; c_10 ; b_1^+, U₀ = a_0^- ; b_1^- ; c_11 and U₁ = b_0^- ; a_1^- ; c_11. Whiskering gives t̄₀ : S ⇛ V₀, χ̄₁ : V₀ ⇛ V₁, r̄₀ : V₁ ⇛ T, r̄₁ : S ⇛ U₁, χ̄₂ = χ₂ ∗₁ 1_{c₁₁} : U₀ ⇛ U₁ and t̄₁ : U₀ ⇛ T. Since ∂(A⊗B) = t₁ − t₀ + r₁ − r₀, requirement 4 forces a 4-cell between the route t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀ from S to T and a route from S to T using r̄₁ and t̄₁. In the free 3-skeleton with exactly the 25 tensor generators plus χ₁, χ₂ and no 2-equations, the first route exists, but the second would have to go S ⇛ U₁ and then U₁ ⇛ U₀ against χ̄₂: in the directed graph of these composites the only S → T path is t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀. So no list of 4-generators recovers A ⊗ B. The naturality cells of χ₁, χ₂ are trivial here (the edge homs have no 3-generators).

The repair must change dimension-3 data: an extra 3-generator such as h : S ⇛ U₀ whose image under the Gray quotient is r̄₁, or a 2-equation such as U₀ = U₁. Inverting χ₂ would also work but breaks requirement 3.

**(K9) The orientation of χ₁, χ₂ in (K7) is forced by substitution (proved).** One might try to avoid (K8) by orienting every inner interchanger by the factor order of the enclosing tensor (always "Θ₂-cell first ⇛ Θ₁-cell first"), which keeps χ₁ and reverses χ₂. This fails requirement 5. The free object on Σ(D² ∨ D²) has, inside its hom, the interchanger of its two 2-cells A′ (left) and B′ (right), oriented as in the Gray case: "B′ first ⇛ A′ first". The assignment A′ ↦ f₀ ⊗ B and B′ ↦ A ⊗ g₁ (with the hom-0-cells and hom-1-cells sent to f₀⊗g₀, f₀⊗g₁, f₁⊗g₁ and to the matching edges) respects all boundaries, so substitution sends that interchanger to a 3-cell from "A ⊗ g₁ first" to "f₀ ⊗ B first", which is exactly χ₂ in its (K7) orientation. The pair χ₁ is forced the same way via A′ ↦ A ⊗ g₀, B′ ↦ f₁ ⊗ B. So χ₂ cannot be reversed: adding the reversed cell as well breaks requirement 3, which is the "inverting χ₂" option above. Inner interchangers are oriented by position (right cell first ⇛ left cell first), as at the outer level, and some repair of dimension-3 data is needed (see (K10)).

**(K10) The smallest case is repaired by a factorization.** The six hom-2 composites P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ of (K8) are the maximal paths of an oriented 3-cube with coordinates A (edges a_j^±), B (edges b_i^±) and C (edges c_ij): S = BCA, V₀ = CBA, V₁ = CAB, T = ACB, U₀ = ABC, U₁ = BAC. The six whiskered hom-3 cells t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀, r̄₁, χ̄₂, t̄₁ are its six square faces (adjacent transpositions); every proper interval carries at most one of them. Add one hom-3 generator and one hom-3 equation:

```
h : S ⇛ U₀,        r̄₁ = h ∗₂ χ̄₂.        (F)
```

Then the maximal hom-3 interval is the free category on S → V₀ → V₁ → T, S → U₀ → U₁, U₀ → T, with 11 non-identity cells with pairwise distinct label sets, two routes S → T (t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ and h ; t̄₁), and no reverse of χ̄₁ or χ̄₂. Killing the inner interchangers gives exactly the free Gray diamond with q(h) = q(r̄₁), and A ⊗ B lifts to one 4-generator W : t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ ⇛ h ; t̄₁ with no non-identity contexts. Supports use one label per whiskered context, so r̄₁ is no longer a basis element (its support is that of h χ̄₂). The equation (F) cannot be dropped or replaced by a 4-cell h χ̄₂ ⇛ r̄₁ without a new class of cells killed by the quotient. h is not an interchanger: S and U₀ each contain a c_ij, and every hom-2 path containing a c_ij spans the whole external hom-0 interval.

h is **not forced**. The mirror repair k : U₁ ⇛ T with t̄₁ = χ̄₂ ∗₂ k works equally well, and so does the symmetric repair with both h and k and both equations (12 cells, second route h ; χ̄₂ ; k, same exact Gray quotient). The alternative 2-equation U₀ = U₁ makes χ̄₂ an endomorphism and needs further equations (at least χ̄₂² = χ̄₂ under set supports), so it is not preferred.

**(K11) The first larger case, ΣD³ ∨ ΣD², is completed through its top cell.** Write the first factor as α₀, α₁ : f₀ → f₁, A⁰, A¹ : α₀ ⇒ α₁, Ω : A⁰ ⇛ A¹, and the second as β₀, β₁ : g₀ → g₁, B : β₀ ⇒ β₁. - **Hom-4 faces.** W^ε = A^ε ⊗ B : a^ε b^ε c^ε ⇛ h^ε f^ε. R_j = Ω ⊗ β_j. The naturalities N₁, N₂ of χ₁, χ₂ have opposite directions: Ω varies the left input of χ₁ and the right input of χ₂. Two inner interchangers along a hom-1 cell, 𝖠 : z_S a¹ ⇛ a⁰ z_{V₀} (of t₀ with Ω) and 𝖥 : f⁰ z_T ⇛ z_{U₀} f¹. Writing 𝖡, 𝖢, 𝖣, 𝖤 for the whiskered N₁, R₀, R₁, N₂, the maximal hom-4 interval has the seven hom-3 paths x₁ … x₇ from S⁰ to T¹.
- **Added data.** Two hom-4 generators and two equations:
  ```
  H : h⁰ z_{U₀} ⇛ z_S h¹,                H e¹ = (h⁰ 𝖤) ; 𝖣                 (variation of the factorization)
  K : a⁰ z_{V₀} b¹ c¹ ⇛ z_S h¹ f¹,       z_S W¹ = (𝖠 b¹ c¹) ; K            (k-type factorization in hom-dimension 4)
  ```
  Then there are exactly two routes x₁ ⇛ x₇: L = (W⁰ z_T) ; (h⁰ 𝖥) ; (H f¹) and R = (a⁰ b⁰ 𝖢) ; (a⁰ 𝖡 c¹) ; K. The top generator Z = Ω ⊗ B : L ⇛ R has the orientation of ∂(Ω ⊗ B) = W¹ − W⁰ − R₁ + R₀.
- **Checks.** On the maximal interval there are 68 hom-3 cells and 98 hom-4 cells, all with distinct supports; the graphs are acyclic, and the reductions have no overlaps. The Gray quotient is exact: q(H) = q(R₁ whiskered), q(K) = q(z_S W¹), and the surviving tensor generators are R₀, R₁, W⁰, W¹.
- **Forcing substitution.** A⁰, A¹ ↦ A, Ω ↦ 1_A sends H ↦ 1_h, K ↦ W and Z ↦ 1_W, so K is not a cell that every degeneration kills.
- **The source/sink pattern.** In both the h-repair and the k-repair of (K10), the face 𝖠 (whose pair flips relative to the tensor faces) creates a second source and a second sink one dimension up. With source-joining (h) in hom-dimension 3, the one-face joining that works in hom-dimension 4 is sink-joining (K); a source-joining alternative would have to factor a composite. Each existing factorization also needs a variation cell and equation under each higher variable (H).

**(K12) The source/sink count fails in the ambient graph.** Reversing one arrow u → v of a finite directed graph changes the number of sources by 1[d⁻(v)=1] − 1[d⁻(u)=0], and the number of sinks by 1[d⁺(u)=1] − 1[d⁺(v)=0].
- Take the interval of λD² ⊗ λD² ⊗ λD² with hom-1 boundary (β₀α₀)γ₀ → (α₁β₁)γ₁. Its hom-2 paths are the 24 orderings of A, B (updates of the first two factors), C (the crossing βα → αβ) and D (the third-factor update).
- Its arrows are BC → CB, CA → AC and DX → XD. The arrow between A and B is positional: AB → BA when C follows the pair, and BA → AB when C precedes it.
- Compare with the graph that uses BA → AB everywhere, which has one source, DBCA, and one sink, ACBD. The positional graph has sources {DBCA, DABC} and sinks {ACBD, BACD}.
- Of the three reversed arrows, BADC → ABDC creates neither a source nor a sink.

So a flipped face does not create one extra source/sink pair in the full comparison graph. It does so only for an isolated polygon.

**(K13) Local factorization does not force alternation.** For a simple oriented polygon with arms pᵢ : sᵢ → tᵢ and qᵢ : sᵢ₊₁ → tᵢ (indices mod r), add hᵢ : s₀ → sᵢ for 1 ≤ i < r, with p₀ = h₁;q₀ and hᵢ;pᵢ = hᵢ₊₁;qᵢ for 1 ≤ i < r − 1. The boundary telescopes to h_{r−1}+p_{r−1}−q_{r−1}, a pair of parallel paths s₀ → t_{r−1}. So source-joining alone always works locally. For r = 2 this is (K10). For r ≥ 3 the equations identify two composites; this is harmless locally (K16).

**(K15) The interchanger-of-a-composite convention.** The substitution in (K14) assumes that crossing against a composite hom-1 path is the composite of the pairwise crossings. That holds in Gray categories, but it does not follow from (B), associativity and units alone.

**(K16) Source-only polygons keep injective supports (proved).** In (K13), every relation runs from the unique source s₀ to a sink, so no relation has a context. The presented category is thin except at (s₀, t_{r−1}), which has two arrows. Orienting the relations gives a one-step normal form, and the edge set of the normal form is an injective support for every r and every arm length. A union-preserving injective invariant with 2|V| + 1 labels also exists. Caveat: overlapping systems such as a;c = b;d, a;d = b;c have no injective union-support.

**(K17) Only one sink matters, and the top cell lifts (proved).**
- *The sink.* Undirected inner interchanges split the 240 words of (K14) into 54 classes. These are the fibres of the tensor hom-2 chain. Only one of the ten sinks, T = apqbrc, lies in the class of the top atom's hom-2 target. The source is S = crqbpa.
- *Paths.* The words reaching T carry 244 arrows and 1,226 paths S → T, with 25 distinct tensor hom-3 chains.
- *The lift.* Keeping all hom-3 data, the top cell lifts as follows:
  - 24 hom-4 operations A_{ij} : P_i ⇒ P_j between chosen paths, 15 of them new and 9 literal whiskerings of the W-cells;
  - six parallel hom-4 routes R₀ … R₅ : P₂₄ ⇒ P₀;
  - six hom-5 generators F_x^±, F_y^±, F_z^± between those routes;
  - one hom-6 generator Ω : F_x^- F_y^- F_z^- ⇒ F_z^+ F_y^+ F_x^+, whose image is the atom x₂ ⊗ y₂ ⊗ z₂ in every dimension.
- *Scope.* This is an extension with a map to ν(K), not yet one whose inner-interchange quotient is ν(K).

**(K18) Source-only completion of a comparison graph (proved).** Let Q be a finite quiver whose generating arrows are split into inner arrows E and positive arrows P, with E acyclic and a rank φ : V(Q) → ℤ constant along E and strictly increasing along P. For every α : s → t in P and every literal directed E-path R : u → t, adjoin j_{α,R} : s → u (with j_{α,1_t} = α), and impose j_{α,e;R} ; e = j_{α,R} for e : u → v in E and R : v → t. Then:
- the set of generators is finite and does not depend on the order of saturation;
- read as contractions (positive arrow followed by an inner arrow), the equations are confluent and terminating, so every path has a unique normal form;
- the completion is acyclic, normal forms have injective edge-set supports, the original path category embeds, and killing E gives exactly the original inner quotient.

On the 240-word interval of (K14) a rank with values 0, …, 7 exists; there are 264 positive seed arrows and 877 positive generators after completion (613 new, 1,117 arrows in all), and 54 inner classes. The completion is natural under maps of comparison categories that send positive seeds to positive seeds and inner arrows to inner paths.

**(K19) The completion is not natural under generator-to-composite maps (proved).** In (K14), take x = bacrqp, u = abpcqr, w₁ = abcpqr, w₂ = bapcqr, v = bacpqr, with inner arrows p : u → w₁, e : w₁ → v, q : u → w₂, f : w₂ → v, and the tensor arrow τ : x → v with its factorization τ = h_z ; f. The completion has j_{τ,e} : x → w₁ with j_{τ,e} ; e = τ. The comparison-category map fixing inner arrows and sending τ ↦ h_z ; f does not extend: an image H : x → w₁ with H ; e = h_z ; f would be j_{h_z,R} ; P with inner paths R, P and P ; e = R ; f as literal inner words, impossible since e ≠ f (contractions preserve the ordered list of positive seeds). This map has not been realised as a substitution of globular generators, so it does not refute requirement 5.

**(K20) Two-sided division is not confluent (proved).** In (K14), with tensor arrows α : bapcqr → bapqcr and β : abpqcr → abpqrc and the inner arrow e : abpqcr → bapqcr, adjoining both a source division h : bapcqr → abpqcr (h ; e = α) and a sink division k : bapqcr → abpqrc (e ; k = β) gives the critical pair h ; e ; k → α ; k and → h ; β, both irreducible and distinct. The presented category satisfies the composite–composite equation α ; k = h ; β. It is not of the crossed shape a;c = b;d, a;d = b;c.

**(K21) Hom-4 comparisons and exactness through hom-dimension 3 (proved).**
- *Disjoint operations.* For two hom-3 operations α (left) and β (right) on disjoint intervals of a hom-2 word, D_{α,β} : (β ; α^β) ⇛ (α ; β^α), right region first ⇛ left region first. On the 240-word interval there are 258 distinct such boundary pairs (50 need eliminated operations expanded through the factorization equations of (K14)).
- *Inner naturality.* For a hom-3 operation α : U ⇛ V and an independent hom-2 update z, comparing "slide z across U, then α" with "α, then slide z across V": for factor degrees (2,1) inner crossing first ⇛ operation first, for (1,2) the reverse. This agrees with the Leibniz signs of (K3). There are 62 distinct local comparisons N. They use (K15) as a convention.
- *Exactness.* On the top atom's hom-1 boundary, imposing I = D = N = 1 (I the inner interchangers) gives 54 hom-2 classes and 874 hom-3 path classes, which are exactly the numbers of 2-cells and 3-cells of ν(λD² ⊗ λD² ⊗ λD²) with that 1-boundary (identities included). Without N it is not exact: the single-arrow equations leave 152 generator classes over 116 tableaux, and two-arrow equations cannot merge them.
- The D and N comparisons introduce no reverse path in the comparison graph they generate on the raw hom-3 paths.

**(K22) The fifteen fresh hom-4 lifts of (K17) (proved after the lower quotient).** After I = D = N = 1, every one of the 15 new operations A_{ij} has an expression through an existing W-cell (W_x, W_y, W_z or their (2,2,0)-type analogues W_{xy}, W_{xz}) with prefix and suffix whiskerings and the same quotient boundary.   - If p ≠ 1, then on Γ_{3,4} with A = a₁a₂a₃, B = b₁b₂, C = b₃b₄, the cells X = B p(A,C) and Y = p(A,B) C are non-identity 4-endomorphisms composable along a 2-cell. Raw support makes X, Y and X ∗₂ Y idempotent, so the elementary 5-interchanger on (X, X, Y, Y) is an endomorphism with the identity as a reverse.
  - If p = 1, then e is invertible and non-identity. The *inverse-input lemma* then gives a reverse elementary 5-interchanger for E = B e(A,C) and K = e(A,B) C: for an invertible E : u → u′, whisker I₅(E⁻¹, K) by Ev on the left and Ev′ on the right and cancel.
  In particular "(C∞): every 4-endomorphism is an identity" is refuted: it forces p = 1 while keeping V ≠ W.
- *Hence* every extension must impose **(R): 12 22 11 32 21 31 = χ(a₁a₂a₃, b₁b₂)**, as a scheme under substitution. Requirement 4 allows (R), since both sides have the same Gray image. None of its degenerations (aᵢ = 1 or bⱼ = 1) is a bare interchange equation: each is already a composite-crossing expansion of a smaller rectangle.
- *Limits of the argument.* The case p ≠ 1 uses T's raw edge-set support equations. Idempotence of X and Y alone would not give idempotence of X ∗₂ Y (in ⟨x, y | x² = x, y² = y⟩ the words (yx)ⁿ are distinct), so requirement 2 in the expanded basis is not refuted.

**(K39) (R) alone is not enough, and neither is (R∞) with (C) (computed).** Computer calculations with the same model as (K37), adding (R) in all substitution instances and both orientations:
- The non-rectangular ("non-guillotine") linear extensions of the smallest grids are V on 3 × 2 and its mirror 13 12 23 11 22 21 on 2 × 3; the latter is the base of (K37)'s rotation R.

The dichotomy of (K38) applies to each remaining pair, so the 3-level repair must be at least **(R∞): every crossing path BA → AB equals χ(A, B)**, an infinite family of base patterns.
- Even T + (R∞) + (C) keeps one extreme endomorphism on Σ[2] ∨ Σ[4]. Under (R∞) every ι-edge between extreme paths is a loop at χ(A, B), and support makes its endomorphism monoid a join-semilattice, so (C) kills each edge of an H instance; but some edges lie in no H instance (e.g. ι((1,3),(2,4)) at b₁b₂b₃a₁b₄a₂). The refutation of (C∞) in (K38) does not apply once (R∞) makes the endpoints equal.
- In the expanded basis (supports of 3-cells as sets of contextual 3-edges (word, crossing)), V's six edges lie inside the union of the edge sets of W's four representatives, but no representative has V's edge set and the four sets differ pairwise. - On 3 × 3 (Σ[3] ∨ Σ[3]), T + (R) + (C) and T + (R∞) + (C) leave no endomorphisms on the part the computation decides; one cell remains undecided, presumably the extreme one.

These survivals are computed, not proved; they depend on the completeness of the list of T's equations used.

**(K40) Why the candidate is restricted.** A uniform statement that subsumes (R∞) and the 4-level repair must be restricted in two ways.
- *Extreme words only.* Between intermediate words, e.g. b₁a₁b₂a₂ → a₁b₁a₂b₂, the two orders of crossings are joined by a 2-whiskered elementary ι, which requirement 3 needs to be a non-identity cell.

Within these limits the statement does not conflict with requirement 4, since all such cells become identities in the Gray quotient.

**(K41) Absorbed interchangers: T + (C) fails in dimension 5 (proved).** For 4-cells X : x₀ ⇒ x₁ and Y : y₀ ⇒ y₁ on consecutive 3-path intervals, the elementary (2,3)-interchanger is the 5-cell (x₀Y)·(Xy₁) ⇛ (Xy₀)·(x₁Y). If Xq = 1 for a 3-path q, then x₀q = x₁q, and for Y = qJ the two boundaries coincide, so the identity is a reverse. Dually, if pY = 1, take X = Jp. With (K35), **no extension of T satisfies requirements 3–5 through dimension 5**. This includes T + (C) + (R∞) + the thin-block principle, and on Γ₃,₂ a smaller witness exists for that theory: X : 12 22 11 ⇒ 12 11 22 and Y : 32 21 31 ⇒ 21 32 31. All four corners are the same extreme block by (R∞), the four whiskered edges are identities by the thin-block principle, and X and Y have distinct endpoints and no reverse. Since T's support equations force (C) ((K35)), the obstruction is to T itself. A theory that omits those equations is not covered: there (C) is forbidden by this argument but not forced.

**(K42) No finite degree-4 basis without (C) (proved).** Assume Godement composition and functorial whiskering as in (K30), the outer law (K15), and requirements 3–5 through dimension 6, with arbitrary data in dimensions 3–5. Then Γ₂,₄ has infinitely many 4-cells; in fact some 4-endomorphism there has pairwise distinct positive powers.
- First, directedness gives two facts, each proved by adjoining a fresh globe as a test cell and contracting. A composite along dimension n−1 that equals an identity has identity factors. Whiskering reflects identities.
- If the degree-4 cells were finite, the doubled H of (K35) would give non-identity idempotents x on u and y on v. For d = (uy)·(xv) the rectangle gives I : d ⇒ d², non-identity.
- For an idempotent P = dᵏ, this produces a non-identity Z′ : P ⇒ P with PZ′ = Z′ = Z′P. The (3,4)-interchanger on (Z′, Z′) is then absorbed, so it has an identity reverse in dimension 6.

This step uses an interchanger whose inputs are *endomorphisms*.

**(K43) Requirement 3′ is consistent in every dimension (proved).** Let S be the free strict theory and G_j the free theory with j levels of cartesian enrichment outside one Gray enrichment, strict inside. The term theory whose terms are generated by the operations of T (without T's raw 4-support equations) and whose equations are exactly those valid in all the coordinates G_j satisfies requirements 1, 2 (in an expanded basis), 3′, 4 and 5 in every dimension, with exact Gray and strict quotients. For any fixed finitary upper data, the least theory satisfying 3′ is the closure under the rule "if e : u → v is elementary and some d : v → u exists, then u = v and e = 1", reapplying term formation after each equality. This closure never identifies cells with different Gray images. These theories have H = 1.

**(K44) The inner composite-crossing law is optional (proved).** In a tagged model M (2-cells of the long hom of G(Σ[1] ∨ Σ[2]) tagged by subsets of {(1,1),(1,2)}, mixed square on positive arrows a, b tagged {(ℓ(a), ℓ(b))}), the direct ι(A, B; C) has tag {(1,2)} and the expanded composite has tag {(1,1)}, with the same Gray boundary. Adding M as a coordinate keeps 3′ and the exact quotients. So the inner law is neither forced nor inconsistent.

**(K45) The support-free presentation P through degree four (proved).** P has the sesquicategory operations, the operations and equations of (K30) except its raw 4-support equations, and the binary join of (K32) at every pair of adjacent outer spans: h(A, B) : S → U₀ with the factorization r̄₁ = h(A, B) · ῑ₋, and W(A, B) between the two routes. In P, H^k ≠ H^ℓ for k ≠ ℓ (on Γ_{2,2r} the r copies of H generate a free monoid), strong elementary directedness holds through degree four, and the Gray and strict quotients are exact there. A positive integer weight on all nonunit contextual 4-edges bounds the length of every 4-path, so the expanded basis is decidable and reachability between parallel 4-cells on a finite diagram is decidable (implemented: `scripts/e6/interchange_free.py`).

**(K46) Degree five of P is not exact (proved).** With two composable 3-generators on the left and one on the right, the direct binary atom on the composite and the paste of the two atoms remain distinct after all inner families are made identities. A Gray cell on a strip of length m has at least 2^{m−1} lifts.

**(K47) The completed theory T₁₂ (proved).** Extending P by: a positive length conserved by every 5-cell and the one-variable tensor operations in every dimension (Response 8); the subdivision equations identifying the fibres of (K46) (Response 9); signed lifts of all binary globe tensors at every cut, including the (3,3)-atom (Response 10); and unguarded equations subdividing the stationary edge of a unary cylinder and transporting it past a fixed outer 1-arrow, together with the auxiliaries H_L, H_R, K, J and the cut-one shifted join (Response 12). **(K48) Directedness in degree ≥ 6 (proved).** In T₁₂ every proper elementary rectangle of output degree at least six has no reverse, including rectangles with nonunit inactive arguments. On every two-row grid Γ_{2,s} strong directedness holds in all degrees.

**(K49) Degree-five directedness (proved).** Write a degree-five temporal rectangle as CDAB ⇒ CADB with active inputs A (early) and D (late) and inactive inputs B, C. In T₁₂ no proper such rectangle has a reverse, on any finite pasting diagram, with arbitrary inactive inputs and arbitrary powers of loops. The proof reduces to one interval (eight consecutive outer factors Σ[1], one 2-slot each, from the 2-word 87654321 to 12345678) by a retaining substitution, and then uses integer scores that every equation preserves, every directed rule weakly raises, and the designated exchange strictly raises. Examples: inversion counts of early-before-late incidences on a common slot; for two inner witnesses on quartets G (early) and S (late), the cross-weight Σ_{i<j} p(v_i) l(v_j) along a 4-route when G ∩ S ≠ ∅, and, when G and S are disjoint, a count of the events of each earlier early unit that lie in the fixed prefix of a later late unit or precede its left event inside the left operand.

**(K50) Requirements 1–5 for T₁₂ (proved).** Free objects are the well-typed contextual paths on the generators of T₁₂ modulo its equations. Positive gradings bound path length at fixed boundaries, so the finite alphabet at fixed boundaries and induction on degree give normal representatives and a decidable word problem. The expanded basis records generator occurrences by position, so the powers of H are pairwise distinct and the basis is infinite already on Γ_{2,2}. Substitution is evaluation of terms. With (K47)–(K49) this gives requirements 1–5 in every dimension. **No normal-form theorem in closed form is known above degree four**: the description is a decidable presentation, not a list of normal forms.

**(K51) What T₁₂ excludes and what is optional (proved).** No extension of T imposing (C) satisfies requirements 1–5, already on Γ_{3,3} in dimension five; with (C) retained one only gets the weakening 3′ of (K43). The inner composite-crossing law is optional for the strong theory too: a joint model with a tagged copy of the marked suspension (only identities above global degree four, tags in the long hom) separates the direct inner crossing from its two-step expansion. Its projection to T₁₂ reflects identities on free diagrams, so requirement 3 holds without the test algebra being directed, and after the inner identities both theories have the same algebras. So there are at least two non-equivalent theories satisfying 1–5 with H ≠ 1, differing in the inner composite-crossing law.

**(K52) Normal forms for T₁₂ in every degree (proved).** Order contextual letters by a fixed well-order and paths with common endpoints by shortlex. By induction on the positive path weight (block crossings weigh the product of their operand lengths; higher letters use the gradings of (K50)), build dictionaries R_j: at weight j, join in one graph the reduced sides of every defining pair of weight j and the reduced branches of every proper overlap of two earlier rules whose union has weight j, and orient each component to its shortlex minimum. The union of the R_j is a convergent rewriting system presenting the path congruence, so the normal d-cells are exactly the typed paths of normal (d−1)-labelled letters that contain no factor in ∪_j dom R_j. On each layer of fixed lower boundary, conserved weight and weight cutoff this is a finite automaton. The forbidden lists are produced recursively, and can be infinite over all weights; no closed combinatorial description of them is known.

**(K53) The absorption closure of T's own data fails requirement 4 (proved).** For T + (C) + (R∞) + the thin-block equations, on Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², the cells of the Gray theory through global degree four together with identities above form a sub-model closed under all of T's operations: T's only outer degree-five operations are the unary families of hom bidegrees (3,1) and (1,3), which have no proper inputs there. Absorption and its inner quotient interpret in this sub-model, so the comparison to Gray misses the atom a ⊗ b. The failure comes from T's signature (it lacks the binary join of (K45)); for upper data with exact quotients, (K43) shows absorption preserves exactness.

**(K54) No initial theory over P (proved).** Morphisms are the interpretations defined in §4 (identity on P, commuting with the Gray and strict projections). There is an admissible target with an involution over P, Gray and strict: two copies of the upper declarations of T₁₂ sharing P and the structural operations, plus a coordinate M finite in each degree. On the arity Σ³(Σ[1] ∨ Σ[1]) the long hom is the directed square grid with **two** generating 2-cells b₀, b₁ : v → h, copy i evaluating the mixed square at b_i. The swap of copies is an interpretation over P, and it acts freely on the possible images of one required comparison, so no candidate admits a *unique* interpretation into this target. Weak initiality fails as well ((K57)).

**(K55) The fixed-signature poset (proved).** With the signature of the T₁₂ completion fixed and theories ordered by inclusion of laws, the admissible law sets form a union of Boolean intervals whose endpoints are given by finite proof supports and the Gray and strict operation tables; this handles simultaneous additions and omissions. Already among the operations of P there are **infinitely many independently selectable crossing laws** L_p (p prime), separated by models M_q with a tag [a ≠ 0][b proper] on ℤ/q, and the right composite-crossing law R is independent of all of them. No decision procedure for membership is given.

**(K56) An infinite divisibility family (proved).** On the single T₁₂ signature there are admissible theories U_N (N ≥ 1; the marked table of (K55) with left arrow group ℤ/N) such that an interpretation U_N → U_M over P, Gray and strict exists iff M | N. They are separated by the left-split laws D_{d,p} (the direct crossing of A^{dp} past B equals the paste of p crossings of A^d), which use only P, with U_N ⊨ D_{d,p} iff v_p(N) ≤ v_p(d). The subfamily U₂, U₄, U₈, … all satisfy the right split R, fail L₂ and satisfy every odd L_p, yet are pairwise inequivalent. So the interpretation preorder contains the divisibility order on positive integers (reversed), and the theories are not classified by the laws R and L_p.

**(K57) No weakly initial theory over P (proved).** For every admissible theory S there is an admissible theory U with no interpretation from S that fixes P and commutes with the Gray projection. Proof idea: on the strip C_{2,1} of (K46) the single crossing W(A₁ ∗₂ A₂, B) and the paste of W(A₁,B) and W(A₂,B) are equal in every admissible theory's Gray image, and S proves the corresponding equality (through its comparison operation K_S) with a finite derivation of bounded dimension; U_m adds two operations so that an algebra D_m over G(C), with bit-decorated 5-cells and a high-dimensional table E_m (cells {1, x, y} with ab = a in degree m), reflects identities below m and separates the two expressions, so no interpretation of S's derivation exists when m exceeds its dimensions. **(K58) The least pasting profile (proved).** Let B consist of the structural operations, P, the face and unit declarations, the outer unary subdivisions in both variables, and the exterior and intervening vertex transports for these unary operations. **Every admissible theory on the fixed signature contains B**: on each generic unary arity, an inner call at cut k would need operands of effective rank > k whose k-faces are the two distinct k-hemispheres of the moving globe (the case k = p − 1 is excluded by a coefficient count in D^{p−1} ⊗ [1]), so all inner operations take their killed values and requirement 4 forces the Gray table. Let **V** be B together with the guarded quotient equations of round 5 (including the guarded identification of shifted joins), the unguarded unary laws at cut zero, and none of the unguarded inner splits. Then F_V(Γ) = F_B(Γ) for every finite pasting diagram Γ (the guarded equations hold in F_B by identity reflection to F_T₁₂), V is admissible, and for every admissible E there is a generator-preserving map F_V(Γ) → F_E(Γ). So **a directed cell x → y, or an equality x = y, between expressions exists in F_V(Γ) iff it exists in every admissible theory.** Equality and reachability in F_V(Γ) are decidable: weights pulled back from T₁₂ bound path length, the contextual alphabet at fixed boundaries is finite, and equality is connectivity on the finite top-weight layer. No explicit description of F_V(Γ) (normal forms, or which T₁₂ identifications it lacks) is known.

**(K59) Visibility of the catalogued laws (proved).** Call a law *visible* if it fails in F_V(Γ) for some finite pasting diagram Γ. The right split R is visible (its two sides differ in the free object of the marked theory of (K51) on the diagram of (K31), hence in F_V), and so is the inner composite-crossing law (the tagged model of (K51), on the strip C_{2,1}). **Every L_p and every D_{d,p} is invisible**: every chronological 3-endomorphism in a free object on a finite pasting diagram is an identity (a nonempty normalized 3-history has positive top Gray chain, and the Gray homs are loop-free). The loops H^k of (K45) are 4-endomorphisms, so these laws do not act on them. Free objects do contain 4-loops: in (K63) two weight-one 4-loops A, B on (Σ[1])^{∨8} generate loops at a 3-cell d with a sorting 5-cell between j_R(B)j_L(A) and j_L(A)j_R(B).

**(K60) No minimal admissible law set (proved).** Below **every** admissible closed law set A there is a descending sequence A_N of admissible law sets, all with the pasting profile of V, with no admissible lower bound. A_N is the common Horn theory of all A-algebras, all F_B(Δ), and tables D_1, …, D_N on the once-suspended (3,3) disk C of round 5, Response 10: D_n tags the bridge auxiliary G with 1 on the proper short pair and labels the bare square at cut m − 2 (m = 7) by [b > 0 and a = nb] on tags a, b; the cut-5 power G^{[n]} detects the mark at exponent n. Every Horn law true in all D_n holds in the limit table D_∞, which satisfies the inner identities with G ≠ 1, whereas Gray has G = 1. Exactness of each A_N uses separation by ultraproducts of the generating tables. So the intersection of all admissible law sets is not admissible, the poset of (K55) has no least or minimal element, and the least object exists only at the level of pasting profiles ((K58)).

**(K61) Upper side and global shape (proved).** T₁₂ is not maximal: the laws σ_m (m ≥ 8; the two Godement schedules at cut m − 2 on adjacent idempotent m-endomorphisms agree) are admissible additions, independent of each other, all with T₁₂'s pasting profile, so subsets of them embed 𝒫(ℕ) into the interpretation preorder. On the fixed signature every admissible closed law set lies under a maximal one (compactness of the finite clauses of (K55), and Zorn), and for a fixed pasting profile the largest law set with that profile and both quotients is the common theory of its free tables and the two benchmarks. With variable signature there is no maximal class, and the marked theory of (K51), extended by named copies b(p) of the (K31) diagram with r(b(p)) = s(b(p)) ⇒ H(p) = 1, has no common target with T₁₂: the preorder is not directed, not a lattice, and has no (weakly) terminal theory.

**(K62) Interpretation-equivalence is not classifiable (proved).** Extending T by a vertex o(p) with loops a_g(p) (g ∈ G) at each H-tuple, with a_g = a_h ⇒ H = 1, gives T_G → T_J iff G embeds in J. Bi-embeddability of countable groups is a complete analytic equivalence relation (Williams, arXiv 1306.1270), so there is no Borel classification by countable structures.

**(K63) Admissibility is undecidable (proved).** Finite signature extensions by unconditional equations (inverse loops on an (m−1)-cell with the relators of a finitely presented group) are admissible iff the group is trivial. On the original signature, the set of single finitary Horn laws whose addition to T₁₂ is admissible is co-r.e.-hard: a Diophantine equation is encoded by gates on the 4-loops of (K59), whose commutation premises force the trace word to be (L^m R)^n, so a weight equation r = mn; the law kills a proper comparison exactly when a solution exists. Both reductions use formats (new symbols, or Horn premises with witness variables and commutation conditions) that a type checker would not add.

**(K64) Forbidden factors (proved).** On a typed layer with words p_0, …, p_{N−1} in shortlex order and raw defining-pair incidence matrix C, the binary digits of b = ord_t det(CCᵀ + diag(t, t², t⁴, …, t^{2^{N−1}})) are the normal-word indicators of (K52); the forbidden factors are the non-normal words whose two maximal proper factors are normal.

## 3. Requirements

A **tensor-shaped lax-interchange ω-category** has, for every k < l, elementary interchangers χ : L ⇛ R for (l+1)-cells with no reverse cell in free objects; one naturality cell per varying argument, oriented by the Leibniz sign; higher cells between routes of lower naturality cells; and coherence equations to be determined. The free objects F(Γ) must satisfy:
1. an explicit combinatorial description by generators with explicit boundaries;
2. two cells of the same dimension using the same generators, in the expanded basis, are equal;
3. every elementary interchanger exists and its reverse does not;
4. making all interchangers with k ≥ 1 identities gives the free Gray-enriched category, and making all interchangers identities gives the free strict ω-category;
5. closure under substitution of cells for generators.


## 4. Questions

*Conventions.* "Admissible" means satisfying requirements 1–5 of §3 in every dimension with H ≠ 1. The *pasting profile* of a theory is the family of its free objects F(Γ) on finite pasting diagrams Γ, with the identifications and directed cells between expressions in the signature. A law is *visible* if it fails in F_V(Γ) for some finite pasting diagram Γ, with V as in (K58). A *pasting-diagram law* is an unconditional equation u = v (or a directed cell u → v) between two expressions over one finite pasting diagram, imposed for all substitution instances; this is the form a law takes when a type checker switches it on.

**Q1 (main: V's free objects and what lies above them on pasting diagrams).**
- (a) **An explicit description of F_V(Γ).** Describe the cells of F_V(Γ) in every degree in the sense of requirement 1: explicit generators and boundaries, or normal forms, uniform in the degree. Equivalently, describe the kernel of the comparison F_V(Γ) → F_T₁₂(Γ): which identifications and which directed cells T₁₂ adds on pasting diagrams, in closed form (for example by decorated Gray cells, as asked for T₁₂ in (K52)).
- (b) **Classification of pasting profiles.** Describe all pasting profiles of admissible theories, ordered by the maps of (K58). Is every profile the profile of V plus a set of visible pasting-diagram laws? Is the poset of profiles a lattice? Are there infinitely many independent visible laws (the catalogued visible laws so far are R and the inner composite-crossing law), and does it contain infinite antichains or the divisibility order of (K56)?
- (c) **A visibility criterion.** Give a criterion, decidable if possible, for whether a given law (a pasting-diagram law, or a finitary Horn law as in (K55)) is visible. If visibility is undecidable, say for which format.

An answer to (a), or to (b) with (c), in every dimension, positive or negative, is the main result we want.

**Q2 (adding visible laws).** For a finite set S of pasting-diagram laws, when is V + S admissible?
- (a) Is this decidable? The reductions of (K63) use new symbols or Horn premises with witness variables and commutation conditions; neither is a pasting-diagram law.
- (b) Is admissibility closed under unions: if V + S and V + S′ are admissible, is V + S ∪ S′? If not, what is the obstruction (compare the non-directedness in (K61), which uses new symbols)?
- (c) When V + S is admissible, are equality and reachability in its free objects decidable, uniformly in S, and is the comparison F_{V+S}(Γ) → F_{V+S′}(Γ) for S ⊆ S′ computable on normal forms?

**Q3 (laws one degree up).** Free objects on pasting diagrams contain 4-loops ((K45), (K59), (K63)), so laws of the shape of L_p and D_{d,p} one degree higher (a crossing of the p-th power of a 4-loop against the paste of p crossings, or splitting a composite of 4-loops) can act on them. Decide their visibility and independence, uniformly in the degree. Are there infinitely many independent visible laws of this kind in some degree, and in every degree above some bound?

**Q4 (the largest theory with V's profile).** By (K61) the largest law set with the pasting profile of V and both quotients is the common theory V^max of the F_V(Γ) and the two benchmarks. Is V^max recursively axiomatizable, and is its Horn theory decidable? Is it axiomatized over B by pasting-diagram laws? Are the free objects on pasting diagrams dense in its category of models (a nerve theorem), so that its models are determined by their restrictions to pasting diagrams?

**Q5 (fallback).** Normal forms for F_V(Γ) through hom degree five on the diagrams Γ_{2,2r}, together with an explicit list of the T₁₂ identifications that V lacks there.

## 5. What counts as a solution

An answer to Q1(a), or to Q1(b) with Q1(c), in every dimension; an answer to any part of Q2, Q3 or Q4; or a published construction, classification or impossibility result. A negative answer (for example, that visibility or admissibility of pasting-diagram laws is undecidable, or that no explicit description of the kind asked for in Q1(a) exists) counts as fully as a positive one.

## Visibility and the maximal Horn theory

For an equation presented on \(\Gamma\), evaluate its sides on the generators of \(\Gamma\). The law is invisible precisely if their normal records agree. For existence of a directed cell, apply the finite reachability test there instead. A positive test on this generic tuple transports its equality or its actual witness along every substitution. Failure on that tuple is already a counterexample. This proves the decision claim for both formats of pasting-diagram law.

For arbitrary finitary Horn laws, enumerate finite pasting trees, terms in them, and tuples of terms of the required sorts by dovetailing. Matching, definedness and equality of their values are decidable by (K58). A tuple satisfying the premises and failing the conclusion certifies visibility. To prove hardness we use the *two separate directions* of the gate construction of (K63). Its conclusion is an identity equation, a feature needed when moving from \(T\) to \(V\).

Concretely, from a Diophantine instance \(\Phi\) over \(\mathbb N\) form the single Horn law \(\lambda_\Phi\) of round 8. Recall the gate: \(A,B\) are the two weight-one 4-loops on the eight-span diagram \(\Delta\), \(L=j_L,\ R'=j_R\) are the two paddings, and \(e:R'(B)L(A)\to L(A)R'(B)\) is the elementary 5-cell. The premises for a multiplication with local loop variables \(X,Y,U\) include a loop \(Z\) commuting with \(L(X)R'(B)\) and a 5-cell from \(Z\) to \(L(U)R'(Y)\), with the face equations specifying all the sorts. The conclusion of \(\lambda_\Phi\) is \(e=1_{s e}\). In the generic eight-span assignment in \(F_T(\Delta)\), the weight of \(U\) is the product of the weights of \(X,Y\): on \(\Delta\) the trace commutes with the primitive word \(L^mR\), so is \((L^mR)^n\), with \(mn\) left letters. Addition is chronological product, and gates with input \(A\) synchronize the weights in the two groups. This translates additions, multiplications and equalities over \(\mathbb N\) effectively. [Full gate, faithfulness of the trace calculation, and contraction to \(\Delta\)](../answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r8-2026-11-20.md#one-additional-horn-law-on-the-original-signature)

* If \(\Phi\) has a solution, the witnesses already exist in \(F_V(\Delta)\). Use the powers \(A^m,B^n,A^{mn}\) and \(Z=(L(A)^m R'(B))^n\). Associativity gives commutation, and successive forward whiskerings of \(e\) sort the word to \(L(A)^{mn}R'(B)^n\). A zero power is an identity, and an already sorted word uses the identity 5-cell. All these expressions use the common padded loops and structural operations. Their typings use the outer block laws in (K45) and functorial whiskering. The image of \(e\) in \(T\) is proper by (K49), so the conclusion fails in \(V\).
* If \(\Phi\) has no solution, (K63)'s no-solution argument proves that every original \(F_T(\Gamma)\) satisfies \(\lambda_\Phi\). Indeed a unit among the eight inputs trivializes \(e\), and otherwise retaining one advance in each span sends a premise assignment back to the gate on \(\Delta\), which would yield a solution. A premise assignment in \(F_V(\Gamma)\) maps to one in \(F_T(\Gamma)\). Its value of \(e\) therefore maps to an identity. Identity reflection in (K58) gives \(e=1_{s e}\) in \(F_V(\Gamma)\).

This proves \(\Phi\) solvable if and only if \(\lambda_\Phi\) is visible. Recursively enumerable completeness follows from the Diophantine representation theorem. [Matiyasevich, *The Diophantineness of enumerable sets*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/dan35274)

Both benchmarks satisfy every \(\lambda_\Phi\), since \(e\) is an inner comparison killed there, on arbitrary assignments. Consequently \(\lambda_\Phi\) belongs to \(V^{\max}\) precisely when \(\Phi\) has **no** solution. In particular its Horn theory is co-r.e.-hard. An effective Horn axiomatization would enumerate its consequences by finite derivations, thereby enumerating the complement of Diophantine solvability. This proves both the nonaxiomatizability and the undecidability claims in Q4.

There is also a direct obstruction to axiomatizing \(V^{\max}\) over \(B\) by pasting-diagram laws, even with an arbitrary set of axioms. Every such valid law holds on the generic tuple in \(F_V(\Gamma)=F_B(\Gamma)\). Evaluation on any tuple in any \(B\)-algebra transports the equality or a witnessing term. Hence adjoining all such laws leaves every \(B\)-algebra a model. But \(L_p=D_{1,p}\) belongs to \(V^{\max}\): 3-loops in the free tables are identities by (K59), and the benchmark inner crossings are identities. The admissible algebraic theory \(U_p\) contains \(B\) by (K58) and fails \(D_{1,p}\) by (K56). Its separating table therefore supplies a \(B\)-model outside \(V^{\max}\).

## Density on the fixed signature

Use the **full** subcategory \(\mathcal A\) of \(V^{\max}\)-models on the free finite pasting diagrams. These are the same free objects as for \(B\): they satisfy \(V^{\max}\) by definition and their free \(B\)-universal property applies to every \(V^{\max}\)-model. We show directly that \(M\mapsto \operatorname{Hom}(-,M)|_{\mathcal A}\) is fully faithful.

Let \(\eta:N(M)\to N(M')\) be natural. Its components on the free globes give maps \(f_d:M_d\to M'_d\), since assigning a globe amounts to choosing its top cell. The face inclusions make these maps globular. On a free pasting diagram, naturality on the inclusion of each generating globe forces \(\eta(a)\) to be the assignment \(f a\); these restrictions determine an assignment uniquely.

It remains to show that \(f\) preserves the operations, including their values on tuples with coincidences. Each basic operation has a generic assignment on a finite pasting diagram. For an identity use one globe. For top composition and fixed whiskering glue the two input globes at the prescribed face. For a mixed disk operation or an auxiliary use the pair of globes adjacent at its cut, suspended to that cut; the bridge restricted to a hemisphere uses the corresponding smaller input globe. The signed routes, joins and fixed contexts are terms on these arities. The guards on *equations* in (K58) impose no additional domain condition on these operations. Godement compositions and general rectangles are obtained from the basic operations. These arity facts follow from the boundary and bridge declarations used in step 1 above.

For a basic \(d\)-dimensional value \(g\) on its arity \(D\), the cell \(g(\mathrm{gen})\) classifies a map \(F(D^d)\to F(D)\) in \(\mathcal A\). Naturality along this map, at any assignment \(a:F(D)\to M\), reads \(f_d(g(a))=g(fa)\). It applies equally to degenerate assignments and to matches which exist only in \(M\). Thus \(f\) is a homomorphism. Its nerve is \(\eta\) on every arity by the preceding generator argument, and its globe components determine it uniquely. This proves density in the sense asked for in Q4. The argument uses all term maps between the free arities, including the maps classifying operations.
