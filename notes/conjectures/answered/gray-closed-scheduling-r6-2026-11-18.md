# Schedules in free Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-categories, round 6

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked. The smallest open case is Question 1.*

## Background

**Schedules.** Fix a finite poset P on labels {1, …, n}, with numerical order as the left/right order of incomparable labels. Let S(P) be the category with:
- objects: the linear extensions of P;
- generating arrows: swaps ji → ij of adjacent P-incomparable labels with i < j;
- equations: commuting squares for swaps at disjoint positions, and the braid hexagon for three pairwise incomparable labels.

Then S(P)(w, v) is a singleton if Inv_P(v) ⊆ Inv_P(w) and empty otherwise. Here Inv_P(w) is the set of pairs i < j, incomparable, with j before i in w. (Proof: move the first differing label left, then resolve the first swaps by a square or a hexagon and induct.)

**Gray monoids.** In the free strict Gray monoid (a monoid in 2-Cat for Gray's lax tensor) on a set of objects and generating 1-cells, fix the image in the free strict monoidal category, i.e. the string diagram. Then the hom-category between the 1-cells with that diagram is S(P). Here P is the diagram's dependency order, with left/right given by planarity. The 2-cells are whiskered interchangers (f ⊗ B′)∘(A ⊗ g) ⇒ (A′ ⊗ g)∘(f ⊗ B), "right factor first ⇒ left factor first".

**The ω-version.** The hom ω-category of the Steiner complex ν(I^⊗n), from bottom to top, is not thin: two hexagon 2-cells are joined by the 3-cell ***. Gray's 2-tensor is its intelligent 2-truncation (Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches*, A.25 and A.31). For a dependency poset P, the subcomplex K_P ⊆ I^⊗n spanned by the faces (order ideal J, S ⊆ min(P∖J)) has paths equal to the linear extensions of P. Its truncated hom is S(P).

## Setting: the biclosed version

Formulas are built from atoms with ⊗ and the residuals B\A and A/B, with no unit. 𝒢 is the free **Gray-monoidal biclosed strict 2-category** on a set of generating 1-cells fᵢ : Pᵢ → Qᵢ between formulas. Its structure:
- a strict 2-category with a Gray monoidal structure (lax interchangers, as above);
- closed structure: isomorphisms of hom-categories Φ : 𝒢(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ 𝒢(B, A\C), and the mirror one for /.
  - Φ is **strictly natural in the context variable B**.
  - Φ is natural in the **bound variable A only up to the interchanger**.
  - Strict naturality in A would force (f ⊗ B′)(A ⊗ g) = (A′ ⊗ g)(f ⊗ B), collapsing distinct schedules into one 1-cell. That is not wanted.

**Two warnings, both established.**
1. *Nesting is not invariant under naive normalization.* For a generator h : U → U with U = X\Y,

   ```
   h = Λ(ev) ∘ h = Λ(ev ∘ (1 ⊗ h))
   ```

   So h can be "ambient" or "inside a closure". β-reduction ev ∘ (1 ⊗ Λk) = k also changes nesting. Any invariant notion of "event inside a closure" must start from a βη-canonical form.
2. *Unit-free planar coherence.* In the free unit-free biclosed monoidal 1-category, a balanced sequent (each atom twice, opposite polarities) has at most one morphism, and exactly one when it is provable in the Lambek calculus with product. So the purely structural parts of 1-cells are unique.

## What is known (proved)

1. **A word invariant.** Sending each generator to its label gives an invariant W of 1-cell equality with values in words (composition order), with W(Λu) = W(u), W(ev) = ε and W(D ⊗ u) = W(u). It is realised by the one-object model whose 1-cells are words, with a unique 2-cell between words with the same multiset. So 1-cells with different generator order are distinct.
2. **Closure trees are not invariants of components.** With generators a : X → A and f : A ⊗ B → C, c = Λ_A(f), e = ev: the interchanger gives θ : s = e∘(a⊗U)∘(X⊗c) ⇒ t = e∘(A⊗c)∘(a⊗B), and t = f∘(a⊗B) by β. Every representative of s has a generator under currying (a Boolean biclosed poset rules out the needed structural prefix), while t is tensor-only. So any βη-normal form fixing tensor-only terms gives s and t different closure trees, though they are connected by a 2-cell. "Cross-level cells are generated by ordinary interchangers" does not imply "the canonical tree is invariant".
3. **A forward comparison.** For φ : P → C/B and b : X → B, the forward comparison α = e_B ∗ χ_{φ,b} : t₁ ⇒ t₂ exists, W distinguishes t₁, t₂, and Λ_X(t₂) = (C/b)∘φ by strict naturality in the context variable. Whether 𝒢(t₁, t₂) = {α} and 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ is open: the tensor-only schedule theorem does not control 2-cells built by currying, uncurrying and η-expansion.
4. **Mapping-space biclosedness is too weak for classifying 1-cells.** Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari (arXiv 2006.14495, Rem. 2.20): Map(X ⊗ Y, Z) ≇ Map(X, Fun^{opgr}(Y, Z)) in general; only cores agree.

5. **An integer model.** Let 𝓜 be the strict 2-category with:
   - objects the integers;
   - for q ≥ p, the hom 𝓜(p, q) equal to the poset (ℤ, ≤) of weights; otherwise empty;
   - composition adding weights;
   - w(p ⊗ u) = w(u) + p·δ(u) and w(u ⊗ q) = w(u), where δ(u) = target − source;
   - p\r = r − p and r/q = r − q, with left currying subtracting p·δ and right currying leaving the weight unchanged.

   𝓜 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category. An interchanger raises the weight by δ(f)·δ(g). A matrix-valued version (objects in ℤ^r) records ordered generator pairs.
6. **No reverse comparisons.** With distinct atoms, 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ and 𝒢(t, s) = ∅. For t₁ and t₂ the weights are 0 and 1, from |B| = 1, |C| = 2 and |P| = |X| = 0. For s and t they are −1 and 0. Every forward 2-cell between them uses **exactly one** interchanger whose two arguments each contain one generator. Every other interchanger in it has a generator-free argument.
7. **Undirected components.** π₀ 𝒢(A, C) ≅ 𝓑_Σ(A, C), the free ordinary unit-free biclosed monoidal category. Two 1-cells are connected by a zigzag of 2-cells iff their images there agree.
8. **The image and W do not classify 1-cells.** Take generators f : A → B and g : B → C with padding L, R, and let m = L ⊗ (g ∘ f) ⊗ R.
   - Currying g with the right padding gives u ⇒ m.
   - Currying g with the left padding gives m ⇒ v.
   - u, m and v have equal images in 𝓑_Σ, and W(u) = W(v).
   - Their weights (with |L| = |R| = |A| = 0, |B| = 1, |C| = 2) are −1, 0 and 1, so the three 1-cells are distinct.
   - The side of currying is data of the 1-cell, and schedules with only generator events cannot describe this component.

## Further observations (proved)

9. **Subtractive models cannot see interchangers with a structural argument.** Call a model *additive* if 1-cells carry a weight in an ordered abelian group, δ(u) = weight(target) − weight(source) is additive under composition and tensor, and an interchanger χ_{f,g} adds a bilinear term β(δ(f), δ(g)). If moreover the residuals are group subtraction (A\C = C − A, as in the model 𝓜 of item 5 and its matrix version), every structural 1-cell (ev, curried evaluations, units) has δ = 0, so every interchanger with a structural argument has weight 0. Such models cannot decide whether two 2-cells differing in those interchangers are equal. This blindness is specific to subtractive residuals; see item 13.
10. **The axioms do not make evaluation central.** For ev : A ⊗ (A\C) → C and a generator g : Y → Y′, compare (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y), related by χ_{ev,g}. Currying A on the left (using strict naturality in the context variable and the target) turns this into the same question for m_Y := Φ(ev ⊗ Y) and g, so currying only transports it. Likewise for χ_{ev,ev′} at independent positions. Unless centrality is imposed (as in Garner–Hirschowitz–Pardon's premonoidal construction), structural maps are non-central events in 𝒢. In the quotient 𝒢_c of 𝒢 in which every interchanger with a structural argument is an identity, Question 1 follows at once from item 6.

11. **Structural loops.** Take atoms A, B, C, D and U = A\(A⊗B), X = A⊗B, X′ = A⊗U, V = (C⊗D)/D, Y = C⊗D, Y′ = V⊗D. Let u = 1_A ⊗ Λ_A(1) : X → X′, e = ev : X′ → X, v = Λ^R_D(1) ⊗ 1_D : Y → Y′ and d = ev^R : Y′ → Y, so that e u = 1 and d v = 1 by β. Put T = X ⊗ Y and (composition rightmost first)

    ```
    H = (e ⊗ 1_Y)(1_{X′} ⊗ d)(u ⊗ 1_{Y′})(1_X ⊗ v) : T → T.
    ```

    - Whiskered interchangers give ι = (e⊗1) ∗ χ_{u,d} ∗ (1⊗v) : 1_T ⇒ H and π = [(e⊗1)(1⊗d)] ∗ χ_{u,v} : H ⇒ 1_T. The Gray axiom for χ_{u,dv} gives **π ∘ ι = 1**, so E = ι ∘ π is an idempotent on H.
    - **H^n are pairwise distinct.** In the model whose hom-categories are chaotic on the free group ⟨ℓ, r⟩ (objects integers, composition multiplication, left currying k ↦ ℓ^a k, right currying k ↦ r^a k), H ↦ ℓ⁻¹ r⁻¹ ℓ r.
    - All H^n have image 1_T in 𝓑_Σ, empty generator word and zero weight in every model of item 5. Since 2-cells H^m ⇒ 1_T ⇒ H^n exist, the category over 1_T is not isomorphic to any product of schedule categories.
    - This particular model does not satisfy strict equality of the two mixed curryings of (A\C)/B = A\(C/B). Item 14 removes that dependence.
    - **Forward comparisons.** Item 6's count factors each forward comparison as z₊ ∘ m ∘ z₋, with z_± using only interchangers with a generator-free argument and m a typed instance of the one generator–generator interchanger. Different typed instances of m must still be identified.
12. **Evaluation is not central in 𝒢.** In the same model with a third free generator γ for a generating 1-cell g, (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) ↦ ℓ⁻¹γ and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y) ↦ γℓ⁻¹, which are distinct. So χ_{ev,g} joins distinct 1-cells, and this uses only left closure. A complete invariant of 1-cells must schedule structural events (units, evaluations), not only generator events.

13. **A polarized model sees structural interchangers.** Let 𝓟 have objects (P, N) ∈ ℤ², A ⊗ B = A + B and A\C = C/A = C + swap(A), where swap(P, N) = (N, P). A 1-cell X → Y exists iff (P − N)(X) = (P − N)(Y), and it is an integer weight w. Put d(u) = P(source) − P(target).
    - Composition adds weights, w(A ⊗ u) = w(u) + P(A)·d(u) and w(u ⊗ B) = w(u).
    - Left currying is w ↦ w − P(A)·d(u), and right currying keeps the weight.
    - The homs are codiscrete.
    - 𝓟 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category in the sense above.
    - **ev is not central.** Here d(ev_A) = P(A) + N(A) ≠ 0. With A ↦ (1, 0), C ↦ (0, 0), X ↦ (1, 1), X′ ↦ (0, 0) and g : X → X′, the 1-cells (X′ ⊗ ev) ∘ (g ⊗ A ⊗ A\C) and (g ⊗ C) ∘ (X ⊗ ev) have weights 1 and 2. The same separates the two orders of two independent evaluations, with no generator involved.
    - Together with item 12, the central quotient 𝒢_c of item 10 is a proper quotient of 𝒢.

14. **Strict mixed currying does not help, and left closure alone suffices.**
    - *The model 𝒟.* Keep the chaotic free-group Gray monoid of item 11. Choose any k_c ∈ ⟨ℓ, r⟩ for c ∈ ℤ, put h_{a,c} = k_{c−a} k_c⁻¹, and let both curryings with bound object a and codomain c be f ↦ h_{a,c} f.
    - *Properties.* The multipliers telescope, so the two mixed curryings are equal, R_b R_a = R_{a+b}, and h_{0,c} = 1.
    - *Separation.* With |A| = |B| = 1, |C| = |D| = 2, k₁ = ℓ, k₄ = r⁻¹ and every other k_c = 1, H ↦ ℓ⁻¹r⁻¹ℓr. So H ≠ 1_T and the H^n are pairwise distinct even in the theory 𝒢_mix that imposes strict mixed currying.
    - *Left closure only.* Replace v and d by v_L = 1_C ⊗ Λ_C(1_{C⊗D}) and d_L = ev, through W = C\(C⊗D). The resulting H_L also maps to ℓ⁻¹r⁻¹ℓr. So the free left-closed theory already has non-identity structural loops 1_T ⇒ H_L ⇒ 1_T with π ∘ ι = 1.
15. **Countermodels for ι ∘ π = 1_H need non-invertible retractions.** In a Gray monoid, if f is strictly invertible, then χ_{f,g} is invertible, with inverse (f ⊗ B′) ∗ χ_{f⁻¹,g} ∗ (f ⊗ B). Hence in any model where u, e, v and d are strict isomorphisms, ι ∘ π = 1. That covers every refinement of the free-group, 𝒟 and 𝓟 models. A countermodel must have u ∘ e ≠ 1.
16. **Two different "centrality" questions.**
    - *Outer comparison.* For functors e, g between strict ω-categories, (e ⊗ 1)(1 ⊗ g) and (1 ⊗ g)(e ⊗ 1) are both e ⊗ g. So the interpretation of 𝒢's 1-cells as functors and its 2-cells as transformations sends every interchanger, generator or structural, to an identity, and cannot test directedness.
    - *Internal interchanger.* Evaluation applied to an interchanger of internal cells can be non-invertible. For S = J ⊗ J (J = [1]) with square γ : h₁v₀ ⇒ v₁h₀, let q : J → S/J curry 1_S and let α : v₀ → v₁ be the image of the arrow of J. Then e(χ_{α,u}) = γ.
17. **𝒢's two hom-category isomorphisms do not hold in the intended model.**
    - In S/J there is an arrow α : v₀ → v₁.
    - In J\S there is none: such an arrow would need the reverse of γ.
    - A single 2-category of strict ω-categories, with one orientation of transformation as 2-cells and ℋ(I, Z) ≅ Z, would identify both S/J and J\S with ℋ(J, S), identically on objects. That is impossible.

    So the Gray tensor's left and right closures are matched by 2-cells of opposite orientation (lax for one side, oplax for the other; Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari, Ex. 2.5). Campion's tensor gives (A\C)/B ≃ A\(C/B) canonically, but not literally.
18. **The correct structure is two-sided Gray enrichment.** For strict 2-categories with the Gray tensor, put ℍ(A, C)(P, Q) = 2Cat(P ⊗ A ⊗ Q, C), with probes P, Q on either side. Its one-sided restrictions are two different hom 2-categories: ℍ_ℓ (left probe, D_k ⊗ A → C) and ℍ_r (right probe, A ⊗ D_k → C). Then:
    - left currying is an isomorphism ℍ_r(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ ℍ_r(B, A\C), and right currying is an isomorphism ℍ_ℓ(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ ℍ_ℓ(A, C/B);
    - with probes on both sides, the bound argument absorbs the probe on its side: ℍ(A ⊗ B, C)(P, Q) ≅ ℍ(B, A\C)(P ⊗ A, Q) and ℍ(A ⊗ B, C)(P, Q) ≅ ℍ(A, C/B)(P, B ⊗ Q);
    - the squares 2Cat(J ⊗ A ⊗ J, C) give a strict double hom with mixed lax/oplax modifications.
19. **The free unit-free model 𝒢′_Σ.** Models have a probe sort and a data sort, adjunctions only between data types, and gluing along designated probe colimits (needed only for one-hole contexts). The free model 𝒢′_Σ on a signature Σ of generating 1-cells exists as a term model with an explicit universal property. The Gray model is a model. An erasure model (probes act by identities) shows that the free unit-free biclosed monoidal category 𝓑_Σ embeds faithfully in 𝒢′_Σ.
20. **What collapses and what survives in 𝒢′.**
    - Data arrows (functors) tensor bifunctorially, so the structural loops of items 11 and 14 are trivial: H = H_L = 1_T, and the maps ι, π of item 11 are identities.
    - Non-invertible Gray interchange survives one dimension up. For two transformations, the two schedules are related by the modification F(χ ▷ 1_A), which has no inverse in 𝒢′ (in the Gray model, with the atom A sent to the terminal 2-category, it is the 2-cell of the walking Gray square).
    - So in 𝒢′ the schedule structure of the Background lives on generators of probe degree J (transformations X ⊗ J → Y or J ⊗ X → Y, and more generally cells X ⊗ D_k → Y), with interchangers as modifications.

# Researcher Response

**Question 1 is false, even with two distinct generating transformations, each used exactly once, and no structural events.** There is a composite whose endomodification monoid contains a copy of \((\mathbb N,+)\). The modifications witnessing this are generated entirely by the proposed interchangers \(F(\chi\triangleright1)\).

The counterexample also obstructs the higher product description in Question 3. For Question 2, evaluation has strict naturality in the compatible enrichment, but varying its bound argument produces an opposite-sided transformation whose interchange with another transformation can be noninvertible.

## 1. A counterexample to Question 1

Take one data atom \(A\), no nonidentity generating data arrows, and two distinct generating left-parameter transformations
\[
\alpha,\beta:1_A\Rightarrow1_A.
\]
Thus their parameter maps are
\[
\alpha,\beta:J\triangleright A\longrightarrow A,
\]
and both endpoint restrictions of each map are \(1_A\).

This is unit-free: \(A\) is an atom, and \(1_A\) is its identity arrow. No tensor-unit formula is used.

We work over the arrow \(1_A\) of the ordinary biclosed erasure. The endpoint equations impose only \(1_A=1_A\), so they impose no additional equation there.

Write \(\alpha\cdot\beta\) for composition of transformations, executing \(\beta\) first. This is composition in \(\mathbb H_\ell(A,A)\); it is not ordinary composition of the maps \(J\triangleright A\to A\).

### 1.1 Two forward crossings give an endomodification

Let
\[
\kappa:D_2\longrightarrow J\otimes J
\]
select the Gray square, oriented as in the question:
\[
\text{right factor first}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{left factor first}.
\]

Define
\[
F_{\alpha,\beta}
=
\alpha\circ(1_J\triangleright\beta):
(J\otimes J)\triangleright A\longrightarrow A.
\]
Its right-first boundary executes \(\beta\), then \(\alpha\). Its left-first boundary executes \(\alpha\), then \(\beta\). Hence
\[
\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
=
F_{\alpha,\beta}\circ(\kappa\triangleright1_A):
\alpha\cdot\beta
\Longrightarrow
\beta\cdot\alpha.
\tag{1}
\]

Interchanging the assignments of the two generators to the parameter positions gives another **forward** crossing:
\[
\sigma_{\beta,\alpha}:
\beta\cdot\alpha
\Longrightarrow
\alpha\cdot\beta.
\tag{2}
\]

Neither construction uses an inverse to \(\kappa\). Their composite is an endomodification
\[
q
=
\sigma_{\beta,\alpha}\circ\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}:
\alpha\cdot\beta
\Longrightarrow
\alpha\cdot\beta.
\tag{3}
\]

We will prove that
\[
1,\ q,\ q^2,\ldots
\]
are pairwise distinct.

### 1.2 A strict \(2\)-category separating all powers

Define a strict \(2\)-category \(\mathcal X\) by the following data.

| Dimension | Cells and operations |
|---|---|
| \(0\) | One object \(*\) |
| \(1\) | The natural numbers; composition is addition; identity is \(0\) |
| \(2\) | No cells \(m\Rightarrow n\) when \(m\ne n\); cells \(n\Rightarrow n\) are labelled by \(k\in\mathbb N\) |
| \(2\)-cell identities | Label \(0\) |
| Vertical and horizontal \(2\)-composition | Add labels |

The underlying \(1\)-cell and the \(2\)-cell label are separate data: a \(2\)-cell is specified by \(n\) and \(k\), with source and target both \(n\).

Associativity and units follow from addition. Interchange holds because
\[
(a+b)+(c+d)=(a+c)+(b+d).
\]

There is a parameter map
\[
T:J\otimes\mathcal X\longrightarrow\mathcal X
\tag{4}
\]
with the following assignments:

- each endpoint copy of \(\mathcal X\) maps identically to \(\mathcal X\);
- the \(J\)-arrow at \(*\) maps to the \(1\)-cell \(1\);
- the Gray interchanger between that \(J\)-arrow and the \(1\)-cell \(n\) of \(\mathcal X\) maps to the \(2\)-cell labelled \(n\).

Both boundary composites of this last cell have underlying \(1\)-cell \(1+n=n+1\), so this assignment is typed.

Here are the relations needed to check (4):

- The interchanger with the identity \(1\)-cell \(0\) has label \(0\).
- The interchanger with \(m+n\) has label
  \[
  m+n,
  \]
  equal to the sum of the labels of the two successive interchangers.
- Naturality with respect to a \(2\)-cell labelled \(k:n\Rightarrow n\) is the equality
  \[
  n+k=k+n.
  \]

These are precisely the unit, composition, and \(2\)-cell naturality relations for the parameterized transformation. Thus \(T\) is a strict \(2\)-functor. Equivalently, it is a transformation \(\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal X}\Rightarrow\operatorname{Id}_{\mathcal X}\), with value \(1\) at \(*\) and naturality cell labelled \(n\) at \(n\). The parameter-map/internal-hom description is the usual lax Gray one; see Nikolić, §1 and §4.1. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.12213v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Interpret \(A\) as \(\mathcal X\) in the stipulated strict \(2\)-category model of \(\mathcal G'\), and interpret **both** \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) as \(T\). Distinct free generators may receive the same interpretation.

Each crossing (1)–(2), evaluated at \(*\), is the naturality cell of \(T\) at its own value \(1\). Its label is therefore \(1\). Consequently,
\[
\llbracket q^r\rrbracket_*=2r
\qquad(r\in\mathbb N).
\tag{5}
\]

The identity modification has label \(0\). Equation (5) proves that every power of \(q\) is distinct in the free theory.

It also proves that \(q\) is not invertible: an inverse would require a label \(k\in\mathbb N\) satisfying \(2+k=0\).

### 1.3 Consequence for the schedule proposal

A category equivalent to a poset has exactly one endomorphism of each object. Indeed, a fully faithful equivalence \(F:\mathcal C\to P\) gives
\[
\mathcal C(x,x)\cong P(Fx,Fx),
\]
and the latter set is a singleton. Every product of schedule posets is again a poset.

Our category has an injective monoid homomorphism
\[
\boxed{
(\mathbb N,+)\hookrightarrow
\operatorname{End}(\alpha\cdot\beta),
\qquad r\longmapsto q^r.
}
\tag{6}
\]

Therefore it is not equivalent to any product of schedule categories.

This counterexample fixes:

- the erased arrow \(1_A\);
- the underlying data arrow \(1_A\);
- the two distinct generator names \(\alpha,\beta\);
- the number of uses of each generator: exactly one.

Its obstructing modifications use only the specified crossings. Adding evaluations, units, counits, or curry-side labels to a dependency poset cannot eliminate (6).

The distinction from the Background is precise. A fixed planar diagram fixes left/right positions. Here \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), both endotransformations of \(1_A\), can be assigned to the two probe positions in either order. Fixing the erased arrow and generator multiset does not fix those assignments. Restricting to a diagram that excludes one of (1)–(2) would define a smaller category.

One generator already suffices: for \(\tau:1_A\Rightarrow1_A\), its self-interchanger is a nonidentity endomodification of \(\tau^2\). The two-generator construction avoids any dependence on repeated generator names.

## 2. Question 2: evaluation, units, and currying

There are three different cases:

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Interchange with the identity transformation on an evaluation data arrow | Identity modification |
| Fixed-bound evaluation/unit naturality in the compatible enrichment | Strict equality |
| Interchange involving a bound-variable mate and another genuine transformation | Directed modification; it can be noninvertible |

The parameter positions determine which case applies.

### 2.1 A bare evaluation is not a second transformation event

Let \(e:U\to V\) be any data arrow, including an evaluation. Its left-parameter identity transformation is
\[
1_e^\ell
=
e\circ(!\triangleright1_U):
J\triangleright U\longrightarrow V,
\]
where \(!:J\to I\). There is an analogous right-parameter identity transformation.

An interchange involving this identity transformation factors through
\[
!\otimes J
\quad\text{or}\quad
J\otimes!.
\]
Both maps send the Gray square to an identity \(2\)-cell. Hence, whenever typed,
\[
\chi_{1_e,\gamma}=1,
\qquad
\chi_{\gamma,1_e}=1.
\tag{7}
\]

The same statement applies to the identity transformations on units and counits. A nontrivial comparison involving evaluation must specify two genuine parameter directions.

### 2.2 Fixed-bound enriched naturality is strict

For left closure, put
\[
R_A C=A\backslash C,
\qquad
e_{A,C}:A\otimes R_A C\to C.
\]

For a right-parameter family
\[
\gamma:C\otimes P\longrightarrow C',
\]
define
\[
R_A^P(\gamma)
=
\Lambda_A\!\left(
\gamma\circ(e_{A,C}\otimes P)
\right):
R_A C\otimes P\longrightarrow R_A C'.
\]
Then the counit equation is exactly
\[
\boxed{
e_{A,C'}\circ(A\otimes R_A^P(\gamma))
=
\gamma\circ(e_{A,C}\otimes P).
}
\tag{8}
\]
This is \(\beta\), applied to the definition of \(R_A^P(\gamma)\).

For the unit
\[
\eta_X^A
=
\Lambda_A(1_{A\otimes X})
:
X\to A\backslash(A\otimes X)
\]
and \(\delta:X\otimes P\to Y\), one likewise has
\[
\boxed{
(R_A L_A)^P(\delta)\circ(\eta_X^A\otimes P)
=
\eta_Y^A\circ\delta,
\qquad L_A=A\otimes-.
}
\tag{9}
\]
Uncurrying either side gives \(A\otimes\delta\), proving the equality.

These formulas apply to \(P=J,D_2\), and the pasting probes. Thus evaluation and the unit are strict in \(\mathbb H_r\), including at transformation and modification degrees.

For right closure, the corresponding statements are strict in \(\mathbb H_\ell\). Explicitly, for \(\gamma:P\otimes C\to C'\),
\[
R_B^P(\gamma)
=
\Lambda^R_B\!\left(
\gamma\circ(P\otimes e^B_C)
\right):
P\otimes(C/B)\to C'/B,
\]
and
\[
e^B_{C'}\circ(R_B^P(\gamma)\otimes B)
=
\gamma\circ(P\otimes e^B_C).
\tag{10}
\]

These equalities do not contribute directed scheduling events.

### 2.3 Varying the bound argument changes sides

Now fix
\[
H=A\backslash C,
\qquad e=e_{A,C},
\]
and take a **right-parameter** transformation
\[
a:X\otimes J\longrightarrow A
\]
with endpoint data maps \(f_0,f_1:X\to A\).

Define
\[
f_i^*
=
\Lambda_X\!\left(e\circ(f_i\otimes H)\right)
:
H\longrightarrow X\backslash C
\]
and
\[
\bar a
=
\Lambda_X\!\left(e\circ(a\otimes H)\right)
:
J\otimes H\longrightarrow X\backslash C.
\tag{11}
\]

Thus a right-parameter transformation in the bound argument gives a **left-parameter** transformation between precomposition maps.

Let
\[
b:J\otimes Y\longrightarrow H
\]
be another left-parameter transformation, with endpoints \(g_0,g_1:Y\to H\). Form
\[
W=e\circ(a\otimes b):
X\otimes J\otimes J\otimes Y\longrightarrow C.
\]
Restrict along the Gray square and curry \(X\):
\[
M_{a,b}
=
\Lambda_X\!\left(
W\circ(X\otimes\kappa\otimes Y)
\right):
D_2\otimes Y\longrightarrow X\backslash C.
\tag{12}
\]

Its direction is
\[
\boxed{
(\bar a\,g_1)\cdot(f_0^*b)
\ \Longrightarrow\
(f_1^*b)\cdot(\bar a\,g_0).
}
\tag{13}
\]
Here
\[
\bar a\,g_i=\bar a\circ(J\otimes g_i),
\qquad
f_i^*b=f_i^*\circ b.
\]

The source executes the second parameter, belonging to \(b\), first. The target executes the first parameter, belonging to \(a\), first. Thus (13) uses exactly the direction stipulated in the question.

There is no extra comparison generator hidden in this construction. Strict context naturality gives
\[
\Lambda_X(W)
=
\bar a\circ(J\otimes b),
\]
so (12) is precisely the ordinary transformation interchanger for \(\bar a\) and \(b\), transported through evaluation and currying.

### 2.4 The right-closure formula

For
\[
K=C/B,\qquad e=e^B_C,
\]
start instead with
\[
a:J\otimes X\to B,
\qquad
b:Y\otimes J\to K.
\]
Set
\[
f_i^*=\Lambda^R_X\!\left(e\circ(K\otimes f_i)\right),
\qquad
\bar a=\Lambda^R_X\!\left(e\circ(K\otimes a)\right)
:
K\otimes J\to C/X.
\]
Then
\[
N_{a,b}
=
\Lambda^R_X\!\left(
e\circ(b\otimes a)\circ(Y\otimes\kappa\otimes X)
\right)
:
Y\otimes D_2\to C/X
\]
has direction
\[
\boxed{
(f_1^*b)\cdot(\bar a\,g_0)
\ \Longrightarrow\
(\bar a\,g_1)\cdot(f_0^*b).
}
\tag{14}
\]

The parameter order in the uncurried expression is now \(b\), then \(a\). Equation (14) is again right-factor-first to left-factor-first.

### 2.5 A noninvertible comparison made entirely from structural maps

The comparisons above can be noninvertible even without generating transformations.

Choose data types \(X,Y\), and set
\[
A=X\otimes J,
\qquad
C=A\otimes J\otimes Y,
\qquad
H=A\backslash C.
\]
Use
\[
a=1_A:X\otimes J\to A,
\qquad
b=\eta^A_{J\otimes Y}:J\otimes Y\to H.
\tag{15}
\]

Although \(a\) is an identity **data map**, its endpoints as a parameterized transformation are the two maps
\[
X\longrightarrow X\otimes J.
\]
It is therefore not an identity transformation.

The triangle equation gives
\[
e_{A,C}\circ(a\otimes b)
=
e_{A,C}\circ(A\otimes\eta^A_{J\otimes Y})
=
1_C.
\]
Consequently (12) becomes
\[
\boxed{
M_{a,b}
=
\Lambda_X(X\otimes\kappa\otimes Y).
}
\tag{16}
\]

Interpret \(X\) and \(Y\) as data-tagged terminal strict \(2\)-categories. Then \(A\) is interpreted as \(J\), \(C\) as \(J\otimes J\), and (16) as its generating Gray square. That square has no reverse \(2\)-cell. Thus (16) has neither an inverse nor a reverse modification in the free theory.

This is a unit-free syntactic example: terminal \(2\)-categories are used only as interpretations of data types.

The consequence for structural events is therefore:

- an evaluation, unit, or counit **as a data arrow** contributes no \(J\)-event;
- its identity transformation has trivial interchange;
- a structural term whose type designates a genuine \(J\)-parameter can be a transformation event;
- the side of that parameter determines its orientation and its possible crossings.

A dependency poset cannot be assigned merely by counting uses of the symbols “evaluation” or “unit.”

## 3. The higher product claim is obstructed as well

For the higher analogue of the transformation/modification category in Question 1, the same counterexample survives. The required transfer does not assume that the inclusion of strict \(2\)-categories preserves the Gray tensor.

### 3.1 Transfer to Campion’s setting

The strict \(2\)-category \(\mathcal X\) above is gaunt: its only invertible \(1\)-cell is \(0\), and its only invertible \(2\)-cells have label \(0\).

There are compatible strong monoidal reflections
\[
L:\mathrm{Cat}_\omega\longrightarrow\mathrm{Gaunt}_2,
\qquad
G:\mathrm{sCat}_2\longrightarrow\mathrm{Gaunt}_2.
\tag{17}
\]
For the source statements, Campion identifies strict and gaunt categories as the relevant localizations in Examples 1.25 and 1.30; Theorem 3.14 and Corollary 3.15 provide the strong monoidal reflections, and Example 4.7 identifies the strict Gray structure. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00205?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Apply \(G\) to (4), obtaining
\[
T_g:J\otimes_g\mathcal X\to\mathcal X.
\]
If \(i:\mathrm{Gaunt}_2\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Cat}_\omega\) is the fully faithful right adjoint, the adjunction \(L\dashv i\) gives
\[
T_\infty:
J\otimes_\infty i\mathcal X\longrightarrow i\mathcal X.
\tag{18}
\]
Indeed,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Map}_{\mathrm{Cat}_\omega}
(J\otimes i\mathcal X,i\mathcal X)
&\simeq
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Gaunt}_2}
(L(J\otimes i\mathcal X),\mathcal X)\\
&\cong
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Gaunt}_2}
(J\otimes_g\mathcal X,\mathcal X).
\end{aligned}
\]
The endpoint restrictions of (18) are identities.

Interpret both generators by \(T_\infty\). Applying the strong monoidal \(L\) to their tensor/composition/probe pastings recovers the calculation in \(\mathcal X\). Each crossing still has evaluated label \(1\), and \(q^r\) has label \(2r\).

Distinct labels cannot become equivalent through higher cells: \(\mathcal X\) has only identity cells above dimension \(2\). Thus the powers of \(q\) remain distinct even after passing to the homotopy category of the transformation/modification hom.

### 3.2 Why cubical schedule homs cannot have this behavior

Here the relevant objects of
\[
\nu(K_P)(\bot,\top)
\]
are bottom-to-top \(1\)-paths \(w\). Its \(1\)-morphisms are \(2\)-cells of \(\nu(K_P)\).

There is no nonidentity \(2\)-cell \(w\Rightarrow w\). One can check this directly on chains. Give an edge that adds \(i\) to an ideal \(J\) the weight
\[
\rho(J,i)
=
\#\{j\in J:j>i,\ j\parallel_P i\}.
\]
Extend \(\rho\) linearly to \(1\)-chains. Its value on a path is that path’s inversion number.

For every oriented \(2\)-face \(F\), with the direction used in the question,
\[
\rho(\partial F)=-1.
\]
Thus for a positive \(2\)-chain \(c=\sum_F n_FF\),
\[
\rho(\partial c)=-\sum_F n_F.
\]
If a \(2\)-cell has the same source and target path, then \(\partial c=0\), forcing every \(n_F=0\). Its top \(2\)-chain is zero, so it is the identity \(2\)-cell.

Therefore the homotopy category of each proposed cubical schedule hom has only identity endomorphisms of its path objects. Products retain this property. Our higher transformation hom has the distinct endomorphisms \(q^r\), so it cannot be equivalent to such a product.

The obstruction again uses only two generating transformations at \(1_A\). Including structural events does not repair it.

## 4. A typing correction to item 18

The second bullet of item 18 has a transcription error: \(P\otimes A\) is a data type, so it cannot occupy the probe argument of \(\mathbb H\).

The correctly typed absorption formulas are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb H(A\otimes B,C)(P,Q)
&\cong
\mathcal D\bigl(B\otimes Q,(P\otimes A)\backslash C\bigr),\\
\mathbb H(A\otimes B,C)(P,Q)
&\cong
\mathcal D\bigl(P\otimes A,C/(B\otimes Q)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently, their right-hand sides are
\[
\mathbb H\bigl(B,(P\otimes A)\backslash C\bigr)(I,Q)
\]
and
\[
\mathbb H\bigl(A,C/(B\otimes Q)\bigr)(P,I).
\]
These are the formulas used in the mate calculations above.
