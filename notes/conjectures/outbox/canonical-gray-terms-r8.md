# Canonical CaTT terms for the cells of Gray tensors of pasting diagrams: all pairs, all cubes, iterated tensors

*A self-contained problem. It assumes familiarity with the type theory CaTT, strict ω-categories and Steiner's directed complexes, and nothing else. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked.*

**What we are asking for.** The most general theorem: a construction for **all** pairs and all finite families of globular pasting diagrams, compatible with associativity, or a proof that none exists for some family together with the equations such a counterexample forces. CT now holds for every iterated tensor **through degree four** (item 24), for all products of globes of total dimension five, for every tensor with a path factor, with I ⊗ I or with D² (items 19–21), and for **D^M ⊗ D³ and D³ ⊗ D^M for every M** (item 31). For globe pairs, CT has been reduced to **partial naturality of CaTT operations on ordinary globular pasting arities** (item 30), and that in turn to one coherence equation between normalization squares in a few residual cases (items 32–34). Results for one more case are not what we want: we want a uniform argument for all pairs (or at least all globe pairs and all cubes).

## 1. Setting

**CaTT** (Finster–Mimram, LICS 2017, arXiv 1706.02866). Types are `⋆` or `u →_A v`. Terms are variables or `coh_{P, u →_A v}[σ]`, where P is a context presenting a globular pasting diagram, σ is a substitution into P, and either (coherence) `Var(u : A) = Var(P) = Var(v : A)`, or (composition) `Var(u : A) = Var(∂⁻P)` and `Var(v : A) = Var(∂⁺P)`. The type of `coh_{P, u→_A v}[σ]` is `u[σ] →_{A[σ]} v[σ]`. **Definitional equality is syntactic identity.** A context is well formed when, for each variable `x : u →_A v`, the terms u and v have type A syntactically.

**Gray tensors.** For globular pasting diagrams Θ, Δ, let λΘ, λΔ be their Steiner complexes and ν(λΘ ⊗ λΔ) the strict ω-category of the tensor (Steiner, *Omega-categories and chain complexes*, HHA 2004). Its generators are the pairs y ⊗ x of cells, of dimension dim y + dim x, with boundary given by the Leibniz formula. Its cells are molecules (Hadzihasanovic, *Combinatorics of higher-categorical diagrams*, arXiv 2404.07273) and are determined by their sets of generators.

**The family context.** Fam(Θ ⊗ Δ) has one variable v_g per generator g, typed `T(∂⁻g) → T(∂⁺g)`, where T(c) is a CaTT term over the earlier variables denoting the cell c.

## 2. Statement

> **Conjecture CT.** For all finite globular pasting diagrams Θ, Δ there is an assignment T from cells c of ν(λΘ ⊗ λΔ) of dimension ≥ 1 to CaTT terms over Fam(Θ ⊗ Δ) such that
> - (a) T(g) = v_g for every generator g;
> - (b) T(c) is built from the variables by coh terms, and its strict interpretation (v_g ↦ g) is c;
> - (c) `T(c) : T(∂⁻c) → T(∂⁺c)` holds **syntactically** for every cell c. In particular Fam(Θ ⊗ Δ) is well formed.

The same question for iterated tensors λΘ₁ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ λΘ_k.

## 3. What is known

1. **Low dimensions.** For Θ, Δ of dimension ≤ 1, binary composites can be chosen consistently by hand. CT holds for D² ⊗ D¹.
2. **Layered terms fail for D¹ ⊗ D¹ ⊗ D¹.** "Layered" means T(c) is the vertical composite, along a layering of the molecule c, of whiskered generators. The source hexagon of the top cell has a unique layering whose first two layers are a square whiskered on the right and a square whiskered on the left, meeting at a 3-edge path. The first layer's target contains a subterm denoting the 2-edge path bc, and no subterm of the second layer's source denotes bc (in CaTT every term uses exactly the variables of its strict image). So the two sides bracket the 3-edge path differently and (c) fails. Non-layered terms are not ruled out.
3. **The weak form holds through dimension 4.** With (c′) — T(c) has a type u → v with u, v connected to T(∂⁻c), T(∂⁺c) by invertible coherence cells, inserted into the variables' types — CT holds for generators of dimension ≤ 4.
4. **Single coherence heads do not avoid the problem directly.** Taking T(c) to be one composition coherence coh_{P, u→v}[σ] requires T(∂^±c) to be literally u[σ], v[σ]; this fails when the boundary is a composite of lower cells that are not among σ's arguments.
5. **Semantics is settled.** The (∞,ω) Gray tensor of pasting diagrams is the free (∞,ω)-category on the Steiner tensor (Campion, arXiv 2311.00205, Cor. 3.5; Chanavat, arXiv 2603.22242, Thm 3.29). CT is only about syntax.
6. **Suspension closure (all dimensions).** With I = D¹ and P a finite globular pasting diagram, CT(I ⊗ P) ⇒ CT(I ⊗ ΣP). Proof: the hom (I ⊗ ΣP)(A, D) between the two extreme vertices is strictly isomorphic to I ⊗ P (checked on chains, f_r bijective on bases for r > 0, degree 0 giving the two kinds of A → D paths); every other hom is globular. Whiskerings R_r, L_r over D^r ∨₀ D¹ and D¹ ∨₀ D^r with recursively chosen types satisfy the composition-case support condition. In a globular pasting diagram every positive-dimensional generator has generators as source and target, so the endpoint declarations of Fam(I ⊗ P) have variable boundaries; a context Γ′ with a substitution σ : Γ′ → Σ_{A,D} Fam(I ⊗ P) then declares the mixed variables by σ-substituted suspended types, and T′(c) = (ΣT(c̄))[σ] satisfies (c) by stability of typing under suspension and substitution. Consequences: CT holds for I ⊗ Dⁿ, Dⁿ ⊗ I (via the odd-dimensional opposite) and I ⊗ Σ^r[m], for every n, r, m.
7. **Wedge closure for cylinders (all dimensions).** For finite globular P, Q: CT(I ⊗ P) ∧ CT(I ⊗ Q) ⇒ CT(I ⊗ (P ∨ Q)). Write z for the wedge point, u : z₀ → z₁ for the seam, a ∈ P₀∖{z}, b ∈ Q₀∖{z}, U = P(a,z), V = Q(z,b), L = (I⊗P)(a₀,z₁), R = (I⊗Q)(z₀,b₁).
   - *Strict part.* i : U → L (p ↦ p₀ # u) is a full sieve and j : V → R (q ↦ u # q₁) a full cosieve. This follows from the sign test: a nonzero positive chain of positive degree in a strongly loop-free unital basis has a differential with both signs.
   - The crossing hom (I⊗(P∨Q))(a₀,b₁) is the full sub-ω-category of L × R on the vertices (l, r) with l ∈ iU₀ or r ∈ jV₀. On Steiner tableaux the inverse is l + r − E_u, with E_u the tableau of u.
   - Every crossing cell from (ip, r) to (l, jq) factors strictly as (1_p, β) # (α, 1_q) through d = (p, q). Crucially, d depends only on the cell's 0-endpoints, so it is unchanged under higher faces.
   - *Terms.* Horizontal composites of the given terms on X = L × V and Y = U × R. One 2-coherence γ_d : S_Y(d) → S_X(d) per vertex d of U × V, over the generic path context, with identity image. Composition heads A_m (∗₀), V_m (∗₁) and W_m (∗₁ with a 2-term) whose types are prescribed recursively, so that ∂A_m(a,b) = A_{m−1}(∂a, ∂b) etc. literally.
   - The four endpoint cases are assigned S_X, S_Y, W_m(S_Y(h), γ_d) and V_m(W_m(S_Y(h_Y), γ_d), S_X(h_X)). Literal boundary agreement then holds in every dimension.
   With item 6 and the Batanin-tree decomposition P = ⋁ ΣP_j, this gives **CT(I ⊗ P) and CT(P ⊗ I) for every finite globular P**.
8. **The three-interval cube.** CT holds for I ⊗ I ⊗ I, the same terms serving both parenthesizations. Choose right-associated composites for paths. Declare each square with its two path terms. For a 2-cell (a sequence of adjacent swaps), whisker each square and pre- and post-compose with path coherences (identity image) so that consecutive steps share the same path term, then compose vertically. Declare the top cell between the two corrected full routes 321 → … → 123. All nonidentity cells (30 paths, 18 2-cells, 1 top cell) have been machine-checked against a certified CaTT checker. So the failure of *layered* terms (item 2) is a failure of that particular choice only.
9. **Where the cylinder argument stops.** For a general first factor Θ in place of I, reconstructing a cell across a wedge of the second factor subtracts a varying cell of the common copy of Θ, not the fixed seam tableau E_u. Positivity conditions then occur in degrees above one, and the intermediate vertex d of item 7 is no longer determined by the 0-endpoints, so a single 2-coherence per vertex no longer suffices.

10. **A strict comparison for the diagonal hom of two suspensions (all dimensions).** For finite globular P and Q, write ΣP and ΣQ for their suspensions, with vertices 0 and 1, and Sp for the suspended basis element of p. Let ε be the augmentation, extended by 0 in positive degree. The degree-+1 chain map
    - F(p ⊗ e ⊗ q) = Sp ⊗ Sq,
    - F(p ⊗ v ⊗ q) = ε(q) Sp ⊗ (1 − v) + ε(p) v ⊗ Sq (v = 0, 1),
    
    has nonnegative coefficients and commutes with ∂ in positive degree. Here e is the arrow of I, and at total degree 0 the image is one of the two directed paths from (0,0) to (1,1). Prefixing the row ((0,0),(1,1)) to the image of each tableau row gives a strict functor **Φ : P ⊗ I ⊗ Q → (ΣP ⊗ ΣQ)((0,0),(1,1))**. Φ sends the atom p ⊗ e ⊗ q to the atom Sp ⊗ Sq, so every mixed atom of ΣP ⊗ ΣQ has a source of one lower dimension. Φ is not injective.

11. **Φ has a pinned globular section for all finite globular P and Q.** There is a map s of underlying globular sets, commuting with both faces and preserving identities, with Φ s = id and s(Sp ⊗ Sq) = p ⊗ e ⊗ q on every mixed atom. Proof outline:
    - For two paths J = [r] and K = [s], cells of the diagonal hom are lifted directly. Arrows become sorted words (P-moves before Q-moves on each route, with the middle move at the crossing). 2-cells become sequences of oriented square swaps, whose extra squares lie in the endpoint panels and have identity image. 3-cells become whiskered oriented cubes, after strict exchange of disjoint swap blocks.
    - For general P and Q, a diagonal cell y is cut into local data over pairs (u, w) of nodes of the Batanin trees, by restriction to node paths and augmentation. Each local triple (U on J ⊗ I, V on I ⊗ K, W on J ⊗ K one degree lower), subject to three compatibility equations, is lifted by the path case. The bottom rows of the local lifts are then assembled into a tableau of P ⊗ I ⊗ Q. A coefficient bound (each prospective bottom row has mass ≤ 1, by positive chain maps to globes) makes each local bottom row a single vertex triple.
    - Restricted to the faces of the mixed atoms, Φ is injective, and the face-closure of the mixed atoms is recovered row by row from the image.
    
    The section is checked by machine on every diagonal cell of D⁴ ⊗ D⁴ and of a pair of branching trees.
12. **Transfer theorem: CT(P ⊗ I ⊗ Q) ⇒ CT(ΣP ⊗ ΣQ) for all finite globular P, Q.** Let U be a CT assignment for B = P ⊗ I ⊗ Q, and s the section of item 11. Build a substitution ρ from the family context of ΣP ⊗ ΣQ into Σ Fam(B), in order of dimension. Each endpoint-panel generator of B is sent to a boundary lift, inside the route pasting context R_v (R₀ = ΣQ ∨₀ ΣP, R₁ = ΣP ∨₀ ΣQ), of its strict image with the literally prescribed substituted endpoints. This is possible because strict interpretation on a pasting context is a trivial fibration, and the support of a term is that of its strict image. Each mixed generator Sp ⊗ Sq is declared with the ρ-substituted suspended type of p ⊗ e ⊗ q. Then T(c) = (Σ U(s c̄))[ρ] on the diagonal hom, and boundary lifting on the pure sides, satisfies (a)–(c). Composition preservation of s is not needed. For P = Q = I the panel lifts can be taken to be single exchange coherences over D² ∨₀ D²; this case was also checked by a certified CaTT checker.
13. **CT through dimension 3, and in dimension 4 when the top cells are atoms.** For any family context, choose terms degree by degree. Take the variable on a generator and the identity on an identity. For any other cell, compose the terms of a minimal strict expression, then correct its j-faces for j = 1, …, d−1 by composing at cut j with identity-image (j+1)-terms. Those terms live in the ≤ j-dimensional subcontext and exist by RC(j) there. RC(1) and RC(2) hold in every CaTT context, so this gives (a)–(c) for every cell of dimension ≤ 3. In degree 4 only declarations are needed when every non-identity 4-cell is an atom, which holds for all iterated tensors of globes of total dimension 4 (checked exhaustively). Making the choices on flattened basis tuples gives the same terms for every parenthesization. **The same argument works in every dimension given RC(j) for the j-skeleta of the family context.** These skeleta are strongly loop-free Steiner, so relative coherence on finite strong-Steiner contexts (any two parallel n-terms with the same strict image are joined by an (n+1)-term) would give CT for every tensor of globular pasting diagrams.
14. **Consequences.** CT holds for: every iterated tensor of globes of total dimension ≤ 4, including the four-cube I^{⊗4} compatibly with every parenthesization; D² ⊗ D² (with its identity towers), D³ ⊗ D² and D² ⊗ D³; and ΣJ ⊗ ΣK for all paths J = [r], K = [s] (each factor a vertical string of 2-generators of any length), since [r] ⊗ I ⊗ [s] has dimension ≤ 3.

15. **Wedge closure with a finite path factor (all dimensions).** For every finite path J = [r] and finite globular P, Q: CT(J ⊗ P) ∧ CT(J ⊗ Q) ⇒ CT(J ⊗ (P ∨₀ Q)). Write z for the common vertex. For a vertex l (a path) on the P side, let A(l) be the time of its last P-edge; for a vertex q on the Q side, let B(q) be the time of its first Q-edge.
    - The crossing hom is the full subcategory of L × R on the pairs with A(l) ≤ B(q), with inverse x + y − E_ij. Both A and B are nondecreasing along arrows.
    - Every crossing cell h factors as (1_{l₋}, β) #₀ (α, 1_{q₊}) through d = (l₋, q₊). The two factors split horizontally at the times A(l₋) and B(q₊), and all faces of h share the same d and the same two times.
    - The terms are cut-1 composition heads of horizontal composition heads, padded by path comparisons over the path contexts.
16. **A single seam path is impossible for three factors.** In I_A ⊗ I_B ⊗ [2] (spatial edges C, D), take the strict 2-cells CDBA → CDAB → CADB → ACDB → ACBD → ABCD and CDBA → CBDA → BCDA → BCAD → BACD → ABCD.
    - They are the two faces of one 3-cell: the image of the generating cube under the map that sends the last edge of I to CD.
    - The first can only factor through the seam path CABD, and the second only through CBAD, because the position in [2] of the BA → AB square is invariant.
    - The same happens in P ⊗ I ⊗ (Q ∨₀ Q′) whenever P, Q and Q′ each have an edge, via a strict retraction.

    So wedge closure with an interval in the middle cannot use one intermediate vertex per cell, as items 7 and 15 do.
17. **Cylinder factorization of crossing cells.** Let K be any finite strong-Steiner complex (for example P ⊗ I), W = U ∨_z V, and X, Y endpoints strictly on opposite sides of z. Each n-cell h of (K ⊗ W)(X, Y) factors uniquely as a normalized strict map D^n ⊗ [3] → K ⊗ W, with constant ends at X and Y and three cylinders in K ⊗ U, K ⊗ {z} and K ⊗ V.
    - The columns are L(g), Z(g), R(g): the parts of each signed row off the seam in U, at z, and off the seam in V. The moving vertices A(g) and B(g) are the incoming and outgoing edge sums at z.
    - The factorization commutes with faces, identities and hom composition.
    - Two projected cells l and r glue (as c = l + r − θ_z) **iff the middle column is a directed cylinder** D^n ⊗ I → K. Every positive degree can be the first degree in which this fails, so no finite set of low rows decides it.
18. **Suspension in any slot is a pullback.** (A ⊗ ΣP ⊗ B)((x,0,b), (y,1,b′)) ≅ (A ⊗ I ⊗ B)(X, Y) ×_{A ⊗ B′} (A ⊗ P ⊗ B′), where B′ is B with its differential negated in positive degrees, the right map augments the middle factor, and the left map takes the interval-edge column.

19. **Path factors (all dimensions).** CT([r] ⊗ P) and CT(P ⊗ [r]) for every finite path [r] and every finite globular P, with the ordinary cylinder declarations on every generator e ⊗ g. A crossing cell over s path edges is a stack of 2s + 1 strict cylinders (layer, edge, layer, …). Each is lifted in a globular context with prescribed caps and sides (boundary lifting on pasting contexts), the stack is formed by weak cylinder stacking (BMOSV, arXiv 2501.11620, Thm 4.3), and the two constant ends are normalized by an operation N_m that pads with whiskered identities. N_m only uses comparisons over generic pasting contexts, so no relative coherence in the ambient context is needed. This settles the constant-end part of the cylinder-lifting problem for arbitrary K.
20. **Wedge closure with a low-dimensional seam.** For every finite strong-Steiner K of dimension ≤ 2 and finite globular U, V: CT(K ⊗ U) ∧ CT(K ⊗ V) ⇒ CT(K ⊗ (U ∨₀ V)). The proof lifts the three strict cylinders of item 17 with caps fixed in the seam. It corrects at cuts 1, …, d with RC(1), RC(2) (d = dim K ≤ 2), and above disk degree d with the identity-cap comparisons of item 19. For dim K ≥ 3 the same lemma needs comparisons of parallel j-terms for j ≤ dim K.
21. **Polygons and D².** CT(B_{r,s} ⊗ P) for all r, s ≥ 2 and all P, where B_{r,s} is one 2-generator between two paths of lengths r and s. Hence CT(I ⊗ I ⊗ P), CT(D² ⊗ P) and the reverse orders, for every P. The suspension step uses a strict comparison Ψ : H ⊗ P → (B ⊗ ΣP)((x,0),(y,1)) with H the diagonal hom of B ⊗ I, a pinned section built from first-edge and last-edge collapses, and wedges by item 20. The same induction does not repeat with seam D² ⊗ I: its diagonal hom has a commuting square of indecomposable moves, so its underlying 1-category is not free.
22. **D³ ⊗ D³.** For every a, b ≥ 0 there is a strict comparison D^a ⊗ B_{3,3} ⊗ D^b → (D^{a+1} ⊗ I ⊗ D^{b+1})(bottom, top) with a pinned globular section (B_{3,3} the directed hexagon of traversal orders). The CaTT transfer is done for a = b = 1, giving CT(D² ⊗ I ⊗ D²) and, by item 12, CT(D³ ⊗ D³).
23. **The permutohedron route of the previous round is limited.** The comparison from a permutohedral shape to the diagonal hom of ΣP₁ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ ΣP_k has a pinned section for the four-cube, but for five axes an explicit 3-cell has no lift under the natural orientation, and for six or more axes no interchange orientation depending only on the swapping pair and its block order makes the trace surjective. Other sources of terms are not excluded.
24. **CT through degree four for every iterated tensor; dimension five for products of globes.** Choose terms through dimension three as interpretations of biased tricategorical expressions. For a 3-presentation with acyclic generating 1-graph and nonempty boundary paths of 2-generators, the free tricategory is faithful over the free strict 3-category on parallel 3-cells (the thin interchanger subgroupoid on the words of a strict 2-cell is connected with unique arrows; Guthmann's strictification, arXiv 1903.05777). CaTT 3-terms modulo 4-terms in both directions interpret the tricategory operations, so each needed equality gives literal CaTT 4-comparisons at cut three. This gives a CT assignment through degree four, composite 4-cells included, for every iterated Gray tensor of finite globular pasting diagrams, compatibly with every parenthesization. A product of globes of total dimension d has exactly one nonidentity d-cell (its top atom), so CT holds for every product of globes of total dimension five, the five-cube included. **Composite 5-cells need a correction at cut four, which neither argument supplies.**
25. **Globe pairs in arbitrary dimensions (one uniform assignment).** For D^m ⊗ D^n, all m, n: CT on the subpresentation generated by the bidegrees (i, j) with i ≤ 2 or j ≤ 2, including cells containing both (2,3) and (3,2). A section extends when either globe grows. Adjoining (3,3) requires the atom and 120 padded single-generator shapes (s_i s_j inputs for bidegree (i, j), s the little Schröder numbers 1, 3, 11, 45, …). Twenty-five shapes are done, all 121 central singletons lift with one common choice of five-faces, and compatibility with the rest of the section is outstanding on exactly 192 five-face shapes. Moreover all 121 central singletons lift with five-dimensional boundaries that are words in **one** family of elementary terms: pure moves are depth-zero actions in every dimension, every exchange of a pure move with an independent move is the naturality operation applied to the other move, and two smaller mixed exchanges (index pairs (1,3) and (2,4)) are proved in D³ ⊗ D³. The usual lexicographic word works on 112 inputs; a single amended rule (add the precedence 4 ≺ 1 when 2 ≺ 1, 2 ≺ 3 and 4 ≺ 3) covers all 121 by changing nine negative faces. Keeping the lexicographic word everywhere instead needs one more mixed exchange, index pair (1,4), on a two-event tableau in D⁴ ⊗ D⁴ with top labels (A_{1,+}, B_{4,*}) and (A_{4,*}, B_{1,+}), and with the four elementary 5-terms already fixed. These lexicographic five-terms have not been identified with the rest of the width-two assignment. In degree six (one more round of the same method): a word construction with two matchings W₀, W₁ needs only short lifts plus comparisons W₀(z) ≅ W₁(z) on single runs that change direction (2, 10, 40, 156 shapes in degrees 2–5); both signs of the (1,3) and (2,4) mixed exchanges exist; and the whole degree-six layer for all globe pairs needs, besides short tests at (2,4) and (4,2), exactly two signed distant exchanges in D⁴ ⊗ D⁴, which this method cannot avoid. **For D³ ⊗ D⁴, CT is reduced to two explicit 6-terms** (the short (2,4) lifts with stationary (3,1) padding, of type W(∂⁻w) → W(∂⁺w)); everything else, including the top 7-atom, is assembled and machine-checked from them. So the globe-pair route is advancing one bidegree per round, which is why we are asking for a uniform argument instead (Q1–Q2). Any two paths of elementary changes between serializations of the same 3-cell admit a CaTT 5-comparison with exact endpoints.
26. **Reduction of route (iii).** For the relative-coherence route it suffices, in every iterated tensor, to compare parallel terms whose strict top chain is a single generator with coefficient one (lower padding allowed). On finite strong-Steiner presentations, full relative coherence is equivalent to comparing parallel (k+1)-terms over the first identity on each intrinsic k-cell of the k-skeleton, for all k ≥ 1.
27. **One suspended factor.** D^m ⊗ D^n lifts strictly into the diagonal hom of D^{m+1} ⊗ D^n, pinning every generator of maximal right rank; every diagonal cell is in such a lift on a face, adds one pure right factor to it, or lies on a horizontal wedge of pure globes. A typed substitution realizes the lift on all pure variables and all variables of right rank one, in every dimension. The remaining substitution problem is explicit.
28. **Why the cubes matter now.** In the companion problem on relative coherence, a CaTT presentation of the Gray m-cube whose face subcontexts present the faces (CT for I^{⊗m}) is exactly what turns an (m−1)-sphere in a geometric fibre of a "seam" computad into a failure of relative coherence. CT for m ≤ 5 decides those computads with seam cycles of length ≤ 5. The first undecided one needs the six-cube. That case has since been pushed down to three dimensions without the six-cube (relative coherence r6, Response 19), but CT for all cubes would still decide every seam presentation at once.

29. **Reductions for globe pairs (round 7).** A full diagonal row of D^m ⊗ D^n has a cubical presentation with a pinned globular section, uniformly in the width and both dimensions; it suffices to compare terms on full diagonals, then only the two end decompositions of a full row when both end sites are mixed, then a single distant exchange per mixed rectangle and degree (equivariance under total opposition). The *double shift* has explicit face maps that lift through transverse rank two for every bound, but its left and right maps cannot commute literally. Alternatively, every cell of a globe pair is recovered from its collapse to a smaller globe pair by one signed substitution and at most one change of a stationary variable, and the image at transverse rank t+1 is always constructible from the preceding threshold (arbitrary attaching terms and first rank). For width four, any two paths of elementary exchanges between the same serializations have a CaTT comparison in every ambient dimension.
30. **Globe pairs from partial naturality.** Arbitrary-depth partial naturality (an ordinary partial-cylinder action, with prescribed raw faces, for every CaTT operation on an ordinary globular pasting arity and every up-closed selection of variables) implies CT for every pair of globes; for transverse bound n ≥ 2, depth ≤ n − 1 in arbitrary dimension suffices. At depth two only generic heads with a generating edge between two lowest-dimensional marks are still needed; a lowest mark can be arbitrarily far below the head when it is a leaf. The threshold construction needs these tests only on a prefix or suffix of one string of parallel variables and its cofaces, and every strict cell of those test contexts (indeed of every partial cylinder on a finite globular pasting arity) has a CaTT representative with literally matching faces; what remains is that the filler have **prescribed face terms**.
31. **D^M ⊗ D³ and D³ ⊗ D^M for every M.** If Λ has dimension ≤ m with all positive-dimensional declarations having variable faces, and Γ adjoins (m+1)-generators between parallel m-terms whose (m−1)-faces are variables, then Γ has RC in the relevant degree (the skeleton just below is freely generated by a globular set; pullback along L → T and boundary lifting). This gives uniform depth-two naturality, including when both ends of an edge are marked, hence CT for these pairs by item 30.
32. **Classes of operations with partial naturality.** The ordinary partial naturality action, with the two prescribed raw faces, exists (i) for every generic head whose two immediate poles are variables, all dimensions and up-closed selections; (ii) for the unbiased composite of every Σ^{m−2}B, B any 2-dimensional globular pasting arity, every m ≥ 2 and selection; (iii) for **every unbiased operation on every finite globular pasting arity, for selections generated in one degree**, whatever the dimension, depth of the marks and branching.
33. **Thin-head tests.** For the remaining selections, at each rank the thin-head test reduces to one comparison per *elementary substitution*. When the pure support has the height of the compared terms, every elementary substitution whose lower labels are variables passes (arbitrary subdivisions of top strings, empty blocks, different recursive bracketings). The complete collapse passes in every dimension (a spatial unit law at depth one for the already chosen temporal change), and a top argument labelled by an identity on any term can be eliminated.
34. **What is left for globe pairs.** Binary temporal composition passes the elementary substitution test at every rank, even when the two normalized inputs meet at an arbitrary composite face (a composition law for two spatial changes, and a temporal action on the square that moves a thin bridge across a spatial change). At rank k it suffices to test targets of height < k (a height-k target reduces to its signed boundary, with normal bridges as new top inputs). Positive-height composition heads reduce to thin heads, and among thin heads to the comparisons e → N(e) from an elementary term to its normal writing under substitutions with normal entries. What remains is a **coherence equation (6) between two pasted normalization squares** (the square chosen for e followed by the normalization of n[τ], against the normalization of e[τ]): it needs only normal outer operations, and in its last substitution only a binary split, an elementary collapse, or a labelling that uses each target top generator in exactly one input; it holds for all subdivisions of temporal strings, with empty blocks, at every rank and temporal depth. In the deep-threshold tests the last substitution may split or collapse any number of top edges with no equation on the choices (the elementary squares chosen together), even with arbitrary branching and differently written lower poles in the middle substitution. **The remaining case is (6) for the singleton labellings** (each target top generator used in exactly one input, once), or alternatively for last labellings in which each source top edge advances one edge at every target site of its lower pole, together with the extension of the choices to the exceptional heads. These are sufficiency reductions; none is known to be necessary.

## 4. Questions

**Q1 (main).** Decide CT for **all** pairs Θ, Δ of finite globular pasting diagrams and for all iterated tensors Θ₁ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ Θ_k, compatibly with associativity. By item 24 everything through degree four is done, so the question is about composite cells of dimension ≥ 5. A positive answer may use any terms. A negative answer should give a family with a proof that no assignment satisfies (a)–(c).

Natural routes:
- (i) **Higher-categorical coherence one level up.** Item 24 chooses 3-dimensional terms from tricategorical operations, so that faithfulness of free tricategories over strict 3-categories supplies the 4-comparisons. A version for degree n would choose (n−1)-dimensional terms from operations of a free algebraic (n−1)- or n-category (for example a Batanin–Leinster or Trimble-style operad, or Gray-enriched iterations) and needs **faithfulness of the free weak structure over the strict one on parallel top cells** for the presentations that occur (strong-Steiner skeleta of Gray tensors). Prove this faithfulness, or find where it fails.
- (ii) **Relative coherence on strong-Steiner skeleta.** By item 13 and item 26, CT follows from identity-image comparisons between parallel terms whose strict top chain is a single generator, on the skeleta of the family contexts. Prove this. (The companion relative-coherence problem shows that such comparisons can fail on positive non-Steiner computads, so the Steiner hypothesis must be used.)
- (iii) **Cylinder lifting in general layers.** Items 17, 19 and 20 reduce a three-factor tensor to lifting strict cylinders with shared caps. Item 20 does this when the seam has dimension ≤ 2. Extend it to seams of every dimension, for example K = P ⊗ I.

**Q2 (fallback, still every dimension).** If Q1 is out of reach, prove CT for **every pair D^m ⊗ D^n of globes** and **every Gray cube I^{⊗n}** (all n, compatibly with every parenthesization), by a construction uniform in the dimensions. For globe pairs, item 30 makes this a question about partial naturality on ordinary globular pasting arities: prove the ordinary partial naturality action with prescribed raw faces for every CaTT operation and every up-closed selection (Conjecture N on globular arities), or at least the coherence equation (6) for the singleton labellings of item 34. A proof that one of those tests fails, with the forced equation, would also be useful. For the cubes, a CaTT presentation of the n-cube in which each face subcontext presents that face is what item 28 needs.

**Q3 (if CT fails).** If CT fails for some pair or family, prove that no assignment satisfies (a)–(c) and identify the minimal extra rule that makes the family contexts well formed (for example a typing rule for generators whose boundaries are molecules, or a definitional equation between bracketings). A precise description of the forced equations is the useful answer.

## A minimal failure of boundary lifting has a superperfect loop group

Let $S$ be a nonempty set of generators of an iterated tensor as in §1, closed under their dependencies. Give its generators any well-formed CaTT declarations presenting their specified strict boundaries. Write $A_S$ for the free weak algebra and $C_S$ for the strict category. Suppose that on **every proper dependency-closed subset** the projection lifts objects and every strict cell at every prescribed pair of parallel raw faces. Put $m=\dim S$.

Then the projection on $S$ lifts all prescribed globes **through rank $m+1$**. If lifting fails at any higher rank, there is a unique strict cell $c$, up to identity padding, whose support is $S$. It has intrinsic rank $m\ge3$. At some fixed raw poles over its boundary, the space of its raw writings and their invertible higher comparisons is connected and integrally acyclic, with a nontrivial fundamental group $G$ satisfying

$$
                         H_1(G;\mathbb Z)=H_2(G;\mathbb Z)=0 .
$$

Equivalently, the group of $(m+1)$-loops at a writing of $c$, modulo $(m+2)$-comparisons, is nontrivial and superperfect. The first failed lifting rank in such a minimal support is exactly $m+2$.

**Support at a cut.** A strict cell is determined by its closed generator support and its dimension. This applies also inside any dependency-closed subset, by inclusion of its tableaux. The intrinsic dimension is the height of the support. In particular there is at most one unpadded full-support cell. At any cut $k$, the relation on parallel strict $k$-cells given by existence of a $(k+1)$-cell is a partial order. To see the strict increase, linearly extend the strong loop-free order and assign the degree-$k$ basis elements successive powers of an integer larger than one plus the largest sum of absolute boundary coefficients. On every $(k+1)$-generator the resulting functional has strictly positive value on the differential: every input precedes every output and both are nonempty. Apply it to the difference of the two $k$-pole chains. A nonidentity higher cell at this cut has nonzero $(k+1)$-row on at least one side. Indeed the tableau differential equations and strict positivity in each degree imply upwards from an empty pair of rows that all higher rows are empty. Thus all strict cells with equal $k$-poles are identities on that pole. [Closed-support reconstruction](../answered/steiner-support-formalizable-2026-11-18.md)

Fix a sequence of actual weak poles of ranks $0,\ldots,k-1$. Denote the resulting weak and strict $k$-fold homs by $U,V$. Use the path category $J(U)$ of relative-coherence round 7, with raw objects, morphism spaces $M_U(a,b)$ the nerves of bipointed labelled path arities, and $X(U)=BJ(U)$. We use three results from that round. There is a natural zigzag $M_U(a,b)\simeq X(U(a,b))$. Boundary lifting gives weak equivalences on these bars at every sequence of poles. Finally $X(A_S)\to X(C_S)$ represents the geometric comparison after groupoidification. For the last identification one uses the labelled-arity cocone on the weak side and the comparison from weak arities labelled in a strict category to its strict category of elements on the other side. This identification of the two bar models is natural and does not require boundary lifting on $S$. The geometric comparison is an equivalence here: the restricted basis is still strong Steiner and unital. Inductively its strict cellular attachments are homotopy pushouts. Under the comparison on each preceding skeleton the chosen weak attaching maps project to the specified attaching maps in the gaunt strict target. [Relative-coherence Responses 81–82](../answered/relative-coherence-all-dimensions-r7-2026-11-20.md) · [Campion’s pasting theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00200)

**Isolating a morphism space by support.** We prove by induction on $0\le k\le m$ that $X(U)\to X(V)$ is an integral homology equivalence for every sequence of raw poles. The preceding paragraph gives $k=0$. Here is the step for $k<m$.

1. At the fixed poles choose one raw object for each strict $k$-cell. Its support is proper since $k<m$. All prescribed raw poles already lie there by the support lemma, so lifting on this subset gives the representative. Two writings of the same object are equivalent in $J(U)$: within this proper support lift an identity in each direction and comparisons of the two composites with the identities. In $J(U)$ this gives inverse arrows in the category of components. An endomorphism space of a chosen object is weakly contractible. Every one of its labelled arities lies in that object's support, since all strict cells between its equal poles are identities. On that proper subset the fixed-pole comparison is an equivalence, and the corresponding iterated strict hom is the point. These arguments apply on the strict side as well. Thus use the full subcategory on the representatives, and inside it retain only the identity in each endomorphism space. The latter is a subcategory by the strict order at the cut. These two changes preserve the classifying space, by equivalence of objects and equivalence on morphism spaces. They commute with restriction to proper supports containing the poles: a representative lies in the least support of its image. Empty restrictions on the two sides agree.
2. In horizontal degree $p>0$, a vertical simplex of the path bar is a list of $p$ flags of labelled arities. Its labels are represented by one map from the wedge of the last arities of the flags, suspended $k$ times to return to $S$. The wedge has a full globular composite, also when some factors are points. The suspended composite uses all arity variables including its poles. Substituting the actual labels and applying the typed support lemma shows that the closed support of the resulting **single strict cell** is precisely the union of the labels' supports, including the fixed poles. Earlier flags factor through the last labels. The outer $k$-poles are the projections of the first and last objects of the string. Consequently, if the union is $S$, the endpoints must be $u=\partial^-_k c$, $v=\partial^+_k c$, for the unique full-support cell $c$. If no such cell exists, every simplex belongs to a proper subset. A horizontal zero-simplex always belongs to a proper subset at this cut.
3. Compare the two bars **relative to the union of their restrictions to proper subsets**. Finite intersections of restrictions are restrictions to intersections, including the empty case. The maps on absolute bars of proper subsets are equivalences by boundary lifting, and taking the union preserves a homology equivalence by gluing along simplicial subcomplexes. The induction hypothesis on the absolute bars therefore gives an integral homology equivalence on the relative total complexes.
4. Normalize horizontally. Every remaining string of positive length has strictly increasing objects. For length $p\ge2$, fix an object sequence. If the strict product is empty, so is the weak product by projection. Otherwise choose one strict vertex in each factor. A strict simplex with full support in any one factor could be combined with constant simplices at the chosen vertices elsewhere. Step 2 would then force the whole string and that factor to have the same endpoints, contrary to strict increase. Thus every simplex of every strict factor belongs to a proper restriction. The same holds for weak factors because projection preserves the support of the labelled composite. Each factor comparison is therefore a homology equivalence by gluing on proper restrictions. The product comparison is a homology equivalence by Eilenberg–Zilber and the integral Künneth theorem. The proper part of the product is the union of the products in which all factors belong to the **same** proper subset. Comparison there too is an equivalence on homology, by lifting on each subset and gluing their intersections. The relative column comparison therefore induces an isomorphism on vertical homology for all $p\ge2$.
5. In the mapping cone of the relative comparison, the vertical-homology spectral sequence now has at most column one: column zero vanishes by step 2. The total cone is acyclic by step 3. Consequently the column-one comparison is a homology equivalence. Its relative chain groups split by ordered endpoint pair and only $(u,v)$ can contribute. Adding back the proper unions, which already compare by gluing, proves the absolute homology equivalence on every morphism space. In case no cell has full support the same conclusion follows directly by gluing. Finally the zigzag with $X(U(a,b))$ proves the induction step. Returning from representatives to arbitrary raw endpoints uses composition with the equivalences from step 1, whose strict images represent identities in the category of components.

**The top fibre.** At $k=m$ the strict hom is discrete as a strict category, so its bar is equivalent to the discrete set of strict $m$-cells at these poles. The homology comparison implies a bijection on components and integral acyclicity on each component. In particular each strict label has a raw representative at these prescribed poles. The weak hom is groupoidal in positive dimensions since all these dimensions exceed the generating height. The groupoidal path-space calculation of Response 81 turns connectedness into an actual $(m+1)$-comparison between any two writings over a label. All lower prescribed lifts have proper support. This proves the lifting assertion. A component over a proper support is already weakly contractible by the hypothesis. In a full-support component $Y$, if $\pi_1Y=1$, Hurewicz and Whitehead give weak contractibility, and the groupoidal calculation then supplies all higher prescribed lifts there. Otherwise a loop representing a nonidentity element admits no $(m+2)$-comparison to the identity. For $G=\pi_1Y$, abelianization gives $H_1(G)=0$, and the Hopf exact sequence $\pi_2Y\to H_2(Y)\to H_2(G)\to0$ gives $H_2(G)=0$. For heights at most two the positive two-computad theorem already gives boundary lifting in every rank. ## Wedge closure in dimension three and binary tensors of total dimension five

**CT holds for every pair of globular pasting diagrams of total dimension at most five.** The new case includes composite five-cells. There is also an unbounded family: $D^3$ tensored with any finite object-wedge of globes of arbitrary dimensions, in either order.

**Wedges.** Let $K$ be finite strong Steiner of dimension at most three, and $U,V$ finite globular pasting diagrams. Then
\[
  CT(K\otimes U)\ \land\ CT(K\otimes V)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad CT(K\otimes(U\vee_0 V)).
\]
The reversed-order statement follows by the odd-dimensional opposite. Indeed the tensor pieces and the seam are strong Steiner. Their two-skeleta have no nonidentity two-cell on an identity path: the generating graph is acyclic and a two-generator has distinct outer endpoints. The relative-coherence degree-three theorem therefore gives identity-image comparisons of parallel three-terms with the same strict value. One can work on the three-skeleton whenever invertible comparisons are needed. Degrees one and two have the same comparison property. [Relative-coherence round 7, Response 16](../answered/relative-coherence-all-dimensions-r7-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-16)

Use the left presentation on the seam. Send each right seam generator of dimension $j$ to its left namesake corrected at cuts $1,\ldots,j-1$ to have the substituted right type. At each cut the preceding corrections make the two faces parallel, their strict values agree, and comparison in the seam suffices. Send each exterior right generator to a fresh variable of its substituted type. On the seam itself take the left section, so all its generators are pinned. ### Reading a facet step off its source

Take \(d=n-1\), numbering axes from zero. A ridge missing \(i,j\) has two fixed bits \(s_i^{ij},s_j^{ij}\). ### Why a second fork is impossible

On each remaining axis \(j\) complement the fixed bit if the number of marked axes before \(j\) is odd. This compensates precisely for the change of sign of that differential term. Boundary terms dropped along a marked axis have no marked coface contribution in the retained lower row. Consequently the retained rows satisfy both differential equations; all coefficients remain positive. Either everything is empty or the result is a strict cell. In degree zero there is at most one vertex on each side by the direction-set bound. A nonzero positive chain in a cube cannot have zero differential, and its differential has both signs, so a nonempty positive-degree row forces nonempty rows down to degree zero. Alternatively this follows from the strong-Steiner basis argument in Response 7. Both augmentations are then one. This operation preserves the relevant signed faces and takes a native top atom to the native top atom. We call the result the transverse tableau on the unmarked axes.

In a three-cube two outgoing squares on the same path require two adjacent descents. The decreasing word is the unique such path, and the missing axes of the two squares are the first and last coordinate. In the four-cube suppose facets missing \(i,j\) can both start at one two-cell. Now let \(n>4\). If two facets can start at the same vertex, their missing axes are distinct because the row fixes each facet's station. Keep those two axes and any other two, marking the complement.
