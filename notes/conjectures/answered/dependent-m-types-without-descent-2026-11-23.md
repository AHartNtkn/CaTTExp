# Final coalgebras of dependent polynomials without disjoint coproducts

## Setting

Let 𝒱 be a category, or more generally an (∞,1)-category, with:

**(a) Finite limits and finite colimits.** No compatibility between them is assumed. In particular, finite coproducts need not be disjoint, and colimits need not be stable under pullback.

**(b) A biclosed monoidal structure (⊗, I) whose unit is the terminal object 1.** There are internal homs A\C and C/A with
  Hom(A ⊗ B, C) ≃ Hom(B, A\C) ≃ Hom(A, C/B),
natural in all variables. The cartesian closed case (⊗ = ×) is the main example. The Gray tensor on (∞,ω)-categories is the motivating non-cartesian example.

**(c) Initial algebras of positive signatures.** A *positive signature* in one sort X consists of:
- a finite list of operations o_i : S_i(X) → T_i(X), where
  - each *source* S_i is built from X and fixed objects by finite limits, finite colimits, and ⊗ or × with fixed objects;
  - each *target* T_i is X, 1, an internal hom K\X or X/K with K fixed, or a finite limit of these (so T_i preserves limits);
- finitely many equations between parallel composites of the operations.

An algebra is an object X with maps o_i satisfying the equations, and a morphism of algebras is a map commuting with the operations. In the (∞,1) case, morphisms are coherent (strong) homomorphisms, and the equations are specified homotopies. Axiom (c): every positive signature has an initial algebra. Examples are the natural numbers μX.(1 + X) and lists μX.(1 + X ⊗ E).

**Dialgebras.** For a map p : E → A, a *p-dialgebra* is a triple (C, h : C → A, t : C ×_A E → C), where C ×_A E is the pullback of p along h. A morphism (C, h, t) → (C′, h′, t′) is a map g : C → C′ with h′g = h and t′ ∘ (g ×_A E) = g ∘ t, coherently in the (∞,1) case.

When pullback along p has a right adjoint Π_p, a p-dialgebra is the same as a coalgebra of the polynomial endofunctor P_p(X) = Σ_A Π_p(p*X) on 𝒱/A, composed to 𝒱. A terminal p-dialgebra is then the M-type (object of non-wellfounded trees) of p. The dialgebra formulation needs only pullbacks, so it makes sense in every 𝒱 of the setting.

## What is known

1. **Constant arities hold in every 𝒱 satisfying (a)–(c).** For objects A and E, let L := μX.(1 + X ⊗ E) be the initial algebra of (n : 1 → X, c : X ⊗ E → X). Coalgebras (C, h : C → A, t : E ⊗ C → C) then have a terminal object, namely L\A. The proof transposes a coalgebra morphism g : C → L\A to a map L ⊗ C → A, and then to an algebra morphism L → A/C for the algebra (A/C, h̄, curry(ev ∘ (1 ⊗ t))), which is unique by initiality. In the cartesian case this is the terminal p-dialgebra for the projection p : A × E → A. Streams (E = 1) are the special case νX. A × X = ℕ\A.
2. **The literature constructions all use extensivity.**
   - Abbott–Altenkirch–Ghani, *Containers: constructing strictly positive types* (TCS 342, 2005), Prop. 4.1: M-types exist in every Martin-Löf category (an LCCC with disjoint coproducts and W-types). The proof uses disjointness of coproducts.
   - van den Berg–De Marchi, *Non-well-founded trees in categories* (APAL 146, 2007; arXiv:math/0409158): M-types exist in every locally cartesian closed pretopos with W-types (Cor. 2.5). Prop. 2.9 uses only an NNO when A has decidable equality, but still works inside a pretopos.
   - Ahrens–Capriotti–Spadotti, *Non-wellfounded trees in homotopy type theory* (TLCA 2015; arXiv:1504.02949): the construction is the terminal chain Pⁿ(1), defined as a family ℕ → U by recursion into a universe. It uses funext; univalence is used only to show that finality is a proposition.
3. **Why disjointness enters.** Every construction known to us does one of two things:
   - it splits E × C into C ×_A E and its complement (to extend t to all addresses), which needs decidable equality on A and disjoint, universal coproducts; or
   - it computes the fibres of a family over ℕ defined by initiality (the terminal chain), which needs pullback-stability and disjointness of the coproduct 1 + ℕ.
4. **Case analysis on coproduct inputs is free.** Because ⊗ is biclosed, X ⊗ – and – ⊗ X preserve finite coproducts. For a polynomial with finitely many constructors, P(X) = ∐_{j∈J} A_j × (E_j\X), a coalgebra c : C → P(C) has a candidate final object: the equaliser, inside L\((∐_j A_j) + 1), of the consistent label functions on addresses L = μX.(1 + X ⊗ ∐_j E_j). Existence and uniqueness of maps into it follow by transposition and initiality, as in item 1. The one step that seems to need more is the structure map M → P(M). It requires splitting M along its root-label map M → ∐_j A_j + 1, that is, **universality** of finite coproducts (stability under pullback). Disjointness does not appear to be needed for that step.
5. **Locally presentable models are no counterexample.** If 𝒱 is locally presentable, every accessible endofunctor has a terminal coalgebra: the coalgebra category is accessible, and its forgetful functor creates colimits. So in every locally presentable 𝒱 satisfying (a)–(c), the coalgebra-form M-types of finitary polynomials exist. For dialgebras, the same argument works when pullback along p preserves colimits.
6. **Universality is not forced.** Let T : Set → {0 < 1} send X to "X is inhabited", and let Gl(T) be its Artin gluing: sets X with a flag y, where y = 1 only if X is inhabited, and maps that do not lower the flag. Gl(T) is locally presentable and cartesian closed, with coproducts (X ⊔ X′, y ∨ y′). For z : (1, 1) → (1, 0) + (1, 1) hitting the first summand, the pullbacks of the two injections are (1, 0) and (∅, 0), whose sum (1, 0) is not (1, 1). So Gl(T) satisfies (a)–(c) with ⊗ = ×, its finite coproducts are disjoint but not universal, and it still has all coalgebra-form M-types by item 5.
7. **Conatural numbers without universality (in-house; machine-checked in Agda for a 1-category with strict equations).** In any cartesian closed category with finite limits, finite coproducts and an NNO, the decreasing sequences M ↪ 2^ℕ are the terminal coalgebra of X ↦ 1 + X. The structure map is (! + 1) ∘ δ ∘ ⟨ev₀, – ∘ s⟩, where δ : 2 × M ≅ M + M is distributivity. Uniqueness follows by post-composing with 1 + M → 2 and with [0, 1] : 1 + M → M, then using initiality of ℕ. No universality or disjointness is used. The same idea, where an inconsistent branch "stays at the current node", appears to handle finitely many constructors over a cartesian base. It uses the diagonal, so it does not transfer to a non-cartesian ⊗ in general. It does transfer when ⊗ agrees with × on maps into the label objects (for example when maps A ⊗ W → B and A × W → B correspond for every set B), because every target of the construction is such a label object.
8. **Internal arities in coalgebra form (in-house, sketch).** For P(X) = List X = μY.(1 + X × Y), the coalgebra form of p : Fin → ℕ, the same pattern gives the terminal coalgebra over a cartesian base. Its carrier is the label functions List ℕ → ℕ satisfying equations of maps into 2. The corecursion never needs 1 + C as a target. What remains is the dialgebra form. Extending t : C ×_ℕ Fin → C to a total step needs the subobject {i < h x} ↪ C × ℕ to be complemented, that is, universality of 1 + 1 along lt : C × ℕ → 2.

## Questions

**Question 1 (main).** Determine the class of maps p : E → A for which **every** 𝒱 satisfying (a)–(c) has a terminal p-dialgebra. Either:
- give a construction of the terminal p-dialgebra from (a)–(c), uniform in 𝒱 (as in Known 1), for a class of p that includes at least all maps between finite coproducts of 1 and, if possible, all maps between objects that are initial algebras of positive signatures (such as ℕ, lists, finitary W-types); or
- give a 𝒱 satisfying (a)–(c) and a map p between finite coproducts of 1 (or between such initial algebras) with no terminal p-dialgebra, and identify which extra axiom restores existence. Candidates for that axiom are disjointness of finite coproducts, universality of finite coproducts, and decidable equality of A.

**Question 1′ (internal shapes and non-cartesian tensors).** Known 7 leaves two cases open. Treat them together:
- (i) p : E → A with A an arbitrary object, not a finite coproduct of 1. For example A = ℕ with E → ℕ the family of finite ordinals. By Known 8 the coalgebra form is available, so the question is the dialgebra form: is there a terminal p-dialgebra without universality, or a 𝒱 in which coalgebras of List exist and are terminal but p-dialgebras have no terminal object?
- (ii) ⊗ not cartesian, where the diagonal used by Known 7 is unavailable.

In either case, give a construction from (a)–(c), or a 𝒱 satisfying (a)–(c), necessarily not locally presentable by Known 5, without the terminal coalgebra.

**Question 2 (directed case).** Take 𝒱 = Cat_(∞,ω) with the Gray tensor, or 𝒱 = Cat_(∞,1) with ×. For which maps p : E → A does the category of p-dialgebras have a terminal object?
- Is exponentiability of p (for example, p a cocartesian or cartesian fibration) sufficient? Is it necessary?
- In particular, what happens for the inclusion {0 < 2} → [2]?

If Question 1 is answered by a uniform construction, the most useful form also says whether the construction is natural in p (stable under pullback of p along maps A′ → A).

Fallback, only if the general questions resist: the case A = 1 + 1, E = 1 → A the first injection (the polynomial X ↦ 1 + X, whose final coalgebra is the conatural numbers), in a 𝒱 satisfying (a)–(c) in which 1 + 1 is not disjoint.

## Why it matters

The axioms (a)–(c) give inductive types without any universe or descent principle. Constant-arity coinductive types follow from them. The question is whether non-wellfounded trees with label-dependent branching, already including the conatural numbers, also follow, or whether some form of extensivity (descent for coproducts) is the price of dependent coinduction.

## What would count as a solution

A proof (a uniform construction) for an explicit class of p, a countermodel for a map between finite discrete objects, or a reference to either.

## Literature search (2026-11-23)

The searches covered M-types from W-types (Abbott–Altenkirch–Ghani; van den Berg–De Marchi; Ahrens–Capriotti–Spadotti) and final coalgebras of accessible functors on locally presentable categories (Adámek, Worrell). Every positive construction found assumes a locally cartesian closed category with disjoint coproducts, or a universe. No result was found on dependent M-types in non-extensive settings, and no counterexample was found. van den Berg–De Marchi note that it is open whether there are interesting coinductive pretoposes that are not predicative toposes.

## Researcher Response 1

**Axioms (a)–(c) do not imply a terminal dialgebra, even for the first injection $1\to1+1$.** The same model separates the terminal List coalgebra from the dialgebra for finite ordinals. It has disjoint finite coproducts and a complemented diagonal on both label objects. For a finite function $f:J\to I$, its copower map works in every model exactly when $f$ is bijective or $|I|\leq1$. Universality of finite coproducts restores existence for every map between finite copowers. A product model gives both failures with a noncartesian tensor and nondisjoint coproducts. For the two directed categories in Question 2, a terminal-chain criterion characterizes every map. Exponentiability suffices, the inclusion skipping the middle of $[2]$ fails the test, and a different map to $[2]$ shows that exponentiability is unnecessary.

### Rectangular bands separate two notions of branching

Let $R$ be the full subcategory of ${\rm Set}^2$ on pairs of nonempty sets and the pair $0=(\varnothing,\varnothing)$. View $(L,R_0)$ as the rectangular band on $L\times R_0$ with multiplication $(l,r)(l',r')=(l,r')$. Maps are pairs of functions. Allowing the empty algebra gives precisely the finitary variety of rectangular bands, hence a locally finitely presentable category. Equivalently, colimits are componentwise, finite pairs are finitely presentable, and every pair is their filtered union. Limits are componentwise followed by replacement of a pair with an empty coordinate by $0$, the right adjoint to inclusion. Products are componentwise. If $X,Y$ are nonempty, their exponential is the pair of ordinary function sets. Also $Y^0=1$ and $0^X=0$ for nonempty $X$. This proves (a) and (b) with cartesian tensor. ([Bergman and Berman, Example 1.12](https://faculty.sites.iastate.edu/cbergman/files/inline-files/modes.pdf))

Here is the verification of (c), including equations. In a locally presentable category of the kind used here, the source and target expressions are accessible functors. Finite limits are accessible, as are finite colimits, tensoring with a fixed object, and the fixed-object internal homs (right adjoints between locally presentable categories). A category of objects with the specified operations is a finite inserter construction for these functors. Each equation takes an equifier of its two term transformations. These constructions preserve accessibility, taking a sufficiently large accessibility rank. The forgetful functor creates small limits. Indeed the operation at a limit $X=\lim X_j$ is induced by $S(X)\to S(X_j)\to T(X_j)$ and $T(X)\cong\lim T(X_j)$. Equalities of parallel composites hold after every projection from their target, hence at the limit. A complete accessible category has an initial object by the solution-set form of Freyd's theorem, since a small set of presentable generators is weakly initial. Thus every signature in (c) has its required algebra. ([Positselski, *Notes on limits of accessible categories*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16773))

Write $2=\{0,1\}$. The copower $A=1+1=(2,2)$ has four points. For the first injection $p:1\to A$, put $u=(0,1)$, $v=(1,0)$. Pulling back $p$ to either point gives $0$, since one coordinate pullback is empty. Their product in the **band operation** is the active point $uv=(0,0)$. Form two dialgebras on $A$, both with root map the identity and with respective constant step maps $1\to A$ at $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$. Suppose $g_0,g_1$ were their maps into a terminal dialgebra. The singleton over $u$ has empty step domain and maps into both, so $g_0(u)=g_1(u)$. The same holds at $v$. Band homomorphisms preserve multiplication, so $g_0(uv)=g_1(uv)$. Apply the common target step at this point with the unique input over $uv$, then apply the target root. Step and root preservation give $(0,0)=(1,1)$, a contradiction.

Coproduct injections in $R$ are disjoint, since their componentwise intersection is empty. Universality fails because along $u:1\to1+1$ *both* summands pull back to $0$. The label object has decidable equality even in the sense of a complemented diagonal. For $A=(2,2)$, the complement in $A^2$ is the pair of off-diagonal subsets of $2^2$. Its coproduct with the diagonal is $A^2$, their intersection is $0$, and the two componentwise equality tests define $A^2\to1+1$. Thus disjointness and this equality test can hold together in the countermodel. A requirement that the equality decomposition persist under every base change is stronger. A mixed truth value already witnesses the failure of pullback stability here.

For the internal example take $N=(\mathbb N,\mathbb N)$, with its componentwise zero and successor. This is the NNO by ordinary recursion in each coordinate. The componentwise less-than map $N^2\to1+1$ gives the family $p=(q,q)$, where $q:\{(n,i)\mid i<n\}\to\mathbb N$ is projection. The same $u,v$ in $N$ have empty pullback because one coordinate is zero. Now use the reverse product $vu=(1,1)$, which has an input $((1,0),(1,0))$. With the same two constant steps on the carrier $N$ over its identity, the preceding proof gives the same contradiction. The diagonal of $N$ is complemented by the same formula. On the other hand the functor $X\mapsto{\rm List}(X)$ is componentwise, including at $X=0$. The two set list algebras give the initial algebra of $Y\mapsto1+X\times Y$. Let $T$ be the set of ordered, finitely branching rooted trees, allowing infinite branches. Sending a tree to its list of immediate subtrees makes it the final Set List coalgebra. A coalgebra unfolds uniquely by recursion on finite child addresses. Then $(T,T)$ is the final List coalgebra in $R$, by the same two componentwise universal properties. Local presentability in Known 5 applies to this endofunctor. It supplies no terminal object for these dialgebras.

For a single model that also has a noncartesian tensor and nondisjoint coproducts, take $R\times Q$, where $Q=\{0,\tfrac12,1\}$ is the ordered category with tensor $x\otimes y=\max(0,x+y-1)$ and residual $x\Rightarrow y=\min(1,1-x+y)$. Its tensor unit is top and $\tfrac12\otimes\tfrac12=0$, while the cartesian square is $\tfrac12$. The product category is locally presentable and biclosed, and the argument for (c) applies. Its two injections into $1+1$ intersect in $(0_R,1_Q)$, distinct from the initial object $(0_R,0_Q)$. For both families above the second component of $p$ is $1_Q\to1_Q$ (the NNO and the less-than truth object there are top). Give each witness dialgebra and singleton second component top. The same contradiction follows under first projection. In a terminal dialgebra the maps from the two singleton witnesses still have to agree. Lists in the second factor are top, for the cartesian list signature as well as for the tensor list signature, since $1_Q+Z=1_Q$. Thus the product still has the terminal List coalgebra and supplies the fallback for nondisjoint $1+1$.

### Classification for maps induced by finite functions

Let $f:J\to I$ be a function of finite sets and let $p_f:J\cdot1\to I\cdot1$ be its interpretation by copowers of the terminal object. Then every category satisfying (a)–(c) has a terminal $p_f$-dialgebra **if and only if** $|I|\leq 1$ or $f$ is bijective. This statement concerns the maps specified by finite functions. A particular category may have additional maps between the same copowers. The first injection also refutes the proposed bound by initial-algebra carriers. Indeed, with fixed objects allowed as sources, every object $B$ is the initial carrier of the one-operation signature $B\to X$, via its identity.

If $|I|=1$, the terminal underlying object with its unique label and transition is terminal. If $f$ is bijective, a dialgebra is a labeled object with an endomorphism, so the constant unary construction of Known 1 applies. This also covers the unique function with empty codomain.

For the converse suppose $|I|\geq2$. The following three cases exhaust the other functions. Each model below satisfies (c) by the accessibility and limit argument above.

**Nonempty domain, non-surjective function.** In the rectangular-band model choose $a$ in the image of $f$ and $b$ outside it. In the label band $(I,I)$, the points $(a,b)$ and $(b,a)$ both have empty fiber. Their band product $(a,a)$ has an input. The two constant transitions on the identity-root carrier with distinct output labels give the same contradiction as for the first injection.

**Surjective, non-bijective function.** Use affine spaces over $k=\mathbf F_3$, including the empty affine space. This is the finitary variety of affine combinations over $k$. The tensor of $U,V$ is the free affine space on $|U|\times|V|$ modulo the relations making the universal map separately affine. The affine maps $U\to W$ form an affine space under pointwise affine combinations. Currying identifies biaffine maps with affine maps into this internal hom. Symmetry and associativity follow by representing separately affine maps of two and three variables, respectively. The singleton is its tensor unit and terminal object. The tensor is genuinely noncartesian. For the affine line $B=1+1$, $B\otimes B$ is free on four generators and has dimension three, while $B\times B$ has dimension two.

Here $A=I\cdot1$ has dimension $|I|-1>0$, and the affine map $p_f$ is surjective with translation kernel $K$ of dimension $|J|-|I|>0$. An affine section identifies $p_f$ with $A\times K\to A$. If $(T,h,t)$ were terminal, the map $h t:T\times K\to A$ would be jointly affine. A map from the singleton dialgebra with constant label and unique transition gives a point $z\in T$ at which $h t(z,-)$ is constant. Affinity forces every slice $h t(z',-)$ to have the same linear part in $K$, hence to be constant. Choose a nonconstant affine map $u:K\to A$. A map from the dialgebra $(A,\mathrm{id},(a,k)\mapsto u(k))$ to $T$ would furnish a slice of $h t$ equal to $u$, a contradiction.

**Empty domain.** Use the slice $\mathrm{Vect}_k/k$, again with $k=\mathbf F_3$. Write an object as an augmentation $v:V\to k$. Tensor two augmented spaces by $(V,v)\otimes(W,w)=(V\otimes_kW,v\otimes w)$, using $k\otimes_k k\cong k$. The unit $(k,\mathrm{id})$ is terminal. The internal hom from $(V,v)$ to $(W,w)$ is the vector space of pairs $(g,s)\in\operatorname{Hom}_k(V,W)\times k$ satisfying $wg=sv$, augmented by projection to $s$. Indeed, currying an augmented map $(Z,z)\otimes(V,v)\to(W,w)$ sends $x\in Z$ to $(g_x,z(x))$, and evaluation gives the inverse. This proves biclosedness by symmetry. The slice is cocomplete and generated under filtered colimits by the finite-dimensional augmented spaces, which are finitely presentable.

In this category $A=(k^I,\sum)$ and $p_f:0\to A$. Choose distinct coordinate vectors $a,b\in k^I$. Take $C=(k^3,\sum)$ with $h(e_1)=h(e_3)=a$ and $h(e_2)=b$. Its transition domain is $\ker h=k(e_1-e_3)$, with zero augmentation. Define two linear transitions by sending $e_1-e_3$ to $0$ and to $e_1-e_2$, respectively. Each preserves augmentation. The three singleton-unit probes selecting the vectors $e_i$ have zero transition domain, so each maps to both dialgebras. They span $C$, forcing the two maps to any terminal dialgebra to coincide. Preservation of transitions would identify the images of $0$ and $e_1-e_2$, whose labels are $0$ and $a-b\ne0$. This rules out terminality even for the empty-domain function.

### Universality alone restores the finite-copower case

Suppose finite coproducts are stable under pullback. Let $E=\coprod_{j\in J}1$, $A=\coprod_{i\in I}1$ and take **any** arrow $p:E\to A$. The following proof works with coherent morphisms and with a noncartesian tensor.

First, every coproduct injection is a monomorphism. Pull the coproduct back to one summand $B$. Its self-pullback is a summand of the terminal object in the slice over $B$, with a point supplied by the diagonal. A pointed summand of a terminal object is terminal. Extending its identity across the other summands using the point gives an inverse to its structure map. Moreover $A$ is 0-truncated. Given $h:X\to A$, write $X\simeq\coprod_i X_i$ by base change. The component of each $h|_{X_i}$ in $\operatorname{Map}(X_i,A)$ is contractible since it factors through the monomorphism $1_i\to A$. Taking the product proves the same for the component of $h$.

Write $D$ for the category of $p$-dialgebras and $K$ for machines with a label in $A$ and a total endomorphism for each $j\in J$. The restriction functor $R:K\to D$ uses the $j$-th endomorphism on $C\times_{A,p_j}1$, where $p_j:1\to A$ is the image of the $j$-th generator. These domains sum to $C\times_A E$ by universality. Conversely there is a functor $J_0:D\to K$ with $R J_0\simeq\mathrm{id}$. To construct its $j$-th endomorphism, pull the label coproduct back along $p_j$, writing $1\simeq\coprod_k U_{jk}$. With $C_i=C\times_A1_i$, universality gives
$$
 C\simeq\coprod_{k,i} U_{jk}\times C_i,\qquad
 C\times_{A,p_j}1\simeq\coprod_k U_{jk}\times C_k.
$$
The second formula follows by restricting to $U_{jk}$, where $p_j$ factors through $1_k$. On $U_{jk}\times C_i$, use the given transition for $i=k$ and the projection to $C$ for $i\ne k$. Restricting recovers the given transition by the second formula. All decompositions are pullbacks, and the maps out of them are copairings, so these operations are functorial, including on coherent maps. The refinement by $U_{jk}$ allows $1$ to be disconnected.

By Known 1, $K$ has a terminal object $T$, since biclosedness gives $E\otimes C\simeq\coprod_j C$. Applying $R$ to the terminal cocone on $J_0$ gives a natural transformation $\eta:\mathrm{id}_D\to\mathrm{const}_X$, where $X=R T$. To obtain the terminal object, take the equalizer in $D$ of $\mathrm{id}_X$ and $\eta_X$. This equalizer exists even in the higher setting. The forgetful functor $U:\mathcal V/A\to\mathcal V$ preserves parallel-pair limits because the limit of the constant parallel pair on $A$ represents free loops in $A$ and is equivalent to $A$ by 0-truncation. Thus the equalizer in the slice inherits the transition by the limit property of its underlying object, with the same property for structured maps. For each $Z\in D$, naturality of $\eta$ homotopes postcomposition by $\eta_X$ on $\operatorname{Map}_D(Z,X)$ to the constant map at $\eta_Z$. Its homotopy equalizer with the identity is the space of paths ending at $\eta_Z$ with free starting point, hence contractible. This proves finality using (a)–(c) and universality alone.

### Exponentiable maps admit a terminal chain

Let $p:E\to A$ be exponentiable and suppose inverse $\omega$-chains have limits. The polynomial has domain and codomain $\mathcal V$. Its composite is
$$
 \mathcal V\xrightarrow{X\mapsto(E\times X\to E)}\mathcal V/E
 \xrightarrow{\Pi_p}\mathcal V/A
 \xrightarrow{\Sigma_A}\mathcal V.
$$
The first functor is base change from the terminal object and preserves limits, as does $\Pi_p$. The slice projection $\Sigma_A$ creates limits of inverse chains because their indexing category has contractible classifying space, so the limit of the constant $A$-diagram is $A$. Consequently $P_p$ preserves the limit of $1\leftarrow P_p1\leftarrow P_p^21\leftarrow\cdots$. Its limit $W$ is equivalent to the limit of its tail, hence has a structure $W\simeq P_pW$. For any coalgebra $C\to P_p C$, iteration from $C\to1$ gives the compatible maps to its stages. More explicitly, the coalgebra mapping space is the homotopy equalizer on $\operatorname{Map}(C,W)$ of the identity and the map induced by $f\mapsto P_p(f)\circ c$, using $W\simeq P_p W$. On the tower $\operatorname{Map}(C,P_p^n1)$ this second map shifts the preceding stage forward using $c:C\to P_p C$. The zeroth stage is contractible. At each successor, the new component is specified up to a path from the image of the preceding one, a contractible choice including its compatibility with the preceding projection. Taking the limit gives contractibility (the terminal-chain argument cited below). Transposing its structure gives precisely the terminal $p$-dialgebra. The proof works in an ordinary category or an $(∞,1)$-category and uses only the cartesian pullbacks in the dialgebra definition. ([Ahrens, Capriotti and Spadotti, terminal-chain construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.02949))

Pullback need not preserve the terminal carrier, even for exponentiable maps. In Set, pull $p={\rm id}_2$ back to a singleton. The original carrier consists of binary streams, and fixing their first entry leaves arbitrary tails, whereas the pulled-back signature has just one stream.

### A presheaf criterion characterizes the directed maps

For $\mathcal V={\rm Cat}_{(\infty,1)}$ use complete Segal spaces on $B=\Delta$. For $\mathcal V={\rm Cat}_{(\infty,\omega)}$ use complete Segal $\Theta$-spaces on $B=\Theta$. Write $i:\mathcal V\hookrightarrow\mathcal P(B)$ for the full inclusion into space-valued presheaves. The representables are local objects. Local objects are closed under limits and under objectwise coproducts. In a map from a Segal spine into a coproduct, all cells lie in the same summand because the spine is connected. The same holds for the connected shapes testing completeness (including their higher-dimensional versions). Thus these tests commute with disjoint unions. We use the cartesian pullbacks of these models throughout, also when the chosen tensor is Gray. ([Rezk, *A model for the homotopy theory of homotopy theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9811037), [Loubaton, *Categorical Theory of (∞,ω)-Categories*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05425))

For an arbitrary $p:E\to A$, form $W=\lim_n P_{ip}^{n}(1)$ **in presheaves** as above. Every map of presheaves of spaces is exponentiable. The necessary and sufficient test is
$$
  p\text{ admits a terminal dialgebra in }\mathcal V
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  W\text{ satisfies the Segal and completeness conditions on }B .
$$
The right side uses only $p$, presheaf dependent products and a specified inverse limit. When it holds, $W$ with the ambient root and step is terminal by fullness and closure under pullbacks.

For necessity, let $r:W\to iA$ and $s:W\times_{iA}iE\to W$ be the ambient root and step. Define an endofunctor on the presheaf slice over $W$ by
$$
 H(D\xrightarrow d W)=
 \big(D\times_{iA}iE\xrightarrow{d\times1} W\times_{iA}iE\xrightarrow{s} W\big),
$$
where the first pullback uses $rd$. An $H$-algebra is exactly a dialgebra equipped with a dialgebra map to $W$. Since $W$ is terminal in ambient dialgebras, forgetting that map is an equivalence. Also $H$ preserves coproducts, by universality of presheaf coproducts. For any generalized element $x:y b\to W$, the free $H$-algebra has carrier $F_x=\coprod_{n\geq0}H^n x$ over $W$, with the shift as structure. Indeed a map from $x$ to an algebra extends recursively along the summands, and at each stage the space of extensions with prescribed preceding stage is contractible. The underlying presheaf of $F_x$ is local. It starts with a representable, its iterates use pullbacks of local objects over $iA$, and their coproduct is local.

If $T$ is terminal among local dialgebras, equip $iT$ with its ambient map to $W$. The free-algebra property identifies $\operatorname{Map}_{/W}(x,iT)$ with the space of dialgebra maps $F_x\to iT$, which is contractible by locality and terminality. By Yoneda this is the homotopy fiber of $iT(b)\to W(b)$ at $x$. All fibers are contractible for every $b$, so $iT\simeq W$, establishing necessity. In particular, taking a localization of a nonlocal $W$ cannot supply a terminal dialgebra. The test concerns the infinite limit itself and makes no assertion of finite-time decidability.

### Two maps to the walking composable pair distinguish exponentiability from existence

First take $p:\{0<2\}\hookrightarrow[2]$. Form a dialgebra on an arrow over $0\to1$ and an isolated object over $2$. Both active objects, over $0$ and $2$, step to the isolated $2$. Form another on an arrow over $1\to2$ and an isolated $0$, with both active objects stepping to that $0$. In each case the pullback step domain is discrete, so these are functors. The singleton over $1$ has empty step domain and embeds in both. In a terminal dialgebra their mapped middle objects would therefore be equivalent. Compose the mapped first arrow, that equivalence and the mapped second arrow. This gives an arrow over $0\to2$. Pair it with the edge of $E$ and apply the terminal step. The resulting arrow starts over $2$ and ends over $0$, by the prescribed two steps. Its root would be an arrow $2\to0$ in $[2]$, a contradiction. Only objects, arrows, equivalences and composition were used, so the argument applies in both directed categories. Their disjoint, universal coproducts show the scope of the restoration theorem: internal label objects can still fail.

For a positive, nonexponentiable example in **both** directed categories, let $p:D\to[2]$ be the rank map of the diamond $b<m_0,m_1<t$. To test exponentiability, observe that the two adjacent intervals glue along their shared endpoint to give $[2]$. On pullback one obtains the two halves of the diamond. Their coherent pushout cannot be $D$. To see this, map the two halves into the ordinary free category on the four edges of the diamond. These maps agree on the middle fiber, but their two bottom-to-top composites differ, whereas $D$ equates them. The free category has only identity isomorphisms, so an equivalence of functors cannot repair the difference. The adjacent-interval pushout is valid in both categories by the Segal condition. Pullback along an exponentiable map would preserve it (being a left adjoint). In $\mathrm{Cat}_{(\infty,1)}$ all cartesian and cocartesian fibrations are exponentiable, so the terminal-chain result applies to both. See [Ayala and Francis, *Fibrations of $\infty$-categories*, Lemmas 1.11 and 2.15](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.02681).

Construct the terminal diamond dialgebra as a poset. Take the set of possibly infinite trees with root label $a\in[2]$ and one child for each $e\in p^{-1}(a)$ at every node of label $a$. For two trees $x,y$ a matched pair of finite paths starts at their roots and repeatedly chooses $e\le f$ in $D$ over the two current labels, moving to the indicated children. Put $x\preceq y$ if every such pair ends at labels in weakly increasing order, including the empty path. This is reflexive because the fibers are discrete, so paths matched in a tree to itself choose identical addresses inductively. If $x\preceq y\preceq z$, insert a path in $y$ into any matched paths in $x,z$, one step at a time. At each prefix the middle label lies between the two other labels. For $e\le f$ over those outer labels there is a $d$ of this middle rank with $e\le d\le f$, by inspection of the diamond. This constructs the middle path and proves transitivity. If both comparisons hold, root labels agree and each common child address can be matched with itself. Recursing on address length shows that all labels and all addresses coincide. Hence this relation is antisymmetric.

The root map to $[2]$ is monotone by the empty-path test. The child operation is monotone because any matched child paths extend to matched paths by prepending the chosen $e\le f$. For a directed dialgebra $C$, unfold each object using its step on objects. For an arrow $c\to c'$, any matched choices $e\le f$ give an arrow in $C\times_{[2]}D$, and the step supplies an arrow between the chosen children. Iteration along any finite matched paths gives an arrow whose roots are weakly increasing. Thus the objectwise unfolding preserves arrows and defines a functor to the tree poset. A poset target has a unique arrow for comparable objects and all higher compatibility data are forced. It preserves roots and steps by its definition. Conversely, preservation determines every label at every finite address. Maps into a poset form a discrete mapping space, including in the higher directed model, so this objectwise uniqueness gives coherent terminality. The two middle choices account simultaneously for the interval witnesses needed for transitivity and for the two distinct composites obstructing exponentiability.
