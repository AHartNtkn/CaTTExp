# Relative coherence in CaTT in every dimension: free weak ω-categories are strict exactly on homotopical computads

*A self-contained problem. It assumes familiarity with globular weak ω-categories, strict ω-categories, polygraphs (computads) and Steiner complexes, and nothing else. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked.*

**What we are asking for.** One theorem valid in every dimension: the exact class of finite computads on which the free weak ω-category is strict (in the sense of §1.2), ideally "exactly the homotopical ones". Individual new cases (one more context, one more dimension) are not useful to us. Since the last round: degree three is settled in every dimension (RC_id(3) ⟺ bubble-free 2-skeleton, item 39); the six-seam ring Γ₆ fails RC, and (⇒) holds for the whole class of unary seam presentations (items 43–44); the homotopy hypothesis holds for groupoidal CaTT algebras (item 45); under RC_id(∞) the comparison Free(Γ) → F(Γ) is a weak equivalence after groupoidification at every iterated hom (item 46); and (⇐) holds for positive 2-computads (item 48). What is missing is the passage from these groupoidified statements to the directed geometric fibres for arbitrary computads (for (⇒)), and the contraction for homotopical computads (for (⇐)).

## 1. Setting

### 1.1 CaTT

CaTT (Finster–Mimram, LICS 2017, arXiv 1706.02866) is a dependent type theory whose models are weak ω-categories (Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, arXiv 2106.04475: models are exactly Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis weak ω-categories).

```
Types          A ::= ⋆ | u →_A v
Terms          t ::= x | coh_{P, A}[γ]
Contexts       Γ ::= ∅ | Γ, x : A
Substitutions  γ ::= ⟨⟩ | ⟨γ, x ↦ t⟩
```

- `dim ⋆ = −1`, `dim(u →_A v) = dim A + 1`, and a term of type A has dimension `dim A + 1`.
- A **pasting context** `P ⊢ps` presents a globular pasting diagram. It has boundaries `∂⁻P`, `∂⁺P`.
- **The only term former.** If `P ⊢ps`, `P ⊢ u →_A v` and `Δ ⊢ γ : P`, then `Δ ⊢ coh_{P, u→_A v}[γ] : (u →_A v)[γ]`, provided either
  - (coherence) `Var(u : A) = Var(P) = Var(v : A)`, or
  - (composition) `Var(u : A) = Var(∂⁻P)` and `Var(v : A) = Var(∂⁺P)`.
- Definitional equality is syntactic equality. There are no other equations.
- Binary composites `u ∗_k v` and whiskerings are composition coherences over two discs glued along a k-disc.
- Contexts are exactly finite computads, and the terms of Γ are the cells of the free weak ω-category on Γ (Benjamin–Markakis–Sarti, arXiv 2405.00398). Coherence terms are invertible in the coinductive sense (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2406.12127).

### 1.2 Strict image and relative coherence

Every context Γ is a polygraph. Write `F(Γ)` for the free strict ω-category on it. Strict ω-categories are CaTT models, so every term t has a **strict image** `π(t) ∈ F(Γ)`: a coherence is sent to the unique strict composite of its arity.

> **RC(n) for Γ.** Any two parallel n-terms `T, T′` of Γ with `π(T) = π(T′)` are connected by an (n+1)-term `T → T′`.

Because coherence terms are invertible, RC(n) is symmetric. Write **RC(∞)(Γ)** for "RC(n) holds for Γ for every n ≥ 1".

> **RC_id(n) for Γ.** As RC(n), with the connecting term required to have identity image: `π(T → T′) = 1_{π T}`. Write RC_id(∞) as above.

RC_id implies RC. They agree when every endo-(n+1)-cell on an n-cell of F(Γ) is an identity, which holds in the loop-free class of Question 1. With loops, plain RC is too weak: in (x; f : x → x; α : f ⇒ f), the pair (f, f) is joined by α itself, whose image is not an identity. Wherever loops can occur, RC_id is the statement that matters.

*Equivalent form (corrected 2026-11-20).* Strictification is surjective on cells of every finite context in degrees 0–3, but **not in general**: in every degree ≥ 4 there is a finite context with a strict cell that is the image of no term (item 38). Under RC_id(∞)(Γ), however, every strict cell lifts (lift a strict expression by induction, correcting shared boundaries by identity-image comparisons). Hence RC_id(∞)(Γ) is still equivalent to: **π_Γ : Free(Γ) → F(Γ) is a trivial fibration of CaTT models**, and also to π_Γ being a weak equivalence (F(Γ) is gaunt: its only invertible cells are identities, since the multiset of top generators is additive). The trivial-fibration form says: for every pair of parallel (n−1)-terms u, v and every strict n-cell c : π(u) → π(v) there is an n-term t : u → v with π(t) = c.

### 1.3 Contexts we need

All of the following are finite. The full cylinders (Y = Var(Q)), the joins, the Gray products of globes and the orientals present strongly loop-free Steiner complexes (Steiner, *Omega-categories and chain complexes*, HHA 2004; Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches pour les ∞-catégories strictes*), so F(Γ) = ν(K) has cells determined by their supports. The partial cylinders (Y ≠ Var(Q)) also present strongly loop-free unital Steiner complexes (§3 item 9), and they are positive in every dimension (the source of x↑ has x⁻ as a factor and its target has x⁺).

- **Cylinders.** For a pasting context Q and an up-closed set `Y ⊆ Var(Q)` (if y ∈ Y occurs in the type of x then x ∈ Y), the context `Q↑Y` keeps each `x ∉ Y` and replaces each `x ∈ Y` by two copies `x⁻, x⁺` and a cell `x↑ : a_x → b_x`, where for x of dimension n

  ```
  a_x := ( … ((x⁻ ∗₀ (τⁿx)↑) ∗₁ (τⁿ⁻¹x)↑) … ) ∗_{n−1} (τx)↑
  b_x := (σx)↑ ∗_{n−1} ( … ((σⁿ⁻¹x)↑ ∗₁ ((σⁿx)↑ ∗₀ x⁺)) … )
  ```

  with every factor `w↑` for `w ∉ Y` omitted. For Y = Var(Q) the strict ω-category F(Q↑Y) is the Gray cylinder `D¹ ⊗ Q`. The smallest example is the **lax square** (x⁻, x⁺, y⁻, y⁺; x↑, y↑, f⁻, f⁺; F : f⁻ ∗₀ y↑ ⇒ x↑ ∗₀ f⁺).
- **Joins.** For a pasting context Q, `Cone(Q; c)` adds an object c and, for each variable t of Q of dimension m, a variable `p_t : p_{τt} → p_{σt} ∗_{m−1} (p_{σ²t} ∗_{m−2} ( … (p_{σᵐt} ∗₀ t)))` (with `p_x : c → x` for objects). F(Cone(Q; c)) is the Ara–Maltsiniotis join `c ⋆ Q`.
- **Gray molecules.** Gray products `D^{n₁} ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ D^{n_k}` of globes, and more generally pasting diagrams in Steiner's or Hadzihasanovic's sense, presented in CaTT by giving each cell the type `⌈∂⁻x⌉ → ⌈∂⁺x⌉` with chosen composite terms for its boundary molecules.
- **Orientals.** For example the **triangle** (x, y, z; f : x → y, g : y → z, h : x → z; α : f ∗₀ g ⇒ h).


## 2. Why it matters

RC is the single missing ingredient in several constructions in CaTT: lax naturality of every coherence with respect to every up-closed set of its variables (equivalently a lax cylinder Γ ⊗ D¹ for every context Γ); composites, unique up to higher cells, for non-globular pasting diagrams (Gray products of globes, joins); cones over every coherence; and the comparison of the free CaTT model on a computad with its strict free ω-category, which is the unit condition in comparing CaTT models with (∞,ω)-categories.

## 3. What is known

1. **RC(1) and RC(2) hold in every context.** For 2-terms: normalise to vertical composites of whiskered 2-variables; two composites with the same strict image are related by exchange moves (Ara–Burroni–Guiraud–Lafont–Métayer, *Polygraphs*, arXiv 2312.00429, §4.1), and each move is one coherence.
2. **Support lemma.** supp(π t) = supp(t) in every context, and if t has no variable of dimension dim t in its support then supp(t) = supp(σt) = supp(τt). Parallel terms whose image is an identity on a cell w are joined by one coherence over supp(w) whenever supp(w) is a pasting context. For a pasting context P, π_P is a trivial fibration (this is the coherence and composition rule).
3. **RC(3) is known** for the lax square, the triangle, every cylinder Q↑Y, the 2 × 2 and 2 × 3 grids, the Gray cube D¹ ⊗ D¹ ⊗ D¹, and joins over vertical chains of 2-discs. Each proof is a hand-built normalisation for one context; in each, RC(n+1) needed a separately constructed coherence of the normalisation one dimension up.
4. **Adjoining top generators in dimension 3.** If Λ has dimension ≤ 2 and an acyclic generating 1-graph, and Γ adds 3-generators between parallel 2-terms of Λ, then RC(3)(Γ) ⟺ RC(3)(Λ) (using depth-1 naturality, Benjamin–Markakis–Offord–Sarti–Vicary, arXiv 2501.11620, Thm 3.1). In dimension 4 only a conditional version (assuming RC(3) and RC(4) for Λ) is known.
5. **Exchange combinatorics in dimension 2 are settled.** For a 2-dimensional context with an acyclic generating 1-graph and non-empty boundary paths, the 2-paths representing a strict 2-cell c are the linear extensions of an event poset, and the graph of exchange moves with squares and hexagons as 2-cells is simply connected; squares and hexagons are single coherences.
6. **Loops obstruct.** In Γ = (x; a : 1_x ⇒ 1_x) the Eckmann–Hilton 3-cell a ∗₀ a ⇛ a ∗₀ a and the identity are parallel with equal strict image, and no 4-term joins them (the weak free object groupoidifies to S², the strict one B²ℕ to K(ℤ, 2), and π₃(S²) ≠ 0).
7. **Gaunt targets.** F(Γ) is gaunt for every finite Γ (generator counts), so an FHM ω-weak equivalence into F(Γ) is a trivial fibration. RC_id(∞)(Γ) ⟺ π_Γ is a trivial fibration ⟺ π_Γ is a weak equivalence.
8. **Homotopical computads.** Γ is *homotopical* when the comparison from the free (∞,ω)-category on Γ (generators attached by homotopy pushouts) to F(Γ) is an equivalence. Strongly loop-free Steiner complexes with unital basis, and regular directed complexes with frame-acyclic molecules, are homotopical (Campion, arXiv 2311.00200; Chanavat, arXiv 2603.22242, Thm 3.17). **A finite 2-polygraph is homotopical if and only if F(Γ) has no bubble** (no non-identity 2-cell 1_x ⇒ 1_x); in particular every 2-polygraph whose 2-generators all have non-empty target (or all non-empty source) is homotopical, loops and cycles in the 1-graph allowed. Bubble-freeness is undecidable. For a homotopical context, RC_id(∞)(Γ) is equivalent to the unit of the realization adjunction between CaTT models and (∞,ω)-categories being a weak equivalence at the free CaTT model on Γ.
9. **Partial cylinders are strong Steiner.** For Y ⊆ Var(Q) up-closed, the complex K_Y obtained from the Steiner tensor I ⊗ λQ by identifying c⁻ = c⁺ and killing Ic for c ∉ Y is strongly loop-free and unital, with ∂⁻-iterates (∂⁻)^j Ix = (σ^{j−1}x)⁻ + I(τ^j x), (∂⁺)^j Ix = (τ^{j−1}x)⁺ + I(σ^j x) (omitted terms zero); so F(Q↑Y) ≅ ν(K_Y).
10. **Cartesian strictification.** Call Γ *positive* if every generator of dimension ≥ 2 of its polygraph has non-identity source and target. For every generator-preserving map f : Δ → Γ of positive CaTT contexts (variables to variables, attaching terms preserved), the square Free(Δ) → Free(Γ) over F(Δ) → F(Γ) is a pullback in every dimension: a term t of Γ and a strict cell d of F(Δ) over π(t) have exactly one lift. (Proof: Henry's theorem that composition is cartesian on positive polygraphs, *Non-unital polygraphs form a presheaf category*, Cor. 2.3.13, 2.4.9, plus induction on terms.)
11. **Reduction to polyplexes.** For a strict cell c of F(Γ), Γ positive, let P_c → Γ be its generic (Henry) polyplex, with the uniquely lifted weak attaching terms. Then π_Γ is a trivial fibration iff every π_{P_c} is; the same holds for RC(n) and RC_id(n) for each n. Positive 2-polyplexes are strong Steiner, so **RC(n) for every finite positive context of dimension ≤ 2 is equivalent to RC(n) for every finite strong-Steiner context of dimension ≤ 2** (no hypothesis on the 1-graph).
12. **The fibre over one generator.** If Γ = (Λ, z : a → b) is a strong Steiner atom polygraph with z of top dimension m, then Free(Γ) ×_{F(Γ)} 𝔻^m is the free extension, by one m-cell, of Free(Λ) ×_{F(Λ)} ∂𝔻^m (𝔻^m the strict m-globe mapped onto π(z)). So the trivial-fibration property over the cell π(z) would follow from: *adjoining an m-cell to a trivial fibration B → ∂𝔻^m, along an attaching sphere over the two faces, gives a trivial fibration B[ẑ] → 𝔻^m.* That statement is unproved, and it does not cover strict composites of z with other generators.
13. **Arity lemma.** If a coherence coh_{P,A}[γ] has a substituted strict boundary using a top generator z exactly once, the variables of P labelled by terms involving z form one suspended subtree Σ^m Q of P's Batanin tree.
14. **Maximal variables.** If Y ⊆ Var(P) consists of variables occurring in no other variable's boundary, P↑Y is a pasting context, and every term over P (any dimension) has a naturality term over P↑Y with its full cylinder boundary. This does not give compatibility under substitutions that change a selected variable's boundary.
15. **Not counterexamples.** Chanavat's 4-dimensional regular directed complex (arXiv 2509.26563, Comment 2.67) has a polygraph Q whose strict free category differs from the "stricter" one, but the extra equations appear only in Q → r^>Q, so they do not give equal strict images in F(Q). Benjamin–Markakis's hom-computads (hom weak ω-categories of free computads are free) do not transport π: for (x), the hom computad has distinct 0-cells id_x and id_x ∗₀ id_x, while Hom_{F(x)}(x, x) has one.
16. **A related open conjecture.** Dean (*Globular multicategories with homomorphism types*, thesis, Thm 5.4.0.1) proves an all-dimensional acyclic-fibration strictification for fibrational homomorphism types with a strict computation law; the weak analogue is his Conj. 5.4.0.9. No equivalence with RC is known.

17. **Two-dimensional bubbles obstruct RC.** Let Λ be a finite context of dimension ≤ 2 such that F(Λ) has a non-identity 2-cell c : 1_x ⇒ 1_x. Then there are parallel 3-terms T, T′ with π(T) = π(T′) and no 4-term T → T′ at all, so RC(3)(Λ) fails. Proof:
    - Choose a 2-term U with π(U) = c, and correct its boundaries by coherences over the context (x) to get a : 1_x ⇒ 1_x literally, with π(a) = c.
    - Take T = eh(a, a), the Eckmann–Hilton 3-term a ∗₁ a → a ∗₁ a (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2402.01611, §6.1), and T′ = 1_{a∗₁a}.
    - Interpret Λ in the double delooping 𝒢 of the strict braided monoidal groupoid with objects ℕ, End(p) = ℤ, ⊗ = + and braiding β_{p,q} = pq. Every 1-cell goes to the unique 1-cell, and every 2-generator to 1.
    - 𝒢 is a Gray category, so it is an algebra of the Gray-category 3-operad. That operad is contractible with top-dimensional uniqueness (Batanin–Cisinski–Weber, arXiv 1209.2776, Ex. 3.6, §4.5, Ex. 5.7), and extended by identities it is a CaTT model with only identity 4-cells.
    - There, [[T]] = ±N² and [[T′]] = 0, where N > 0 is the number of 2-generators in c.
18. **Heredity.** If Δ ⊆ Γ is dependency-closed, RC_id(n)(Γ) ⇒ RC_id(n)(Δ): an identity-image filler H has supp(H) = supp(πH) ⊆ Var(Δ), by item 2. With item 17: if F(Γ_{≤2}) has a bubble, then RC_id(3)(Γ) fails, whatever the higher generators of Γ. For finite contexts of dimension ≤ 2: RC_id(∞) ⇒ RC(3) ⇒ bubble-free ⇔ homotopical.
19. **Higher endo-cells obstruct homotopicality.** If F(Γ) has a non-identity n-cell q : 1^{n−1−k}_c ⇒ 1^{n−1−k}_c (0 ≤ k ≤ n − 2, c a k-cell), then Γ is not homotopical. Proof: the n-skeleton inclusion is discrete Conduché, so homotopicality passes to Γ_{≤n}. Counting n-generators gives F(Γ_{≤n}) → Bⁿℤ, and q gives Σ^{n−2}B²ℕ → F(Γ_{≤n}). Steenrod powers then detect nonzero mod-p cohomology of the groupoidification in unboundedly many degrees, while the free cellular n-skeleton has dimension n. The converse ("no such endo-cell ⇒ homotopical") is proved in dimension 2 (item 8) and for positive-to-one polygraphs in every dimension. It is **false** in dimension 3 (item 28).
20. **Higher bubbles obstruct RC_id(∞) in every dimension.** Suppose F(Γ) has an n-cell q : 1_b ⇒ 1_b, q ≠ 1, n ≥ 2, where b is an (n−2)-cell (possibly itself an iterated identity). Then π_Γ is not a trivial fibration, so RC_id(∞)(Γ) fails. Composite q of even weight and b of positive dimension are included. Proof:
    - *A list model.* Fix n and a set C of colours. Take one cell in each dimension below n, finite lists in C as n-cells, colour-preserving bijections of positions as (n+1)-cells, and identities above. This is a CaTT model, natural in recolourings. Every n-head concatenates its input lists, one block per n-generator occurrence of its generic strict cell, in a fixed order. Every (n+1)-head matches positions: give the positions in each *slot* distinct auxiliary colours, transport them along the labelled bijections, and match equal colours. A slot is a class of n-variables of the pasting context joined by (n+1)-variables. Two facts about generic pasting contexts make this well defined: each strict n-cell uses each slot at most once, and the two n-faces of a higher cell use the same slots. Both are proved by the decomposition of a Batanin tree into suspended subtrees, and checked by machine on all trees with at most 8 edges. (n+2)-heads are well defined because colour-preserving bijections between lists without repeated auxiliary colours are unique.
    - *The separation.* With distinct colours, the suspended Eckmann–Hilton term eh_{n−2}(a, b) : a ∗_{n−1} b → b ∗_{n−1} a (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2402.01611, §§6.1, 6.3) must be the block exchange, and by naturality it stays the block exchange after recolouring to one colour. For a = b of length m > 0 this is not the identity.
    - *The reduction.* By heredity (item 18), pass to the n-skeleton Λ. Boundary lifting gives a with literal type 1_d → 1_d and π(a) = q. Send each n-generator to a one-element list, so a has length m = (number of n-generators in q) > 0. Then eh(a, a) and 1_{a∗a} are parallel, have the same identity image, and are separated in the model.
21. **The unrestricted extension theorem is false.** Adjoin α : 1_f ⇒ 1_f (a 3-generator on the weak identity of f) to the 2-disc (x, y; f, g : x → y; β : f ⇒ g). The 2-disc satisfies RC_id(∞) (item 2), but the extended context fails RC(4) by item 20 with n = 3. The same works in each attachment dimension m ≥ 3. So adjoining generators can preserve RC_id(∞) at best for attachments that keep the context homotopical.
22. **Split criterion and the reduction of necessity to reconstruction.** Let A = Free(Γ), let C = F(Γ) as a CaTT model, and let q : KC → C be the computadic cofibrant replacement. KC has one generator for each parallel pair (u, v) of cells of KC and each strict cell c : q(u) → q(v) (Dean–Finster–Markakis–Reutter–Vicary, arXiv 2208.08719, §5; Markakis, arXiv 2303.11978, §5). Sending each generator x of Γ to the generator labelled by π(x) and its already-embedded boundary gives a generator inclusion i : A → KC over C. KC is A with the remaining generators freely attached.
    - **RC_id(∞)(Γ) holds iff there is a strict CaTT map r : KC → A with r i = id_A and π r = q.** (⇒) extends id_A across the remaining generators by boundary lifting. (⇐) lifts through q and applies r.
    - Let L be realization from CaTT models to (∞,ω)-categories, and N the nerve. Let e : LA → NC and d : LKC → NC be induced by π and q. Then e is the comparison defining homotopicality, and d is the *reconstruction map* of the strict target C. We have d ∘ Li = e. Under the split criterion we also have e ∘ Lr = d and Lr ∘ Li = id, so **e is a retract of d over NC**. Hence RC_id(∞)(Γ) together with "d is an equivalence for C = F(Γ)" implies that Γ is homotopical. More precisely, under RC_id(∞)(Γ), Γ is homotopical iff there is s : NC → LKC with d ∘ s ≃ id and s ∘ e ≃ Li.

23. **Inductive reconstruction fails; coinductive reconstruction suffices.** With the notation of item 22, the inductive reconstruction map d_C : L(KC) → NC is **not** an equivalence for any nonempty strict C. Proof:
    - Let D be the free strict ω-category with one object p, a loop ℓ, and in each dimension k ≥ 2 one generator for each ordered parallel pair of (k−1)-cells. D is gaunt, because top-generator counts are additive, and D → 1 has boundary lifting in every dimension.
    - K1 has one object v and a loop generator u. Sending u to ℓ and to 1_p gives two maps L(K1) → ND. They restrict to different points of the discrete space Map(D¹, ND), so they lie in different components, while Map(N1, ND) is a single point. So d₁ is not an equivalence.
    - For nonempty C, 1 → C → 1 exhibits d₁ as a retract of d_C.
    
    However, D → 1 is an FHM weak equivalence, so Q(ND) ≃ 1, where Q is coinductive completion. For Γ of generator dimension ≤ m, both ends of e lie in (∞,m)-categories. That subcategory is coreflective, with the m-core as right adjoint, so it is closed under the colimits building LA, and its objects are coinductively complete (Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde, arXiv 2603.11005, Lemmas 4.4, 5.22–5.23). Hence **e is an equivalence iff Qe is**, and by the retract of item 22, **RC_id(∞)(Γ) together with Q d_{F(Γ)} being an equivalence implies that Γ is homotopical**. Equivalently, with η the unit at A and W the FHM weak equivalences: given RC_id(∞), Γ is homotopical iff η ∈ W; given homotopicality, RC_id(∞) holds iff η ∈ W.
24. **Simultaneous algebraic lifts of strict simplices.** Suppose π : A → C has globular boundary lifting. Call a strict CaTT map h : K Oₙ → A with π h = σ q an *algebraic lift* of a strict simplex σ : Oₙ → C (Oₙ is Street's oriental). The pairs (σ, h) form a simplicial set, and forgetting h is a trivial fibration onto the Street nerve of C. Proof: the face suborientals satisfy O(F) ∩ O(G) = O(F ∩ G) (Steiner's tableaux), and the subcomputads K O(F) ⊆ K Oₙ satisfy the same formula. So compatible face lifts glue, and the remaining generators lift one at a time. This does not give the geometric section s of item 22, which needs a comparison from geometric simplices to the realizations of K Oₙ.
25. **The dimension-3 exchange step reduces to a wedge lemma.** Assume (H): Γ is finite, of dimension ≤ 2, has an acyclic generating 1-graph, and every 2-generator has nonempty source and target paths. Then the 2-paths representing a strict 2-cell are exactly the linear extensions of an event poset, and exchange moves are swaps of adjacent incomparable events (item 5).
    - Under (H), a contiguous antichain of events in a 2-path spans, in its generic polyplex (item 11), a wedge A₁ ∨₀ ⋯ ∨₀ A_k of single-generator contexts glued at cut vertices. So, by the polyplex reduction, the part of RC(3) that concerns permuting such a block is exactly **the wedge lemma**: RC(3)(A₁ ∨₀ ⋯ ∨₀ A_k) follows from RC(3)(A_i) for each i.
    - For k = 2 this is known: two independent letters with a cut vertex are handled by horizontal normal forms and one interchange coherence.
    - The rest of RC(3) under (H) consists of forced-order statements: letters joined by connectors that cross their boundary blocks, whose fibres have a single linear extension. This includes a fusion statement comparing normalisers along substitution. These are of the kind already proved for cylinders and grids.
    - The smallest wedge with k = 3 is the polyplex of the hexagon triple in the 3 × 3 grid: three lax squares glued at two cut vertices. Its key ingredient is the three-factor interchange (α ∗₀ β ∗₀ γ) · (α′ ∗₀ β′ ∗₀ γ′) ⇛ (α·α′) ∗₀ (β·β′) ∗₀ (γ·γ′).

26. **Both comparisons are 0-truncations.** In coinductively complete (∞,ω)-categories, Q d_C : QL(KC) → NC and Q e : QLA → NC both exhibit NC as the **0-truncation** of their source, in the mapping-space sense: every space of maps into a 0-truncated object is a 0-type, though such an object can have non-identity directed cells in every dimension.
    - *Algebraic descent.* For a globular trivial fibration p : X → C of CaTT models with C strict, and any strict D in which coinductively invertible positive-dimensional cells are identities, precomposition Hom_str(C, D) → Hom_CaTT(X, D) is a bijection. Two cells over the same strict cell are joined by a lifted globe cylinder whose mixed generators lie over identities. Those generators are therefore coinductively invertible, so they become identities in D.
    - The 0-truncated objects are exactly the discrete, coinductively complete Segal Θ-nerves (Berger; Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde). Precomposition with q (by descent) and with π (by freeness) is bijective on maps into them.
    - **Consequence: Γ is homotopical iff π_j Map(𝔻ⁿ, QLA) = 0 for all n ≥ 0 and j ≥ 1, at every base point.** Reconstruction at C is the same vanishing for QL(KC).

27. **Support localization (all dimensions).** For a strict n-cell c of F(Γ), let E_Γ(c) be the homotopy fibre over c of Map(𝔻ⁿ, L Free(Γ)) → {strict n-cells}. For dependency-closed Δ ⊆ Γ, F(Δ) → F(Γ) is discrete Conduché, so L Free(Δ) is the pullback of L Free(Γ) along NF(Δ), and E_Δ(c) ≃ E_Γ(c) whenever c ∈ F(Δ). By item 26, Γ is homotopical iff every E_Γ(c) is contractible. Under RC_id(∞), E_Γ(c) is contractible whenever supp(c) has generator dimension ≤ 2. A minimal counterexample to (⇒) has a non-contractible fibre over a **full-support** cell in its top generator dimension m ≥ 3, and the m-globe suffices.
28. **Positivity is not enough; no endo-cells does not imply homotopical (the middle wire).** Take objects 0 → 1 → 2 → 3, edges f₀, f₁ : 0 → 1, g : 1 → 2, h₀, h₁ : 2 → 3, 2-cells α₀, α₁ : f₀ ⇒ f₁ and β₀, β₁ : h₀ ⇒ h₁, and 3-generators A : α₀g → α₁g, B : gβ₀ → gβ₁. This Γ is positive, has an acyclic 1-graph and no endo-cells, and its 2-skeleton has RC_id(∞); RC holds through degree 3. There is one strict 3-cell c : α₀gβ₀ → α₁gβ₁ using A and B, but E(c) ≃ S¹: the diagonal hom is two squares (the two simultaneous diagrams (Ah₀) ∗₁ (f₁B) and (f₀B) ∗₁ (Ah₁)) glued along their perimeter. The two exchange 4-terms through the two diagonals have equal (identity) image and no 5-term between them, so RC(4) fails. The mechanism is a **two-sided idle overlap**: the path g is an idle right suffix of both faces of A and an idle left prefix of both faces of B. So the positive fallback of the previous Question 2 is false, adjoining generators to a bubble-free context can destroy RC, and the converse of item 19 is false.
29. **Transition presentation of 3-cell fibres.** For a 3-computad with homotopical 2-skeleton B, form B_k by adjoining k fresh parallel copies h(z, i) of each 3-generator z as 2-generators. The strict 2-cells of F(B_k) form a simplicial set T whose mapping spaces over c are E(c). A simultaneous diagram of d events gives a cube (Δ¹)^d, and in the mapping space a (d − 1)-dimensional permutohedral cell; a fibre of weight r (number of top occurrences) has a CW model of dimension ≤ r − 1. Under RC_id(∞): every 3-cell fibre is connected with π₁ = π₂ = 0 (edges and the square and hexagon 2-cells are realized by CaTT 4- and 5-terms). For positive 3-computads under RC_id(∞), every fibre of weight ≤ 4 is contractible, and H_{r−1}(E(c)) = 0 in weight r. A positive counterexample to (⇒) in generator dimension 3 needs weight ≥ 5 and its first nonzero homotopy group in degree j with 3 ≤ j ≤ r − 2.
30. **No idle overlap ⇒ homotopical (positive 3-computads, unconditional).** Let all 2-generators have nonempty input and output paths and all 3-generators nonidentity faces. If no idle right suffix of a generating 3-face is an idle left prefix of a generating 3-face, the transition complex is a CAT(0) cube complex with directed intervals given by order ideals of an event poset, and **every fibre is contractible**. In general, weight-two components are points, intervals or circles, and a circle occurs iff some nonempty path is an idle suffix of both faces of one generator and an idle prefix of both faces of another (item 28); such a circle rules out RC.
31. **Strip products.** If lower generators are unary and, at every cut between strips, the events crossing the cut are strictly ordered in the same way on both sides, then E(c) ≃ ∏_b K_b over strips, and under RC_id(∞) each factor (hence E(c)) is contractible. Without the cut hypothesis two locally possible exchanges can fail to glue.
32. **Seam rings: spheres in every degree.** For k ≥ 2 there is a finite positive 3-computad Γ_k with acyclic 1-graph (signal lanes s_i with gates L, M, R; seam lanes p_i with loops d_i, e_i; 3-generators a_i, b_i, and a bridge z spanning the whole line) and a full-support strict 3-cell c_k with E(c_k) ≃ S^{k−1}; every other fibre is contractible. More generally, for unary seam-signal presentations E(c) ≃ Z_K, the subcomplex of the k-cube whose faces are the sets of seams with acyclic induced *seam graph* (i → j when b_i ≤ a_j in the forced temporal order). The fibre is contractible iff the seam graph is acyclic; a chordless directed cycle of length m gives a nonzero class in H_{m−1}. Distributed seams realize S^{r−1} for every max(2, k − s) ≤ r ≤ k in one presentation.
33. **Detecting seam spheres by CaTT terms, through Gray cubes.** Colour the seams of a minimal non-face of size m by fresh 2-generators h_i : p_i ⇒ p_i (positive, generator-preserving maps to Γ transfer boundary lifting by item 10), erase colours by literal substitutions to weak identities, and label the threefold suspensions of the faces of a CaTT presentation of the Gray m-cube. If a CaTT presentation of the Gray m-cube exists whose face subcontexts present the faces (canonical Gray terms, CT, for I^{⊗m}), then RC_id fails for the seam presentation in some degree ≤ m + 2. CT is known for m ≤ 5, so: Γ₄ fails RC_id(n) for some n ≤ 6, Γ₅ for some n ≤ 7, and under RC_id(∞) every unary seam presentation whose induced directed cycles have length ≤ 5 has contractible fibres. The same holds for iterated suspensions. The literal pullback of two opposite erasures has no 3-cell at all between the needed endpoints, so literal gluing of the two coloured facets is impossible.
34. **Reflections and fixed points.** If an involution of the presentation fixes c and its weak poles, reverses all faces above dimension m, and there is an equivariant map E(c) → S^{k−1} (antipodal), then RC_id fails in some degree ≤ m + k − 1. This decides symmetric seam presentations with fibre S^{k−1} for every k. Under RC_id(∞), any two literally fixed parallel weak lifts lie in the same component of E(c)^{hC₂}.
35. **Substitution and many-to-one attachments.** Substituting d-fold suspensions of presentations K_g at the top generators g of a positive d-presentation P gives E_D(w, (u_o)) ≃ E_P(w) × ∏_o E_{K_{g(o)}}(u_o). Under RC_id(∞), iterated substitutions starting from dimension ≤ 2 have all fibres contractible. If every generator of dimension ≥ 3 of a positive context has variable faces, the context is homotopical. If the top rank is many-to-one (each new (d+1)-generator has a single generating d-variable as target), E_D(c) ≃ E_B(target of c), so the extension is homotopical iff the base is. For any generator-preserving map of positive contexts the square of geometric comparisons is cartesian.
36. **RC(3) under (H) for biased terms.** For a 3-presentation with acyclic generating 1-graph and nonempty boundary paths of 2-generators (3-generators arbitrary), the free tricategory is faithful over the free strict 3-category on parallel 3-cells (Gray pasting theorem plus Guthmann's strictification). Hence any two parallel CaTT 3-terms that are interpretations of biased tricategorical expressions with the same strict image are joined by 4-terms in both directions. This contains the wedge lemma of item 25. (Extending it to arbitrary raw 3-terms needs each raw 3-term to be conjugate, by identity-image comparisons, to such an interpretation.)
37. **The six-seam ring decided a lot (now decided: Γ₆ fails RC, item 43).** Γ₆ (item 32 with k = 6) has a full-support fibre S⁵, and the method of item 33 would need CT for the Gray six-cube, that is, terms for its composite 5-cells, which is not known. Using only CT through degree 4 for the six-cube, one obtains a boundary computad B on its 728 proper faces with integral fundamental cycle Σ_i (−1)^{i−1}([g_{i,+}] − [g_{i,−}]), and the following alternative: **either Γ₆ fails RC_id in some degree ≤ 8, or one of twelve explicit full-support strict 4-cells, in finite strong-Steiner contexts with generator counts (57, 156, 165, 80, 15), has two parallel weak lifts with no 5-term between them.** If Γ₆ satisfied RC_id(∞) it would therefore refute (⇒) (it is not homotopical) and (⇐) (a strong-Steiner, hence homotopical, context would fail RC(4)). Moreover (Response 19 of r6) each of the 114 adjacent ridge exchanges in those twelve contexts has a CaTT 5-term filling its square, and the gluing of these squares reduces to RC(3) and RC(4) on 148 explicit **three-dimensional** strong-Steiner contexts S_r, each with 42 vertices, 96 edges, 75 squares and 20 cubes (enumerated by `tools/relative_coherence_ridge_exchanges.py`). So: **if Γ₆ satisfies RC through degree 8, one of these 148 three-dimensional contexts fails RC(3) or RC(4).**

38. **Surjectivity of strictification (corrects §1.2).** Strictification is surjective on cells of every finite context in degrees 0–3. In each degree ≥ 4 it fails: the seven-generator context D on one object x with a bubble a : 1_x ⇒ 1_x, b = a ∗₀ i, d = i ∗₀ a, J : b → d and the structural exchange X : b ∗₁ d → d ∗₁ b, has a strict 4-cell c : 1_x³ → 1_x³ (from the parallel 3-terms J ∗₁ b and (b ∗₁ J) ∗₂ X) with **no weak representative**, even with freely chosen boundary (an 𝔽₂-charge on the double delooping of a braided monoidal groupoid; both faces of any lift have support in the point context). Suspensions give every degree ≥ 4, and the obstruction already occurs in the universal bracket computad P({x}). A rational matrix invariant (a Gray category from the double delooping of nonnegative rational matrices) separates every power of this based 4-loop. Since D has a bubble, this does not contradict the conjecture; under RC_id(∞) every strict cell lifts, so the trivial-fibration form of §1.2 survives. Certificate: `tools/relative_coherence_spherical_lift.py` (Agda checked).
39. **Degree three in every dimension.** For a finite context Γ of **any** dimension, RC_id(3)(Γ) ⟺ F(Γ_{≤2}) has no bubble (empty input and output paths and loops in the 1-graph allowed). Proof: normalizing right exchanges in the free Gray category on the 2-skeleton (a boundary-disjoint diagram component would be a bubble), plus RC(3) for arbitrary raw terms under the tricategorical hypotheses (H) via a translation of generic heads into biased tricategorical operations and faithfulness of free tricategories. So RC_id(3) holds for every finite strong-Steiner context, and for 2-dimensional contexts RC(3) ⟺ homotopical.
40. **Cuts and suspension.** For a 0/1 colouring of objects nondecreasing along generating arrows, restricting to the crossing generators (one dimension down) reflects fillers, with prescribed strict image; in particular suspension reflects fillers. In strong-Steiner contexts strict homs are computed from cuts one dimension lower.
41. **Literal nerves cannot decide seam rings.** A raw term can realize at most one separator sign at each seam; the nerve of signed geometric hemispheres for Γ₆ is the boundary of the 11-simplex (S¹⁰), the literal nerve is an S⁵ whose inclusion is null. The seam sphere is the strict limit of contractible lane projections. So any detection argument must use more than lists of nonempty intersections.
42. **Degree tests for Γ₆.** RC_id through degree 8 on Γ₆ would give an integral character D on H₅ X({0,…,6}) (X the groupoidified realization of the bracket algebra) with D(b) = 1 on an explicit class b, the image of the subdivided simplex boundary. The six-seam alternative is also detected by a relative simplex degree in the bracket computad P(V): a nonzero relative k-degree in P(J) is equivalent to one bracket at a point (two based d-terms with a single bracket between them), or to one bracket per simplex with exactly the alternating cellular boundary; integral potentials give a single-term divisibility test for every one-bridge ring, reducible to head equations with labels two ranks below; a degree certificate must use an operation whose root vertices see every colour (sharp in every dimension). Integral and modular versions (any ℤ/m) are equivalent to the original 64-term cube class. Finite reductions: at least seven distinct 3-dimensional states would have to fail RC(4) (sharp); twelve cubical representatives suffice and two smallest lists exist; a square test decides all signed state permissions in arbitrary cube width; Γ₆ reduces to seven ordered comparisons on five simplex wedges W_i, or to 295 explicit strict 3-labels, or to twelve labels in homotopical 3-contexts.
43. **Γ₆ fails relative coherence.** *Integral acyclicity theorem:* if A is free on a weak computad and has a strict CaTT map with globular boundary lifting onto a finite product of strict globular pasting arities (for example the bracket algebra P(V) over the point), its augmented cellular chain complex λA (j-generators, differential = target count − source count) is acyclic. The proof resolves top counts by labelled coherence heads, compares λA with the bar construction of the category of labelled paths by acyclic models, and inducts on the homology degree through the strict homs (finite products of pasting arities). Hence H₅ X({0,…,6}) = 0 for the groupoidified realization X of P({0,…,6}), contradicting the class b with D(b) = 1 that RC_id through degree 8 on Γ₆ would produce (item 42). **So Γ₆ fails RC_id in some degree ≤ 8**, and the alternative of item 37 is decided in the direction the conjecture predicts.
44. **(⇒) for unary seam presentations.** More generally, in the unary seam-signal presentations of item 32, a minimal nonface of size m forces RC_id to fail in some degree ≤ m + 2 (face chains in X(V_F) chosen by acyclicity replace any Gray m-cube). So **in that class, RC_id(∞) forces every geometric fibre to be contractible**, i.e. (⇒) holds for all seam rings, every chordless unary seam cycle being detected.
45. **The homotopy hypothesis for groupoidal algebras.** For any CaTT algebra U with x, y, the space of labelled paths M_U(x,y) is weakly equivalent to the realization X(U(x,y)) of the weak hom. Consequently, for groupoidal U, **π_j^CaTT(U,x) ≅ π_j(X(U),x) for all j ≥ 1** (algebraic groups: j-loops modulo (j+1)-comparison). Attaching a walking bi-invertible cell i_k to a simply connected groupoidal free algebra preserves all homotopy groups. Facewise bracket algebras P[K] realize every finite simplicial complex: π_j^CaTT(P[K], o) ≅ π_j(|K|, o). Hurewicz: for a 2-connected free groupoidal algebra, top counts give π₃ ≅ H₃(λA), and in the first possible nonzero degree n ≥ 2 top counts decide comparison of based n-loops.
46. **What RC gives after groupoidification.** If p : A → C is a strict CaTT map from a free algebra to a strict ω-category lifting objects and all prescribed globular boundaries (by §1.2 this is RC_id(∞) when C = F(Γ)), then |LA| → |NC| is a weak homotopy equivalence, and so is every iterated weak hom of A to the corresponding iterated strict hom (with lifting through rank d + 1, λA and the Street nerve of C agree in homology through degree d). There is also a natural map from |L(A(x,y))| to the first geometric mapping space of the groupoidification-in-dimensions-≥ 2, V₁LA, comparing them in every homological degree. What is not yet known is how to pass from these *groupoidified* statements to the *directed* geometric fibres E_Γ(c) of item 27 for a general computad (groupoidification alone does not detect homotopicality, compare the companion problem on homotopy polygraphs).
47. **Exact integer tests high above the generators.** For a context of dimension ≤ m and n ≥ max(m + 2, 2), parallel n-terms u, v admit an (n+1)-term between them iff the difference of their edge counts in the (n−1)-fold iterated weak hom computad is a boundary (the relevant homs are groupoidal and their loop groups abelian). The threshold is sharp: in every finite 2-dimensional context with a bubble, the group of 3-loops modulo 4-comparison at a fixed 2-cell contains a free group on two explicit crossings X, Y, so (XY)^k and (YX)^k have equal strict images and identical top counts in the context and its first two homs, yet no 4-term joins them.

48. **Positive 2-computads satisfy RC in every degree.** For every finite 2-computad whose 2-generators have nonempty source and target paths, RC_id(n) holds for every n. Proof: the generic positive 2-polyplex of a serial expression is a homotopy polygraph (Chanavat, Cor. 3.39), so the bar comparison of item 46 applies at its outer vertices; the twice-fixed homs are groupoidal with trivial fundamental group (RC_id(3), item 39) and acyclic, hence contractible, which gives actual fillers in every degree. So (⇐) holds for positive 2-computads. A universal one-vertex bracket algebra also classifies the extra writings in every suspension hom (raw mixed terms lift uniquely along generator-to-generator maps).

## 4. Questions

**Question 1 (main: the exact class).** Prove that for every finite CaTT context Γ,

> RC_id(∞)(Γ)  ⟺  Γ is homotopical,

equivalently (items 7, 26, 27) that π_Γ is a trivial fibration exactly when every geometric fibre E_Γ(c) is contractible, or give a counterexample to either direction with a separating invariant. Γ₆ no longer separates the directions (item 43), and (⇒) is known for unary seam presentations (item 44); the question is the general computad.

- **(⇒, necessity).** Show that RC_id(∞) forces every geometric fibre to be contractible. Routes:
  - (o) **From groupoidified to directed.** Item 46 gives, under RC_id(∞), weak equivalences |L(A(x₁,y₁)…)| → |N F(Γ)(x₁,y₁)…| at every iterated hom, and a comparison of algebraic homs with first geometric mapping spaces. Deduce contractibility of the directed fibres E_Γ(c), for instance by an induction on iterated homs in which each step only needs the groupoidified statement one level down, or by extending the acyclicity method of item 43 (which handled seam rings without Gray cubes) to arbitrary non-contractible fibres.
  - (a) **A general detection theorem.** Turn any non-contractible full-support fibre (item 27) into parallel CaTT terms of equal strict image with no filler, as items 28, 33 and 34 do for circles, seam spheres and symmetric spheres. For instance: a CaTT-definable parameter space for an arbitrary sphere in E(c), or a "cellular realization" of the CW model of item 29 by terms, cell by cell, with literal boundaries.
  - (b) **Via canonical Gray terms.** Item 33 needs CaTT presentations of the Gray m-cube and its faces (CT for I^{⊗m}). A uniform construction for all m (the companion problem on canonical Gray terms asks for this) would settle every seam presentation; a proof that such presentations cannot exist would also be informative.
  - (c) **A contraction under RC.** Prove the vanishing of π_j E(c) under RC_id(∞) directly, extending items 29–31 (π₁, π₂, weight ≤ 4, strip products) to all degrees and weights, and then beyond generator dimension 3 (item 35 handles substitution-built contexts).
- **(⇐, the contraction).** Prove RC_id(∞) for every homotopical Γ. Routes as before:
  - (a) the attachment statement of item 12 for arbitrary trivial fibrations B → ∂𝔻^m, extended to strict composites;
  - (b) contractibility of the fibre of π_Γ over each strict cell, described CaTT-definably;
  - (c) a relative Guiraud–Malbos normalisation strategy;
  - (d) an equivalence with Dean's Conj. 5.4.0.9 or another named open statement;
  - (e) the retraction r : KC → A of item 22;
  - (f) a wedge theorem in every dimension. In dimension 3 under (H) item 36 already gives the comparisons for biased terms.
  - The new geometric input may help: for homotopical Γ all E(c) are contractible, and items 29–31 show how cells of E(c) are realized by CaTT terms in dimension 3.

**Question 2 (fallback for (⇐), still every dimension).** Prove RC_id(∞) for every finite strong-Steiner context (RC_id(3) is now known by item 39; positivity alone is not enough, item 28), or for every finite 2-dimensional bubble-free context (item 48 does the positive ones; empty source or target paths remain) together with an extension theorem: adjoining generators of any dimension ≥ 3 so that the result is still homotopical preserves RC_id(∞). The first open degree is four. Please aim for every degree.

## 5. What counts as a solution

A proof of either direction of Question 1, or of Question 2 (with the extension theorem in the 2-dimensional version); (⇒) for a whole class beyond the seam presentations (for example all positive 3-computads, or all computads whose non-contractible fibres are spheres); a counterexample in one of the named classes with a proof that no connecting term exists; an equivalence with a named open problem; or a published reference. An existence proof of the connecting cells is enough, provided it is a proof about CaTT terms or CaTT models.

# Researcher Response 1

## Finite presentations for testing both sides at one strict cell

Fix a finite context Γ, put \(A=\mathrm{Free}(Γ)\) and \(C=F(Γ)\), and mark a strict \(n\)-cell \(c\) of \(C\). There is a finite context \(P_c\), with a generator-preserving map to Γ and a distinguished full-support strict cell \(d_c\) over \(c\), with the following properties.

* At \(d_c\), projection is a bijection on raw writings of \(c\). It is also a bijection on fillers over any specified strict cell upstairs, at the corresponding literal faces downstairs.
* The map of geometric fibres \(E_{P_c}(d_c)\to E_Γ(c)\) is an equivalence.
* \(F(P_c)\to C\), pointed at \(d_c\), is initial among discrete Conduché strict functors to \(C\) equipped with a lift of \(c\). This characterization also specifies which occurrences have to be identified when a source or target is an identity.

In particular the family \(P_c\), over all strict cells, detects and preserves each property in Question 1. Each test uses a finite context even if there are infinitely many tests. The construction applies to strict cells having no raw representative.

**Lifting terms.** A discrete Conduché functor uniquely lifts factorizations of an already specified cell, both by identities and by binary composition in each direction. Let \(p:D\to C\) be any such functor. Guetta's basis theorem makes \(D\) free on the inverse images of the generators of \(C\). Give each of these generators the unique lift of its original weak attaching terms at its specified strict faces. The result is a weak computad \(P\) with
\[
       \mathrm{Free}(P)\ \cong\ A\times_C D
\]
as CaTT algebras. Here \(D\) in the pullback has its strict algebra structure. [Guetta, Theorem 6.11(1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.05332)

For the induction justifying both assertions about weak terms, consider a head of rank \(j\) over a pasting arity \(T\). Its generic strict value gives an algebraic map \(D_j\to T_{\rm str}\) in Θ. Indeed its support contains every variable of the arity by the CaTT head condition and the support lemma (item 2), so it cannot factor through a proper globular subarity. This includes an identity on the total composite of a lower arity. The unique lifting property against algebraic maps now lifts the entire strict diagram of its substitution from a lift of the value of the head. It specifies a strict value for *every* argument, including those on stems of smaller height. Recurse on the raw arguments and retain the head. For a variable take its basis lift. Induct first on rank and then on term size in that rank. At each new rank the boundaries of its basis elements are supplied by the preceding rank. The reconstructed substitution is typed in declaration order because two required lower faces with the same raw projection and the same lifted strict value coincide by lower-rank uniqueness. Its evaluated strict value is the specified one by the lifting square in Θ. Conversely every raw lift of a head must have that head, and strict factorization uniquely prescribes its arguments' values. These two inductions give the claimed isomorphism, including the stated bijections with prescribed faces. Guetta's identity and binary lifting conditions are precisely the globe tests in Loubaton's definition when evaluated on the discrete nerves of the free strict categories. [Loubaton, Definition 2.2.2.2 and Proposition 2.2.2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425)

**Lifting the geometric attachments.** There is also a cartesian square
\[
 L\mathrm{Free}(P)\ \simeq\ L A\times_{NC} ND.
\]
Here one needs preservation of homotopy pushouts along \(Np\). Both strict nerves are discrete complete Θ-nerves since the free strict categories are gaunt. Algebraic orthogonality above therefore gives discrete Conduché in the geometric category, and Loubaton's Theorem 2.2.2.10 applies. Pull back the cellular presentation of \(LA\) in the slice. To see exactly what happens at a generating \(j\)-globe, consider the strict pullback \(D' = D\times_C D_j\to D_j\). For any labelled arity in \(D'\), factor its projection into the globe as an algebraic map followed by a globular inclusion. A globular subarity of a globe is a single face globe, possibly the whole globe. Unique algebraic lifting then gives the unique labelling of this smallest globe in \(D'\). Thus the nerve of \(D'\) is assembled from the lifted face globes along their literal faces. This computes a homotopy colimit already in presheaves of spaces because for each test labelling the category of containing labelled face globes has its smallest one as initial object. The same argument using proper faces treats the boundary. Consequently the pulled-back pair is obtained by attaching one \(j\)-globe for each lift of the generating cell. Its attaching map is the lift already constructed on the two weak faces. The natural map of the lifted cellular presentation to the pullback commutes with the characteristic maps, and on the boundary it is the comparison from the preceding skeleton. Starting with the lifted objects proves the square by induction and colimits. Applying the globe mapping-space functor and taking fibres at a specified lift gives the claimed equivalence of geometric fibres. [Loubaton, Proposition 1.1.2.15 and Theorem 2.2.2.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425)

**Why the initial test is finite.** In the ordinary slice of strict ω-categories over \(C\), freely impose unique lifting against the identity and binary composite tests on the marked globe \(D_n\to C\). Explicitly, for each square attach its missing diagonal by a pushout of the test \(K\to L\), and impose equality of each pair of parallel diagonals by a pushout of the fold \(L\amalg_K L\to L\). Perform both operations for all current squares and iterate to the sequential colimit. The test shapes and their doubles are finitely presentable, being finite colimits of free globes. Thus every lifting or equality problem appears at a finite stage. A map to a pointed discrete Conduché target extends uniquely throughout, which proves the required initiality. Denote the resulting strict functor and marked cell by \(I\to C\) and \(d\).

Guetta's theorem and the term lifting above apply even to this possibly infinite construction. Take the dependency-closed support of \(d\) in its basis. It is finite because any strict expression for \(d\) is finite, and taking the attaching terms of its finitely many generators recursively decreases rank. The inclusion of this support in \(I\) is discrete Conduché by item 27 (equivalently every factor's support lies in that of the composite). \[
 H_{m+s}(\operatorname{Cone} C_*(e);\mathbb Z)
 \ \cong\ \bigoplus_{\substack{c\in C_m\\\operatorname{supp}(c)=P}}
                   \widetilde H_{s-1}(E_P(c);\mathbb Z).

Use augmented reduced homology, so the empty fibre contributes \(\mathbb Z\) in degree \(-1\). In particular relative coherence forces each of these directed fibres to be nonempty, connected and acyclic. The sum has a separate summand for each strict label.

**Rectangles.** Work in complete \(m\)-fold Segal spaces, which present the same geometric categories as complete Θ-spaces. This applies since a presentation of height \(m\) realizes in \((\infty,m)\)-categories (item 23). Total realization computes groupoidification by adjunction against constant spaces. The strict target, being gaunt, has the discrete rectangular nerve. In multi-degree \((1,\ldots,1)\) its cells are the \(m\)-globes. More generally the Segal equivalences express the fibre over a strict rectangular labelling as the homotopy limit of the fibres at its elementary globes and their gluing faces. This follows by splitting off one unit interval at a time in each simplicial direction. Globularity identifies a zero-length direction with data of smaller globular dimension. All the gluing faces have dimension less than \(m\), so their supports omit every \(m\)-generator, and their fibres are contractible by item 27 and the proper-subcontext hypothesis. The fibre over a positive-size grid is therefore equivalent, by the restriction maps, to the product of the fibres at its elementary \(m\)-boxes. In the strict nerve, any rectangle containing a full-support elementary box \(c\) is a degeneracy of this single box. Indeed, enlarge the chosen box into any subrectangle containing it by adjoining one slab at a time. For a slab in direction \(k+1\), strict interchange computes the total as a \(k\)-composite of the previous total with the total of the slab, in the appropriate order. Inductively the previous total is \(c\). Hence the slab total is a \(k\)-unit and the new total is still \(c\). To test a given layer outside the chosen box, take its complete transverse extent and take the adjoining subrectangle up to that layer containing the chosen box. The same argument gives a \(k\)-unit for the total of the *entire* layer. All its elementary labels are factors of this total, so their support has height at most \(k\) by the support lemma. Consequently the elementary cells in this layer are identities above rank \(k\), precisely the strict degeneracy condition in direction \(k+1\). Removing all such layers recovers the original box. Conversely a degeneracy of a full-support box has only one such box, since inserted layers are identities on lower cells.

**Homology.** Take the degreewise chain cone of the map of rectangular spaces to the discrete nerve and filter the total complex by the sum of its simplicial degrees. First take homology in the space direction. Over a discrete target this splits as a direct sum over labellings of the reduced fibre homologies shifted up by one. Grids with only proper-support boxes have zero summand. For a degeneracy of \(c\) its summand is carried isomorphically from that of \(c\) by the degeneracy map, since restriction to its unique full-support box is an inverse on fibre homology by the product calculation. Normalizing in all \(m\) simplicial directions therefore leaves only multi-degree \((1,\ldots,1)\). This normalization commutes with taking homology by the split simplicial degeneracy decomposition. Geometrically the free point realizes as the point, so on a composite this collapse composes the left input with a unit. # Researcher Response 13

## At least three top generators suffice in a minimal strong Steiner context

Let \(S\) be a finite strong Steiner context of height \(m\ge 3\), and suppose every proper dependency-closed subcontext satisfies \(\mathrm{RC}_{\rm id}(\infty)\). If \(S\) has at least three \(m\)-generators, then \(S\) satisfies \(\mathrm{RC}_{\rm id}(\infty)\). The same holds for two \(m\)-generators if a positive \((m-1)\)-facet of one is a negative facet of the other. In particular the test of Response 8 needs only minimal subcontexts with **one top generator or two unordered top generators**. In the one-generator case a remaining full-support label must still be a composite by Response 7. The count here is in the original context \(S\), even when a single slot of a strict subdivision contains several of its generators.

**The top row.** Fix a full-support strict \(m\)-cell \(c\). Let \(r\) be the number of \(m\)-generators of \(S\). Its top chain is the sum of all \(r\), each once. Call these occurrences events. The same description with a subset of events applies to any strict \(m\)-cell \(p\) of \(S\), including the empty subset for an identity. A cell missing an event has proper support. The following facts describe serializations at the cut \(m-1\).

Write \(s,t\) for the two \((m-1)\)-rows of \(p\) and \(E\) for its top row. The positive chain
\[
       s+\sum_{e\in E}\partial^+e
       =t+\sum_{e\in E}\partial^-e
\]
has every coefficient at most one. This is Steiner's intermediate chain \(g_{m-1}(p)\). Thus a consumed facet is either initially present or supplied by exactly one event. No two events consume the same facet. Put a precedence from its supplier to its consumer, when both exist. These precedences are acyclic by the strong basis order. After the events of a prefix \(I\) the row is \(s+\sum_{e\in I}\partial e\). An event may run as soon as its negative facets are present. Indeed the updated row is nonnegative, and these two rows, the original lower rows, and the single event in the top row satisfy the defining tableau equations for a strict cell in \(\nu K\). Conversely nonnegativity at each step forces every supplier to run before its consumer. Thus the elementary temporal words for \(p\), ignoring identity steps, are exactly the linear extensions of this finite order. For a given order **all strict intermediate cells and individual steps are specified**, since their lower rows are fixed. Identity steps may be inserted at any state. [Steiner, definition of the intermediate chains and Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.6723)

Work at prescribed raw poles below rank \(m-1\). Use the homotopy bicategory of the \((m-1)\)-fold fixed hom: objects are raw \((m-1)\)-terms, arrows are \(m\)-terms, and tracks are \((m+1)\)-terms modulo \((m+2)\)-comparison at their literal ends. Every track is invertible since \(S\) has height \(m\). We compose arrows in traversal order. The bicategory laws used below can also be obtained directly: take binary temporal composites and whiskerings, lift the associativity and unit identifications in their free globular arities, and compare parallel lifts of the same strict pasting there by contraction. This gives functorial horizontal composition of tracks, its associator, unitors, pentagon and triangle on classes. We work with all choices of literal poles, applying this description anew at each choice.

**Serial words and their tracks.** An elementary word is a bracketed temporal path of raw \(m\)-terms whose strict steps have at most one event. Its two exterior ends are prescribed. Put \(q=2\) when \(r\ge3\), and \(q=1\) in the ordered two-generator case. Allow the following tracks between words:
* rebracket by the bicategory constraints;
* replace a consecutive block of at most \(q\) events by an elementary word with the same strict value at the same literal ends, by a track on that block, whiskered by the unchanged prefix and suffix.

Include composites and inverses. Every block in this rule has proper support, so its track exists and is unique on classes *at those ends*. This includes insertion and absorption of an idle step by replacing its neighbouring step, or replacement of an entirely idle word. In particular the internal \((m-1)\)-terms need not stay fixed.

Here is a more precise calculation of this groupoid of tracks **in the homotopy bicategory**. For a fixed order of the events, choose one raw representative of each strict intermediate state at the given lower poles, retaining the prescribed ends. Choose one raw arrow over each one-event transition between these states, by proper-support lifting. Use weak identities at repeated states. If the entire word is idle and its exterior raw ends differ, choose a single idle arrow at those two ends by proper-support lifting. Every other idle word with those ends compares to it by whole-block replacement. For a word with raw state \(y_i\) choose an invertible arrow \(\eta_i:y_i\to\bar y_i\) over its strict identity, using identities at the exterior ends. These arrows exist on the support of the state and are invertible there since it has height at most \(m-1\). On each elementary factor there is a unique track
\[
                  a_i\,\eta_{i+1}\ \Longrightarrow\ \eta_i\,\bar a_i .
\]
Paste these from the right end to the left and use unitors at the ends. This is a serial track to the chosen word. In elementary moves it inserts a bridge on one side, rebrackets, and absorbs it on the other. Only idle or single-event blocks are needed. For a block replacement, normalization at the two exterior states of the block gives a commuting square on track classes, since both routes on the block have the same ends and its support is proper. Whisker by the other factors. Rebracketings give the same result by bicategory coherence. This proves that after these connections a replacement acts either as an identity or as the single chosen adjacent swap of two incomparable events. In the latter case choose its block track at the canonical states, independently of the surrounding factors. Conversely every permissible adjacent swap is realized in this way. This argument handles arbitrary raw labellings at internal faces, including changes between different weak writings of an identity.

**Resolving all operations.** We show that every track at a full-support label, after connecting its endpoints to elementary words, is represented by the serial tracks above. The details of substitution matter here: an argument of an outer operation can itself carry the whole label, and whiskering a proper label by lower-dimensional terms can increase its support.

At an \(m\)-term \(u\) a resolution is a choice of elementary word \(W\) at its literal ends and a track \(\alpha:u\Rightarrow W\). Regard two resolutions as equivalent when their connections differ by a serial track on the word ends. At a proper-support label there is just one class of such choices. Indeed its strict elementary steps lift at chosen representatives of the intermediate states on that support, and a connecting track exists and is unique there at fixed ends. Any two of the resulting words are joined by the state changes and adjacent swaps above. In the case \(q=1\) no swap of two positive events is needed, even on a proper label containing both: the supplier and consumer have the same forced order there.

We use two elementary consequences of globular contraction.

1. With the lower slots of an arity \(P\) fixed, a generic \(m\)-term \(b\) acts functorially on tracks between its top input terms at fixed faces. In a formal extension of \(P\) by a column of steps at each top slot, apply the same strict lower pasting to these columns that the strict \(m\)-cell of \(b\) applies to its top slots. Units from lower dimensions give identity columns. Lift the resulting strict \((m+1)\)-cell at the two evaluations of \(b\). Composition, identity and independence of the chosen lifts follow by contraction in the formal globular sets of columns, as in Response 10. Parallel input tracks representing the same class can be joined using a formal \((m+2)\)-variable at the slot. This construction is compatible with substituting generic terms: use the lifted columns at the terms of the inner substitution as the columns of the outer operation. The two routes have the same strict total and literal ends in the original formal column arity, hence the same track after evaluation. A generic identity-image comparison \(b\Rightarrow b'\) at parallel generic ends likewise gives a natural comparison of the two actions: the two sides of its naturality square lift the same column pasting. No naturality for a moving lower slot is assumed.

2. Suppose every \(m\)-slot of \(P\) is labelled by a term of at most one event in \(S\). Any \(m\)-term \(b\) over \(P\) has a resolution after evaluation, chosen *in the generic arity*. Temporally serialize its strict value in \(F(P)\). At each stage choose a generic writing of the intermediate \((m-1)\)-state at the common lower poles of \(b\), using its actual exterior ends. Lift each elementary generic step at these writings. Lift the identity comparison from \(b\) to the composite by globular contraction in \(P\). Each output step evaluates to at most one event: it contains at most one formal \(m\)-occurrence, and lower whiskers add no \(m\)-chain. For an entirely idle value one may take a single idle step. This resolution class is independent of the generic choices. With fixed strict order, change intermediate writings by the bridge calculation above in \(P\), using identity-image comparisons there. Under evaluation the affected elementary blocks still have at most one event. Two strict orders in \(P\) differ by adjacent incomparable swaps by the top-row description applied to the tree. Each swap evaluates to a block of at most two events. If \(q=1\), they cannot both be positive: generic interchange realizes both orders with the same two occurrence images, whereas both positive events in \(S\) have forced order. These block comparisons and rebracketings compare the two generic words. Their composites with the chosen connections out of \(b\) agree on classes by contraction in \(P\) at the common literal ends. The same proof shows compatibility with a generic identity-image comparison of two parallel \(m\)-terms, by composing it with the connection at its target.

We record why this generic resolution works under arbitrary nested heads. Given resolutions of the \(m\)-entries in a substitution for an \(m\)-operation, apply its track action in 1 to their connections. In the resulting expression each of those slots is a temporal word. Insert its word arity at that slot: it is just a string with the same two \((m-1)\)-faces as the original disk, so no lower boundary of an adjacent slot has to be altered. This gives an \(m\)-tree with elementary-labelled top inputs. Apply 2 to the resulting generic expression. This defines the output resolution class from the input classes.

To check independence of the representatives in this rule, consider one elementary move of one input word, with all other input words already inserted. A rebracketing uses one common formal string arity; both comparisons there have the same strict value. For a block move, first view the block as a single slot with a comparison between its two composite values. Use the same serialization order for this arity on both sides. At the position of the block the two serialized outputs differ by a track over at most \(q\) events, and all other elementary steps can be chosen identical: their lower state terms do not involve the changing top entry. Formally, in the arity with one column step at the changing position, lift the corresponding strict step between the two chosen block writings, then temporally paste it with the identities at the other positions. This realizes the operation's same strict column and endpoints, so agrees with its action after the two serializations by contraction. Subdivide the block back into its displayed elementary steps on either side. One can keep these steps consecutive in the inserted string order, choose intermediates with the prescribed exterior faces, and compare the single block writing to their composite there. Its evaluated label still has at most \(q\) events, including after all the outer lower whiskers. The difference is therefore an allowed serial replacement. Different interleavings after subdividing give the same class by 2. Together with functoriality in 1 this checks independence for a composite of moves, without requiring the track groupoid of serial words to be thin.

The rule also commutes with substitution of \(m\)-terms. For explicit verification first replace the original top inputs by their resolution words using 1; the inner-then-outer track actions equal the single substituted action. It remains to work in the resulting elementary-labelled tree \(P\). For each term of rank \(m\) in the inner substitution choose its generic serialization in \(P\) by 2. Insert these strings into the outer arity. The composite of the generic inner connections, acted on by the outer term, and the outer generic serialization is now a track in \(P\) from the substituted term to a serialized word. Each output step, pulled back to \(P\), has at most one **formal** \(m\)-event, since each inserted slot was a formal elementary step and its lower whiskers add none. Its value is thus a permitted elementary generic serialization of the substituted strict cell, allowing idle steps. The connection has the identity strict value there. Statement 2 compares it with the direct serialization in \(P\), with contraction identifying connections to the same word. This proves the substitution assertion. Lower-rank entries of the inner substitution have no top variables and pass through unchanged. In particular proper inputs of large weight are resolved *before* lower whiskering; their being proper alone would not bound the weight of the whiskered block.

Choose resolution classes of raw \(m\)-terms by recursion. Variables are on proper supports (a single top generator has weight one), so use their proper classes. At a head apply the rule to the recursively resolved arguments; an \(m\)-term of lower arity has an idle strict value. A proper term receives its unique proper class. A word of actual elementary factors has its identity resolution, by inserting their one-letter identity resolutions into its temporal arity. The substitution check gives the usual evaluation rule for **any** generic expression in already resolved inputs.

Call a track compatible if, after the two resolution connections, it is serial. Every track on a proper support is compatible. A generic identity-image comparison between parallel \(m\)-terms in any labelling is compatible: apply naturality from 1 to the input resolution connections and then 2 in the elementary-labelled arity. The action of a generic \(m\)-operation on compatible input tracks is compatible as well. Write each input track as its source connection, a serial track, and the inverse of its target connection. The output connections use those very actions, and the intervening serial moves were checked in the block-move argument. These statements concern the original literal exterior faces.

Finally induct on a raw \((m+1)\)-term. A variable has a proper label (indeed \(S\) has height \(m\)). A head in an arity of height \(m\) is covered by the generic identity comparison. One of height less than \(m\) is on a proper support. At height \(m+1\) write the arity as columns over its common \(m\)-boundary tree. Its generic faces are two \(m\)-terms \(b_-,b_+\) over that tree with the same strict total and parallel lower boundaries. Apply the action of \(b_-\) to the column paths, then a generic identity comparison from \(b_-\) to \(b_+\) at the final tuple. This and the given head have the same literal ends and full strict value in the formal \((m+1)\)-arity and so agree after evaluation on track classes. The actual column entries are proper syntactic subterms of the given head, hence compatible by induction. Both operations just proved to preserve compatibility apply. This finishes the resolution proof for all raw tracks, independently of their height of nesting.

Thus its group is a quotient of the based path group of the adjacent-swap graph at a chosen strict order, with the relations verified by actual tracks below. The groupoidal fixed-hom calculation makes each simply connected acyclic \(W(c)\) contractible and gives all higher prescribed comparisons. Proper-support labels already lift. * At a proper label, choose a word of the read order at the specified boundary and any comparison to it in that support. Existence follows from prescribed-boundary lifting there. Two such choices agree up to the fixed-order changes just described.
* At a head, apply its action on top-entry tracks to these resolution connections, insert the resulting temporal strings at their slots, and serialize the formal operation in the resulting elementary-labelled arity. Use the read order and lift the connection from the operation to the word in the generic arity. All lower entries stay fixed. A single elementary input may use its one-letter identity resolution.

We verify the substitution property of this recursion, since it determines how a path at a vertex can be used in several frames. In an elementary-labelled arity, two generic serializations with the same evaluated order are connected by generic adjacent swaps whose evaluations involve at most one positive event. In fact impose the specified order between the two positive *formal* events in the finite precedence poset, when there are two. Both proposed orders are linear extensions of this augmented poset. Sorting one into the other uses only swaps of incomparable elements of the augmented poset. Bridge changes of the generic intermediate writings also evaluate on single steps. Contraction in the arity identifies the generic connections after these moves. This applies to every generic term at its literal exterior faces.

Changing an input resolution by one permitted block replacement can be checked before expanding that block, treating it as a single top slot of weight at most one. Use the same order on this contracted arity at both ends, reading each input word consecutively in the order of the outer operation. In its column at the changed slot lift the track between the two block writings and paste identities at the other steps. This is the same action on track classes by contraction. Subdividing the slot again gives a permitted replacement, since outer lower whiskers add no top events. Any other generic order with the prescribed positive order gives the same connection by the augmented-poset argument. Rebracketings use a single common string. This proves independence of input representatives as in Response 13. For nested substitution, first act on the input connections; the two actions compose by the column calculation of that response. In the resulting elementary-labelled tree, serialize each inner term *formally*, then serialize the outer term with these strings inserted. The resulting elementary steps each contain at most one original formal top occurrence. They form a generic serialization of the substituted term, with its specified evaluated order by the literal substitution rule for readings. The augmented-poset argument and generic contraction compare this connection with the direct one. These checks also apply when an inner term has proper label but uses both events: its generic resolution and its proper resolution agree by lifting on that support at the same order. Thus one can compute the connection to a word for $b[\sigma]$ by resolving the entries of $\sigma$ first and then serializing the generic $b$.

For a primitive vertex $v$ use this connection on its actual raw head, take its path in $G$, and follow the unique ordered-subdivision path to $b_{o(v)}$. This defines $N_v$. The same calculation on **any** writing of vertex $v$ gives $N_v$. To see this, trace a nonprimitive head to its unique marked top argument. Its outer strict labelling lifts to the marked globe by Response 8. The action of this head on a connection in the marked slot therefore reads as exactly that path in the strict interpretation in the iterated suspension of $G$; all other slots act by units there. The other top entries have weight zero and can keep their one-letter resolutions. After the marked argument has been replaced by a word, the outer operation and its generic serialization both lie over the ordered subdivision. Indeed the new strict labels in the inserted string belong to that subdivision, and all other labels belong to the marked globe inside it. All input labels are now proper. Its remaining path therefore agrees with the reference path there. Induction down the marked slot proves the claim, also when the original writing has different raw lower poles. Everything here maps to the marked pullback by its strict label.

**What one frame can contribute.** For a transport $t=T_C(n,n') : v\to w$ in Response 10, identify its two ends with the reference vertices by forming $N_v^{-1}tN_w$. If the two orders agree, this arrow is the identity. To prove it first distribute the two positive events among the top slots of $C$.

If they are in different slots, all input writings are elementary. The connection of a name to a serial word can be chosen generically in the frame by the substitution calculation above. When the orders agree, choose the *same* formal order of the spatial tree at both ends. This is possible even if the two generic named operations read different orders of the idle slots, by the augmented-poset argument. Use identical generic intermediate lower writings for the two serializations. The simultaneous column transport between the resulting two words can be computed step by step in that order: lift the column of each formal top event at its serialized step and compose the lifted tracks horizontally, taking identities on the lower pieces. The strict value is the simultaneous column by interchange, so contraction gives the transport after the two connections. Each evaluated step column is on an idle or one-event block. Thus this last path belongs to the thin ordered subdivision.

If both events lie in one slot, its label has proper support, by the definition of a frame. For each order used there choose one actual two-step word at that slot's literal boundary, with the prescribed order. Between the input writing and this word choose its proper resolution; any choice has the same class at fixed ends. In each other slot choose a common writing of its idle label and a proper comparison to it. Transport first to these common inputs. At either original name this is the input-action part of its resolution, by the substitution rule; for an idle input the common writing is itself elementary. Insert the two-step word at the distinguished slot. At two names of the same order the resulting generic arity and its elementary labelling are the same. The two positive steps have forced order in that arity. Generic serialization and contraction compare the remaining transport there, and all equal-order serial changes land in the same thin ordered subdivision. This proves the identity in the second case too.

The composition law for transport on names now shows that a frame has at most one normalized arrow $k_C:b_L\to b_R$ changing order from $L$ to $R$, and that the reverse change has value $k_C^{-1}$. Indeed connect any two source names and any two target names by the same-order transports. This argument does not require an injective arity map or a unique name for an evaluated vertex.

**Every letter of an expansion is itself transport in the parent frame.** This additional fact is needed because a relation can have more than one change of order. Consider an expansion of Response 10 on a tree $Q$ of height $m$ or $m+1$, with spatial tree $P$ obtained by collapsing the top columns in the latter case. Write $d$ for the total $m$-cell of $P$. Every primitive marked edge in the expanded word originates in a generic subexpression of $Q$: the input labels have proper support. Consider the strict $(m+1)$-value $r$ of this subexpression in $Q$. Its evaluated value is $1_c$. Collapsing columns gives an $m$-cell $p$ of $P$, with identity padding understood.

There is a one-hole strict expression extending $p$ to $d$ using compositions at cuts strictly below $m$. Here is a proof for any $m$-cell of a tree of height at most $m$. The dimension-zero tree is a point. At the root the tree is a string of suspensions of its branches. A cell spanning a nonempty interval is a tuple of cells of one lower rank in those consecutive branches; its hom is the product of the branch categories. By induction extend each component to its branch total. Perform each such extension on the tuple by increasing its cut by one and using the matching units in the other components. Then append the branch totals before and after the chosen interval at cut zero. For an empty interval the cell is the identity at that vertex and these last two extensions suffice. Pad branch totals by identities to the displayed rank. This proves the assertion and uses the original cell only once. [Campion, Fact 2.28 on homs across a wedge](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.00200)

Perform these extensions on the actual weak subexpression over $Q$. Each added $m$-factor can be taken at a section of the columns over its support, selecting a stage independently in each column. All sections agree through rank $m-1$. At the required cut $j<m$, lift the added factor at the actual matching $j$-face in that section, by globular boundary lifting in the tree (choose the remaining higher boundaries successively). Use weak binary whiskering. After evaluation in $S$, every such added factor is a strict $j$-unit, since $c$ admits only unit extensions. On the strict suspended word interpretation these whiskerings leave the original primitive letter and its two vertex values unchanged.

The resulting track projects to the whole spatial total $d$. Its two strict ends are full slices of $P$ in $Q$, choosing one stage per column. Indeed an $m$-cell over $d$ uses each spatial top variable once, so it uses exactly one copy in each column. Its lower rows are fixed by the spatial boundary of $d$, since the column collapse is the identity there. The $(m+1)$-value between the slices is the simultaneous subpath in those columns. To check this, on each column its top chain must be the interval between the two selected stages: the boundary gives the difference of endpoint copies in a linear string. The sum of these intervals with the given lower rows determines the strict tableau. In height $m$ all the intervals are empty. The raw ends belong to their respective slices by the support lemma in the tree. The whole track belongs to the tree consisting of just those intervals and stationary entries, by the same lemma: each face of an interval step and each stationary copy is included there. The proper labels on the two slices thus give two names of the parent frame after the common normalization of poles. The transport formula of Response 10 identifies the track with the transport between these names: it is a lift of the indicated simultaneous subpath at their ends. Normalizing the poles preserves the word. This proves the assertion for each separate letter, including when the subpaths in different columns start at different stages.

**The signed count.** Let $K$ be the set of *actual* normalized arrows $k_C$ as $C$ ranges over frames with a change of order. Give each such arrow its own basis vector in the free abelian group $\mathbb Z^{(K)}$. Assign zero to a same-order transport symbol and assign $+[k_C]$ or $-[k_C]$ to an order-changing symbol according to direction. This is a functor from $G$ to the one-object group $\mathbb Z^{(K)}$. For the identity and composition laws on names, the signed count telescopes within a single frame. For an expansion relation each letter is also a transport in the parent frame by the preceding paragraph, with exactly its original two vertex values. Conjugating by the same two $N$'s therefore gives the same actual arrow. Every order-changing letter in the expansion has the parent frame's colour with its appropriate sign. The sum telescopes to the prescribed count of the right side. These are all relations by Response 10. In particular taking the colours in $G$ has not assumed that this finer signed count respects the relations; the letter-by-letter enlargement proves it.

Each connection $N_v$ has count zero. At a primitive head its proper-input resolution is one transport of its frame to the word inputs, without changing the read order. After grafting the strings its generic connection to a chosen serialization is a second transport between names of the same order in the elementary-labelled arity. If generic poles require normalization, the same lower whiskers on both ends preserve the word and the order, by the marked-globe calculation. The final ordered-subdivision path has only edges whose two ends have its order. This verifies the assertion on the chosen connections themselves.

If two frames contribute $k,k'\in K$, take their forward transports and use the connections to form the loop $k(k')^{-1}$ at $b_L$. Its signed count is $[k]-[k']$. But every vertex group of $G$ is perfect by the connected acyclicity of the writing space in Responses 3 and 8. Every such loop has zero image in an abelian group. Hence $k=k'$. All generating transports, after the reference connections, are therefore identities or the two directions of a single arrow. Any based loop has trivial product: each change in one direction is followed by a change in the other. The same conclusion holds if there are no changes. Thus the vertex groups of $G$ vanish. The fixed-pole test of Response 8 now gives the claimed all-dimensional result.
