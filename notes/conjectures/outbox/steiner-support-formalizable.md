# A formalizable proof that support determines cells in Gray tensors of pasting diagrams

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. The result itself is known. What is asked for is a proof short and elementary enough to be machine-checked, avoiding Steiner's theorem that cells are generated by atoms.*

## Background

We use Steiner's augmented directed complexes (R. Steiner, *Omega-categories and chain complexes*, HHA 6 (2004); Ara–Maltsiniotis, arXiv 1607.00668, §2).

**Complexes.** An augmented directed complex K is a chain complex of free abelian groups Kₙ (n ≥ 0) with augmentation ε : K₀ → ℤ and a distinguished basis Bₙ of each Kₙ. Kₙ* is the monoid of non-negative combinations of Bₙ. For a chain c, supp(c) ⊆ B is its set of basis elements with non-zero coefficient, and ∂c = (∂c)₊ − (∂c)₋ with (∂c)₊, (∂c)₋ ∈ Kₙ₋₁* of disjoint supports.

**Cells.** An n-cell of ν(K) is a table x = (x₀⁻, x₀⁺, …, xₙ⁻, xₙ⁺) with xₖ^± ∈ Kₖ*, xₙ⁻ = xₙ⁺, ∂xₖ^± = xₖ₋₁⁺ − xₖ₋₁⁻ for 1 ≤ k ≤ n, and ε(x₀^±) = 1. The atom ⟨b⟩ of b ∈ Bₙ has ⟨b⟩ₙ^± = b, ⟨b⟩ₖ⁻ = (∂⟨b⟩ₖ₊₁⁻)₋ and ⟨b⟩ₖ⁺ = (∂⟨b⟩ₖ₊₁⁺)₊.

**Support.** Supp(x) ⊆ B is the smallest set containing every supp(xₖ^±) and closed downward: if b ∈ Supp(x) has dimension ≥ 1, the elements of supp(∂b) are in Supp(x).

**The complexes of interest.** A finite globular pasting diagram Θ (a Batanin tree) gives a complex λ(Θ): its basis is the set of cells of Θ, ∂a = t(a) − s(a) for a cell a of dimension ≥ 1, and ε sends every 0-cell to 1. For pasting diagrams Θ₁, …, Θₘ let

    K = λ(Θ₁) ⊗ … ⊗ λ(Θₘ),

with basis the tuples a₁ ⊗ … ⊗ aₘ, dimension Σ dim aᵢ, and the Leibniz boundary ∂(a ⊗ b) = ∂a ⊗ b + (−1)^{dim a} a ⊗ ∂b. Equivalently, K is the hom complex between the first and last object of the free strict Gray-enriched category on the wedge of suspensions ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₘ. K is a Steiner complex: its basis is unital and strongly loop-free (Steiner 2004, Ara–Maltsiniotis 1607.00668 Thm A.15).

A convenient presentation of Θ for a proof assistant is a list of cells built by the rule "start with one 0-cell; repeatedly, at a current cell x of type A, add a new cell y of type A and a new cell f : x → y, then possibly move the current cell to a target". In that presentation every cell is the target of at most one cell, and the target of f is the cell created just before f.

## Statement

Let K = λ(Θ₁) ⊗ … ⊗ λ(Θₘ) as above.

**(P) Support determines the cell.** If x and y are n-cells of ν(K) with Supp(x) = Supp(y), then x = y.

(P) is known: it follows from Hadzihasanovic, *Combinatorics of higher-categorical diagrams*, arXiv 2404.07273, Prop. 8.3.17 and Thm 11.2.18, since K comes from an acyclic regular directed complex. It also follows, for every Steiner complex, from the three facts below, by rebuilding a cell top-down from its dimension and support.
- **(Rad)** Every xₖ^± of every cell is a sum of distinct basis elements (Forest, *Unifying notions of pasting diagrams*, HHA 2022, Lemma 3.4.5; Campbell, arXiv 2605.09427, Prop. 2.20).
- **(F2)** For every cell x, every m < dim x and every b ∈ supp(x_{m+1}^±): x_m⁻ and (∂b)₊ have disjoint supports, and so do x_m⁺ and (∂b)₋ (Forest Lemma 3.4.11; Campbell Prop. 2.23).
- **(G)** For every cell x and m < dim x, the common part R = x_m⁻ ∧ x_m⁺ is disjoint from the faces of the (m+1)-dimensional elements of Supp(x). This follows from (F2) and a lifting lemma whose proof uses that every cell is a composite of atoms.

The known proofs of (Rad), (F2) and the lifting lemma go through Steiner's theorem that ν(K) is generated by its atoms under composition, whose proof is a long induction using loop-freeness.

## Questions

**Q1.** Give a direct proof of (P) for K = λ(Θ₁) ⊗ … ⊗ λ(Θₘ) that does not use the atom-generation theorem. Natural tools: an explicit linear order on the basis (for instance, lexicographic on tuples, with each Θᵢ ordered by creation order in the presentation above), extremal arguments on chains, or induction on the presentation of one Θᵢ (adding the two cells y and f).

**Q2.** The same for (Rad) and (G) separately, if that is easier. A proof of (Rad) alone for K would already be useful.

**Q3.** The two instances we actually need, if the general statement has no short proof:
- (Q3a) Let d = dim K, let ⟨Θ⟩ be the cell of ν(λΘ) composing all of Θ, and let T = ⟨Θ₁⟩ ⊗ … ⊗ ⟨Θₘ⟩ be the "total cell", the d-cell of ν(K) whose top chain is the sum of all top-dimensional basis elements. Show that any (d − 1)-cell x with Supp(x) = Supp(∂⁻T) equals ∂⁻T, and similarly for ∂⁺T.
- (Q3b) Two parallel n-cells x, y with Supp(x) = Supp(y) = B have equal top chains. This one has a short argument: Supp = B forces n ≥ d; for n > d both top chains are 0; for n = d the top chains have the same boundary, and ∂ is injective on K_d (it is injective on the top degree of a factor λ(Θᵢ) of positive dimension, by an extremal argument on creation order, and stays injective after tensoring with the other factors' top degrees).

## Why it matters

A type theory for lax Gray-enriched higher categories has been proved sound for its strict model in the proof assistant Agda, modulo exactly one cited fact: (P), used in the instance Q3a. The rest of the model, including that T above is a cell, is now machine-checked. A proof of Q3a or (P) that a proof assistant can follow would make the strict model fully checked. Chains in the formalization are finite multisets of tuples of de Bruijn levels, and supports are computed by a downward-closure function, so arguments by counting, extremal elements and induction on lists are the easiest to formalize.

## What would count as a solution

- An elementary proof of (P), or of Q3a, for these complexes, written at the level of chains and supports, with each step checkable.
- Or a reduction of (Rad) and (G) to properties of the creation order of the Θᵢ that are visibly true in the presentation above.
- Or a reason why no proof avoiding atom generation is expected, with the shortest known route.

## Literature status (2026-11-18)

- (P) for these K: Hadzihasanovic 2404.07273, Prop. 8.3.17 and Thm 11.2.18 (molecules as closed subsets).
- (Rad) and (F2) for all Steiner complexes: Forest (HHA 2022) Lemmas 3.4.5 and 3.4.11; Campbell 2605.09427 Props. 2.20 and 2.23.
- Atom generation: Steiner 2004, for loop-free unital bases.
- No elementary or machine-checked proof of (P) was found.
