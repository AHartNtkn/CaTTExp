# Interpreting CaTT in (∞,ω)-categories, round 3: reconstruction and the coinductive target

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Rounds 1 and 2 and their verified answers are archived in `answered/catt-models-in-infinity-omega-2026-11-18.md` and `answered/catt-models-in-infinity-omega-r2-2026-11-18.md`. The smallest open case is Q1.*

## Background

**CaTT.** CaTT (Finster–Mimram, LICS 2017) is a dependent type theory with one sort former, `⋆` and `arr A t u` (cells of type A from t to u), and one term former, `coh`. For a *pasting context* Θ (a context presenting a globular pasting diagram) and two parallel terms u, v over Θ satisfying a support (free-variable) condition, `coh Θ (u → v) σ` is a cell, for every substitution σ into Θ. Substitution is strict and syntactic. The models of CaTT are exactly Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis weak ω-categories: presheaves on the CaTT coherator sending globular sums to globular products (Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, arXiv 2106.04475, §3).

**A directed variant.** A variant L of CaTT keeps the same syntax but changes the side condition of `coh` so that, across objects, interchange is a directed cell (lax, not invertible). Its intended strict models are strict ω-categories with lax transformations (Ara–Maltsiniotis's ∞-Cat_lax, arXiv 1607.00668, Ex. C.18), and its intended homotopical models are oriented (∞,ω)-categories in the sense of Gepner–Heine (arXiv 2510.10504), whose homs carry Campion's Gray tensor (arXiv 2311.00205). The strict soundness of L is proved, using that cells of free strict ω-categories on Steiner complexes are determined by their supports.

**What is known about the homotopy theory.**
- Strict ω-categories have the Lafont–Métayer–Worytkiewicz model structure, cofibrantly generated by the boundary inclusions ∂Oₙ → Oₙ (arXiv 0712.0617, §4).
- Leinster's operad is the universal cofibrant replacement of the strict ω-category operad for that weak factorisation system (Garner, arXiv 0804.2663). Markakis extends this to generalised signatures: free algebras on computads are the cofibrant objects (arXiv 2303.11978).
- For Batanin–Leinster weak ω-categories, Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara prove 2-out-of-3 for ω-weak equivalences and call a (left semi-)model structure future work (arXiv 2406.13240, introduction). We know of no model structure on weak ω-categories.
- Cat_ω (Loubaton, arXiv 2406.05425, the inductive notion lim Cat_n) is presentable and cartesian closed (Prop. 2.2.1.54). Campion's Gray tensor is built on flagged (∞,∞)-categories and transferred to Cat_n for n ≤ ω and to the version with coinductive equivalences.
- CaTT's invertible cells are the coinductively invertible ones (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2406.12127).

**What round 1 established.**
- **A relative category.** Models of CaTT are equivalent to Batanin–Leinster weak ω-categories (Benjamin–Markakis–Sarti, arXiv 2405.00398, §7). With W the Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara ω-weak equivalences (2-out-of-6, arXiv 2406.13240 Thm 3.3.3, Cor. 3.3.4), 𝓗_CaTT := Mod(CaTT)[W⁻¹] exists. FHM (arXiv 2511.09849) characterise trivial fibrations as W ∩ ω-equifibrations (Prop. 4.1.5) and equifibrations as J-injectives (Thm 4.3.1). No (semi-)model structure is known.
- **Strict coherent choice, one direction.** Let 𝓜 be the injective complete Segal Θ_ω-space model category and 𝒜 its algebraically fibrant objects (Nikolaus). For each globular pasting shape P, yP is discrete and fibrant, so F Sp(P) → yP is a trivial fibration in 𝒜. Lifting the CaTT coherator's cell attachments against these gives R : 𝕋 → 𝒜 preserving globular sums, and S(Y) := 𝒜(R(−), Y) is a CaTT model with strict substitution and S(Y)_n = (UY)(D_n)₀. S sends weak equivalences to W, so it induces Cat_ω → 𝓗_CaTT. It has a formal left adjoint 𝓛_R(A) = ∫^P A(P) · R(P).
- **(2,2)-categories are presented** (Campbell, arXiv 1907.01999: 2-truncated (∞,2)-categories are bicategories; strictify).
- **Obstruction to the raw inductive nerve.** The coherent walking ω-equivalence E (Hadzihasanovic–Loubaton–Ozornova–Rovelli, arXiv 2404.14509, Thm 1.33) is a gaunt strict ω-category with E → 1 a folk weak equivalence, so no functor 𝓗_CaTT → Cat^ind_ω agrees with the raw inductive nerve on strict ω-categories. The Street nerve marking coinductively invertible cells does send E → 1 to an equivalence (Henry–Loubaton, arXiv 2301.11424, Prop. 4.58).
- **Free shapes agree.** Finite globular pasting shapes and their Gray products are the same free objects in the inductive and coinductive theories (Campion, arXiv 2311.00205, Cor. 3.5, Thm 3.14, Cor. 3.15).

**What round 2 established.**
- **S factors through coinductive completion.** Write Q : Cat^ind_ω ⇄ Cat^coind_ω : j for coinductive completion. By Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde (arXiv 2603.11005: Lemma 5.9, Prop. 5.29 and 5.30, Cor. 5.31), completion is the localisation at ∞-surjective maps, and its units are ∞-surjective. S₀ sends every ∞-surjective map to W, because for an 𝒜-fibration, π₀-surjectivity of the Kan fibration Map(yDₙ, UY) → Map(Bₙ, UY) ×_{Map(Bₙ,UZ)} Map(yDₙ, UZ) gives strict globular boundary lifts, which are FHM trivial fibrations. Hence S ≃ S̄ ∘ Q with S̄ = S j.
- **The underived left adjoint is not relative.** The coend 𝓛_R ⊣ S₀ is an ordinary adjunction. For gaunt strict C with discrete nerve NC (canonically algebraically fibrant), π₀ Map(𝓛_R A, NC) ≅ Mod(CaTT)(A, C). Applied to the coherent walking equivalence w : E → 1, this would make Mod(1, E) → Mod(E, E) a bijection if 𝓛_R(w) were an equivalence, which is false. So 𝓛_R does not send W to inductive equivalences.
- **S₀ preserves categorical level.** If Y presents an (∞,n)-category, every cell of S₀(Y) above dimension n is coinductively invertible. The proof extends the cell strictly over N(Σ^{k−1} I), then uses that FHM weak equivalences reflect invertibility.
- **A necessary test for reconstruction.** If the ordinary counit ε_Y : 𝓛_R S₀(Y) → Y is an equivalence, then 𝒜(Y, NC) → Mod(CaTT)(S₀Y, C) is bijective for every gaunt strict C. In particular, for contractible Y every strict CaTT map S₀(Y) → E must be constant.
- **Marked targets.** Marking the coinductive equivalences of C does not change C. Localising at them gives QC (ORW Prop. 5.29).
- **Directed version, conditional.** Suppose the coherator of L has a Gray-enriched cellular presentation, and suppose the hom maps K(Q_a, Q_b) → K(Q_a, Y_b) are trivial fibrations. Then lifting into the enriched category of commuting squares gives an enriched R_L that strictly preserves composition. Still to be supplied are arity colimits in the semantic category, ordered tensor domains D^r ⊗ D^s (for example via a coloured operad), and invertibility declarations.

## Questions

**Q1 (the gaunt test, smallest case).** Let Y be a contractible algebraically fibrant Θ-space, for instance a fibrant replacement of the point with chosen fillers. Is every strict CaTT map S₀(Y) → E constant, where E is the coherent walking ω-equivalence (Hadzihasanovic–Loubaton–Ozornova–Rovelli, arXiv 2404.14509, Thm 1.33)? Is every strict CaTT map S₀(Y) → C constant for every gaunt strict C? A non-constant map refutes the ordinary counit theorem. A proof that all such maps are constant would be the first step of reconstruction.

**Q2 (derived reconstruction).** Is there a derived version of 𝓛_R, for example via cofibrant replacement of CaTT models by free models on computads (Markakis, arXiv 2303.11978), such that the derived counit is an equivalence for (∞,1)- or (∞,2)-categories? Equivalently, is S̄ : Cat^coind_ω → 𝓗_CaTT fully faithful on (∞,n)-categories for small n?

**Q3 (the equivalence).** Is S̄ : Cat^coind_ω → 𝓗_CaTT an equivalence? A precise conjecture with the needed inputs, or a counterexample (two coinductive (∞,ω)-categories with W-equivalent S-images that are not equivalent), would help.

**Q4 (directed version).** For the directed variant L, give the Gray-enriched (or coloured-operadic) cellular presentation of its coherator needed by the lifting theorem above, at least through dimension 3.

## What would help most

A proof or counterexample for Q1; a precise derived statement for Q2 with references; for Q4, the presentation in low dimensions.

# Researcher Response

**Q1 has a counterexample.** Under the stated hypotheses on \(J\), \(R\), and \(S_0\), there is a contractible algebraically fibrant Θ-space \(Y\) with
\[
S_0(Y)\cong E
\]
as CaTT models. Thus \(S_0(Y)\to E\) need not be constant, and the ordinary counit fails even when \(Y\) presents the point.

For Q2–Q3, computadic replacement gives a precise conditional derived adjunction, with separate conditions for reconstruction and equivalence. For Q4, the description of \(L\) does not determine its cellular presentation; it does determine the ordered tensor arities and directed interchanger described below.

## 1. Q1: a contractible \(Y\) with \(S_0(Y)\cong E\)

The construction proves a more general statement.

### Proposition

Fix \(J\) and \(R\) as in the question. Let \(C\) be a gaunt strict \(\omega\)-category such that
\[
C\longrightarrow 1
\]
is a folk weak equivalence. There is \(Y\in\mathcal A\) and an algebraic morphism
\[
i:NC\longrightarrow Y
\]
such that:

1. \(UY\to1\) is a trivial fibration in the injective complete Segal Θ-space model structure;
2. \(i\) induces a bijection on the vertices at every globe;
3. \(S_0(i):C\to S_0(Y)\) is an isomorphism of CaTT models.

Here we use the round-2 properties that \(NC\) is discrete and fibrant, has its unique algebraically fibrant structure, and satisfies \(S_0(NC)\cong C\). The construction below does not change \(J\) or \(R\).

### Step 1: retain cells, boundaries, and identities

Let
\[
j:\mathbb G_r\hookrightarrow\Theta_\omega
\]
be the **full** subcategory on the globes \(D_n\). Presheaves on \(\mathbb G_r\) are reflexive globular sets: the maps between globes encode sources, targets, and identities.

Set
\[
V=j^*(NC).
\]
Thus \(V(D_n)=C_n\), with the source, target, and identity functions of \(C\).

Every strict \(\omega\)-category is folk fibrant, and the folk cofibrations are generated by globe boundary inclusions. Consequently, the assumed weak equivalence \(C\to1\) is a trivial fibration. It supplies a \(0\)-cell and an \(n\)-cell filling every parallel \((n-1)\)-boundary. These model-structure facts are recalled in Hadzihasanovic–Loubaton–Ozornova–Rovelli, Theorem 1.20; their Theorem 1.33 supplies the required weak equivalence for \(E\). [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14509v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

It follows that \(V\) is injective against **every monomorphism of reflexive globular sets**. To verify this directly, take a monomorphism \(A\hookrightarrow B\) and a map \(A\to V\). Extend it dimension by dimension:

- choose images of the new \(0\)-cells;
- at dimension \(n>0\), send identity cells to identities of their already assigned sources;
- retain the prescribed values on \(A_n\);
- fill the assigned parallel boundary of every remaining \(n\)-cell.

The identity prescription is consistent with the map on \(A\). Writing \(e_n\) for the identity function,
\[
A_n\cap e_n(B_{n-1})=e_n(A_{n-1}),
\]
because \(s_ne_n=\mathrm{id}\). Thus an identity cell belonging to \(A\) cannot have a source outside \(A\).

### Step 2: extend to an injective set-valued Θ-presheaf

Take the right Kan extension
\[
I=j_*V=\operatorname{Ran}_{j^{\mathrm{op}}}V.
\]
Explicitly,
\[
I(P)=
\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathbb G_r}}
\bigl(j^*yP,V\bigr).
\]

Restriction \(j^*\) preserves monomorphisms. By the adjunction \(j^*\dashv j_*\), injectivity of \(V\) therefore implies injectivity of \(I\) against all monomorphisms in \(\operatorname{Set}^{\Theta_\omega^{\mathrm{op}}}\).

Full faithfulness of \(j\) gives
\[
I(D_n)\cong V(D_n)=C_n.
\]

The adjunction unit
\[
u:NC\longrightarrow I
\]
is a monomorphism. At \(P\), it sends a strict functor \(P\to C\) to its functions on cells, preserving sources, targets, and identities. Those functions determine the strict functor, so \(u_P\) is injective. At every \(D_n\), it is a bijection.

### Step 3: add simplicial homotopies without adding globe vertices

Define the simplicial presheaf
\[
\widetilde Y=\operatorname{cosk}_0 I,
\qquad
\widetilde Y(P)_k=I(P)^{k+1}.
\]
Faces delete entries and degeneracies repeat entries.

The simplicial \(0\)-coskeleton is right adjoint to evaluation in simplicial degree \(0\):
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_{s\widehat\Theta}(X,\operatorname{cosk}_0 I)
\cong
\operatorname{Hom}_{\widehat\Theta}(X_0,I).
\]
If \(A\hookrightarrow B\) is a monomorphism of simplicial presheaves, then \(A_0\hookrightarrow B_0\) is a monomorphism. Injectivity of \(I\) therefore extends every map \(A\to\widetilde Y\) to \(B\to\widetilde Y\).

Hence
\[
\widetilde Y\longrightarrow1
\]
has the right lifting property against **every monomorphism**. Since these are exactly the cofibrations of the specified model structure, this map is a trivial fibration. This proves both contractibility and injective fibrancy.

The map \(u\) induces a monomorphism
\[
i:NC\longrightarrow\widetilde Y,
\]
sending a strict functor to the corresponding constant tuple in every simplicial degree. At globe vertices,
\[
(NC)(D_n)_0=C_n
\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}
\widetilde Y(D_n)_0=I(D_n)=C_n.
\]

### Step 4: choose fillers making \(i\) algebraic

First, the \(J\)-fillers of \(NC\) are unique. For a generating trivial cofibration \(a:A\hookrightarrow B\), fibrancy of \(NC\) makes
\[
\operatorname{Map}(B,NC)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Map}(A,NC)
\]
a trivial fibration. Both mapping simplicial sets are discrete, so this is a bijection.

Now choose the \(J\)-fillers of \(\widetilde Y\). For a map \(h:A\to\widetilde Y\):

- if \(h=i\bar h\) factors through \(NC\), choose \(i\) composed with the unique filler of \(\bar h\);
- otherwise, choose any filler in \(\widetilde Y\).

The first prescription is unambiguous because \(i\) is monic. The second is possible because \(\widetilde Y\to1\) lifts every monomorphism.

These are exactly the data required for an algebraically fibrant object: Nikolaus’s Definition 2.1 requires chosen fillers, and morphisms preserving them, without additional naturality equations between different lifting problems. Thus these choices define \(Y\in\mathcal A\), with \(UY=\widetilde Y\), and make \(i:NC\to Y\) an algebraic morphism. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.1342?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Step 5: apply the already fixed \(S_0\)

Functoriality gives
\[
S_0(i):S_0(NC)\longrightarrow S_0(Y).
\]
Using \(S_0(NC)\cong C\), its function on \(n\)-cells is
\[
C_n=(NC)(D_n)_0
\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}
(UY)(D_n)_0=C_n.
\]

A morphism of CaTT models that is bijective on every globe is an isomorphism: its values on pasting contexts are the corresponding limits of its globe values. Therefore
\[
\boxed{S_0(Y)\cong C.}
\]

This proves the proposition.

### Apply it to \(E\)

Take \(C=E\). The inverse
\[
h=S_0(i)^{-1}:S_0(Y)\longrightarrow E
\]
is a strict CaTT morphism. It is nonconstant because \(E\) has two distinct objects; these are the two objects in the polygraphic construction of the coherent walking equivalence. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.14509v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Thus both universal assertions in Q1 are false.

Moreover, choosing either object of \(E\) gives
\[
1\longrightarrow NE\longrightarrow UY.
\]
This is a monomorphism and a weak equivalence, since its composite with \(UY\to1\) is the identity. Hence this \(UY\) is indeed a fibrant replacement of the point in \(\mathcal M\), equipped with chosen fillers.

### Why the ordinary counit fails

Each
\[
(UY)(P)=\operatorname{cosk}_0 I(P)
\]
is nonempty and connected. Nonemptiness follows, for example, from the constant strict functor \(P\to E\) at either object. Because \(NE(P)\) is discrete, every simplicial-presheaf map
\[
UY\longrightarrow NE
\]
factors through \(1\). Such maps induce constant CaTT morphisms.

Suppose the ordinary counit
\[
\varepsilon_Y:\mathcal L_RS_0(Y)\longrightarrow Y
\]
were a weak equivalence. All simplicial presheaves are cofibrant in \(\mathcal M\), and \(NE\) is fibrant. Mapping into \(NE\) would therefore give a weak equivalence of mapping spaces. Those mapping spaces are discrete, so precomposition would induce a bijection of ordinary Hom sets.

Every underlying map into \(NE\) preserves algebraic fillers, because its fillers are unique. The adjunction would consequently make
\[
\mathcal A(Y,NE)
\longrightarrow
\mathcal A(\mathcal L_RS_0Y,NE)
\cong
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathrm{CaTT})(S_0Y,E)
\]
a bijection. Its image consists of constant maps, and it misses \(h\). This is a contradiction.

Therefore
\[
\boxed{\varepsilon_Y\text{ is not an inductive Θ-space weak equivalence}.}
\]

The construction does not identify \(Y\) with the particular free algebraically fibrant object \(F1\). It also does not refute a counit after coinductive completion.

## 2. Q2: a conditional derived adjunction

Write
\[
\mathcal C=\operatorname{Mod}(\mathrm{CaTT}),\qquad
\mathcal H=\mathcal C[W^{-1}],\qquad
\mathcal D=\operatorname{Cat}^{\mathrm{coind}}_\omega.
\]
Reserve \(Q\) for coinductive completion, and write \(K\) for computadic cofibrant replacement.

Markakis’s Proposition 19 and Corollaries 21–22 supply the boundary-generated cofibrant replacement comonad and identify the cofibrant algebras with algebras free on computads. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.11978?utm_source=chatgpt.com) Write its counit and comultiplication as
\[
q_A:KA\longrightarrow A,
\qquad
\delta_A:KA\longrightarrow K^2A.
\]
FHM explicitly study this comonad: \(q_A,\delta_A\in W\), and \(K\) preserves and reflects \(W\). This is Proposition 4.2.2 of *\(\omega\)-weak equivalences between weak \(\omega\)-categories*. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13240v3?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Here is one sufficient input for the proposed derived realization:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{(H)}\quad&
f:B\to B'\text{ belongs to }W,\quad
B,B'\text{ computad-cofibrant}\\
&\Longrightarrow
QU\mathcal L_R(f)\text{ is an equivalence in }\mathcal D.
\end{aligned}}
\]

**Under (H), there is an adjunction**
\[
\boxed{
\Lambda:\mathcal H\rightleftarrows\mathcal D:\bar S,
\qquad
\Lambda[A]\simeq QU\mathcal L_R(KA).
}
\]

This conclusion does not require a model structure on \(\mathcal C\).

### Proof using the co-Kleisli category

Let \(\mathcal C_K\) have the same objects as \(\mathcal C\), with
\[
\mathcal C_K(A,B)=\mathcal C(KA,B).
\]
Declare a morphism weak when its representing strict map \(KA\to B\) belongs to \(W\). These are FHM’s weak \(\omega\)-functors and weak \(\omega\)-weak equivalences, Definitions 4.1.11 and 4.2.1. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13240v3?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The co-Kleisli adjunction is
\[
K_*:\mathcal C_K\rightleftarrows\mathcal C:J.
\]
For a representing map \(p:KA\to B\),
\[
K_*(A)=KA,\qquad K_*(p)=K(p)\delta_A,
\]
while \(J\) sends \(f:A\to B\) to the map represented by \(fq_A\). This is the cofibrant-replacement approach to categories of weak maps developed by Bourke–Garner. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6560?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Both functors preserve the specified weak equivalences. Their counit is \(q_A\), and their unit is represented by \(\mathrm{id}_{KA}\). Both become equivalences after localization, giving
\[
\mathcal C_K[W_K^{-1}]\simeq\mathcal H.
\]

Compose ordinary adjunctions:
\[
\mathcal L_RK_*:\mathcal C_K
\rightleftarrows
\mathcal A:JS_0.
\]
Localize \(\mathcal A\) at the maps \(g\) for which \(QU(g)\) is an equivalence. Its localization is \(\mathcal D\).

Condition (H) makes the left adjoint preserve weak equivalences: \(K_*(p)\) is a \(W\)-map between computad-cofibrant algebras. The assumed descent of \(S_0\) through coinductive completion makes the right adjoint preserve equivalences after localization.

For precision, replace \(W_K\) by its saturation—the maps inverted in \(\mathcal C_K[W_K^{-1}]\). This changes no localization and makes the last assertion an ordinary relative-functor assertion. The adjunction’s unit, counit, and triangle identities then descend through localization. This gives \(\Lambda\dashv\bar S\).

### The reconstruction condition is separate

Let \(Y\) present \(jC\), where \(C\in\mathcal D\). Define
\[
d_Y:
\mathcal L_RKS_0Y
\xrightarrow{\mathcal L_R(q_{S_0Y})}
\mathcal L_RS_0Y
\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_Y}
Y.
\]
Under (H), the derived counit is
\[
\boxed{\epsilon_C^{\mathrm{der}}=QU(d_Y).}
\]

Thus reconstruction requires the additional assertion
\[
\boxed{
\text{(R)}\qquad QU(d_Y)\text{ is an equivalence}.
}
\]
For Q2, require (R) for \(C\) ranging over \((\infty,1)\)-categories or \((\infty,2)\)-categories.

There is a useful equivalent formulation. For a map \(f:X\to C\) in the inductive theory whose target is coinductively complete,
\[
Qf\text{ is an equivalence}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
f\text{ is }\infty\text{-surjective}.
\]
One implication follows because \(Q\) inverts \(\infty\)-surjective maps. Conversely, if \(Qf\) is an equivalence, naturality of the completion unit expresses \(f\), up to equivalence, as the completion unit \(X\to QX\) followed by an equivalence to \(C\). Completion units are \(\infty\)-surjective by ORW Proposition 5.29; localization at these maps is Corollary 5.31. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11005v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Consequently, (R) is exactly the concrete geometric claim
\[
\boxed{
U\mathcal L_RKS_0Y\longrightarrow UY
\text{ is }\infty\text{-surjective}.
}
\]

The fact that \(q_{S_0Y}\) is a trivial fibration of CaTT models does not by itself establish this geometric lifting statement. I do not have a proof of (H) or of this reconstruction condition for all \((\infty,1)\)- or \((\infty,2)\)-categories.

### A qualification to “equivalently” in Q2

For an adjunction \(\Lambda\dashv\bar S\), equivalence of **all** counits is equivalent to full faithfulness of \(\bar S\).

For a full subcategory \(\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal D\), equivalence of counits at every \(C\in\mathcal B\) implies full faithfulness of \(\bar S|_{\mathcal B}\). The converse needs an additional hypothesis, such as
\[
\Lambda\bar S(\mathcal B)\subseteq\mathcal B.
\]

Without such a hypothesis, the converse is false. For the free-group/forgetful adjunction, the forgetful functor restricted to the full subcategory consisting only of the trivial group is fully faithful. Its counit there is nevertheless
\[
F(\{*\})=\mathbb Z\longrightarrow1,
\]
which is not an isomorphism.

## 3. Q3: a precise equivalence conjecture

The following three assertions suffice for the proposed equivalence:

1. **Homotopical realization:** condition (H).
2. **Counit reconstruction:** condition (R) for every coinductively complete \(C\).
3. **Unit reconstruction:** for every CaTT model \(A\), the map induced by
   \[
   A
   \xleftarrow[\in W]{q_A}
   KA
   \xrightarrow{\eta_{KA}}
   S_0\mathcal L_RKA
   \]
   is an equivalence in \(\mathcal H\).

Here \(\eta\) is the unit of the ordinary adjunction. Descent of \(S_0\) identifies the last term, after localization, with \(\bar S\Lambda[A]\). Requiring \(\eta_{KA}\in W\) would be a sufficient, stronger version of the third condition; membership in the saturation of \(W\) is enough.

Under (H), these are precisely the derived counit and unit tests. They imply
\[
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{CaTT}}\simeq
\operatorname{Cat}^{\mathrm{coind}}_\omega.
\]

The counterexample in §1 is compatible with this conjecture:
\[
S_0Y\cong E,\qquad [E]\simeq[1]\text{ in }\mathcal H,
\qquad Q(NE)\simeq1.
\]
It therefore does not produce two inequivalent coinductively complete categories with equivalent \(S\)-images.

## 4. Q4: what can be specified through dimension \(3\)

The supplied description of \(L\) does not determine its coherator through dimension \(3\). The missing datum is the actual admissibility predicate
\[
\operatorname{Adm}_L(\Theta,A,u,v)
\]
for a head
\[
\operatorname{coh}(\Theta:u\to_Av).
\]
Strict soundness verifies the interpretations of admitted heads; it does not enumerate which heads are admitted. Nor does equality of two strict interpretations identify the corresponding syntactic terms.

The advertised strict target does determine the following tensor data.

### Ordered arities and the directed interchanger

Ara–Maltsiniotis use **left Gray enrichment** for \(\infty\text{-Cat}_{\mathrm{lax}}\), so fix composition in the order
\[
m_{xyz}:H(x,y)\otimes H(y,z)\longrightarrow H(x,z).
\]
Their C.16 describes the reversed tensor convention, and C.18 identifies the lax-transformation example. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.00668?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For
\[
\alpha:f_0\Rightarrow f_1:x\to y,\qquad
\beta:g_0\Rightarrow g_1:y\to z,
\]
the tensor square gives
\[
\boxed{
\chi_{\alpha,\beta}:
(g_1\alpha)\circ(\beta f_0)
\Longrightarrow
(\beta f_1)\circ(g_0\alpha).
}
\]
Composition \(\circ\) performs the rightmost \(2\)-cell first. Both boundaries are \(2\)-cells
\[
g_0f_0\Rightarrow g_1f_1.
\]

The orientation follows from the directed tensor differential. For the interval generator \(e\),
\[
d(e\otimes e)
=
(1\otimes e)+(e\otimes0)
-(0\otimes e)-(e\otimes1).
\]
The negative boundary performs the second tensor factor first, giving the displayed source.

A global \(p\)-cell is a \((p-1)\)-cell in a hom. Consequently, the tensor of global cells of dimensions \(p,q\ge1\) has top global dimension
\[
(p-1)+(q-1)+1=p+q-1.
\]
The necessary binary tensor arities through global dimension \(3\) are therefore:

| Global input dimensions | Hom tensor domain | Induced global operation |
|---|---|---|

### The dimension-\(4\) distinction matters

The same calculation places two further comparisons in global dimension \(4\):

- a \(3\)-cell tensored with an adjacent \(2\)-cell gives the comparison expressing interchanger naturality;
- three adjacent \(2\)-cells give the cube comparing the two sequences of three interchangers.

In an untruncated Gray-enriched theory, these are \(4\)-cells between composites of \(3\)-cells. A strict \(3\)-truncation replaces them by equalities. Thus the literal \(3\)-skeleton of an infinite theory and a strict three-dimensional presentation require different specifications.

For a fully specified strict comparison theory, Morehouse’s Definition 2.4 gives directed Gray-category axioms: interchanger boundaries, compatibility with identities and composites, extremal and medial whiskering, and naturality. It also specifies the reversed lax orientation. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.08783?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Identifying that comparison theory—or a cellular resolution of it—with \(L\) requires the actual replacement `coh` rule and its invertibility declarations. Those data are necessary to give the requested cellular presentation of \(L\), even through dimension \(3\).
