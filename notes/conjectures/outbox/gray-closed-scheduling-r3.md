# Schedules in free Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-categories, round 3

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Rounds 1 and 2 and their verified answers are archived in `answered/gray-closed-scheduling-2026-11-18.md` and `answered/gray-closed-scheduling-r2-2026-11-18.md`. The smallest open case is Question 1.*

## Background

**Schedules.** Fix a finite poset P on labels {1, …, n}, with numerical order as the left/right order of incomparable labels. Let S(P) be the category with:
- objects: the linear extensions of P;
- generating arrows: swaps ji → ij of adjacent P-incomparable labels with i < j;
- equations: commuting squares for swaps at disjoint positions, and the braid hexagon for three pairwise incomparable labels.

Then S(P)(w, v) is a singleton if Inv_P(v) ⊆ Inv_P(w) and empty otherwise. Here Inv_P(w) is the set of pairs i < j, incomparable, with j before i in w. (Proof: move the first differing label left, then resolve the first swaps by a square or a hexagon and induct.)

**Gray monoids.** In the free strict Gray monoid (a monoid in 2-Cat for Gray's lax tensor) on a set of objects and generating 1-cells, fix the image in the free strict monoidal category, i.e. the string diagram. Then the hom-category between the 1-cells with that diagram is S(P). Here P is the diagram's dependency order, with left/right given by planarity. The 2-cells are whiskered interchangers (f ⊗ B′)∘(A ⊗ g) ⇒ (A′ ⊗ g)∘(f ⊗ B), "right factor first ⇒ left factor first".

**The ω-version.** The hom ω-category of the Steiner complex ν(I^⊗n), from bottom to top, is not thin: two hexagon 2-cells are joined by the 3-cell ***. Gray's 2-tensor is its intelligent 2-truncation (Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches*, A.25 and A.31). For a dependency poset P, the subcomplex K_P ⊆ I^⊗n spanned by the faces (order ideal J, S ⊆ min(P∖J)) has paths equal to the linear extensions of P. Its truncated hom is S(P).

## Setting: the biclosed version

Formulas are built from atoms with ⊗ and the residuals B\A and A/B, with no unit. 𝒢 is the free **Gray-monoidal biclosed strict 2-category** on a set of generating 1-cells fᵢ : Pᵢ → Qᵢ between formulas. Its structure:
- a strict 2-category with a Gray monoidal structure (lax interchangers, as above);
- closed structure: isomorphisms of hom-categories Φ : 𝒢(A ⊗ B, C) ≅ 𝒢(B, A\C), and the mirror one for /.
  - Φ is **strictly natural in the context variable B**.
  - Φ is natural in the **bound variable A only up to the interchanger**.
  - Strict naturality in A would force (f ⊗ B′)(A ⊗ g) = (A′ ⊗ g)(f ⊗ B), collapsing distinct schedules into one 1-cell. That is not wanted.

**Two warnings, both established.**
1. *Nesting is not invariant under naive normalization.* For a generator h : U → U with U = X\Y,

   ```
   h = Λ(ev) ∘ h = Λ(ev ∘ (1 ⊗ h))
   ```

   So h can be "ambient" or "inside a closure". β-reduction ev ∘ (1 ⊗ Λk) = k also changes nesting. Any invariant notion of "event inside a closure" must start from a βη-canonical form.
2. *Unit-free planar coherence.* In the free unit-free biclosed monoidal 1-category, a balanced sequent (each atom twice, opposite polarities) has at most one morphism, and exactly one when it is provable in the Lambek calculus with product. So the purely structural parts of 1-cells are unique.

## What is known from round 1 (proved)

1. **A word invariant.** Sending each generator to its label gives an invariant W of 1-cell equality with values in words (composition order), with W(Λu) = W(u), W(ev) = ε and W(D ⊗ u) = W(u). It is realised by the one-object model whose 1-cells are words, with a unique 2-cell between words with the same multiset. So 1-cells with different generator order are distinct.
2. **Closure trees are not invariants of components.** With generators a : X → A and f : A ⊗ B → C, c = Λ_A(f), e = ev: the interchanger gives θ : s = e∘(a⊗U)∘(X⊗c) ⇒ t = e∘(A⊗c)∘(a⊗B), and t = f∘(a⊗B) by β. Every representative of s has a generator under currying (a Boolean biclosed poset rules out the needed structural prefix), while t is tensor-only. So any βη-normal form fixing tensor-only terms gives s and t different closure trees, though they are connected by a 2-cell. "Cross-level cells are generated by ordinary interchangers" does not imply "the canonical tree is invariant".
3. **Question 1, partial.** For φ : P → C/B and b : X → B, the forward comparison α = e_B ∗ χ_{φ,b} : t₁ ⇒ t₂ exists, W distinguishes t₁, t₂, and Λ_X(t₂) = (C/b)∘φ by strict naturality in the context variable. Whether 𝒢(t₁, t₂) = {α} and 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ is open: the tensor-only schedule theorem does not control 2-cells built by currying, uncurrying and η-expansion.
4. **Mapping-space biclosedness is too weak for Question 4.** Gagna–Harpaz–Lanari (arXiv 2006.14495, Rem. 2.20): Map(X ⊗ Y, Z) ≇ Map(X, Fun^{opgr}(Y, Z)) in general; only cores agree.

## What is known from round 2 (proved)

5. **An integer model.** Let 𝓜 be the strict 2-category with:
   - objects the integers;
   - for q ≥ p, the hom 𝓜(p, q) equal to the poset (ℤ, ≤) of weights; otherwise empty;
   - composition adding weights;
   - w(p ⊗ u) = w(u) + p·δ(u) and w(u ⊗ q) = w(u), where δ(u) = target − source;
   - p\r = r − p and r/q = r − q, with left currying subtracting p·δ and right currying leaving the weight unchanged.

   𝓜 is a Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category. An interchanger raises the weight by δ(f)·δ(g). A matrix-valued version (objects in ℤ^r) records ordered generator pairs.
6. **Question 1, reverse direction.** With distinct atoms, 𝒢(t₂, t₁) = ∅ and 𝒢(t, s) = ∅. For t₁ and t₂ the weights are 0 and 1, from |B| = 1, |C| = 2 and |P| = |X| = 0. For s and t they are −1 and 0. Every forward 2-cell between them uses **exactly one** interchanger whose two arguments each contain one generator. Every other interchanger in it has a generator-free argument.
7. **Undirected components.** π₀ 𝒢(A, C) ≅ 𝓑_Σ(A, C), the free ordinary unit-free biclosed monoidal category. Two 1-cells are connected by a zigzag of 2-cells iff their images there agree.
8. **The image and W do not classify 1-cells.** Take generators f : A → B and g : B → C with padding L, R, and let m = L ⊗ (g ∘ f) ⊗ R.
   - Currying g with the right padding gives u ⇒ m.
   - Currying g with the left padding gives m ⇒ v.
   - u, m and v have equal images in 𝓑_Σ, and W(u) = W(v).
   - Their weights (with |L| = |R| = |A| = 0, |B| = 1, |C| = 2) are −1, 0 and 1, so the three 1-cells are distinct.
   - The side of currying is data of the 1-cell, and schedules with only generator events cannot describe this component.

## Two further observations (proved)

9. **Additive models cannot see interchangers with a structural argument.** Call a model *additive* if 1-cells carry a weight in an ordered abelian group, δ(u) = weight(target) − weight(source) is additive under composition and tensor, and an interchanger χ_{f,g} adds a bilinear term β(δ(f), δ(g)). The model 𝓜 of item 5 and its matrix version are additive. Every structural 1-cell (ev, curried evaluations, associators, unitors) has δ = 0, so every interchanger with a structural argument has weight 0 in every additive model. Such models therefore cannot decide whether two 2-cells differing in those interchangers are equal.
10. **The axioms do not make evaluation central.** For ev : A ⊗ (A\C) → C and a generator g : Y → Y′, compare (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y), related by χ_{ev,g}. Currying A on the left (using strict naturality in the context variable and the target) turns this into the same question for m_Y := Φ(ev ⊗ Y) and g, so currying only transports it. Likewise for χ_{ev,ev′} at independent positions. Unless centrality is imposed (as in Garner–Hirschowitz–Pardon's premonoidal construction), structural maps are non-central events in 𝒢. In the quotient 𝒢_c of 𝒢 in which every interchanger with a structural argument is an identity, Question 1 follows at once from item 6.

## Questions

1. **(Forward uniqueness, smallest case.)** Prove 𝒢(t₁, t₂) = {α} and 𝒢(s, t) = {θ}, or show that they fail. By item 6 it suffices to show that interchangers with a generator-free argument are coherent: any two 2-cells between the same 1-cells that differ only in such interchangers are equal by the Gray and closure equations. By item 9 this needs a rewriting or coherence argument, or a non-additive model; by item 10 it is not automatic, since structural interchangers need not be identities.
2. **(1-cells within a component.)** For a fixed arrow of 𝓑_Σ, describe the 1-cells of 𝒢 over it. Is a 1-cell determined by its image together with the matrix-valued weight of item 5, or with a "curry side" label on each generator? Is each hom-category a product of schedule categories S(P) whose events include curry-side choices, as for u ⇒ m ⇒ v of item 8? By item 10, should the invariant schedule structural events (evaluations, curried evaluations) as well as generator events?
3. **(Thinness and rewriting.)** Is every hom-category of 𝒢 thin? Give a terminating and confluent presentation of its 2-cells, or a pair of distinct parallel 2-cells.
4. **(ω-version.)** With biclosedness on full hom ω-categories, are the hom ω-categories of the free Gray-monoidal biclosed (∞,ω)-category over a fixed arrow of 𝓑_Σ products of ν(K_P)-homs for suitable P?
5. **(Is evaluation central?)** In the free Gray-monoidal biclosed 2-category 𝒢, is χ_{ev,g} an identity (equivalently, is ev central), or are (ev ⊗ Y′) ∘ (A ⊗ (A\C) ⊗ g) and (C ⊗ g) ∘ (ev ⊗ Y) distinct 1-cells? In the (∞,ω) setting, is evaluation for Campion's Gray tensor (biclosed on mapping spaces) central, i.e. is the corresponding interchanger invertible or trivial? The answer decides whether the right theory is 𝒢 or its quotient 𝒢_c of item 10, and whether 𝒢_c is still sound for the (∞,ω) Gray tensor.

## What counts as a solution

A proof of Question 1; a decision for Question 5; a complete invariant of 1-cells for Question 2; a rewriting system or a non-thin example for Question 3; or a reference treating 2-cells of free Gray-monoidal (bi)closed 2-categories.
