# Which initial algebras follow from finite limits, finite colimits and descent?

## Setting

Let 𝓔 be an (∞,1)-category with finite limits and finite colimits. Consider the following hypotheses, each a "universe-free" axiom (no object classifies other objects):

- **(D) descent:** every finite colimit in 𝓔 is van Kampen for all maps (universal and effective), as in an ∞-topos (Lurie, HTT §6.1.3).
- **(D_c) restricted descent:** 𝓔 carries a class of maps 𝓒 (in the intended example, cocartesian fibrations in the (∞,1)-category of (∞,ω)-categories) containing all maps between objects that are groupoids, closed under base change and composition, such that every finite colimit is van Kampen for 𝓒, and colimits indexed by finite groupoids are van Kampen for all maps.
- **(L) local cartesian closure**, and **(Ω) a subobject classifier**.
- **(T) truncation:** n-truncations exist and families with n-truncated fibres over a groupoid Y descend uniquely along `Y → τ_{≤n+1} Y`.

A **positive signature** over a presentable 𝓔 is a finite list of operations `S(X) → T(X)` (and cells between composites of operations), where each source S is an accessible functor and each target T is an accessible limit-preserving functor; models are taken with strong homomorphisms. In a presentable 𝓔 every positive signature has an initial model (adjoint functor theorem plus presentability of categories of algebras; Kerodon 9.4.9, 9.5.1–9.5.2), and limit-preserving targets are the largest class that can be paired with every source. The question is what survives when 𝓔 is only finitely complete and cocomplete.

Known:
- Rasekh (*Every elementary higher topos has a natural number object*, TAC 37 (2021), arXiv:1809.01734) proves that a finitely bicomplete locally cartesian closed (∞,1)-category with a subobject classifier and descent (HTT 6.1.3) has a natural number object, built from the loop space ΩS¹ of the circle `S¹ = * ⊔_{*⊔*} *`. No universe is used for this; universes enter only in his §5.
- In 1-category theory the analogue fails: the topos of finite sets has no natural number object, and finite colimits in a 1-topos are not effective for non-free group actions.
- In the univalent setting, the integers are the initial pointed type with an equivalence (Altenkirch–Scoccola, arXiv:2007.00167, with bi-invertible data; the quasi-inverse presentation gives a non-set, Basold–Geuvers–van der Weide 2017).

## Questions

**1 (main).** Under (D) + finite limits and colimits (without (L) or (Ω)), does every positive signature whose sources are built from finite limits, finite colimits and products with fixed objects, and whose targets are X, ⊤, finite limits and cotensors by finite objects, have an initial model? If not, give the largest such class and a model of (D) in which a signature just outside it fails. In particular: does (D) alone give a natural number object (is Rasekh's use of (L) and (Ω) necessary)? Does (D) + (L) + (Ω) give initial algebras for all finitary polynomial endofunctors, i.e. finitary W-types, and free monoids?

**2.** The same with (D_c) + (T) in place of (D), for an (∞,1)-category that is the underlying (∞,1)-category of an (∞,2)-category with cotensors by [1] (so that cocartesian fibrations are defined by the usual adjoint condition). The intended model is the (∞,1)-category of (∞,ω)-categories, where (D_c) and (T) hold.

**3.** Non-derivability: is there a model of finite limits, finite colimits, (D) (or (D_c) + (T)), with some positive signature in the class of Question 1 having no initial model? A non-presentable example is required, since every presentable model has all initial models.

## Why it matters

A type theory without a universe must state the consequences of a universe as rules. If finite colimits with descent already produce natural numbers and inductive objects, as Rasekh's theorem suggests, then an "initial model" rule could be replaced, wholly or partly, by descent; if not, a model separating them shows that both rules are needed.

## What would count as a solution

For Question 1 and 2, a proof for a stated class with a countermodel just outside it; for Question 3, an explicit model. A reference with the exact statement counts.

## Researcher Response 2

Full finite descent gives a natural numbers object (NNO). Adding local cartesian closure and a subobject classifier gives the finite-arity W-types of this repository and free associative monoids. The general initial-model rule fails already for two constructors, both with target the carrier. Under restricted descent the NNO itself can fail. Section 5 identifies which readings of the restricted axiom restore it.

### 1. A class of signatures that works, and an exact criterion

In any finitely cocomplete category, start with generators $A\to X$. Their initial carrier is $A$, with its identity structure map. More generally, a finite list of constant sources $A_j\to T_j(X)$ works whenever the displayed targets have left adjoints $L_j$. Maps of models are precisely maps under $\coprod_j L_j A_j$. For example, for $T(X)=X^K$ with $K$ a finite cell space, $L(A)=K\otimes A$ is a finite colimit of copies of $A$. One may also attach finitely many cells between already represented terms. If their boundary is represented by $B\to P$ and the filled cell by $B\to D$, the new representing carrier is $P\sqcup_B D$, by the mapping-space pushout formula. These arguments work under either set of descent axioms. Section 3 exhibits failure after adjoining a single recursive constructor to $A\to X$.

There is also a maximal **semantic** class, with a criterion on carriers that works even when the sources involve pullbacks. Fix the interpretation of the parameters in $E$ and let $H$ denote the category of strong models. For an arbitrary carrier $B$ form the space
$$
 C_\Sigma(B)=\mathop{\rm colim}_{M\in H^{op}}\operatorname{Map}_E(UM,B).
$$
Use a larger external universe for this test. Then $\Sigma$ has an initial model **if and only if** $C_\Sigma$ is corepresentable in $E$. Thus the class guaranteed in every interpretation is obtained by applying this test in every such interpretation. This formulation allows parameters and equivalent presentations. Section 3 shows why no decidable syntax can recognize the whole uniformly valid class under (D).

Here is the sufficiency argument. The category $H$ has finite limits created on carriers, since the targets preserve finite limits. For each operation, the compatible structures on a limit cone are obtained by mapping its source to the limit of the targets. The same mapping property treats each prescribed cell and its boundary. Thus the diagram of all carriers defines a pro-object $P$ of $E$, indexed by the cofiltered category $H$. Its functor of maps into constant objects is exactly $C_\Sigma$. Corepresentability means that $P$ is equivalent to a constant pro-object. Extend each source and target to pro-objects levelwise, by its cofiltered-limit extension. The operations and cells of the diagram equip $P$ with a model structure, which transports to the representing object $I$ in $E$ by full faithfulness on constants.

For any ordinary model $N$ the mapping space from this pro-model into $N$ is
$$
 \mathop{\rm colim}_{M\in H^{op}}\operatorname{Map}_H(M,N).
$$
Indeed a strong map is a carrier map with its operation comparison paths and the finitely many specified cell compatibilities. Its mapping space is a finite homotopy limit of mapping spaces for carriers, sources, targets and boundaries. For a constant target the pro mapping formula computes each entry by the filtered colimit of the levelwise entry. Filtered colimits of spaces commute with these finite limits. The displayed colimit is contractible by Yoneda (the category of arrows into $N$ has terminal object $1_N$). It is therefore also $\operatorname{Map}_H(I,N)$, proving initiality. Conversely an initial model is terminal in $H^{op}$ and computes the colimit defining $C_\Sigma$.

### 2. A natural numbers object from finite van Kampen colimits

Let $E$ be finitely bicomplete and satisfy (D). The argument works in every slice. Write $S^1=\operatorname{coeq}(1\rightrightarrows1)$ and $Z=1\times_{S^1}1$. Van Kampen descent identifies $E/S^1$ with objects of $E$ equipped with an automorphism. Under this equivalence the object $1\to S^1$ gives $(Z,S)$, where $S$ denotes monodromy. Taking maps from $1\to S^1$ shows that evaluation at the constant loop $0$ identifies the space of equivariant maps $Z\to (X,f)$ with the space of points of $X$. Taking the total space of $1\to S^1$ gives $\operatorname{coeq}(1_Z,S)=1$, with quotient the terminal map. Indeed the total-space diagram over the circle is recovered by taking its colimit. Van Kampen descent passes to slices. Slice the descent equivalence at the compatible base-change diagram of any given base object. Finite colimits are universal, so all the circle, fibre and monodromy constructions in $E/T$ are the products with $T$ of the ones above. In particular equivariant recursion and the orbit quotient are available with arbitrary parameters.

Finite coproducts are disjoint and stable by (D). On $Z\sqcup Z$ take the automorphism carrying the first copy identically to the second, and the second to the first by $S$. It is free on $0$ in the first copy. Indeed an equivariant map into $(X,f)$ is a pair $a,b:Z\to X$ with paths $b\simeq fa$ and $aS\simeq fb$. Eliminating $b$ and the first path leaves precisely an equivariant map $(Z,S)\to(X,f^2)$, with the specified value at $0$. Thus $Z\simeq Z\sqcup Z$. Set $A=B=Z$. On $A\sqcup B\sqcup A$ take the automorphism carrying the first $A$ equivalently to the first $A\sqcup B$, and carrying the last $B\sqcup A$ equivalently to the last $A$, using that equivalence and its inverse with a permutation. Freeness gives an equivariant map from $Z$ taking $0$ to the point $0$ in $B$. Pullback gives a partition
$$
Z=Z_-\sqcup R\sqcup P,\qquad 0\in R,\qquad
S:Z_-\simeq Z_-\sqcup R,\quad S:R\sqcup P\simeq P.
$$
Put $M=R\sqcup P$ and $s=S|_M:M\to M$. This sign construction allows an arbitrary middle summand $R$. [Sattler and Wärn, §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18388v1)

Recursion with parameter $x$ gives $d:Z\times Z\to Z$ with $d(x,0)=x$ and $d(x,Su)=S^{-1}d(x,u)$. The two recurrences in $u$ with values $d(Sx,Su)$ and $d(x,u)$ agree at $0$, so $d(Sx,Su)=d(x,u)$. In particular $x\mapsto d(x,x)$ descends along the orbit quotient and is constantly $0$. Write $i<u$ when $d(i,u)\in Z_-$. This relation is decidable, meaning it is a coproduct summand with a specified complement. It has the four properties
$$
i<u\Rightarrow i<Su,\qquad Si<Su\Longleftrightarrow i<u,
\qquad \{i\in M:i<0\}=\varnothing,\qquad i<Si .
$$
These follow, respectively, from $S^{-1}Z_-\subseteq Z_-$, simultaneous translation, $d(i,0)=i$, and $d(i,Si)=S^{-1}(0)\in Z_-$.

A finite colimit now replaces quantification over a variable-length interval. For $u:Z$ put $I_u=\{i\in M:i<u\}$, formed as a single object over $Z$. Given any object with endomorphism $(X,f)$, form over $Z$ a graph with vertices $X\sqcup(I_u\times X)$ and edges $I_u\times X$. The first vertex summand is the endpoint. Each edge $(i,t)$ starts at $(i,t)$. If $i\in R$, its target is the endpoint $t$. If $i=sj\in P$, its target is $(j,f(t))$. This is well-defined because $sj<u$ gives $j<S^{-1}u$ and hence $j<u$. The two cases are morphisms defined on complementary summands, so the construction requires only finite limits, finite coproducts and products. Take the coequalizer of the two incidence maps in $E/Z$, obtaining $Q\to Z$ with endpoint map $j_u:X\to Q_u$ and vertex maps $k_{u,i}:X\to Q_u$. Universality of the colimit identifies every base change with the same graph colimit for the new parameters.

The inclusion $I_u\subseteq I_{Su}$ induces $Q_u\to Q_{Su}$ in families. It is an equivalence. The complement $D_u=I_{Su}\smallsetminus I_u$ is a summand. Each new vertex in $D_u\times X$ has one edge from itself to the old graph. For $i=sk<Su$, the target index $k$ satisfies $k<u$. No old edge has a new target. The extended colimit is the pushout of the old colimit along $D_u\times X\xleftarrow{\mathrm{id}}D_u\times X\to Q_u$, with the first copy the new vertices. This follows by specifying a graph cocone, and proves equivalence in the slice over $Z$. The endpoint maps commute with these equivalences. They therefore give a morphism between cartesian diagrams on $Z\rightrightarrows Z$ with arrows the identity and $S$. Apply (D) to its colimit $1$. Both diagrams are pulled back from $1$. Evaluating along $0:1\to Z$ recovers the descended morphism. Since $I_0=\varnothing$, $j_0$ is an equivalence. Consequently $j:Z\times X\to Q$ is an equivalence over $Z$, naturally in $(X,f)$.

Define $g(m,t)=j_{Sm}^{-1}k_{Sm,m}(t)$ for $m\in M$, using $m<Sm$. The edge at a root gives $g(r,t)\simeq t$ for $r\in R$. The edge at $sm$ in $Q_{S^2m}$, followed by compatibility with $Q_{Sm}\to Q_{S^2m}$, gives $g(sm,t)\simeq g(m,f(t))$. Functoriality in $(X,f)$, applied to its endomorphism $f$, gives $g(m,f(t))\simeq f(g(m,t))$. In particular if $a:1\to X$ is a point, $m\mapsto g(m,a)$ is a strong map of pointed endomorphisms $(M,0,s)\to(X,a,f)$. These are the components of a natural transformation from the constant functor at $(M,0,s)$ to the identity on the category $H$ of pointed endomorphisms. For coherence, perform the entire graph construction in the functor category of $(X,f)$ (or of $(X,a,f)$) with the tautological diagram and tautological natural endomorphism $f$. Pullbacks, colimits and inversion of equivalences there are objectwise. Each of the displayed paths is then a path of natural transformations. A map of pointed endomorphisms is precisely a map of carriers together with its point and endomorphism comparison paths, by the mapping-space pullback for this freely specified signature. Thus no compatibility condition between two distinct paths is required.

For completeness, the following two retract observations turn this construction into initiality.

1. **Idempotent completeness.** Any finitely bicomplete $E$ satisfying (D) is idempotent complete. Embed it in its idempotent completion $K$, where a given coherent idempotent $e$ on $X$ factors as $X\xrightarrow pY\xrightarrow qX$ with $pq\simeq1$ and $qp\simeq e$. The embedding preserves existing finite limits and colimits since their mapping-space universal properties extend to retracts of test objects. Write $T_h=\operatorname{coeq}(1,h)$, with the natural projection to $S^1$. In $K$, $T_{1_Y}\simeq Y\times S^1$ over $S^1$. Indeed $T_{1_X}\simeq X\times S^1$ by universality of finite colimits in $E$. Take the retract induced by $e$ on the first coordinate. Testing maps shows that this retract represents the coequalizer for $Y$, as the equalizer formula for maps out is functorial in retracts. The product statement follows in the same way by testing maps in. The mapping tori $T_{qp}$ and $T_{pq}$ have equivalent fibres over the circle's basepoint. To see this, form the cyclic two-edge diagram with vertices $X,Y$ and edges $X\xrightarrow pY,\ Y\xrightarrow qX$, each edge having identity source incidence and the displayed target incidence. Project it onto the analogous graph on two terminal vertices. Collapsing the $p$-edge first computes its colimit as $T_{pq}$. Collapsing the $q$-edge first computes it as $T_{qp}$. This is associativity of colimits, or elimination of one vertex and its comparison path in the space of cocones. Each collapse in the base identifies the two-edge circle equivalently with $S^1$ and sends the marked vertex to its basepoint. This proves the fibre equivalence over the identified pointed bases without requiring a choice of homotopy of rotations over the circle. Consequently $Y\simeq1\times_{S^1}T_e$, and the latter belongs to $E$. Thus every retract in $K$ is represented in $E$.

2. **Functorial weak initiality.** In an idempotent-complete category $H$, a natural transformation $\mathrm{const}_M\to\mathrm{id}_H$ suffices for initiality of a retract of $M$. It gives a section of $\mathrm{Map}_H(M,-)\to1$, making the terminal covariant presheaf a retract of a corepresentable. The covariant Yoneda image is closed under retracts by idempotent completeness. Its object representing $1$ is initial. Here $H$ is idempotent complete because functor categories with idempotent-complete target are idempotent complete (split levelwise), and idempotent-complete categories are closed under limits in $\mathrm{Cat}_\infty$. Apply this to endomorphisms $\mathrm{Fun}(B\mathbb N,E)$ and then add a point by pullback along carrier evaluation of the coslice $1/E$. Coslices themselves are limits of arrow-category diagrams. This proves existence of an NNO solely from finite bicompleteness and (D). All the same constructions performed in slices give parametrized recursion.

### 3. The countermodel for both descent axioms

Let $E$ be the full, replete subcategory of $\prod_{i\ge1}\mathcal S^{[1]}$ consisting of sequences $x=(x_i:U_i\to V_i)$ such that for every fixed $k$, all sufficiently late $x_i$ are $k$-connected. Work with small spaces in a fixed external universe. All operations below concern ordinary homotopy limits and colimits.

**Closure and descent.** The terminal sequence belongs to $E$. A pullback of a diagram of three $k$-connected comparisons gives at least a $(k-1)$-connected comparison of pullbacks. For example, factor the comparison via base change at the two end objects and then compare the paths in the middle object. On those paths the connectivity loss is at most one, by the fibre sequence for the diagonal. A fixed finite number of pullbacks therefore preserves the defining condition. For a finite colimit, all entries of the diagram at coordinate $i$ are $k$-connected once $i$ exceeds a common bound. Their colimit is $k$-connected because $k$-connected maps form a left orthogonal class and such classes are closed under colimits in the arrow category. The same proof works for any indexing space $K$ with finitely many components. Choose one vertex per component to obtain the common bound, since transitions in $K$ act by equivalences. [Lurie, Higher Topos Theory, §6.5.1](https://www.math.ias.edu/~lurie/papers/HTT.pdf)

These limits and colimits are computed in the ambient presheaf category. There the slice descent equivalence restricts in both directions. Restriction takes pullbacks of objects in $E$. Its inverse takes the colimit of total objects of a cartesian diagram, which belongs to $E$ by the same closure argument. Fullness gives the assertion on mapping spaces. This proves (D), and also the full-slice groupoid clause of $(D_c)$ under either finiteness convention. Regard $E$ as a locally groupoidal $(\infty,2)$-category. Its cotensor by $[1]$ is the object itself, since evaluation on the arrow space of a groupoid is an equivalence. All maps between groupoids have cocartesian lifts up to equivalence, so take the specified class to be all maps. [Kerodon, Theorem 7.7.6.1](https://kerodon.net/tag/05VZ)

**Truncation.** Truncated objects and truncated maps of $E$ are detected at both vertices in every coordinate. The two represented evaluation functors have probes supported at that coordinate, empty elsewhere, and these probes belong to $E$. Conversely, mapping spaces of diagrams are limits of the corresponding mapping spaces. Pointwise $n$-truncation gives eventual equivalences of the transition arrows and satisfies the reflection property in $E$.

For the family clause of (T), first work over one arrow $y:U\to V$. A family of $n$-types is a pair $P_0/U,P_1/V$ with $h:P_0\to y^*P_1$ over $U$. For spaces, restriction along $U\to\tau_{\le n+1}U$ is an equivalence on categories of $n$-type families. Indeed each degree of the complete Segal nerve of the category of $n$-types is an $(n+1)$-type. Its loops are equivalences in the mapping space of a diagram of $n$-types, which is $n$-truncated. Mapping out of $U$ therefore factors through its truncation in every degree. Naturality in $y$ gives the same equivalence on the triples just described, including their maps.

It remains to show that the inverse takes families back into $E$. The maps $h_i$ are $n$-truncated, as maps between $n$-truncated objects over $U_i$. They are eventually $n$-connected as well. Their composites with $y_i^*P_{1,i}\to P_{1,i}$ are the total transition maps of $P$, and these composites and the second factors have connectivities tending to infinity. The fibre sequence gives the claim (use bounds $n$ and $n+1$ respectively). Thus $h_i$ is eventually an equivalence. Its descended comparison is then an equivalence at those indices, and the truncated base transitions are eventually equivalences too. The descended total transitions are consequently eventually equivalences. Pullback in the forward direction stays in $E$ by finite-limit closure. This proves (T).

**Two constructors.** Put $LX=X\times_{X\times X}X$, using the diagonal twice. In spaces this is $X^{S^1}$. Consider the signature
$$
                       A\longrightarrow X,\qquad LX\longrightarrow X.
$$
The recursive source is one finite pullback of finite products and both targets are the identity. In spaces its initial carrier at a coefficient $B$ is
$$
 W(B)=\coprod_{r\ge0} B^{(S^1)^r}.
$$
The first constructor includes the term of length zero. Connectedness of $S^1$ gives $L(\coprod_r Z_r)\simeq\coprod_r LZ_r$, so the second constructor includes the terms of the next length. For any target algebra, maps from the first summand and the path comparing the generators form a contractible space. At each next summand the comparison path specifies a map together with a path to its prescribed value. The extension space is again contractible. The inverse limit of this tower computes the full mapping space of strong algebra maps. The construction is natural in $B$, hence works pointwise on arrows, including the comparison along the coefficient arrow.

Choose $A_i=(*\to K(\mathbb F_2,i))$, with the zero basepoint. Its transition connectivity tends to infinity. Any initial algebra in $E$ must at each coordinate be the just-computed initial algebra of arrows. Indeed, put an arbitrary test algebra at that coordinate and the terminal algebra at all other coordinates. It belongs to $E$, and maps to it test precisely that coordinate. At length $r=i$ the source has a single component. The target components at that length are
$$
 [(S^1)^i,K(\mathbb F_2,i)]=H^i((S^1)^i;\mathbb F_2)=\mathbb F_2.
$$
The torus has one top cell and zero cellular differential, as follows by tensoring the cellular chains of the circle. The source maps to the zero class, and every other length maps into its own summand. Hence the transition misses a component for every $i$. The forced carrier does not belong to $E$.

One can check non-presentability independently. For every $i$ take the sequence supported at $i$ with arrow $\varnothing\to *$ there and initial arrows elsewhere. These objects belong to $E$. Testing a proposed coproduct against arbitrary arrows supported at a single coordinate, terminal elsewhere, forces its transition to be $\varnothing\to *$ at every coordinate. This coproduct cannot exist. In particular the obstruction above occurs in a genuinely non-presentable model. The constant sequence of identity arrows on the usual natural numbers is an NNO in this example, by pointwise recursion.

**An exact boundary inside the finite-limit syntax.** Take a connected nonempty finite cell space $K$ and the two constructors $A\to X$ and $X^K\to X$. Cotensor by such a $K$ is a finite-limit expression, by reversing its finite cell presentation. Under (D), these constructors have an initial model in every interpretation of $A$ **exactly when $K$ is contractible**. In that case the second constructor is unary. The natural operation $g(m,t)$ of §2 gives a strong map out of the algebra on $M\times A$ into every $(X,a:A\to X,f)$ by $(m,v)\mapsto g(m,a(v))$. The same idempotent-completeness and covariant Yoneda argument gives the initial algebra on generators $A$.

For the converse let $K$ be noncontractible. There exists a field $k$, a local system $V$ of $k$-vector spaces on $K$, and a nonzero class $c\in H^d(K;V)$ for some $d>0$. If $K$ is simply connected, Hurewicz, finite generation of its cellular homology and the universal coefficient theorem give such a class with constant coefficients. If $G=\pi_1 K$ is nontrivial, use the augmentation ideal of $k[G]$. For finite $G$ choose the characteristic to divide its order. For infinite $G$ any field works. The invariant elements of $k[G]$ have augmentation zero (in the infinite case the only invariant of finite support is zero). The coefficient connecting map therefore takes $1$ to a nonzero class in degree one.

Write $G=\pi_1 K$ in both cases. For each $i$ take as coefficient arrow the zero section over $B(G^i)$ of the Eilenberg–Mac Lane fibration with fibre $K(V^{\otimes i},di)$ and the external tensor product action of $G^i$. Its connectivity tends to infinity, since its homotopy fibre is the loop space of that fibre. Over the classifying map $K^i\to B(G^i)$, homotopy classes of sections of the pulled-back fibration are the cohomology classes with that local system. The external product $c^{\times i}$ is nonzero. Indeed the finite cellular chains of the universal cover are degreewise finite free over $k[G]$, so the local-coefficient cochains of the product identify with the tensor product of the cochain complexes, and the field Künneth formula applies. A lift representing this class cannot be freely homotopic to a zero section composed with any map to $B(G^i)$. Transport along any homotopy of base maps preserves the zero class. Replacing $S^1$ by $K$ in the length calculation above gives $\coprod_r B^{K^r}$ because $K$ is connected. At length $i$ the transition thus misses the nonzero component. This proves the claimed boundary in the very same model $E$.

In particular a decidable grammar recognizing exactly all uniformly valid finite presentations under (D) would decide contractibility of finite connected simplicial complexes. Connectedness is decidable and contractibility in this class is undecidable. This explains the use of a semantic criterion in §1. [Tancer, Appendix A, Theorem 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.6254#page=20)

### 4. Finitary W-types and free monoids

For the repository's finitary polynomials the answer under (D), (L) and $(\Omega)$ is **yes**, including arbitrary higher objects of labels. Write the polynomial as
$$
                    P(X)=\coprod_{j=1}^k A_j\times X^{d_j},
                    \qquad d_j\in\mathbb N .
$$
This is the definition used in [Ordinary mathematics, §§4.7–4.8](../../ordinary-math.md). The proof works more generally with a set $K$ of shapes, any set family $D\to K$ of directions, and an arbitrary label object $A\to K$, giving $P(X)=\sum_{a:A}X^{D_{k(a)}}$. The finite formula takes $K$ to be the finite set of constructor names.

The $0$-truncated objects of $E$ form an ordinary elementary topos, with an NNO. Indeed their finite limits, exponentials and subobject classifier are inherited, and Rasekh's Peano NNO is $0$-truncated. In an ordinary elementary topos with NNO, every set polynomial has a W-type. Apply this to $D\to K$ and call the set of shapes $W_0$. For $t:W_0$, its node set $V(t)$ consists of valid finite paths of directions from the root. Explicitly, pair a list of directions with a list of successive subtrees starting at $t$, requiring each direction to belong to the current constructor and to select the next subtree. The compatible list of subtrees, if it exists, is unique since the shapes and directions are sets. This definition uses ordinary internal lists and the constructor decomposition of $W_0$. Write $k(v)$ for the constructor at the node reached. [Rasekh, §§2–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.01734) and [Moerdijk and Palmgren, Proposition 3.6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-0072(00)00012-9)

In $E$ form the decorated tree object
$$
                    W=\sum_{t:W_0}\prod_{v:V(t)} A_{k(v)} .
$$
The dependent product exists by (L). Root and child decomposition gives the structure $P(W)\to W$. For an algebra $c:P(X)\to X$, define a run on a decorated tree to consist of a value of $X$ at every node and, at each node, a path from this value to $c$ applied to its label and its child values. This is one family of objects over $W$, defined using products and identity types. The assertion that every decoration of $t$ has contractible run space is a subobject of $W_0$ by (L). It is therefore an induction predicate in the ordinary topos of sets. Given the predicate for each child, a run is a product of the contractible child run spaces followed by the root value with its path to a prescribed value. Both are contractible. W-induction proves the assertion for all shapes.

This argument also works for a displayed algebra over $W$. At each node take a value in the fibre indexed by its decorated subtree. The structure of the displayed algebra prescribes the value from those at the children. Run contraction gives the section by evaluating at the root, with the constructor paths supplied by restriction to children and uniqueness of a run. All families here exist before applying induction, so the argument uses no recursion into an object of types. Dependent elimination implies homotopy initiality, including the higher paths between strong maps. [Awodey, Gambino and Sojakova, Theorem 5.10](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.05531)

For clarity, the free associative monoid assertion also holds for coherently associative monoids. Fix $A$ and write $L_A=\sum_{n:N}A^{\operatorname{Fin}(n)}$. The free algebra for the endofunctor $F(X)=A\times X$ on arbitrary generators $B$ has carrier $T(B)=L_A\times B$. This is the preceding tree construction for $B+A\times X$, with a word above one terminal label. Thus the forgetful functor on $F$-algebras has a left adjoint. It detects equivalences and creates underlying split simplicial colimits. A split augmented diagram has an absolute colimit, hence $F$ preserves it, and the mapping-space definition of an algebra lifts that colimit. Barr–Beck gives monadicity. Its monad $T$ is algebraically free on $F$ and therefore free as an associative algebra in endofunctors under composition. [Gepner, Haugseng and Kock, Proposition 4.2.9 and Corollary A.5.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.06469)

Augment $F$ to the identity by the projection. The resulting augmentation of $T$ reads the terminal label $b$. The strong monoidal functor $J$ sending $C$ to the augmented endofunctor $C\times(-)\to\mathrm{id}$ is fully faithful. Indeed the first coordinate of any natural map $C\times B\to D\times B$ over $B$ is determined by its value at $B=1$, by naturality of $B\to1$. Thus the coherent monad structure on the augmented $T=J(L_A)$ gives a coherent monoid structure on $L_A$. For any monoid $M$, take the fibre of the free-monad mapping equivalence at the given augmentation $F\to\mathrm{id}$. Full faithfulness of $J$ identifies this fibre equivalence with
$$
                \operatorname{Map}_{\rm Mon}(L_A,M)
                    \simeq\operatorname{Map}_E(A,UM).
$$
In particular this calculation does not require an externally indexed countable coproduct of powers of $A$.

The finite-power convention matters. A finite cover of an arbitrary higher parameter object need not be pulled back from a set of constructor names, and an exponent by a compact space can even have positive homotopy dimension. The finite polynomial claim made in the repository uses ordinary powers of specified finite arity.

### 5. Restricted descent and the natural numbers

There are two distinctions in Question 2. The stated axiom allows a specified class $\mathcal C$. Requiring it to consist of **all** cocartesian fibrations is stronger. Also, a finite groupoid in the usual group-theoretic sense may have finite automorphism groups, whereas a space with a finite cell presentation may have infinite fundamental group. With the literal class axiom and finite groupoids in the former sense, **an NNO need not exist**. This holds even if the groupoid clause covers every $\pi$-finite space. The counterexample in §3 already settles the general signature question in both senses.

**A model without an NNO.** Fix a nonprincipal ultrafilter $\mathcal U$ on the positive integers. Take $G=\prod_i\mathcal S$ and the functor to ordinary spaces
$$
 F(x)=\mathop{\rm colim}_{J\in\mathcal U}\prod_{i\in J}x_i
$$
with transition maps given by restriction. This functor preserves finite limits, truncations and the initial object. For truncations use the product formula for homotopy groups and components, then the corresponding formula for filtered colimits. We work in classical external mathematics. For any constant $\pi$-finite space $K$, the diagonal $K\to F(\mathrm{const}\,K)$ is an equivalence. This follows on components and all based homotopy groups since an ultraproduct of a fixed finite set is that set. Moreover $F$ preserves **all colimits indexed by such a $K$**. Realize a diagram in each coordinate as the fibres of its total space over $K$, and apply $F$ to the total spaces. The resulting space over $F(\mathrm{const}\,K)\simeq K$ has exactly the required fibres by finite-limit preservation. For a general finite diagram the comparison from the colimit of the images onto the image of the colimit is at least surjective on components. Each coordinate of a point is in the component of some diagram vertex, and there are only finitely many vertices from which to choose.

Let $H$ be the ordinary category of sequences of bounded join-semilattices that are one-element semilattices outside a finite set of indices. Maps preserve joins and both bounds. Enrich its hom sets by pointwise order. It has finite limits and every colimit of nonempty shape, computed coordinatewise. For the latter, choose any input vertex. At every coordinate where that input is trivial its two bounds coincide, so they coincide in the colimit. Its cotensor by $[1]$ has at each coordinate the ordered pairs $a\le b$, with pointwise operations. Write $t$ for its terminal sequence.

Take $E$ to have objects $(x,P)$ for $x\in G$ and a local system $P:Fx\to H$. A map to $(y,Q)$ consists of $f:x\to y$ and a transformation $P\to (Ff)^*Q$. Use the pointwise order enrichment on comparisons over a fixed $f$, with equivalences of base maps acting by transport. More explicitly, the mapping category is the family over the space $\operatorname{Map}_G(x,y)$ whose fibre at $f$ is the poset of these transformations. Composition is substitution and pointwise composition. The cotensor of $(y,Q)$ by $[1]$ is $(y,Q^{[1]})$ pointwise. Indeed an arrow in the mapping category can be transported along its base equivalence to a comparison between transformations over the same $f$. A square is then a pointwise square in those posets.

Finite limits are computed on bases and by limits of the pulled-back labels. For a colimit with base $y=\operatorname{colim}x_j$, the label at $v\in Fy$ is the colimit of all labels at pairs $(j,u)$ with $u\in Fx_j$ equipped with a path from its image to $v$. Morphisms are those of the diagram of spaces with the path compatibility. This is the left Kan extension formula, as one sees by mapping the result into a test label. The indexing category is nonempty at every $v$ for a nonempty finite diagram by surjectivity on components. At the empty colimit there is no $v$. The same formula works for $\pi$-finite groupoid indices.

The groupoidal objects are exactly $(x,t)$, meaning terminal labels everywhere. In one direction every comparison into $t$ is unique. For the converse choose a point of $Fx$ with a label having distinct bounds in some coordinate. Represent the point by a map $w\to x$, with $w_i=*$ on an ultrafilter-large set and empty elsewhere, so $Fw=*$ up to equivalence. Use a probe with the free bounded join-semilattice on one generator at the offending coordinate and the given label at the others. Sending that generator to the two bounds gives a strict inequality of transformations over the same base map. Equivalences in a mapping category over a space are detected after transport to a single fibre. This arrow in the mapping category is noninvertible.

Take $\mathcal C$ to be the maps with invertible label comparison. These contain all maps of groupoidal objects, are stable under composition and pullback, and their slice over $(x,P)$ is equivalent to $G_{/x}$. Hence their finite descent comparison is an equivalence by descent in $G$. For universality, pulling a colimit back along such a map changes only its base and restricts its label. The bases satisfy universal colimits in $G$. In the formula at a new point $v'$, the points of each pulled-back input and their paths to $v'$ give the same indexing category as the old formula at the image of $v'$, by left exactness of $F$. This proves universality and also shows that cartesian families glue by their ordinary colimit. For a $\pi$-finite groupoid index $K$, there is full descent. Given any compatible family over the base diagram, first glue the bases in $G$. Applying $F$ preserves both this $K$-colimit and the cartesian squares. Local systems on the resulting total space glue uniquely because $\operatorname{Fun}(-,H)$ takes space colimits to limits of categories. The label comparisons glue by the same statement on morphisms. Equivalently, at each total point the Kan extension formula transports its label by equivalences over a contractible category of representatives. This verifies both clauses of $(D_c)$.

To verify (T), observe that projection to $G$ preserves finite limits, hence preserves truncated maps by the iterated diagonal criterion. Mapping-space fibres over base maps are discrete sets of transformations. It follows that an object over $(y,t)$ is relatively $n$-truncated for $n\ge0$ exactly when its base map is. For $n=-1$ it must also have terminal labels. Indeed the label of its diagonal is $P\to P\times P$, which is invertible precisely for one-element semilattices. Absolute $n$-truncation for $n\ge0$ takes base $\tau_{\le n}x$ and left Kan extends the labels along $Fx\to F(\tau_{\le n}x)$. Every point of the target has a nonempty fibre, and the Kan extension adjunction proves the reflection property. For $n=-1$ take $(\tau_{\le-1}x,t)$. For groupoidal $(y,t)$ and $n\ge0$, descend an $n$-truncated base family to $x'\to\tau_{\le n+1}y$ coordinatewise in spaces, as in §3. Then $Fx\to Fx'$ is a pullback of $Fy\to\tau_{\le n+1}Fy$ and induces an equivalence on $1$-truncations. Local systems in the ordinary category $H$, including their transformations, descend uniquely across that map. At $n=-1$ only the base mono remains. This proves the claimed equivalence of truncated families.

Finally, projection to $G$ has the terminal-label inclusion as right adjoint, also for pointed endomorphisms. A putative NNO must therefore have base the coordinatewise ordinary $N$. The space $FN$ is the discrete ultrapower of the natural numbers. The class $\alpha=[i]$ has arbitrarily many predecessors. Its two-sided successor orbit $O$ is disjoint from zero, and $O$ and its complement are closed under successor. Let $P$ be the proposed label at $\alpha$, and choose an index outside its finite support. Make a test algebra over the same base $N$ with label the two-element bounded semilattice at that index on $O$, and trivial labels everywhere else. Identity comparisons give the successor and the zero has its required point. The only base algebra map is the identity. At $\alpha$ a lift would have to preserve distinct bounds under a map from a one-element semilattice to a two-element semilattice. There is no such map. This proves failure of the literal restricted NNO assertion.

**When the NNO implication holds.** Here are the additional positive implications, including the intended cocartesian reading. In an $(\infty,2)$-category with $[1]$-cotensors, $1$ and $Z=1\times_S1$ are representably groupoidal for any $S$ and any chosen point. For the terminal object, the two evaluations $1^{[1]}\rightrightarrows1$ are equivalent. For every test $V$ this makes source and target evaluation on the arrow category of $\operatorname{Hom}_2(V,1)$ naturally equivalent. The square from the identity of the source to any arrow $f$ then shows $f$ invertible. For $Z$, the comparison of mapping categories to the pullback of the mapping categories into $1,S,1$ induces an equivalence on cores by the ordinary pullback property. Its target is a groupoid since the two outer categories are groupoids. Apply this comparison at $V=Z^{[1]}$ to the universal arrow between evaluations. The images of its endpoints are equivalent, so the evaluations themselves are equivalent by the equivalence on cores. The same square argument proves the claim.

Take $S=1\otimes S^1$ and write $b:1\to S$. In fact $b\in\mathcal C$. Its descent data over the circle diagram has fibre $Z\to1$, which belongs to $\mathcal C$. Glue these data to $B\to S$ in $\mathcal C$. The colimit of the total diagram has a canonical comparison $p:B\to b$ in the slice. Effectiveness makes $b^*p$ an equivalence. By the adjunction for the point, maps from $b$ in the slice correspond to points of the fibre. Lift the point representing $1_b$ along $b^*p$ to get $t:b\to B$ with $pt\simeq1_b$. Then $b^*(tp)$ is an equivalence, and descent detects equivalences between objects of $\mathcal C$ over $S$, so $tp$ is an equivalence as well. Consequently $B\simeq b$. In particular $\operatorname{coeq}(1_Z,S_Z)=1$, where $S_Z$ denotes the shift.

* If $\mathcal C$ is all cocartesian fibrations, every map to a representably groupoidal base is cocartesian. Representably, lifting an equivalence uses an inverse and has contractible space of lifts in the usual equivalence-invariant comma formulation. Thus $\mathcal C_{/1}=E$ and the slice of allowed objects over $S$ is the category of all automorphisms in $E$. Its object $b$ represents evaluation at zero as in §2. Pullback along $T\to1$ preserves slice colimits, so this gives the same parametrized integer recursion in every product family used there. The orbit diagram on $Z$ with colimit $1$ now has full descent since its vertices and colimit are groupoidal. The graph colimit in §2 needs pullback preservation along the shift (an equivalence) and at $0:1\to Z$ (in $\mathcal C$). Its other displayed vertex and edge maps are restrictions of a cocone, which always exist. The idempotent argument needs $X\times S\simeq\operatorname{coeq}(1_X,1_X)$, supplied by universality along $X\to1$. This proves an NNO in the intended reading.
* If finite indexing spaces include the finite cell space $B\mathbb Z=S^1$, the same conclusion holds for the literal class. Both the circle diagram and the shift orbit are colimits indexed by $B\mathbb Z$, hence have full descent. A colimit of a free-loop diagram with invertible transition computes the colimit of the corresponding $B\mathbb Z$-action, by the mapping-space equalizer formula. These full van Kampen colimits supply parametrized integer recursion, orbit descent and the product formula for the idempotent argument. The point $0:1\to Z$ belongs to $\mathcal C$ by groupoidality, giving the remaining graph base change.
* With (L) and $(\Omega)$ the finite-arity W-types and free monoids of §4 exist even for the literal $\pi$-finite restricted axiom. Here integer recursion on $Z$ is available for automorphisms of **groupoidal** objects by $b\in\mathcal C$ and the restricted descent equivalence. For groupoidal parameters $T$ it is also available on product families by applying the argument over $T$. Indeed products preserve colimits by (L), the new point is a pullback of $b$, and the family maps in question are pullbacks of maps to $1$ in $\mathcal C$. The sign construction uses pointed finite coproducts of $Z$. To see that these are groupoidal, first identify $2=1+1$ with the loops of $1\otimes B C_2$. Full finite-group descent glues the regular two-element $C_2$-set, with total object $1$ because the translation action groupoid is contractible. For pointed groupoidal $A,B$ the evaluations of $(A+B)^{[1]}$ have equivalent gradings in $2$. Split their common domain by this grading using extensivity. On the first summand apply the retraction $A+B\to A$ to the universal arrow and then include again. This gives an equivalence between the two evaluations on this summand. The retraction to $B$ does the same on the second. Hence the evaluations are equivalent, which proves groupoidality by the square argument above.

  Thus the signs and the difference function of §2 exist. Form the same graph and the shift equivalences. By (L) the locus where $j_u:X\to Q_u$ is an equivalence is a subobject of $Z$, defined by contractibility of all fibres. Its classifier $Z\to\Omega$ is shift invariant, hence factors through the shift coequalizer $1$. At zero it is true, so $j$ is an equivalence globally. This replaces full orbit descent in §2. The idempotent splitting uses only the product formula, which follows from (L). This constructs the NNO. For its use in §4 it is a set. Indeed initiality gives dependent induction by taking the total algebra, and $N\simeq1+N$ by Lambek's lemma. Double recursion gives the Boolean equality code on $N$, true on the two zeros, false on mixed constructors and unchanged on two successors. Double induction gives a path from any true code and a true code from any path, using reflexivity at zero and congruence under successor. This gives decidable equality and hence $0$-truncation by Hedberg's theorem. The $0$-truncated objects form an ordinary elementary topos by finite limits, exponentials and the classifier. The proof of §4 now applies.

The separating examples concern the abstract axioms. In the intended presentable category of higher categories the presentability result in the question already supplies the initial models.
