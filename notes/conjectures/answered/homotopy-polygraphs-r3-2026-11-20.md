# Which polygraphs are homotopy polygraphs? An exact criterion in every dimension

*A self-contained problem. It assumes familiarity with polygraphs (computads), strict ω-categories and (∞,ω)-categories, and nothing else. Everything under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked, except item 17, which is marked as evidence.*

**What we are asking for.** An exact characterisation of finite homotopy polygraphs, valid in every dimension. Bubble-freeness in every dimension (§1) is necessary (item 14) and is sufficient in dimension 2 (item 5), but it is **not** sufficient in dimension 3 (item 18): idle overlaps between top generators create circles and higher spheres in the fibres. We want the correct extra condition and a proof that it, together with bubble-freeness, is sufficient in every dimension. Isolated low-dimensional examples are of little use to us.

## 1. Setting

- A **polygraph** Γ (computad) is a set of generators of each dimension, each n-generator having a source and a target which are parallel (n−1)-cells of the free strict ω-category on the lower generators. All polygraphs here are finite.
- `F(Γ)` is the free strict ω-category on Γ.
- `Free(Γ)` is the free (∞,ω)-category on Γ: generators are attached one at a time along their boundaries by pushouts of `∂Dⁿ → Dⁿ` in (∞,ω)-categories (Loubaton's Cat_ω, arXiv 2406.05425, or Campion's framework, arXiv 2311.00200). Strict ω-categories are (∞,ω)-categories, so there is a canonical comparison `c_Γ : Free(Γ) → F(Γ)`.
- Γ is a **homotopy polygraph** (or *homotopical*) when c_Γ is an equivalence, i.e. when every strict pushout attaching a generator is also a homotopy pushout.
- A 2-cell `1_x ⇒ 1_x` of F(Γ) other than the identity is a **bubble**.
- Γ is **positive in every dimension** when every generator of dimension n ≥ 2 has a source and a target that are not identity cells. (For n = 2: both boundary paths are nonempty.)
- More generally, a non-identity n-cell `e : 1^{n−1−k}_c ⇒ 1^{n−1−k}_c` (n ≥ 2, source and target both the (n−1)-dimensional iterated identity of a k-cell c, k ≤ n−2) is an **iterated-identity endo-cell**. Γ is **bubble-free in every dimension** when F(Γ) has none. For n = 2, k = 0 these are the bubbles.
- A strict ω-functor p : C → D is a **discrete Conduché functor** when every factorisation of p(x) in D lifts uniquely to a factorisation of x in C.

## 2. Why it matters

Homotopy polygraphs are exactly the computads whose free (∞,ω)-category can be computed strictly. Finite presentations of Grothendieck constructions over a free base, the comparison of weak and strict free objects in type theories for weak ω-categories, and pasting theorems for non-globular shapes all need this hypothesis, and at present each use checks it by hand. Dimension 2 is settled exactly (item 5 below), necessity of bubble-freeness is settled in every dimension (item 14), and bubble-freeness alone is not sufficient from dimension 3 (item 18).

## 3. What is known

1. **Pasting theorems.** For a strongly loop-free Steiner complex K, ν(K) is the free (∞,ω)-category on its atoms (Campion, *Pasting diagrams in higher categories are free*, arXiv 2311.00200). Chanavat (*A strengthened (∞,n)-categorical pasting theorem*, arXiv 2603.22242) extends this to regular directed complexes with frame-acyclic molecules (Thm 3.17), and deduces that positive 2-polygraphs (Cor. 3.39) and regular 3-polygraphs in Henry's sense (Cor. 3.38) are homotopical. Comment 3.40 there describes the extension to arbitrary regular and positive polygraphs as expected, without proof.
2. **Bubbles obstruct.** If F(Γ) has a bubble q, sending every 2-generator to 1 gives r : F(Γ) → B²ℕ with r(q) = k > 0, and q defines i : B²ℕ → F(Γ) by Eckmann–Hilton; ri = [k] acts by kʲ on H_{2j}(K(ℤ,2); ℚ), so the groupoidification of F(Γ) has nonzero rational homology in every even degree, while that of Free(Γ) is a finite CW complex. Example: (x; σ : 1_x ⇒ 1_x), F(Γ) = B²ℕ ≃ K(ℤ,2) after groupoidification, Free(Γ) ≃ S².
3. **Bubble-freeness is undecidable**, already for one-vertex 2-polygraphs with no generating bubble (reduction from reachability in positive semi-Thue systems via an insertion rule 1 ⇒ L w Z and a deletion rule L v Z ⇒ 1).
4. **Dimension 2, combinatorial core.** For a progressive one-colour string diagram G every component of which meets the boundary, the cell complex K_G whose vertices are the *tier encodings* of G (decompositions into layers of simultaneous generators) and whose faces are products of permutohedra (splitting a layer) has contractible components. Proof: right-exchange runs (which terminate for boundary-connected diagrams, Delpeuch–Vicary arXiv 1804.07832, Cor. 2.16) give a unique maximal cell F(v) with source v; deleting vertices in topological order removes a closed star along its antistar, a ball. Two floating scalars give a circle, so the boundary condition is needed.
5. **Dimension 2, exact answer.** A finite 2-polygraph is homotopical **iff it is bubble-free**. The "if" direction uses item 4 and Heidemann's mesh model of (∞,2)-categories (arXiv 2411.04870): a presheaf X_Γ on meshes with L₂ j*X_Γ ≃ Free(Γ), and an objectwise equivalence j*X_Γ → N₂F(Γ) computed as X_Γ(Q) ≃ B(Q↓t) ≃ ⊔_G BP_G over the diagrams G of shape Q, with each BP_G contractible by item 4 (a bubble-free polygraph has no boundary-disjoint diagram component and no diagram automorphisms). Consequences: homotopicality of finite 2-polygraphs is undecidable (item 3); the finite case of Guetta's conjecture (homologically coherent ⟺ bubble-free, *Homology of strict ω-categories*, arXiv 2104.12662, Conj. 6.6.5) holds; every 2-polygraph whose 2-generators all have non-empty target (or all non-empty source) is homotopical, with any number of vertices.
6. **Suspensions.** Σ^k Γ is homotopical iff Γ is (Hom_{ΣC}(0,1) ≃ C, and suspension preserves and detects the comparison). So for 2-polygraphs, Σ^k Γ is homotopical iff Γ is bubble-free.
7. **Graph assembly, every dimension.** For a finite directed graph Q (loops and parallel edges allowed) and finite polygraphs P_e on its edges, the polygraph B_Q(P_•) with vertices Q₀ and (r+1)-generators ⊔_e (P_e)_r (the free category on Q enriched in the F(P_e)) is homotopical iff every P_e is. In particular **adjoining a 1-generator preserves homotopicality in every dimension**.
8. **Discrete Conduché descent (Theorem A).** If p : Γ → Δ is a polygraph map with F(p) discrete Conduché and Δ homotopical, then Γ is homotopical. Surjectivity on generators alone is not enough ([1] → F(loop)).
9. **Typing into positive templates (Theorem B).** A polygraph map Γ → Δ into a homotopical Δ that is source-positive or target-positive in every dimension makes Γ homotopical (Henry's cartesianness for such polygraphs makes F(p) discrete Conduché).
10. **Mapping cylinders (Theorem C).** Strict double mapping cylinders of finite homotopical presentations along arbitrary strict functors have finite homotopical presentations, with generators Δ_n ⊔ ℰ_n ⊔ Γ_{n−1}; Gray cylinders, cones and suspensions are instances (Loubaton arXiv 2406.05425 Thm 2.3.3.22, Campion's pasting theorem for Dⁿ ⊗ [1]). Items 7–10 give a sound finite-certificate system, but typing into templates from a good class cannot leave that class, so it does not reach all positive polygraphs.
11. **Positivity is not necessary.** (∗; t; η : 1 ⇒ t) is homotopical (Blans–Linskens, arXiv 2605.03150), and so is its suspension, a non-positive 3-polygraph. Regularity is not necessary either: the positive non-regular 3-polygraph x, y, z; p, q : x → y, r : y → z; α, β : p ⇒ q; ζ : α ▷ r ⇛ β ▷ r is homotopical.
12. **Positivity in the top dimension alone is not enough.** One vertex, b : 1 ⇒ 1, Ω : b ⇛ b: Ω has non-identity boundaries, but b is a bubble.
13. **Strict versus "stricter".** Chanavat (*Homotopy theory of stricter n-categories*, arXiv 2509.26563, Comment 2.67) gives a 4-dimensional regular directed complex whose molecules do not form a polygraph under strict composition. This is **not** a counterexample to the positive case: the extra equations appear in the passage from the free strict category to molecules, not in the free strict category itself.

14. **Higher bubbles obstruct in every dimension.** If a nonidentity n-cell is an endomorphism of an iterated identity on any k-cell, k ≤ n − 2, then the strict n-skeleton has nonzero mod-p cohomology in arbitrarily high degrees for some prime p. The free cellular n-skeleton has cohomological dimension at most n, and homotopicality passes to skeleta. This covers composite bubbles and positive-dimensional bases. (Proof: the inclusion of the n-skeleton is discrete Conduché, so homotopicality passes to it; counting n-generators gives r : F(Γ_{≤n}) → Bⁿℤ, the endo-cell q gives i : Σ^{n−2}B²ℕ → F(Γ_{≤n}) with r i of degree m = r(q) > 0 on the bottom class; for an odd prime p ∤ m the iterated reduced powers P^{p^{j−1}}⋯P^1 of the pulled-back fundamental class restrict to m σ^{n−2}(x^{p^j}) ≠ 0.) So bubble-freeness in every dimension is necessary.

15. **Groupoidification does not recognise homotopicality from dimension 3.** Π = (x; a : 1_x ⇒ 1_x; ε : a ⇛ 1_{1_x}) is not homotopical (its 2-skeleton has the bubble a, and homotopicality passes to skeleta), yet both groupoidifications are contractible. Free(Π) groupoidifies to S² ∪_{deg 1} D³. In F(Π) the hom on 1_{1_x} is the free commutative monoid in Cat on (a, ε : a → 0), whose morphisms reduce to ε^{⊗k} ⊗ 1_{a^n}, so it is the poset (ℕ, ≥) with terminal object 0. Π is also homologically coherent. Suspensions give such examples in every dimension ≥ 3.
16. **Positive-to-one polygraphs are homotopical.** If every d-generator (d ≥ 2) has a generating (d−1)-cell as target and a non-identity source, Γ is homotopical. The proof: Zawadowski (arXiv 0708.2658, Cor. 13.5) identifies such polygraphs with presheaves on positive opetopes, which are acyclic atoms forming a full sieve (Leclerc; Hadzihasanovic); then Chanavat's Cor. 3.27 applies (directed complexes with acyclic atoms generate homotopy polygraphs). The same holds after choosing, independently in each dimension, source or target as the generator side (globular opposites). This gives a decidable sufficient test.
17. **The combinatorial core in dimension 3 (evidence, not proved).** Following item 4 one dimension up, fix a 3-dimensional diagram G (a strict 3-cell of F(Γ) together with a representing composite). Its *3-tier sequences* (decompositions into layers of simultaneous 3-steps) are ordered by refinement; call the poset Π_G. When no 3-step has an idle boundary wire (every wire in the source region of a step is touched by it), Π_G appears to be the poset of braid-fan cells inside an open convex cone cut out by a partial order on the 3-steps, hence contractible: checked by computer on about 550 random diagrams of that kind. The step "two 3-steps that can each fire first can fire together" fails in general (item 18). The true fibre also contains *interchange slices* (2-dimensional exchanges between 3-steps), which make it an infinite category. Truncating at K interchange slices is not homotopy-correct (K = 1 already gives a wedge of circles).

18. **Bubble-free does not suffice in dimension 3.** Objects 0 → 1 → 2 → 3, edges f₀, f₁ : 0 → 1, g : 1 → 2, h₀, h₁ : 2 → 3, 2-generators α₀, α₁ : f₀ ⇒ f₁, β₀, β₁ : h₀ ⇒ h₁, 3-generators A : α₀g ⇛ α₁g and B : gβ₀ ⇛ gβ₁. Γ is positive in every dimension, has an acyclic 1-graph and is bubble-free in every dimension (occurrence counts), but the fibre of Free(Γ) over the unique strict 3-cell α₀gβ₀ ⇛ α₁gβ₁ is S¹: the hom from 0 to 3 is (D × Q) ⊔_{Q×Q} (Q × D), with Q the walking parallel pair and D the walking 2-cell. In item 17's terms, A and B overlap on the idle wire g, so each can fire first but they cannot fire together, and the two serial orders are joined by two different exchanges. Further known facts for positive 3-polygraphs (all generating faces non-identity):
    - If no idle right suffix of a generating 3-face equals an idle left prefix of a generating 3-face, every fibre is contractible, so Γ is homotopical. For width-preserving generators this matching test is exact.
    - Every weight-two fibre is a point, an interval or a circle, and a circle occurs exactly when some path is an idle suffix of **both** faces of one generator and an idle prefix of **both** faces of another.
    - For every k ≥ 2 there is a positive 3-polygraph with a full-support fibre ≃ S^{k−1} ("seam rings") whose weight-two fibres are all contractible, so no bounded-weight test is exact.

## 4. Questions

**Question 1 (main: exact criterion in every dimension).** Find a condition C on finite polygraphs such that Γ is homotopical iff F(Γ) is bubble-free in every dimension and Γ satisfies C, and prove it in every dimension. C must reduce to "nothing" in dimension 2 and must exclude item 18's idle overlaps and seam rings. A natural candidate is: no fibre contains a cycle of idle overlaps between top-dimensional steps, suitably defined for n-steps (overlaps along idle lower-dimensional sub-diagrams, not only wires). State whether the resulting criterion is decidable relative to bubble-freeness (bubble-freeness itself is undecidable, item 3).

The expected route generalises item 5's method:
- a mesh or Θ-presheaf model of the free (∞,n)-category (Heidemann's diagrammatic computads give free (∞,n)-categories in all dimensions), with the value over each shape a disjoint union, over the strict cells of that shape, of fibres;
- a reduction of the fibre over a fixed strict n-cell, including interchange slices (item 17), to a finite complex built from n-tier sequences and exchanges between serial orders;
- an identification of that complex, under C, with a contractible one (for example cells of the braid fan in a convex cone, as in item 17), and without C an explicit non-trivial cycle.

Any other route is equally welcome.

**Question 2 (fallback: positive polygraphs in every dimension).** For polygraphs positive in every dimension, extend the dimension-3 sufficient condition of item 18 (no idle suffix of a face matching an idle prefix of a face) to every dimension, and prove it sufficient. Ideally, prove that a positive polygraph is homotopical exactly when its fibres contain no seam ring, with a finite test if one exists. Item 16 (positive-to-one) is a special case to recover.

**Question 3 (last fallback: dimension 3).** Give the exact criterion for finite 3-polygraphs, with non-positive generators allowed. Item 18 covers the positive case under a width hypothesis only.

## 5. What counts as a solution

A proof of Question 1, 2 or 3; a counterexample to a proposed criterion with a proof of non-homotopicality (for example by a homology computation as in item 2); or a published reference.

# Researcher Response 1

## An exact criterion using strict labellings

There is an all-dimensional condition on ordinary categories built from the strict cells. In dimension three it has a smaller form, a finite complex of **possible last simultaneous steps** for each strict cell. Both tests distinguish two exchanges with the same endpoints. The nested-necklace test gives a $\Pi^0_2$ bound. The last-step test also gives a sufficient condition for positive polygraphs in every dimension. The all-dimensional test uses the necklace formula of Barkan and Steinebrunner.

Fix a rank $n\ge3$. Put $B=F(\Gamma_{<n})$, $D=F(\Gamma_{\le n})$, and write $R=N D$ for the rectangular strict $n$-fold nerve. Its indices run from the outermost direction to the innermost. For example, a rectangle with all indices 1 selects an $n$-cell with its boundaries. Form the following **set-valued** rectangular array:
$$
 P=N B\amalg_{\coprod_z N\partial D^n}\coprod_z ND^n ,
 \qquad z\in\Gamma_n .
$$
The boundary maps use the specified strict faces in $B$. Each entry of $P$ has a strict label in $R$. Call a label *old* when it lies in $N B$. One can recognize it by summing the numbers of generating $n$-cells in the elementary $n$-slots of the rectangle. The sum is zero exactly for old labels. If any index is zero, every label is old by globularity. These counts add when rectangular rows are composed along any direction, since the common face has dimension less than $n$.

The following recursion constructs ordinary categories $A_j(\mathbf q)$ over the discrete sets $R(1^j,\mathbf q)$, where $1^j$ means $j$ entries equal to 1. It is functorial in the remaining indices $\mathbf q$.

1. Take $A_0(\mathbf q)$ to be the discrete category $P(\mathbf q)$.
2. At stage $j$, with $1\le j\le n$, take a finite necklace $K=\Delta^{a_1}\vee\cdots\vee\Delta^{a_t}$, joined end to start. Use the ordinary category $\mathrm{Nec}$ of necklaces and endpoint-preserving simplicial maps, including the point necklace. Decorate each bead by $A_{j-1}(a_i,\mathbf q)$, matching its two joints over $A_{j-1}(0,\mathbf q)$. The category $M(K)$ of decorations is the iterated fibre product of these bead categories over the joint category. Use the joint category itself for the point. Equivalently this is evaluation of the simplicial category-valued diagram on the simplicial set $K$ by limits. Thus every necklace map acts by restriction, including maps that collapse beads.
3. Form the ordinary Grothendieck construction of $M:\mathrm{Nec}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Cat}$. Its objects are decorated necklaces. An arrow consists of a necklace restriction and a morphism from the restricted decoration to the indicated new one. Label each object by composing the strict long edges of its beads in direction $j$. Restrictions and decoration morphisms preserve that label. Retain the entire category over each new label. Over each old label use the one-object, one-arrow category. Their disjoint union is $A_j(\mathbf q)$. This prescription is functorial because restriction in a remaining direction takes old labels to old labels. In particular every joint category used in step 2 is discrete.

For an $n$-cell $c$ let $A(c)$ be the summand at $c$ of $A_n(())$. Define condition $C$ by the following tests for every rank $3\le n\le\dim\Gamma$ and every new $c$ in that rank.

* The underlying unoriented graph of $A(c)$ is nonempty and connected. Every closed edge word reduces to the empty word by identity, inverse cancellation and the triangle relations $[g][f]=[gf]$, allowing the relations in both directions.
* Every integral normalized nerve cycle of positive degree is a boundary. The degree-$i$ generators are strings of $i$ composable nonidentity arrows. The differential is the alternating sum of deleting the first or last arrow and composing adjacent arrows, with degenerate terms zero.

**Theorem.** A finite polygraph is homotopical if and only if it is bubble-free in every dimension and satisfies $C$. In particular $C$ is empty in dimension two. Its input consists entirely of strict cells, finite necklaces, their maps and ordinary categorical incidence data.

**Proof.** Fix a skeletal attachment. The array $P$ computes the presheaf homotopy pushout objectwise because the globe-boundary inclusions are monomorphisms of sets, even when an attaching map identifies faces. It is reduced in the globular sense: on a zero index the directions after that index are constant. Its reflection into $n$-fold Segal spaces therefore computes the flagged cellular pushout. Barkan and Steinebrunner's Theorem A computes degree 1 of one-direction Segalification as the space-colimit over $\mathrm{Nec}^{\mathrm{op}}$ of the spaces of bead decorations. Their Proposition 2.16 applies the one-direction reflections in order $1,\ldots,n$ to any reduced input. The finite-rank calculation also gives the pushout in $(\infty,\omega)$-categories: the inclusion of $(\infty,n)$-categories has a right adjoint taking the maximal subcategory with invertible cells above rank $n$, hence preserves colimits. [Segalification and the Boardman–Vogt tensor product](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.08650) · [Campion, introductory remark on the category number](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.00200)

Write $Y_j$ for the result of the first $j$ reflections, and $\mathrm BA$ for the classifying space of an ordinary category. Inductively there is a natural equivalence over the strict labels
$$
  \mathrm B A_j(\mathbf q)\simeq Y_j(1^j,\mathbf q).
$$
It holds at $j=0$. In the necklace formula for the next reflection, all bead pullbacks are over the discrete set of strict joints. Classifying space preserves products and disjoint unions, hence these pullbacks. The classifying space of the Grothendieck construction computes the required homotopy colimit by Thomason's theorem. It remains to justify the old-label instruction in step 3. If the composite label is old, every bead has old long edge by additivity. Every elementary slot of that bead is then old, by additivity again. Over these labels the preceding step has exactly the discrete old diagram. The necklace formula restricted to them is consequently the necklace formula for the *strict Segal nerve of $B$* itself. Its augmentation to the discrete set of old edges is an equivalence. Sending each old component to its label thus induces an equivalence naturally in $\mathbf q$. This proves the induction, including its assertion at a zero index. [Thomason, Homotopy colimits in the category of small categories](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100055535)

The output $Y_n$ is already complete. Here are the details of the flag check. On lower globes the input agrees with $NB$, and the same computation with a remaining zero index gives their original discrete sets. The Segal conditions and globularity recover rectangles by iterated products over these lower globes. Hence the part over old strict rectangles agrees up to equivalence with $NB$ in every multidegree: on top slots this is the induction just proved, and the intersections of slots are lower flags. The strict free nerve is gaunt. In particular an equivalence in any globular hom projects to an identity in $R$. In top degree every such identity has weight zero and its entire fibre is contractible. At lower degrees the flag sets are already discrete and the identity flags are exactly the old ones. Thus the degeneracies identify each object space with its equivalence space in the successive completeness tests. Equivalently, in a directional row an invertible strict arrow must be a directional identity: its top counts and those of its inverse sum to zero, and descending through the faces gives the same assertion down to the direction of composition. Completeness therefore gives the same cellular pushout in the complete model. The complete rectangular model and the globular model present the same $(\infty,n)$-categories. [Haugseng, On the equivalence between $\Theta_n$-spaces and iterated Segal spaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.08480)

It follows that this strict skeletal attachment is a homotopy pushout precisely when each $\mathrm B A(c)$ is weakly contractible. Indeed its comparison is the identity up to equivalence on lower globes, and on the top globe it is the disjoint union of the augmentations to the strict labels. Globes detect equivalences here by the iterated Segal decompositions. The edge and triangle presentation above computes the fundamental groupoid of a nerve. Its normalized chains compute integral homology. For a connected simply connected CW complex, acyclicity implies weak contractibility by the first nonzero homotopy group form of Hurewicz. This proves exactly the two stated incidence tests. Item 14 supplies necessity of bubble-freeness, item 5 handles the two-skeleton, and item 7 handles the graph. Induction up the finite list of skeletal attachments proves the theorem. In particular the circle and spheres of item 18 violate $C$: the circle has a nontrivial edge word and each sphere has a nonbounding integral cycle. ∎

**Effectivity.** The construction gives a uniform $\Pi^0_2$ upper bound. Equality of given cell expressions in a free strict finite-dimensional category is decidable. All decorations, necklace maps, triangle moves and finite integral chains above therefore have effective finite codes. Connectedness asks for finite paths, triviality of an edge word asks for a finite sequence of moves, and a cycle asks for a finite bounding chain. Nonemptiness also has a finite witness. Quantifying over all labels and all challenges gives the stated bound. Bubble existence is computably enumerable, and item 3's reduction from positive semi-Thue reachability makes a bubble-freeness oracle Turing equivalent to the halting oracle. Such an oracle decides each individual finite challenge's *existence of a witness*. The universal test is co-enumerable relative to that oracle, hence decidable with its jump. Whether the bubble-freeness oracle alone decides the whole test is unresolved by this bound. [Ara et al., Appendix D.3, The Word Problem for Free $n$-Categories](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~yves.guiraud/articles/polybook.pdf)

## The finite last-step criterion in dimension three

Here is an exact answer to Question 3 with finite data at each strict label. In the following construction assume bubble-freeness, as required by item 14. For a generating 3-cell $z:s_z\to t_z$ introduce a formal 2-generator $h_z$ parallel to $s_z,t_z$. A **chart** is a strict 2-cell $Q$ over the old 2-polygraph and these markers. Its slots $I$ are its individual marker occurrences. Write $Q^-$ and $Q^+$ for the substitutions of all sources and all targets. Write $[Q]:Q^-\to Q^+$ for the strict 3-cell obtained by performing the changes. One can evaluate it by composing the indicated 3-generators and the identities of the ordinary gates in the strict expression for $Q$. Strict interchange gives the same result in every serialization.

For a strict $c:u\to v$ of positive weight $r$, construct the following space $L(c)$ from strict factorizations:
$$
          (p,Q),\qquad c=[Q]\circ p,\qquad I\ne\varnothing .
$$
Take one simplex with vertex set $I$ for each such pair. Glue its face on each nonempty $J\subseteq I$ as follows. Execute $I\setminus J$ first with the $J$ slots held at their sources, appending that change to $p$. In the final chart replace $I\setminus J$ by their targets and retain $J$. These two operations specify the new pair. The face identifications compose by interchange. Use the individual face maps, including their multiplicities if several have the same image. This defines a space by gluing ordinary simplices, also when two distinct simplices have the same vertices.

**Theorem.** A finite 3-polygraph is homotopical if and only if it is bubble-free in every dimension and every $L(c)$ is connected, has trivial edge-path group and has zero reduced integral homology. Each $L(c)$ is effectively finite. In particular a bubble-freeness oracle decides the test for a specified 3-cell: the finite integral chain complex has computable homology and triviality of the finitely presented edge-path group is a single halting question (search for a proof that every generator is trivial). In particular the test uses only the strict prefixes and possible final charts, and allows units on either side of a generating face.

**Proof of the chart model.** At fixed last simplicial index $k$, the horizontal part of $P$ attaches $k$ parallel markers $h_{z,i}$ for each $z$ to the lower skeleton. Indeed a map $[k]\to[1]$ is constant or changes at exactly one cut $i$. The two constants are the faces in the boundary of the 3-globe. Each change supplies a fresh 2-globe along the common lower boundary. Write $B_k$ for this marked 2-polygraph. At least one of $s_z,t_z$ is nonidentity, since otherwise $z$ itself would be a higher bubble. Substitute such a face for each marker. Generator counts show that the image of any nonidentity 2-cell is nonidentity, so any bubble of $B_k$ would give a bubble in $B_0$. Item 5 makes all $B_k$ homotopical. The first two directional reflections of the attachment therefore give $NF(B_k)$ at time $k$: adjoining just the new markers is the remaining pushout after attaching the old 2-generators. The weight-zero and completeness argument above applies in these horizontal directions as well. This comparison is the strict labelling map, hence natural when a time restriction substitutes a composite face.

At fixed parallel 1-paths the remaining simplicial set $T$ has the 2-cells of $F(B_k)$ as its $k$-simplices. It is obtained by attaching the triangulated cubes $(\Delta^1)^I$ of charts along their faces. In fact, after dropping constant coordinates, a simplex consists precisely of the underlying marked diagram and a change index at each of its slots. These occurrences have no residual permutations: the bubble-free planar diagram has all components incident to the ordered boundary, which fixes each incident port and hence the component, as in item 5. This argument applies also to $B_1$. Restriction either reindexes a change or substitutes its constant face. Thus this cube presentation includes all identifications. It allows a chart to have equal source and target. The horizontal Segal conditions have already been imposed, and the final directional reflection computes the whole attachment by Proposition 2.16. See also the [marked-diagram calculation in Researcher Response 4](../answered/relative-coherence-all-dimensions-r6-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-4).

For precision about the space being tested, rigidify $T$ as a simplicial category. In the triangulated cube on $I$, a flag in the extreme mapping space is a flag of vertex chains from $\varnothing$ to $I$ under inclusion. In its boundary the smallest chain must contain an interior cut: the two beads split at that cut lie in proper faces, whereas a bead containing both extrema spans the whole cube. Removing the extrema from an interior-cut chain gives a nonempty chain of proper nonempty subsets of $I$. Thus the boundary hom is a subdivision of the boundary of a $(|I|-1)$-simplex and the whole hom is its cone (the diagonal is the cone point). At any other comparable endpoints the hom already lies in a proper face. The boundary piece with a prescribed cut $S$ is the product of the homs for $S$ and $I\setminus S$, since chains containing the cut split there. These pieces give the permutohedron with its ordered-partition faces. For one slot this is a point with empty boundary. This calculation follows directly from the flagged necklaces of [Dugger–Spivak, §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.0814).

Rigidification preserves the cube-attachment pushouts. Each such pushout freely attaches just this extreme morphism ball along its boundary: inside the cube there is no incoming nonidentity arrow to its minimum or outgoing one from its maximum. In the resulting enriched category, interiors compose as words, including when the endpoints coincide. Consequently the hom over $c$ is built from products of these balls indexed by strings of nonempty charts with strict composite $c$. A face refines an individual chart into an ordered partition of its slots. Every generator of a strict top composite can be serialized, and its elementary exchanges are the two serializations of a two-slot context. Equivalently, the ordinary fundamental category of $T$ is given by these unary changes and square relations, precisely the strict top extension at the fixed poles. This also identifies the component labels. The empty word gives the point over an identity. Denote the category of these chart strings and face incidences, oriented from a cell to a refinement, by $H(c)$. A morphism records the ordered partitions in the *source* charts. Its classifying space is the described hom: subdivide each product of permutohedra by flags and identify its boundary flags by the indicated face maps. This description retains separate incidences under face identifications. [Ara et al., Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00429)

**Reduction to last steps.** Let $D(c)$ be the face-incidence category of $L(c)$ oriented toward smaller faces. Its nerve realizes the barycentric subdivision, with separate flags for distinct incidences. There is a functor
$$
      f:H(c)\longrightarrow D(c)
$$
taking a string to its last chart and the strict composite of the prefix. A refinement acts by the last block of the ordered partition of that chart. For each $l=(p,Q)$ the comma category $(f\downarrow l)$ retracts up to homotopy onto $H(p)$. Here $H(1)$ is the one empty string. Indeed an object of the comma category specifies a string and a face incidence from its last chart to $l$. In that last chart split off the specified subset as the final block, with its entire complement in a single preceding block if nonempty. The new string ends in $Q$ with strict prefix $p$. For a morphism in the comma category the two final subsets agree under refinement. The retained final subset lies in the last refined block, so the same refinement, restricted to the complements, takes the first split prefix to the second. This makes splitting a functor and the split maps a natural transformation from the identity to the retraction followed by inclusion. On strings already ending at $l$ with its identity incidence it is the identity. Such strings form precisely $H(p)$ by appending $Q$.

Every such $p$ has smaller weight than $c$. When the smaller-weight homs are contractible, Quillen's Theorem A applied to $f$ gives $\mathrm B H(c)\simeq L(c)$. If the comparison is an equivalence all its homs are contractible, so every last-step test holds. Conversely induction from weight zero proves all homs contractible if the tests hold, using Hurewicz as before. The lower flags are already strict by item 5. This proves the equivalence.

**Finiteness.** Start from the given source 2-diagram $u$. At any intermediate state in a weight-$r$ history the number of ordinary gates is at most $|u|+r\max_z|t_z|$, where bars count 2-generator occurrences. In a chart issuing from that state every ordinary gate survives source substitution, and there are at most $r$ markers. All these planar charts can be enumerated by serializing their gates up to this bound. The initial 1-word is fixed and each gate increases its length by at most the maximum output length of a gate or marker. Thus there are finitely many typed choices of gate and cut at each stage, including the cuts for nullary inputs. Quotient by strict equality and enumerate strings with at most $r$ slots in total. This enumerates every $(p,Q)$ and every face map of $L(c)$. A uniform weight bound on the labels to test would contradict the seam families of item 18.

## A sufficient condition on simultaneous steps in every positive dimension

For a positive presentation $G$ of dimension $n=d+1\ge3$, let $G^\sharp$ be its $d$-skeleton with one parallel $d$-marker $h_z$ for each top generator, as above. Define charts, slots, prefixes and $L(c)$ using strict $d$-cells. Here slots are the top occurrences of the marked positive polyplex. Henry's representation theorem gives them canonically up to unique isomorphism: lifting a diagram after colouring its top generators is exactly assigning a colour to every occurrence. In particular substitution of targets at some marked slots retains the identities of the other marked slots, as can also be seen by giving the surviving slots distinct colours. The marked presentation is positive. Indeed the source and target of a nonidentity positive $d$-diagram for $d\ge2$ are nonidentity: its first and last elementary steps have a generating face occurrence at their respective exterior ends. [Henry, Non-unital polygraphs form a presheaf category](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00744)

The following recursive sufficient condition answers Question 2. Accept every positive presentation of dimension at most two. In higher rank require $G^\sharp$ to pass at the lower rank, and impose this strict condition on $G$:

> For each positive-weight strict $c$, its possible one-slot final charts, **including their strict prefixes**, are the vertices of a single simplex of final choices. Precisely, taking the singleton faces gives a bijection from the slots of each $(p,Q)$ to a subset of those vertices, and every finite nonempty subset occurs for exactly one $(p,Q)$.

The simplex may have arbitrarily many vertices across different labels. One can weaken the condition at every rank to the edge and integral cycle tests on $L(c)$. Both formulations concern strict simultaneous diagrams and their face substitutions.

**Proof.** By induction $G^\sharp$ is homotopical. The marked presentations with zero or $k$ available time indices map generator-preservingly to $G^\sharp$, by inclusion on the old skeleton and folding each coloured marker to its marker. Hence all are homotopical by item 9. The horizontal part of the rank-$n$ attachment at time $k$ attaches these $k$ copies of each $d$-marker, by the same globe calculation as in rank three. Horizontal Segalification therefore gives their strict nerves. Fixing all the lower poles, the last simplicial direction is again the union of the triangulated chart cubes. The colouring bijection proves this assertion in rank $d$: each change-index assignment is exactly a colouring of the marked occurrences, and a restriction dropping a change substitutes the appropriate face. The proof with rigidification, strings, and $(f\downarrow l)$ only used this cube description. It applies word for word to give contractibility of every comparison hom from the tests on $L(c)$. Under the displayed condition $L(c)$ is a full simplex, so the tests hold. This proves sufficiency in every dimension.

To see explicitly that this extends the forbidden matching condition in dimension three, use the cubical interval in the proof of item 18. In the no-matching case the signed cubical links are simplicial and flag. In the universal cover the directed histories for $c$ are the paths between the two chosen lifts. Their interval is the lattice of ideals of its separating hyperplanes, with cubes given by adding independent available elements. The possible final steps are therefore exactly the maximal elements of that finite poset. Each subset can be performed in the final cube, and removing it leaves one strict prefix: linear extensions of the remaining ideal differ by adjacent independent swaps. Lifting a chart from its specified path gives the unique cube upstairs, so this gives precisely the simplex of final choices in the displayed condition. The terminal interval description and its lifting argument are given in the [proof of item 18, Researcher Response 5](../answered/relative-coherence-all-dimensions-r6-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-5).

For positive-to-one presentations the marked lower presentation is again positive-to-one, since $h_z$ is parallel to the generating target $g_z$. At the top level read a serial history backwards. Each inverse step picks one particular occurrence of its target generator and replaces it with the source diagram. Name the slots of the final diagram as roots and the newly exposed slots by their parent and their occurrence in the inserted diagram. A history thus gives a labelled forest of expansions. Expanding distinct exposed slots in either order has a unique joint chart by the colouring bijection, so exchanges preserve the forest. Conversely the forest specifies a history for every linear extension of ancestor order: the exposed named slots determine each next expansion and its strict one-hole context. Those linear extensions are related by adjacent exchanges, so the forest determines a single strict label. The possible first expansions backwards are its root nodes, and any nonempty subset has a unique joint chart. Its residual forest determines the strict prefix. This is the required simplex. Induction recovers item 16. [Detailed occurrence and forest argument, Researcher Response 12](../answered/relative-coherence-all-dimensions-r6-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-12)

## Validation

* **Suspension in arbitrary rank.** For a suspended attachment, fix the inner rectangle. The outer row of its array $P$ is the nerve of a category with two poles, identity endomorphisms, and the original array as its set of forward arrows at that rectangle. Indeed an outer string contains at most one forward arrow, and both the boundary inclusion and the attaching map preserve its position. The first directional reflection fixes this nerve. The subsequent reflections on its forward arrows are precisely the original ones. Thus the all-dimensional test transports both a point and the middle-wire obstruction through any number of suspensions, in agreement with item 6.
* **Middle wire.** The two one-slot terminal pairs give two vertices of $L(c)$. The two strict two-slot diagrams, differing in order along $g$, give two edges between these vertices. Its integral first homology is $\mathbb Z$. Independently, running `python3 tools/relative_coherence_middle_wire.py` gives the subdivided necklace graph with four vertices and four edges.
* **Seam rings and a unit face.** The [last-step checker](../../../tools/homotopy_polygraph_last_steps.py) computes strict prefix classes by exchanges internal to the prefix, applies the terminal face maps, and compares with the full chart-string calculation. The two-seam example has 96 strings and gives a last-step square boundary. The three-seam example has 8,418 strings and gives the octahedron boundary with face counts $(6,12,8)$. An independent insertion $1_f\to a$ and a loop $b\to b$ on an adjacent wire have a single joint chart and give an interval. For a repeated deletion $A:a\to1_f$, the strict cell from the word $aa$ to the empty word has the same final unary chart $h$ in both serializations. Its prefixes are $ha$ and $ah$, and the chart $hh$ joins the two distinct terminal pairs by an edge. Forgetting the prefix would turn this interval into a loop. These calculations test the incidence rule, including the use of repeated generators.

The exact all-dimensional test quantifies over nested necklaces. The finite last-step characterization holds for every bubble-free 3-polygraph, including those with non-positive generators. In higher positive dimensions the last-step argument uses the homotopicality of the marked lower presentations. The oracle bound above leaves open a decision procedure relative to bubble-freeness alone, as well as a finite generator-level characterization by forbidden seam rings.
