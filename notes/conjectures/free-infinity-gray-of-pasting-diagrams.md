# Are Gray tensors of globular pasting diagrams free in the (∞,n)-categorical sense?

*Self-contained problem statement. No project context needed.*

> **Status: SOLVED in the literature (2026-11-18).** Campion, arXiv 2311.00205, Example 3.2 and Cor. 3.5, with Campion's pasting theorem (arXiv 2311.00200). Independently, Chanavat, arXiv 2603.22242, Thm 3.29. See "Literature status" at the end.

## Background

**Steiner complexes and ν.** A Steiner complex is an augmented directed complex with a unital, strongly loop-free basis (R. Steiner, *Omega-categories and chain complexes*, HHA 2004). For such K, the strict ω-category ν(K) is freely generated, as a polygraph, by the atoms ⟨b⟩ of the basis elements b.

**Globular pasting diagrams.** For a finite globular pasting diagram Θ (a Batanin tree), λ(Θ) is its Steiner complex.

**Gray tensor products.** The Gray tensor of Steiner complexes, λΘ₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘₘ, is again a Steiner complex, with basis the tuples of cells and boundary given by the Leibniz rule. Moreover ν(K ⊗ L) ≅ ν(K) ⊗ ν(L) for the Gray tensor of strict ω-categories (Ara–Maltsiniotis, arXiv:1607.00668).

**Free (∞,n)-categories on a polygraph.** Given a polygraph P (generators of each dimension, with source and target given as cells of the free strict ω-category on lower generators), there are two ways to build an object freely generated by P:
- the strict one, ν(K) above;
- the (∞,n)-categorical one, F_∞(P): iterated pushouts along the boundary inclusions ∂Dᵏ → Dᵏ in Cat_(∞,n), attaching each generator along a map ∂Dᵏ → (previous stage) chosen to represent its source and target.

Choosing those attaching maps requires the lower-dimensional composites to exist in the (∞,n)-categorical stage. They exist if the relevant pasting shapes have contractible spaces of composites. There is a canonical comparison F_∞(P) → ν(K), with ν(K) regarded as an (∞,n)-category through the strict-to-weak inclusion.

**Known results.**
- **Campion's pasting theorem** (T. Campion, *An (∞,n)-categorical pasting theorem*, arXiv:2311.00200). For a class of "torsion-free" complexes (Forest, *Unifying notions of pasting diagrams*, HHA 2022), the strict ω-category generated by the complex is also free in Cat_(∞,n). In particular, each pasting diagram in it has a contractible space of composites. The class includes Θ-pasting diagrams, orientals and **lax Gray cubes** (Gray tensors of copies of the arrow D¹).
- **Campion's Gray tensor** (T. Campion, *The Gray tensor product of (∞,n)-categories*, arXiv:2311.00205). Cat_n (n ∈ ℕ ∪ {ω}) has a unique monoidal biclosed structure for which the functor from Gray cubes is strong monoidal.

## The problems

Fix finite globular pasting diagrams Θ₁, …, Θₘ, put K = λΘ₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘₘ, let d = dim K, and fix any n ≥ d.

**Problem 1.** Is K torsion-free in Forest's sense, or does it otherwise fall within the scope of Campion's pasting theorem? Equivalently, is the comparison F_∞(K) → ν(K) an equivalence in Cat_(∞,n)?

**Problem 2.** Is F_∞(λΘ₁) ⊗ … ⊗ F_∞(λΘₘ) ≃ F_∞(K), where ⊗ is Campion's Gray tensor of (∞,n)-categories?
- Since ⊗ is cocontinuous in each variable, this reduces to the case of globes: is Dᵖ ⊗ D^q (Campion's tensor) the free (∞,n)-category on the Steiner complex λDᵖ ⊗ λD^q?

**Problem 3 (consequence to confirm).** Suppose Problems 1–2 hold, and x, y are parallel k-cells of F_∞(K) whose images in ν(K) are equal. Then x and y are connected by an invertible (k+1)-cell, and the space of such connecting cells is contractible. Please confirm this, or point out what extra hypothesis is needed.

Partial answers are useful:
- the case where every Θᵢ is a globe;
- the case m = 2;
- a counterexample showing that some Gray tensor of pasting diagrams is not torsion-free, together with the obstruction.

## Why this is asked

Problems 1–3 would show that every category enriched in (Cat_(∞,n), ⊗) satisfies a certain coherence rule:
- whenever two composites of the same shape have equal images in the strict model ν(K), the enriched category supplies an invertible cell between them;
- whenever the strict model has a (non-invertible) cell between two composites, the enriched category supplies one too.

Gepner–Heine (*Oriented category theory*, arXiv:2510.10504) call such enriched categories "oriented categories", and ∞Cat with lax transformations is one. The strict half of the rule is the subject of a companion problem (cells of ν(K) are determined by their supports). This problem asks whether the strict rule transfers to the (∞,n) setting.

## What would count as a solution

Any of the following:
- a proof that Gray tensors of globular pasting diagrams are torsion-free, or otherwise covered by Campion's theorem;
- a reference establishing Problems 1–2;
- a counterexample, with the obstruction identified.

## Literature status (searched 2026-11-18)

**Status: solved (Problems 1 and 2 are in the literature; Problem 3 follows).**

- **Problem 1 (yes).**
  - K = λΘ₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘₘ is a strongly loop-free Steiner complex. Steiner (HHA 2004) and Ara–Maltsiniotis (arXiv 1607.00668) show these complexes are closed under the Gray tensor, and λΘ is one. The thetas, their Gray products and their joins are all strong Steiner complexes (Hadzihasanovic, *Combinatorics of higher-categorical diagrams*, [arXiv 2404.07273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07273), Prop. 11.2.35).
  - Every loop-free Steiner complex is torsion-free (Forest, *Unifying notions of pasting diagrams*, Thm 3.4.4.22; quoted in Campion, [arXiv 2311.00205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00205), §2). So Campion's pasting theorem ([arXiv 2311.00200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00200)) applies, and ν(K) is free in flagged (∞,ω)-categories.
- **Problem 2 (yes).** Campion, [arXiv 2311.00205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00205):
  - Example 3.2: the strongly loop-free Steiner complexes form a *monoidally suitable site*, a site of torsion-free complexes that contains Θ and is closed under the strict Gray tensor.
  - Cor. 3.5: the inclusion of such a site into Cat^f_ω is **strong monoidal**, from the strict Gray tensor to Campion's Gray tensor.
  - Hence ν(λΘ₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘₘ) ≃ Θ₁ ⊗ … ⊗ Θₘ in Cat^f_ω. The Gray tensor on Cat_n is the reflection of this one (Campion §3.2), so the same holds in Cat_n.
  - An independent proof: Chanavat, *A strengthened (∞,n)-categorical pasting theorem* ([arXiv 2603.22242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22242)), Thm 3.29. It shows that Mol/− from directed complexes with acyclic atoms to Cat^f_ω is strong monoidal for the Gray product. Globes and thetas are acyclic (Hadzihasanovic, Lemma 9.1.16), and Gray products of acyclic complexes are acyclic (Prop. 8.3.28). Corollary 3.27 in the same paper gives Problem 1 again.
- **Problem 3 (follows).**
  - Given Problems 1–2, F_∞(K) ≃ ν(K). Since K is loop-free, ν(K) has no non-identity invertible cells, i.e. it is gaunt.
  - A gaunt (∞,n)-category has discrete spaces of k-cells. So two parallel cells with equal images in ν(K) are equal, and the space of invertible cells between them is contractible: it contains only the identity.
  - One caveat: this is about cells of F_∞(K) itself. For an arbitrary enriched category, one still has to pull back along the map from F_∞(K).
