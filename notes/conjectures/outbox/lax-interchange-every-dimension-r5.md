# Free higher categories with directed interchange: every dimension, after the forced 3-dimensional equation

*A self-contained problem. No background beyond standard higher category theory is assumed. Everything in §2 is proved and is not re-asked, except statements explicitly marked as computed.*

**What we are asking for.** The most general decision: a theory of tensor-shaped lax-interchange ω-categories valid in **every** dimension, or a proof that none exists, and in the latter case the strongest consistent weakening of the requirements. §2 contains a uniform theory T satisfying requirements 1–5 of §3 through global dimension 4 ((K30)–(K32)). It also proves that T forces a 4-equation (C) ((K33)–(K35)) and new 3-equations ((K37)–(K39)), and that **every extension of T fails requirement 3 in dimension 5** ((K41)): an elementary 5-interchanger becomes *absorbed* (its source equals its target, so the identity is a reverse) although both inputs are non-identity, non-invertible and not endomorphisms. Without (C), a finite expanded basis already fails in degree 4 ((K42)). So the question is now which theories outside T survive, and how requirement 3 must be read. Hand completions of particular shapes, or a theory that stops at a fixed dimension, are not what we want.

## 1. Setting

**Globular sets and composition.** Work with reflexive globular sets with source and target maps s, t, identities, and for k < n a partial composition ∗ₖ of n-cells along k-cells. Composition is written in **diagrammatic order** (u ∗ₖ v means "first u, then v"), and it is strictly associative and unital. The boundary formulas hold: for k < m and (m+1)-cells u, v,

```
s(u ∗ₖ v) = s(u) ∗ₖ s(v),     t(u ∗ₖ v) = t(u) ∗ₖ t(v).        (B)
```

For k < l < n and n-cells in interchange position put `L(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₗ b) ∗ₖ (c ∗ₗ d)` and `R(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₖ c) ∗ₗ (b ∗ₖ d)`. A strict ω-category requires L = R.

**The Gray case (directed interchange along 0-cells only).** In a category enriched in strict ω-categories with the lax Gray tensor product (Ara–Maltsiniotis, arXiv 1607.00668), for 2-cells α : f ⇒ f′ : x → y and β : g ⇒ g′ : y → z there is a 3-cell

```
χ_{α,β} : (f ◁ β) ∗₁ (α ▷ g′)  ⇛  (α ▷ g) ∗₁ (f′ ◁ β)
```

and nothing in the reverse direction. Composition along k-cells with k ≥ 1 is strict. For a finite globular pasting diagram Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ, the free such structure has homs ν(λΘᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘⱼ). Here λ is Steiner's directed complex, ⊗ is Steiner's tensor with `∂(x ⊗ y) = ∂x ⊗ y + (−1)^{|x|} x ⊗ ∂y`, and ν is Steiner's functor to strict ω-categories (Steiner, HHA 2004). Each hom is generated by tuples of cells, and a cell is determined by the set of generators it uses.

**The aim** is the same for directed interchange along k-cells for **every** k, including inside homs.

## 2. What is already known (all proved)

**(K1) Raw higher interchangers are impossible.** If every pair of n-cells in interchange position had an (n+1)-cell L → R, then applying this to identities and using (B) forces L = R one dimension down. That contradicts directedness.

**(K2) A single comparison for each simultaneous variation is impossible.** The obvious repair of (K1) adds, for 3-cells A : a ⇛ a′, …, D : d ⇛ d′ in interchange position, a 4-cell between the parallel 3-cells

```
L(A,B,C,D) ∗₂ χ_{a′}     and     χ_a ∗₂ R(A,B,C,D).        (N)
```

This cannot be required for every instance. Take Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², with two 3-cells A : α ⇛ α′ and B : β ⇛ β′ side by side along a 0-cell, and the instance (A, 1, 1, B). Its two sides map to the hom 2-cells

```
U = (v₀ ∘₀ u₁) ∘₁ c₁₁,     V = c₀₀ ∘₁ (u₀ ∘₀ v₁)
```

of ν(λD² ⊗ λD²), where `u_j = A ⊗ g_j`, `v_i = f_i ⊗ B` and `c_ij = α_i ⊗ β_j`. There is no 4-cell between U and V in either direction: the degree-3 chain would need a positive coefficient at u₀ (or at v₀), but every basis element of degree 3 has a non-positive coefficient there.

**(K3) The two variables have opposite naturality directions.** The degree-3 basis elements are `r_j = A ⊗ β_j` and `t_i = α_i ⊗ B`. Their differentials are

```
∂r_j = c_1j + u₁ − c_0j − u₀,     ∂t_i = c_i0 + v₁ − c_i1 − v₀.
```

So when only A varies, the Gray structure has a naturality 4-cell from the "χ first" side to the "variation first" side (V ⇛ U). When only B varies, it goes the other way (U ⇛ V). The simultaneous variation is the 5-cell A ⊗ B. It goes between two routes of 4-cells, `S → V → T` ⇛ `S → U → T`, where S and T are the composites through c₀₁ and c₁₀. In short, the higher structure is **tensor-shaped**: one Leibniz-signed naturality cell per variable, and higher cells between routes.

**(K4) Supports must use an expanded basis.** If every composite's support were the union of the supports of its parts, L and R would always have equal support. Then "support determines the cell" would force strict interchange.

**(K5) One finite basis forces finitely many cells.** If one finite set B admits injective support maps in every dimension, the structure has finitely many non-identity cells in total.

**(K6) No single directed complex presents the answer, and neither does a hom-wise family of them.** In ν(K), the two composites defining χ have the same chain table, so they are equal. A description must therefore be an iterated tower: objects, then homs presented again by objects and homs, and so on.

**(K7) The first uniform case through hom-dimension 3.** For Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², directing interchange inside the hom 𝒦(x, z) adds exactly two generators of hom-dimension 3 beyond the 25 tensor generators. They are the interchangers χ₁ of (A⊗g, f′⊗B) and χ₂ of (f⊗B, A⊗g′), the only pairs of hom-2-cells composable along a hom-0-cell. Their two sides have different supports. Neither occurs in the boundary of A ⊗ B.

**(K8) The (K7) 3-skeleton cannot be extended by 4-generators alone.** Write h_i^j = α_i ⊗ g_j, k_j^i = f_i ⊗ β_j, P_ij = k_j^0 h_i^1, Q_ij = h_i^0 k_j^1, so c_ij : P_ij ⇒ Q_ij. The tensor 3-generators are r_j : c_0j ; a_j^+ ⇛ a_j^- ; c_1j and t_i : b_i^- ; c_i1 ⇛ c_i0 ; b_i^+ (a_j^±, b_i^± the whiskered edge cells u_j, v_i), and the interchangers are χ₁ : b_0^+ ; a_1^+ ⇛ a_0^+ ; b_1^+ and χ₂ : a_0^- ; b_1^- ⇛ b_0^- ; a_1^-. The six composite hom-2-cells P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ are S = b_0^- ; c_01 ; a_1^+, V₀ = c_00 ; b_0^+ ; a_1^+, V₁ = c_00 ; a_0^+ ; b_1^+, T = a_0^- ; c_10 ; b_1^+, U₀ = a_0^- ; b_1^- ; c_11 and U₁ = b_0^- ; a_1^- ; c_11. Whiskering gives t̄₀ : S ⇛ V₀, χ̄₁ : V₀ ⇛ V₁, r̄₀ : V₁ ⇛ T, r̄₁ : S ⇛ U₁, χ̄₂ = χ₂ ∗₁ 1_{c₁₁} : U₀ ⇛ U₁ and t̄₁ : U₀ ⇛ T. Since ∂(A⊗B) = t₁ − t₀ + r₁ − r₀, requirement 4 forces a 4-cell between the route t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀ from S to T and a route from S to T using r̄₁ and t̄₁. In the free 3-skeleton with exactly the 25 tensor generators plus χ₁, χ₂ and no 2-equations, the first route exists, but the second would have to go S ⇛ U₁ and then U₁ ⇛ U₀ against χ̄₂: in the directed graph of these composites the only S → T path is t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀. So no list of 4-generators recovers A ⊗ B. The naturality cells of χ₁, χ₂ are trivial here (the edge homs have no 3-generators).

The repair must change dimension-3 data: an extra 3-generator such as h : S ⇛ U₀ whose image under the Gray quotient is r̄₁, or a 2-equation such as U₀ = U₁. Inverting χ₂ would also work but breaks requirement 3.

**(K9) The orientation of χ₁, χ₂ in (K7) is forced by substitution (proved).** One might try to avoid (K8) by orienting every inner interchanger by the factor order of the enclosing tensor (always "Θ₂-cell first ⇛ Θ₁-cell first"), which keeps χ₁ and reverses χ₂. This fails requirement 5. The free object on Σ(D² ∨ D²) has, inside its hom, the interchanger of its two 2-cells A′ (left) and B′ (right), oriented as in the Gray case: "B′ first ⇛ A′ first". The assignment A′ ↦ f₀ ⊗ B and B′ ↦ A ⊗ g₁ (with the hom-0-cells and hom-1-cells sent to f₀⊗g₀, f₀⊗g₁, f₁⊗g₁ and to the matching edges) respects all boundaries, so substitution sends that interchanger to a 3-cell from "A ⊗ g₁ first" to "f₀ ⊗ B first", which is exactly χ₂ in its (K7) orientation. The pair χ₁ is forced the same way via A′ ↦ A ⊗ g₀, B′ ↦ f₁ ⊗ B. So χ₂ cannot be reversed: adding the reversed cell as well breaks requirement 3, which is the "inverting χ₂" option above. Inner interchangers are oriented by position (right cell first ⇛ left cell first), as at the outer level, and some repair of dimension-3 data is needed (see (K10)).

**(K10) The smallest case is repaired by a factorization.** The six hom-2 composites P₀₀ ⇒ Q₁₁ of (K8) are the maximal paths of an oriented 3-cube with coordinates A (edges a_j^±), B (edges b_i^±) and C (edges c_ij): S = BCA, V₀ = CBA, V₁ = CAB, T = ACB, U₀ = ABC, U₁ = BAC. The six whiskered hom-3 cells t̄₀, χ̄₁, r̄₀, r̄₁, χ̄₂, t̄₁ are its six square faces (adjacent transpositions); every proper interval carries at most one of them. Add one hom-3 generator and one hom-3 equation:

```
h : S ⇛ U₀,        r̄₁ = h ∗₂ χ̄₂.        (F)
```

Then the maximal hom-3 interval is the free category on S → V₀ → V₁ → T, S → U₀ → U₁, U₀ → T, with 11 non-identity cells with pairwise distinct label sets, two routes S → T (t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ and h ; t̄₁), and no reverse of χ̄₁ or χ̄₂. Killing the inner interchangers gives exactly the free Gray diamond with q(h) = q(r̄₁), and A ⊗ B lifts to one 4-generator W : t̄₀ ; χ̄₁ ; r̄₀ ⇛ h ; t̄₁ with no non-identity contexts. Supports use one label per whiskered context, so r̄₁ is no longer a basis element (its support is that of h χ̄₂). The equation (F) cannot be dropped or replaced by a 4-cell h χ̄₂ ⇛ r̄₁ without a new class of cells killed by the quotient. h is not an interchanger: S and U₀ each contain a c_ij, and every hom-2 path containing a c_ij spans the whole external hom-0 interval.

h is **not forced**. The mirror repair k : U₁ ⇛ T with t̄₁ = χ̄₂ ∗₂ k works equally well, and so does the symmetric repair with both h and k and both equations (12 cells, second route h ; χ̄₂ ; k, same exact Gray quotient). The alternative 2-equation U₀ = U₁ makes χ̄₂ an endomorphism and needs further equations (at least χ̄₂² = χ̄₂ under set supports), so it is not preferred.

**(K11) The first larger case, ΣD³ ∨ ΣD², is completed through its top cell.** Write the first factor as α₀, α₁ : f₀ → f₁, A⁰, A¹ : α₀ ⇒ α₁, Ω : A⁰ ⇛ A¹, and the second as β₀, β₁ : g₀ → g₁, B : β₀ ⇒ β₁. The maximal hom-3 interval is two copies (ε = 0, 1) of the repaired hexagon of (K10), with arrows a^ε, b^ε, c^ε, h^ε, e^ε, f^ε, joined by six variations z_X : X⁰ → X¹ (Ω whiskered in place of the A-edge of X).
- **Hom-4 faces.** W^ε = A^ε ⊗ B : a^ε b^ε c^ε ⇛ h^ε f^ε. R_j = Ω ⊗ β_j. The naturalities N₁, N₂ of χ₁, χ₂ have opposite directions: Ω varies the left input of χ₁ and the right input of χ₂. Two inner interchangers along a hom-1 cell, 𝖠 : z_S a¹ ⇛ a⁰ z_{V₀} (of t₀ with Ω) and 𝖥 : f⁰ z_T ⇛ z_{U₀} f¹. Writing 𝖡, 𝖢, 𝖣, 𝖤 for the whiskered N₁, R₀, R₁, N₂, the maximal hom-4 interval has the seven hom-3 paths x₁ … x₇ from S⁰ to T¹.
- **Added data.** Two hom-4 generators and two equations:
  ```
  H : h⁰ z_{U₀} ⇛ z_S h¹,                H e¹ = (h⁰ 𝖤) ; 𝖣                 (variation of the factorization)
  K : a⁰ z_{V₀} b¹ c¹ ⇛ z_S h¹ f¹,       z_S W¹ = (𝖠 b¹ c¹) ; K            (k-type factorization in hom-dimension 4)
  ```
  Then there are exactly two routes x₁ ⇛ x₇: L = (W⁰ z_T) ; (h⁰ 𝖥) ; (H f¹) and R = (a⁰ b⁰ 𝖢) ; (a⁰ 𝖡 c¹) ; K. The top generator Z = Ω ⊗ B : L ⇛ R has the orientation of ∂(Ω ⊗ B) = W¹ − W⁰ − R₁ + R₀.
- **Checks.** On the maximal interval there are 68 hom-3 cells and 98 hom-4 cells, all with distinct supports; the graphs are acyclic, and the reductions have no overlaps. The Gray quotient is exact: q(H) = q(R₁ whiskered), q(K) = q(z_S W¹), and the surviving tensor generators are R₀, R₁, W⁰, W¹.
- **Forcing substitution.** A⁰, A¹ ↦ A, Ω ↦ 1_A sends H ↦ 1_h, K ↦ W and Z ↦ 1_W, so K is not a cell that every degeneration kills.
- **The source/sink pattern.** In both the h-repair and the k-repair of (K10), the face 𝖠 (whose pair flips relative to the tensor faces) creates a second source and a second sink one dimension up. With source-joining (h) in hom-dimension 3, the one-face joining that works in hom-dimension 4 is sink-joining (K); a source-joining alternative would have to factor a composite. Each existing factorization also needs a variation cell and equation under each higher variable (H).

**(K12) The source/sink count fails in the ambient graph.** Reversing one arrow u → v of a finite directed graph changes the number of sources by 1[d⁻(v)=1] − 1[d⁻(u)=0], and the number of sinks by 1[d⁺(u)=1] − 1[d⁺(v)=0].
- Take the interval of λD² ⊗ λD² ⊗ λD² with hom-1 boundary (β₀α₀)γ₀ → (α₁β₁)γ₁. Its hom-2 paths are the 24 orderings of A, B (updates of the first two factors), C (the crossing βα → αβ) and D (the third-factor update).
- Its arrows are BC → CB, CA → AC and DX → XD. The arrow between A and B is positional: AB → BA when C follows the pair, and BA → AB when C precedes it.
- Compare with the graph that uses BA → AB everywhere, which has one source, DBCA, and one sink, ACBD. The positional graph has sources {DBCA, DABC} and sinks {ACBD, BACD}.
- Of the three reversed arrows, BADC → ABDC creates neither a source nor a sink.

So a flipped face does not create one extra source/sink pair in the full comparison graph. It does so only for an isolated polygon.

**(K13) Local factorization does not force alternation.** For a simple oriented polygon with arms pᵢ : sᵢ → tᵢ and qᵢ : sᵢ₊₁ → tᵢ (indices mod r), add hᵢ : s₀ → sᵢ for 1 ≤ i < r, with p₀ = h₁;q₀ and hᵢ;pᵢ = hᵢ₊₁;qᵢ for 1 ≤ i < r − 1. The boundary telescopes to h_{r−1}+p_{r−1}−q_{r−1}, a pair of parallel paths s₀ → t_{r−1}. So source-joining alone always works locally. For r = 2 this is (K10). For r ≥ 3 the equations identify two composites; this is harmless locally (K16).

**(K14) The triple tensor through hom-dimension 3.**
- *Words.* Write a, b, c for the hom-2 updates of the three factors. Start from hom-1 order zyx, with crossings p : yx → xy, q : zx → xz and r : zy → yz. The tensor hom-3 generator is τ : rqp → pqr.
- *The maximal interval.* Its hom-2 words are the 240 orderings of a, b, c, p, q, r whose crossings occur as rqp or pqr. Its hom-3 arrows are:
  - the tensor naturalities, where the lower-index update moves earlier past a crossing involving its factor and the higher-index update moves later;
  - the inner interchangers, oriented right-first ⇛ left-first by the current hom-1 order;
  - the contextual instances of τ.

  That makes 240 + 240 + 24 = 504 arrows.
- *Added data.* Six families of new hom-3 arrows, each with an equation old = new ; inner, applied in every typed context:

  | New arrow | Factors | Contexts |
  |---|---|---|
  | h_p : bpa → abp | bpa → bap | 8 |
  | h_q : cqa → acq | cqa → caq | 8 |
  | h_r : crb → bcr | crb → cbr | 8 |
  | λ_x : rqa → arq | rqa → raq | 12 |
  | h_z : crqp → pcqr | the τ-instance crqp → cpqr | 6 |
  | μ : crqa → acrq | cλ_x, that is crqa → carq | 3 |

  μ factors a previously added joining arrow. Without it the interval has two sources, crqbpa and acrqbp, and a composite-valued substitution A ↦ a, B ↦ c;r of the binary h-repair has no image.
- *Result.* The retained basis has 504 arrows, the graph is acyclic, and there are 40,172 non-identity hom-3 paths with pairwise distinct edge sets. The unique source is crqbpa. There are **ten sinks**: apqbrc, apqcbr, bapqrc, braqpc, brcaqp, capqbr, cbapqr, cbraqp, raqpbc and rcaqpb. Killing the inner interchangers gives the same quotient as the unrepaired presentation.
- *Hom-4 lifts.* The eighteen tensor hom-4 generators have typed lifts. Six of them, of pattern (2,2,0), are as in (K10). The other twelve take the three shapes W_x, W_y, W_z of patterns (2,1,1), (1,2,1) and (1,1,2), with four choices each:
  - W_x : rqpa → pqra → pqar → paqr → apqr ⇛ rqpa → rqap → arqp → apqr, through λ_x p;
  - W_y : rqbp → rbqp → brqp → bpqr → pbqr → pqbr ⇛ rqbp → rqpb → pqrb → pqbr;
  - W_z : crqp → rcqp → rqcp → rqpc → pqrc ⇛ crqp → pcqr → pqcr → pqrc, through h_z.

**(K15) The interchanger-of-a-composite convention.** The substitution in (K14) assumes that crossing against a composite hom-1 path is the composite of the pairwise crossings. That holds in Gray categories, but it does not follow from (B), associativity and units alone.

**(K16) Source-only polygons keep injective supports (proved).** In (K13), every relation runs from the unique source s₀ to a sink, so no relation has a context. The presented category is thin except at (s₀, t_{r−1}), which has two arrows. Orienting the relations gives a one-step normal form, and the edge set of the normal form is an injective support for every r and every arm length. A union-preserving injective invariant with 2|V| + 1 labels also exists. Caveat: overlapping systems such as a;c = b;d, a;d = b;c have no injective union-support.

**(K17) Only one sink matters, and the top cell lifts (proved).**
- *The sink.* Undirected inner interchanges split the 240 words of (K14) into 54 classes. These are the fibres of the tensor hom-2 chain. Only one of the ten sinks, T = apqbrc, lies in the class of the top atom's hom-2 target. The source is S = crqbpa.
- *Paths.* The words reaching T carry 244 arrows and 1,226 paths S → T, with 25 distinct tensor hom-3 chains.
- *The lift.* Keeping all hom-3 data, the top cell lifts as follows:
  - 24 hom-4 operations A_{ij} : P_i ⇒ P_j between chosen paths, 15 of them new and 9 literal whiskerings of the W-cells;
  - six parallel hom-4 routes R₀ … R₅ : P₂₄ ⇒ P₀;
  - six hom-5 generators F_x^±, F_y^±, F_z^± between those routes;
  - one hom-6 generator Ω : F_x^- F_y^- F_z^- ⇒ F_z^+ F_y^+ F_x^+, whose image is the atom x₂ ⊗ y₂ ⊗ z₂ in every dimension.
- *Scope.* This is an extension with a map to ν(K), not yet one whose inner-interchange quotient is ν(K).

**(K18) Source-only completion of a comparison graph (proved).** Let Q be a finite quiver whose generating arrows are split into inner arrows E and positive arrows P, with E acyclic and a rank φ : V(Q) → ℤ constant along E and strictly increasing along P. For every α : s → t in P and every literal directed E-path R : u → t, adjoin j_{α,R} : s → u (with j_{α,1_t} = α), and impose j_{α,e;R} ; e = j_{α,R} for e : u → v in E and R : v → t. Then:
- the set of generators is finite and does not depend on the order of saturation;
- read as contractions (positive arrow followed by an inner arrow), the equations are confluent and terminating, so every path has a unique normal form;
- the completion is acyclic, normal forms have injective edge-set supports, the original path category embeds, and killing E gives exactly the original inner quotient.

On the 240-word interval of (K14) a rank with values 0, …, 7 exists; there are 264 positive seed arrows and 877 positive generators after completion (613 new, 1,117 arrows in all), and 54 inner classes. The completion is natural under maps of comparison categories that send positive seeds to positive seeds and inner arrows to inner paths.

**(K19) The completion is not natural under generator-to-composite maps (proved).** In (K14), take x = bacrqp, u = abpcqr, w₁ = abcpqr, w₂ = bapcqr, v = bacpqr, with inner arrows p : u → w₁, e : w₁ → v, q : u → w₂, f : w₂ → v, and the tensor arrow τ : x → v with its factorization τ = h_z ; f. The completion has j_{τ,e} : x → w₁ with j_{τ,e} ; e = τ. The comparison-category map fixing inner arrows and sending τ ↦ h_z ; f does not extend: an image H : x → w₁ with H ; e = h_z ; f would be j_{h_z,R} ; P with inner paths R, P and P ; e = R ; f as literal inner words, impossible since e ≠ f (contractions preserve the ordered list of positive seeds). This map has not been realised as a substitution of globular generators, so it does not refute requirement 5.

**(K20) Two-sided division is not confluent (proved).** In (K14), with tensor arrows α : bapcqr → bapqcr and β : abpqcr → abpqrc and the inner arrow e : abpqcr → bapqcr, adjoining both a source division h : bapcqr → abpqcr (h ; e = α) and a sink division k : bapqcr → abpqrc (e ; k = β) gives the critical pair h ; e ; k → α ; k and → h ; β, both irreducible and distinct. The presented category satisfies the composite–composite equation α ; k = h ; β. It is not of the crossed shape a;c = b;d, a;d = b;c.

**(K21) Hom-4 comparisons and exactness through hom-dimension 3 (proved).**
- *Disjoint operations.* For two hom-3 operations α (left) and β (right) on disjoint intervals of a hom-2 word, D_{α,β} : (β ; α^β) ⇛ (α ; β^α), right region first ⇛ left region first. On the 240-word interval there are 258 distinct such boundary pairs (50 need eliminated operations expanded through the factorization equations of (K14)).
- *Inner naturality.* For a hom-3 operation α : U ⇛ V and an independent hom-2 update z, comparing "slide z across U, then α" with "α, then slide z across V": for factor degrees (2,1) inner crossing first ⇛ operation first, for (1,2) the reverse. This agrees with the Leibniz signs of (K3). There are 62 distinct local comparisons N. They use (K15) as a convention.
- *Exactness.* On the top atom's hom-1 boundary, imposing I = D = N = 1 (I the inner interchangers) gives 54 hom-2 classes and 874 hom-3 path classes, which are exactly the numbers of 2-cells and 3-cells of ν(λD² ⊗ λD² ⊗ λD²) with that 1-boundary (identities included). Without N it is not exact: the single-arrow equations leave 152 generator classes over 116 tableaux, and two-arrow equations cannot merge them.
- The D and N comparisons introduce no reverse path in the comparison graph they generate on the raw hom-3 paths.

**(K22) The fifteen fresh hom-4 lifts of (K17) (proved after the lower quotient).** After I = D = N = 1, every one of the 15 new operations A_{ij} has an expression through an existing W-cell (W_x, W_y, W_z or their (2,2,0)-type analogues W_{xy}, W_{xz}) with prefix and suffix whiskerings and the same quotient boundary. Three of them can be defined before quotienting as a whiskered W-cell followed by forward D-comparisons. A fourth, A_{2,1}, is identified through one new hom-4 generator H and one hom-4 equation whose two sides reduce, after the lower quotient, to H̄ = 1 ∗₂ W̄. The remaining eleven are identified only in the quotient. Support injectivity in hom-dimensions 4 and 5 and exactness through hom-dimension 6 are not established.

**Literature.** No construction and no impossibility result is known.
- Closest: n-sesquicategories, which have no interchange at all (Araújo, arXiv 2202.09293 and 2210.07704; Forest–Mimram, arXiv 2211.16099 and *Rewriting in Gray categories*, MSCS 2022).
- Crans's teisi.

## 3. Requirements

A **tensor-shaped lax-interchange ω-category** has, for every k < l, elementary interchangers χ : L ⇛ R for (l+1)-cells with no reverse cell in free objects; one naturality cell per varying argument, oriented by the Leibniz sign; higher cells between routes of lower naturality cells; and coherence equations to be determined. The free objects F(Γ) must satisfy:
1. an explicit combinatorial description by generators with explicit boundaries;
2. two cells of the same dimension using the same generators, in the expanded basis, are equal;
3. every elementary interchanger exists and its reverse does not;
4. making all interchangers with k ≥ 1 identities gives the free Gray-enriched category, and making all interchangers identities gives the free strict ω-category;
5. closure under substitution of cells for generators.


## 4. Questions

**Q1 (main: a theory outside T, or impossibility).** By (K41), any theory satisfying requirements 1–5 in every dimension must drop T's raw support equations, so that (C) is not forced, and by (K42) it must have infinitely many 4-cells on Γ₂,₄, in particular a non-identity H with pairwise distinct powers. Do one of the following.
- **(A) Construction.** Give a definition of tensor-shaped lax-interchange ω-categories valid in every dimension, for interchange along k-cells for every k, in which H ≠ 1. Requirement 2 must be read in a suitable expanded basis that allows infinitely many cells, for example a free monoid of loops. The definition must contain the joining data of (K32) and the (2,2)-atom A ⊗ B with its Leibniz boundary. Give an explicit description of the free object on every finite globular pasting diagram, and prove requirements 1, 3, 4, 5 and the chosen form of 2 for all Γ and all dimensions, with the inductive step from global dimension n to n + 1 proved once. One candidate: the free theory generated by T's operations with only the equations forced by requirement 4 and substitution, with requirement 2 read as "cells are determined by their expanded support **together with** the loop word they traverse".
- **(B) Impossibility.** Prove that no theory satisfies requirements 1, 3, 4 and 5 through some global dimension, for every choice of lower-dimensional data and with no support equations at all. Identify the smallest Γ where the contradiction appears. The absorbed-interchanger mechanism of (K41) and (K42) is the natural tool: show that some absorbed elementary interchanger with non-identity inputs is unavoidable.

**Q2 (the right reading of requirement 3, and the best consistent theory).** Every obstruction found so far is an absorbed interchanger. Decide whether the following weakening is consistent in every dimension:

> **3′.** Every elementary interchanger exists. It is an identity exactly when its source and target coincide, and otherwise it has no reverse.

Specifically, decide whether T + (C) + the forced 3-equations ((R∞) and the 4-level thin-block equations of (K39)), with absorbed interchangers set to identities, satisfies requirements 1, 2 (in the expanded basis), 3′, 4 and 5 in every dimension. Setting an absorbed interchanger to the identity may force further equations, by substitution and by naturality of the interchangers in their inputs, and these could cascade. Determine the closure, or show that it collapses a cell that requirement 3′ or 4 needs. If 3′ fails, give the strongest consistent weakening with a construction in every dimension. Other candidates: requirement 3 only for inputs that are generators or whiskered generators; requirement 3 for non-endomorphism inputs only ((K42) uses endomorphisms, (K41) does not); requirement 4 up to equivalence.

**Q3 (composite crossings).** (K41) shows that imposing the inner composite-crossing law together with the outer law, the 3-equations and (C) is inconsistent with 1–5. Decide whether the inner law is forced, or consistent, in any theory answering Q1(A) or Q2.

## 5. What counts as a solution

A definition with proofs for Q1(A); an inconsistency proof for Q1(B) covering theories without support equations; a decision of Q2 (consistency of 3′, or the strongest consistent weakening) with a construction in every dimension; or a decision of Q3. A published construction or impossibility result is also a solution.

# Researcher Response 1

**3′ admits an all-dimensional theory with a genuine directed interchanger at every depth.** It admits one with the inner composite-crossing law and one without it. Both have exact Gray and strict quotients. The constructions below choose coherence equations. There is a distinction between exhibiting these theories and proving that all their equations follow from absorption. In particular, the first construction makes the bare \(\iota(\chi(a_1,b_1),\chi(a_2,b_2))\) an identity even before attaching the prefix and suffix in (C).

## One active composition level at a time

Write \(S\) for the free strict theory and \(G\) for the free lax-Gray-enriched theory of §1. For \(j\ge0\), let \(G_j\) have \(j\) levels of ordinary cartesian enrichment outside one Gray enrichment, with strict ω-categories inside that enrichment. Thus \(G_0=G\). Cartesian products of enriched categories here are objectwise products. In particular the free constructions on a finite pasting diagram have the following recursion. For \(\Gamma=\Sigma P_1\vee\cdots\vee\Sigma P_m\) and \(a<b\),
\[
 G_{j+1}(\Gamma)(a,b)=\prod_{a<i\le b}G_j(P_i),\qquad
 S(\Gamma)(a,b)=\prod_{a<i\le b}S(P_i).
\]
The hom on an empty forward interval is terminal, and a backward hom is empty. These formulas follow from the free enriched path category on the generating string. For \(G_0\) use the Steiner tensor hom in §1. Each theory has a map to \(S\), induced at its Gray level by the tensor comparison to the cartesian product. On a basis tensor the comparison to one factor applies the augmentations in all other factors.

Use ordinary compositions, identities and whiskerings, and the following binary operations. At cut \(k\), on an input of hom degree \(p\) and a following input of hom degree \(q\), take the tensor atom in dimension \(k+1+p+q\). Its arity is
\[
                 A=\Sigma^k(\Sigma D^p\vee\Sigma D^q).
\]
In coordinate \(k\) this is the top atom of the tensor of the two globes. In every coordinate \(j\ne k\), \(G_j(A)=S(A)\), and we take the strict image of that atom. For \(j<k\) the active Gray hom sees a single factor. For \(j>k\) the branches at cut \(k\) are composed cartesianly and each individual branch is a globe. This proves the asserted equality, including the lower-dimensional faces. With two positive degrees the strict image is an iterated identity. Degree zero gives the ordinary whiskerings.

These are globular operations. To compute any boundary, take the source or target tableau of the tensor atom in its two-globe arity. Its top signed boundary is \(\partial x\otimes y+(-1)^p x\otimes\partial y\); iterate the negative and positive boundary operators for the lower rows. Express the rows as a pasting of lower atoms in the strict hom, using Godement expressions for strict composites. Such expressions can also be found by enumeration since the strong Steiner hom is generated by its atoms and tableau equality is decidable. Every expression for the same face has the same value in *every* coordinate on this arity. Substitution therefore gives matching boundaries even for inputs that are themselves comparisons.

Define \(F(\Gamma)\) to be the substructure of \(\prod_jG_j(\Gamma)\) generated by the diagonal globular generators under these operations. Equality is coordinatewise. Equivalently, take the term theory with precisely the equations valid in the indicated coordinates, with the above arities and boundaries. A substitution into \(F(\Delta)\) projects to one substitution in each coordinate. Their values on a term are again the value of the substituted term. This proves both the free evaluation property and requirement 5. Taking the generated substructure matters: a tuple of unrelated choices of schedules need not be the value of a single term.

For this computation retain \(S\) and the coordinates \(j\le n-2\). Deeper coordinates agree with the strict image in these dimensions. Each retained table on a finite diagram is finite. Thus saturation terminates, and its operations have the explicit boundaries above. For requirement 2 take the union of the dimension-, interval-, row- and coordinate-tagged basis supports in the retained Steiner tables, recursively through cartesian products. The same boundary rule applies at each dimension. A rectangular adjacent comparison is the binary comparison in its two active slots with the other slots whiskered on. Iteration tensors a varying cell with its neighbour at their cut and pastes its signed faces in the given context. In particular for \(k<l-1\) the elementary \((k,l)\)-rectangle is an identity: in coordinate \(j=k\) both schedules agree by interchange inside the strict Gray hom, in \(j<k\) it is in a strict hom, and in \(j>k\) the independent outer spans compose cartesianly. ## Why the quotient is exactly Gray

A projection from a generated substructure of a product need not have the asserted quotient. The following test arities prove it here.

Let \(P\) be a globe, two globes composed at their top dimension, or a globe with a single basic left or right whiskering. The latter two shapes have the form \(D^a\vee_i D^b\) with \(\min(a,b)=i+1\). For all \(j\), \(G_j(P)=S(P)\). The only possible branching cut is \(i\), and there one of the tensor factors is the point \(D^0\), the Gray unit. In particular for a string \(B=\Sigma P_1\vee\cdots\vee\Sigma P_m\) of these shapes we have \(G_j(B)=S(B)\) for every \(j>0\). On this entire arity
\[
                              F(B)\cong G(B).                    \tag{*}
\]
Indeed the other coordinates of a term are fixed by its strict image. Every tensor-basis generator of the Gray hom is a tuple of faces of generating globes. It is obtained by iterated binary tensoring at cut zero with the appropriate object whiskerings. This follows from associativity of the Steiner tensor and tensoring the globe maps classifying the faces. Those atoms generate the strict hom, proving surjectivity as well. The suspended statement at a single active cut holds by the same argument.

Here is the strictness argument used inside the quotient. On a pair of disks in a strict Gray tensor, imposing identities on all mixed atoms gives commuting pure actions. For an edge in one disk and a cell of dimension \(p\) in the other, the boundary of the \((p,1)\) or \((1,p)\) atom gives the edge sliding past the cell. In that boundary all proper mixed faces are already identities: the two remaining \(p\)-faces are the cell at the two vertices of the edge, whiskered by the edge at the two object endpoints of the cell. This follows inductively from the signed face formula above, starting with the ordinary square. For two higher cells use the Godement expansion in the **strict target**: move the first cell past the 1-source of the second, then the second past the 1-target of the first. Strict interchange in that target identifies either order of the two independent steps. Thus the two orders of serial pure actions agree in all degrees.

More explicitly, suppose the homs at the next depth are strict. At the present cut the separate actions \(L_b\) and \(R_a\) at objects \(b,a\) of the two homs are ordinary whiskering functors. The cartesian composite of equal-degree positive cells \(x,y\) is
\[
                   L_{s_0y}(x)*_0 R_{t_0x}(y)
\]
in the next-depth hom, with indices relative to it. This preserves compositions above 0 by its strict interchange. It preserves composition at 0 by exchanging the two middle pure actions, the disk-pair identity just proved. It preserves identities and is determined by its pure actions. The induced Godement operations are the original ones by their defining formula. Units and associativity follow by composing pure actions and the associativity of whiskering. This gives cartesian enrichment at that cut. In any fixed truncation apply the argument from the deepest internal cut outward, starting with ordinary categories. The disk-pair identities are available under all substitutions because the free object on a suspended disk pair is the one-active-level object there. This proves strictness inside every hom after the inner mixed atoms and their higher families are identities. It uses the higher edge-sliding equations as well as the elementary square.

To see that the strict homs carry Gray composition, take assignments of the inputs of \(P,Q\) in two adjacent quotient homs. By (*) every cell of \(S(P)\otimes S(Q)\) has a representing term and all the equalities of its strict composition table hold on this generic arity. Evaluating those terms gives a strict functor into the composite hom. This works for assignments in the quotient, including newly composable cells, because these are equations of the term theory. The maps commute with selecting a face, a unit, either component of a basic composition, or its composite, by substitution on the generic arities.

These tests suffice for arbitrary strict homs \(U,V\). A strict ω-category \(U\) is the colimit of the globes of its cells with the unit, vertical-composition and basic-whiskering diagrams just described: a compatible family of maps out assigns an image to each cell preserving faces, units, vertical composition and whiskering. Godement expansion shows that it preserves every strict composition. This proves the colimit by its universal property. The Gray tensor is biclosed, so preserves these colimits in each argument. We obtain \(U\otimes V\to W\). The unit and associativity diagrams may be tested on globes in their one or three arguments, again by colimit preservation. They hold there by (*) on the corresponding strings. This gives a Gray-enriched category and interprets its mixed outer operation by the original atom, as is seen on a pair of globes. [Ara–Maltsiniotis, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.00668) and [Steiner, tensor of directed complexes](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0403237).

The map from the free Gray object into this quotient and the coordinate-0 projection are inverse. One composite fixes the free generators in the Gray category. The other fixes the generators, the sesquicategory operations and the outer atoms, and the inner mixed atoms are now identities. These generate the quotient as a model of the term theory. This proves the first isomorphism of requirement 4. Imposing the outer identities as well gives a strict ω-category by the same Godement argument, with every mixed generator an identity. Freeness on the original globular generators gives the second isomorphism.

In this theory the full inner composite-crossing law holds. Evaluate on its generic suspended arity. Only coordinate 1 can supply a nonidentity crossing, and there it is exactly the ordinary Gray composite-crossing law. This proves **consistency** in Q3 for the weakened requirement in every dimension.

## The inner law is optional under 3′

Keep the preceding coordinates and evaluate terms in one more algebra \(M\). The extra coordinate records whether an inner crossing is taken against an entire two-step path. Higher cells in this algebra are important: a separating 4-truncation alone would not decide Q3.

Take \(H=G(\Sigma[1]\vee\Sigma[2])\), with objects \(0,1,2\). Its adjacent strict homs are the two chains. Its long strict hom is their Gray tensor, whose 1-paths are shuffles and whose generating 2-cells are the individual crossing squares. Start with the suspension of \(H\), with terminal endomorphism homs and empty backward hom. Modify the positive hom as follows, using dimensions relative to a strict hom \(H(i,j)\), hence two below the global dimension.

1. In each \(H(i,j)\) for \(i<j\) keep objects and arrows. Replace each 2-cell \(c\) by \((c,S)\) for \(S\subseteq\{(1,1),(1,2)\}\). Both compositions take unions of tags, whiskering carries tags, and identities have empty tag. This is a strict 2-category since union is commutative, associative and unital. Keep \(H(i,i)\) terminal. In higher degree take one cell for each pair of parallel lifted faces and each base cell with those faces as its image boundaries. Faces, compositions and identities are computed componentwise. Induction on dimension verifies the strict ω-category laws: above degree two the two faces and the base value determine the cell. This construction is finite in each degree.
2. Whiskering across an adjacent object acts by the old whiskering and carries the tag of a positive-interval cell. The terminal intervals contribute the empty set. Higher whiskering uses faces and base. These maps are strict on the individual homs. Two nontrivial adjacent intervals can only be \((0,1),(1,2)\).
3. To interpret tensor operations across that pair, choose any two input globes. Define a strict functor out of their disk tensor into the decorated long hom. On the base it is the original tensor evaluation. Its pure atoms are the whiskered input faces with their own tags. Its degree-two mixed atom on two positive arrows \(a,b\) has tag \(\{(\ell(a),\ell(b))\}\), where \(\ell\) counts edges in the corresponding chain. If either arrow is an identity the tag is empty. In higher degree the two lifted boundary pastings and the base atom specify the unique lift. This defines a strict functor by freeness of the disk tensor on its atoms. Parallelism at each step follows by applying the already defined functor on the lower skeleton. Restriction to a face gives the same construction. Restriction along a globe degeneracy also agrees: on pure atoms it preserves the input identity, on a degenerate mixed square the tag is empty, and at higher atoms uniqueness applies. With a terminal interval use the unit projection. Three consecutive intervals contain a terminal interval, so no three-positive-input compatibility is needed here. Inside the strict homs use cartesian operations, and at the outer suspension cut use cartesian composition with the terminal homs.

This specifies the sesquicategory operations and all the binary tensor faces in \(M\), without imposing composition in a positive argument of the marked square. In particular the boundaries of the higher tensor atoms continue to be those of the disk tensor. The generic arity of a *single pair of globes* at the marked cut still satisfies all its disk-tensor equations. Every term there is evaluated by the functor in step 3: a mixed atom on two nontrivial adjacent branches has as operands faces of the two single globes or their identities, and operations in the long hom are strict. This observation does not apply to a chain of two generating arrows as one of the two positive operands.

For each finite \(\Gamma\) adjoin as coordinates all evaluations under globular assignments \(\Gamma\to M\), and again take only joint values of terms. Precomposing an assignment with a term substitution gives one of the assignments in the source table. Thus this is a free term theory closed under substitution. In each fixed dimension the saturation algorithm still terminates, because \(\Gamma\) is finite and the required degrees of \(M\) are finite. Supports can use tagged copies of the base tableaux of these evaluations together with the two generating tag values at degree four, and recursively the supports of each face. In this expansion a tag records an input decoration or an actual marked crossing in that evaluation. No label is indexed by a resulting cell.

For 3′ the only possibly nonidentity elementary operation in \(M\) is at the marked cut in degree four. Its nonempty tag requires two nonempty paths in separate chains. Its two schedules are distinct, and the base Gray tensor forbids a reverse. At every other cut or degree the elementary comparison is an identity. In particular the two schedules for an elementary 5-comparison have the same tags: both take the union of the tags on the four inputs. In higher degree their two faces and base agree. Consequently any elementary comparison with equal endpoints in the joint tables is an identity. If the endpoints differ, a distinguishing coordinate forbids a reverse. The support equations in degree four hold by union, and the outer block equations hold because every outer mixed crossing in the suspension is an identity. ## A necessary condition in every dimension on one fixed grid

Suppose on this diagram that \(X_1,\ldots,X_{2r}\) are nonidentity \(n\)-cells on consecutive \((n-1)\)-intervals. Write \(s_i,t_i\) for their immediate boundaries. Concatenation of these boundaries in this paragraph is along \(n-2\). For \(1\le j\le r\) put

 p_j=t_1\cdots t_{2j-2},\qquad q_j=s_{2j+1}\cdots s_{2r}.
\]

The latter is the initial state for \(Y_{j+1}\) when \(j<r\). Thus \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_r\) are nonidentity \((n+1)\)-cells on consecutive \(n\)-intervals. This construction pads the *inputs* by immediate whiskerings, so the conclusion follows from directedness of elementary comparisons themselves.

# Researcher Response 4

**There is a decidable unbounded expanded basis for the actual support-free 4-presentation \(P\) of Response 3, without imposing further equations.** Every nonidentity 4-endomorphism in it has pairwise distinct powers. The proof gives a positive integer grading on *all* nonunit contextual edges, including the binary join. Its finiteness bound is local to each input path on each finite diagram. Fix a finite pasting diagram. Write \(|a|\) for the number of generating 2-steps in a 2-word, ignoring stationary 1-whiskers. Expand a 3-path \(A\) into elementary updates of one slot and elementary outer crossings of two adjacent slots, using (K15) for block crossings. Both kinds of steps preserve the length of the 2-word. For each step count the numbers of 2-steps strictly before and strictly after its active slots in the current word. Define
\[
 m(A)=\#\{\text{steps in }A\},\qquad
 J(A)=\sum_{e\in A}(\text{number after }e-\text{number before }e).
\]
These are additive under chronological 3-path concatenation. In particular \(m(\chi(a,b))=|a||b|\) and **\(J(\chi(a,b))=0\)** before spatial whiskering. To see the second equality, put \(u=|a|,v=|b|\) and number the letters in each operand from one. Whenever its pair \((i,j)\) crosses, exactly \(i+j-2\) letters precede the pair, and \(u+v-i-j\) follow it. Summing their difference over all pairs gives zero, independently of the order of crossing. With \(l,r\) extra letters at the two sides, the sum increases by \((r-l)uv\). Thus replacing any block by either composite expansion in any context preserves both invariants. Identities, rebracketing and the fixed-whiskering laws also preserve them: a stationary outer 1-cell adds no 2-steps, and a fixed spatial prefix or suffix changes each summand by the same signed length. This proves invariance on the 3-cells of \(P\), including composite-valued arguments.

Let \(L\) bound the 2-word lengths and \(M\) the numbers of expanded atomic steps in a 3-path on this diagram. Both bounds are computable in the finite lower skeleton of (K30): the 2-path graph is finite, as is its directed graph of atomic 3-steps, which has no positive cycle by its Gray image. Take \(c=L+M+1\). For \(A:a\Rightarrow a'\), \(B:b\Rightarrow b'\), assign the weights
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R(A,b)&=|b|\big(c\,m(A)+J(A)\big),\\
 T(a,B)&=|a|\big(c\,m(B)-J(B)\big),\\
 I(A,B)&=m(A)m(B),\\
 K(A,B)&=R(A,b')-I(A,B).
\end{aligned}
\]
These are the weights of \(r,t,\iota,h\), respectively. A generating input 4-cell has weight one. Fixed external contexts of an edge do not change its weight. Sum the weights in a 4-path.

Here is the verification for the equations of \(P\). Additivity of \(m,J\) gives the composition equations of \(r,t\) in a varying argument. Additivity of 2-length gives composition in the other argument. It also gives both composite laws for \(I\), so in particular it handles the restricted (K30) laws. Under appending a 2-word of length \(s\) to \(A\), \(J(A)\) increases by \(s m(A)\); under prepending it decreases by the same amount. In the triple relation the two sides have weight \(c|a||b||d|\) by the zero block moment. The binary join equation holds by the definition of \(K\); its degeneracies have respectively the weight of \(r\) and zero. These verifications work in arbitrary stationary contexts. Substituting new arguments replaces the lengths and moments by those of the new arguments and the same identities apply. The numerical grading itself need not be preserved under a substitution of one diagram into another.

\]
Degenerate edges use their prescribed normalizations. The finite alphabet of contextual 4-edges therefore has strictly positive integer weight after unit steps are suppressed. ## A forced order of schedules in every dimension

There is an all-dimensional consequence for **any** proposed solution of Q1 with (K15), Godement composition and the normalized elementary comparisons. For each \(r\ge2\), fix the two-dimensional diagram \(\Gamma_{2,4r}\). In **every degree \(n\ge5\)** it must contain \(r!\) distinct parallel \(n\)-cells, indexed by permutations, among which directed \((n+1)\)-reachability is exactly the order generated by sorting an adjacent inverted pair. In particular the conclusion specifies absent higher cells, as well as present ones. It applies even if a proposed theory has additional higher operations and arbitrary expanded supports.

First take nonidentity \(n\)-cells \(Z_i:s_i\to t_i\) on consecutive \((n-1)\)-intervals in spatial order \(1,\ldots,r\). For a permutation \(\pi\), form the top path \(P_\pi\) performing the \(Z_i\) in that chronological order. At each step whisker by the current boundary in every other interval, using \(t_j\) if \(j\) has already acted and \(s_j\) otherwise. These paths have common endpoints \(s_1\cdots s_r,t_1\cdots t_r\). An adjacent inverted pair in the permutation can be sorted by one contextual elementary comparison. Put intervening stationary intervals on one input by immediate whiskering, and whisker the resulting comparison by the rest of the context. This constructs a path for every sequence of such sorts.

Suppose further that for each \(i<j\) there is a substitution retaining \(Z_i,Z_j\) as nonidentities and making the other \(Z_k\) identities. Under it the only performed updates in \(P_\pi\) are the retained two. The stationary intervals before, between and after them can be included as immediate whiskers of the two inputs. Those whiskered inputs are nonidentity by the reflection lemma of (K42). The two possible projected paths are therefore the *bare* elementary boundary routes of two proper inputs. Strong directedness forbids a path from their sorted order to the inverted order, regardless of the generators such a path might use. Consequently a path \(P_\pi\to P_\sigma\) requires every inverted pair of \(\sigma\) to be inverted in \(\pi\). Conversely, under that inclusion move the first symbol of \(\sigma\) to the front of \(\pi\): every symbol that must pass it was larger, since a smaller one would give an inversion of \(\sigma\) missing from \(\pi\). Repeat on the suffix. This constructs the required sorting path and proves exact reachability. Two distinct permutations cannot give equal cells since equality gives reachability in both directions.

## Raising a globe in arbitrary bidegree

The induction is given here in hom degrees at the chosen cut. Write
\(p\) for the degree of the *moving* input and \(q\) for the fixed

their fixed parts by identities. Put
\[
 D_a^{i,\epsilon}=C_a^i[r_a^\epsilon(x^i)],\qquad
 \sigma_a=\begin{cases}
 +1&\text{left input moving},\\
 (-1)^{a-p}&\text{right input moving}.
 \end{cases}
\]
For each \(a=p,\dots,p+q\) form a bridge \(b_a^\epsilon\) between
\(D_a^{0,\epsilon}\) and \(D_a^{1,\epsilon}\), in that order when
\(\sigma_a=+1\) and in the opposite order otherwise.

At \(a=p\) the contexts are empty. Each old \(p\)-face has one pure
occurrence of \(x\) in fixed contexts, so the bridge is insertion of
\(v\) there. This single-occurrence condition will be verified below.
At \(a>p\) declare the bridge as an auxiliary generator, except at
the final rank where there is **one** bridge, the new principal disk
operation. To pass from rank \(a\) to \(a+1\), let \(i=0\) for a
positive sign and \(i=1\) for a negative sign, and put \(j=1-i\).

 C_{a+1}^i[z]= C_a^i[z]\; ;\; b_a^1,qquad

next rank go from \(D_a^{i,0}\) to \(D_a^{j,1}\). This verifies
parallelism in the actual sesquicategorical syntax at **each** stage.

hemispheres are the same top cell and the contexts are independent
of \(\epsilon\). Its two faces are the displayed routes through the
old principal cells in their layers and the preceding bridges.

Here is the signed boundary check. Use signs as bits when selecting

\[
 \begin{cases}

 \end{cases}                                                     \tag{1}

## A rectangle with four mixed inputs

Write \(a\) for the crossing of \(z\) with \(x\), \(b\) for its crossing with \(y\), and \(X,Y\) for the successive advances on the indicated slots. Repeated occurrences on a slot are distinguished in chronological order. Use the two outside 3-histories
\[
                    aXX\mid bYY \qquad\text{and}\qquad XXa\mid YYb .
\]
In the early half let \(A,B\) move the first and second \(X\) respectively before \(a\). In the late half let \(C,D\) move the first and second \(Y\) respectively before \(b\). Each is one contextual outer \(r\) with nonzero mixed chain. There is a one-step comparison \(CDAB\Rightarrow CADB\). In the reverse direction the solver found a closed set of **22 raw 4-routes** containing \(CADB\) and excluding \(CDAB\). Among the rewrites inside this set are twelve uses of (15), which trades a wide outer variation followed by an inner correction for the narrow variation, and eight uses of the restricted inner split (21), counting both directions. The [native-term check](../../../scripts/e6/check_two_moving_native.py) independently executes the four inputs and the substituted boundaries of \(\delta(A,D)\) with the actual inactive whiskers.

The same solver verifies reverse obstructions of sizes 12 and 13 for the outside histories \(YaX\mid bYY\to XaY\mid YYb\) and \(aXX\mid bYX\to XXa\mid XYb\). Here the inactive \(B,C\) are still mixed. In the first example the early active input is inner, and the reverse search encounters \(N_L\). In the second the late active input is inner, and it encounters \(N_R\). A separate example in the checker exercises \(U_r\) inside a reverse cut.

## The finite graph and its cut test

A 3-history interleaves \(ab,X^m,Y^n\). The two crossings share \(z\), so their order is forced even in the expansion of a block crossing. The 2-states of the slots are successively \(zxy,xzy,xyz\). After taking out fixed temporal prefixes and suffixes, the positive 4-edges are exactly the following.

- An \(r\)-edge moves a nonempty advance history before a consecutive block of crossings. The block is \(a\) for advances of \(x\), \(b\) for advances of \(y\), or \(ab\) for advances on the two-slot operand. The last case includes an operand with a stationary 2-whisker.
- An \(\iota\)-edge interchanges adjacent positive histories whose slots occupy disjoint spatial intervals, putting the spatially earlier history first. Their slots are read in the actual 2-state just before the pair. Fixed spatial gaps can be placed using the whiskering and balancing equations.

Indeed the only positive outer split is the left branch against \(z\). Its right operand has no positive history. Thus \(t\) is degenerate and \(h\) specializes to \(r\); a triple requires a third outer branch. The nontrivial 4-equations on these edges are (10), (14), (15), (20) and (21). The stationary operand of \(r\) has length one, so has no proper subdivision. A proper varying 2-whisker adds the other moving slot to its operand, exactly the wide cases of (14) and (15). An inner operand splits under (20) or (21) only when the opposite operand is a crossing term. In this interval a nonempty literal crossing has history \(a,b\), or \(ab\).

The directed 5-moves are disjoint temporal exchange \(\delta\), the inner naturalities \(N_L,N_R\), and \(U_r\). The binary \(W\) and \(U_t\) have a unit input. For a variation consisting of several 4-edges, slide one edge at a time, retaining the entire independent operand of the naturality. The intermediate product on the target of one edge is the product on the source of the next; for right variation use the order of (27). This gives the prescribed pair of boundary routes. Likewise an exchange of two composite 4-paths has a forward path swapping their individual edges in their disjoint windows. These decompositions concern reachability and require no composition law on 5-cells. The [interval program](../../../scripts/e6/interchange_two_moving.py) generates these rules in lower contexts. The Response 11 solver applies them in every top prefix and suffix and uses 4-equations in both directions.

The test arity has \(m=n=2\). Its 90 histories give 375 contextual 4-edges and 383 ground rules. The program executes both faces of all 375 edges on their labelled 2-words. All raw 4-paths are finite: count advances already before their *own* crossing, and then count ordered pairs with a letter of \(\{a,X\}\) before one of \(\{b,Y\}\). An \(r\)-edge strictly increases the first count. An inner edge preserves it and strictly increases the second, since its two disjoint spatial windows exchange right-group events with left-group events. Both counts are bounded.

For each initial 3-history enumerate every 4-path out of it, including the empty path. On these vertices form the full graph of contextual equations and directed moves. Number its strongly connected components in topological order. A second pass checks the resulting integer rank against every legal rewrite. It must be nondecreasing everywhere. For the following test it must increase strictly:

1. Take an elementary temporal exchange inside a 4-path, and let \(H\) be the 3-history just before its two edges act.
2. Choose a temporal cut between the end of the earlier window and the start of the later one, allowing any intervening stationary events.
3. Require that the prefix at this cut has the same event set in \(H\), the outside 3-source of the full path and its outside 3-target. Prefix counts identify the named events since each of the three lists \(ab,X^m,Y^n\) has fixed internal order.

Across **191,280 raw 4-paths**, the program checked 221,217 contextual applications of \(\delta\). Exactly **2,887** admit such a common cut; every one strictly increases the rank. Every other rule remains available along a proposed reverse, even when it crosses the chosen cut. A return would have to decrease the rank. The [rank and reachability check](../../../scripts/e6/interchange_two_moving_cuts.py) also counts 10,438 contextual applications of \(N_L\), 6,108 of \(N_R\) and 5,182 of \(U_r\).

The outside-cut condition matters. In a test beginning at \(aXbYYY\), first move \(X\) before \(a\), then move the first \(Y\) before the *whole* crossing \(ab\). At the resulting \(XYabYY\), the inner move \(Ya\to aY\) and outer move \(bY\to Yb\) have disjoint temporal windows. Compare their two orders, then move the final \(Y\) before \(b\) on both routes. Both exterior contexts are positive and mixed. The solver returns from the target to the source using just the 4-equations (15), (10), (15), (10), (21), (15), in that order with the appropriate orientations. Thus the two full routes are already equal in \(P\). The wide move in the prefix uses both crossings: the cut separating the active moves after that prefix does not divide the original inputs. This explains how an exchange can have a return in an arbitrary top context while every four-input rectangle in the theorem is directed.

## Reduction from arbitrary advance counts

Choose a positive 4-edge in each active input. For \(r\), retain one advance of its varying history. For \(\iota\), retain one event in each spatial operand: a crossing is already retained, and a unary event selects its particular advance. Disjoint spatial support means a single witness asks for at most one advance on each of \(x,y\). Keep all three slots and the advances selected by the two witnesses. Send every other unary advance to an identity, mapping the intervening 2-generators to the corresponding successive retained values. This is a globular substitution on each generating string. Each selected \(r\) still varies a positive history across \(z\); each selected inner edge still has two positive operands. Response 4's positive grading therefore keeps **both active composites** proper. There is no requirement on the images of the inactive inputs.

The resulting strings have at most two advances per slot. Include them as initial substrings of the two-by-two arity. Append any missing terminal advances as one fixed chronological 3-suffix after the crossings, whiskering every 4-route and 5-comparison with that suffix. This extends the histories to the enumerated 2-endpoints and leaves the active edges positive.

For a four-input rectangle there is a forward path that exchanges the elementary edges of the two active inputs in succession between their fixed early and late intervals. Throughout this construction all changes, including the inactive prefix and suffix, act inside their own interval. The separating cut consequently has the same event set at the three histories in the rank test. There is at least one exchange because both projected active inputs are proper. If the original rectangle had a reverse, substitution and the fixed whiskering would give a reverse between the projected endpoints. The strict rank increase along this forward path and nondecrease along every possible return rule exclude it. This proves the assertion for all advance counts, including active inputs of zero mixed Gray chain.
