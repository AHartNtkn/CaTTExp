# Cones over coherence terms in CaTT (N^cone), round 5

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Everything listed under "What is known" is proved and is not re-asked. Questions are at the end, smallest first.*

## Background

**CaTT.** Contexts list variables `x : A`, where a type is `⋆` or `a →_A b` with a, b terms of type A. Terms are variables or `coh_{Q,B}[γ]`, where Q is a *pasting context* (a context presenting a globular pasting diagram), B is a type over Q satisfying the usual side conditions (either both boundary terms are full in Q, the *coherence case*, or each is full in the matching boundary of Q, the *composition case*), and `γ : Δ ⊢ Q` is a substitution. Reference: Finster–Mimram, *A type-theoretical definition of weak ω-categories* (LICS 2017); Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, *Globular weak ω-categories as models of a type theory* (2021). Composition order below is diagrammatic, and `u ∗_k w` denotes the binary composite along a k-cell, which is itself a composition coherence over two disks glued along a k-disk. For a term t of dimension m ≥ 1 write `σt, τt` for its source and target, and `σʲt` for the iterated source.

**Cone over a globular context.** Call a context *globular* if every variable's type is `⋆` or `a → b` with a, b variables; every pasting context is globular. For globular Γ, the *specified cone* `Cone(Γ; c)` extends Γ by an object `c : ⋆` and one variable `p_t` for each variable t of Γ, in order:

```
p_x : c → x                                                   (dim x = 0)
p_t : p_{τt}  →  p_{σt} ∗_{m−1} (p_{σ²t} ∗_{m−2} ( … (p_{σᵐt} ∗₀ t)))      (dim t = m ≥ 1)
```

So `f : x → y` gets `p_f : p_y ⇒ p_x ∗₀ f`, and `α : f ⇒ g` gets `p_α : p_g ⇛ p_f ∗₁ (p_x ∗₀ α)`. This is a well-formed context for every globular Γ (by induction on the variables: each composite in the target is well formed because the projections of the boundary variables already have the matching types). It is the cone of T. J. Mikhail's Thm 3.3 (*A type-theoretic definition of lax (∞,∞)-limits*, arXiv 2412.13310), lax at the vertices. When Γ is a pasting context, the strict ω-category freely generated by `Cone(Γ; c)` is the Ara–Maltsiniotis join `c ⋆ Γ`, with `p_t ↦ c ⋆ t`.

**Cones over arbitrary contexts.** In a general context a variable may have composite boundaries, e.g. `τ : f ∗₀ g ⇒ h`. The cone variable over τ then needs a cone cell `P(f ∗₀ g)` over the *term* `f ∗₀ g`. Extend p from variables to all terms:

```
P(x)              := p_x
P(coh_{Q,B}[γ])   := Π(Q, B)[Cone(γ)]
```

where `Cone(γ) : Cone(Γ; c) ⊢ Cone(Q; c)` sends `q ↦ γ(q)`, `c ↦ c`, `p_q ↦ P(γ(q))`, and `Π(Q, B)` is a chosen term of `Cone(Q; c)`. For a term t of dimension m ≥ 1 put

```
T(t) := P(σt) ∗_{m−1} (P(σ²t) ∗_{m−2} ( … (P(σᵐt) ∗₀ t))).
```

For `c₀ := coh_{Q,B}[id_Q]`, of dimension n, the term `Π(Q, B)` must have type

```
Π(Q, B) : P(τc₀) → T(c₀)          (an (n+1)-term of Cone(Q; c)).
```

This type is well formed once P is defined on the boundary terms of c₀, which have dimension < n. So the definition is a recursion on dimension. Within a dimension, the boundary terms of an n-coherence may themselves contain n-dimensional subterms only in the composition case, through lower-dimensional factors, so the recursion is well founded with the order "lower dimension first, then structural" .

## Statement

> **Conjecture N^cone.** For every pasting context Q and every type B such that `coh_{Q,B}` is a valid coherence, there is a term `Π(Q, B)` of `Cone(Q; c)` of type `P(τc₀) → T(c₀)`, where P on the boundary of c₀ is defined from Π at lower dimensions as above. Moreover Π can be chosen so that, in the free strict ω-category on `Cone(Q; c)`, `π(Π(Q, B)) = c ⋆ π(c₀)` (the join of c with the strict image of c₀).

The strict-image clause is part of the statement because the inductive step uses it: it guarantees that `P(u)` has strict image `c ⋆ π(u)` for every term u.

Consequence: for every CaTT context Γ, the extension `Cone(Γ; c)` (with P in place of p in the types of the cone variables) is a well-formed context. This is a precise form of Mikhail's Conjecture 3.6 ("cones exist over arbitrary contexts"), restricted to a specified construction.

## What is known

1. **Dimension 1 (proved).** For the composite of a chain `x₀ —f₁→ … —f_m→ x_m`, Π is the vertical composite of the whiskered cone generators `p_{f_m}`, `p_{f_{m−1}} ∗₀ f_m`, … followed by the associator coherence κ over `c → x₀ → … → x_m`.

2. **Every relative-coherence witness in a join context strictifies to an identity.** The free strict ω-category on `Cone(Q; c)` is the join `c ⋆ Q`, which is ν(K) for a strong Steiner complex K (Ara–Maltsiniotis, *Joint et tranches*). In a strong Steiner complex every positive cycle is zero, so every endo-cell of c ⋆ Q is an identity.

3. **Reduction to relative coherence.** Write **RC(r) for Δ** for the statement: any two parallel r-terms of Δ with equal strict images are connected by an (r+1)-term.
   - If RC(1), …, RC(n) hold for `Cone(Q; c)` for every pasting context Q, then N^cone holds through dimension n, in both the coherence and the composition case. (Lift strict cells to terms, then correct faces one dimension at a time with RC witnesses; the strict image is unchanged.)

4. **Fixed dimension suffices.** For the standard recursively defined binary compositions, a syntactic *cut translation* D lowers the dimension of terms crossing a chosen 0/1 colouring of the 0-variables, with D(Σt) ≡ t, D(e ∗₀ u) ≡ D(u), D(u ∗_k v) ≡ D(u) ∗_{k−1} D(v), and D(t[θ]) ≡ D(t)[D(θ)]. With the substitution `e_Q : Cone(ΣQ; d) ⊢ Cone(Q; c)` (U ↦ d, V ↦ S, Σc ↦ p_S, Σq ↦ p_N ∗₀ q̄, Σp_q ↦ p_{q̄}) it gives a literal retraction D(L_Q t) ≡ t, hence

   ```
   RC(r+1, Cone(ΣQ; d))  ⇒  RC(r, Cone(Q; c)),
   [∀Q RC(n, Cone(Q; c))]  ⇒  [∀Q ∀ r < n RC(r, Cone(Q; c))]  ⇒  N^cone through dimension n.
   ```

5. **Dimension 2 holds.** RC(1) and RC(2) hold in every CaTT context, so N^cone holds in dimension 2. There is also an explicit term for `α ∗₀ β`.

6. **The first 3-dimensional instance.** With generic schemes for P(1_f) and P(α ∗₁ β) over D¹ and D² ∗₁ D², the unitor `c₀ : α ⇛ α ∗₁ 1_g` over D² has an explicit 4-term `P(α ∗₁ 1_g) → P(α) ∗₂ (p_f ∗₁ (p_x ∗₀ c₀))`, a chain of ten 4-cells built from six auxiliary coherences over pasting contexts, with strict image `1_{p_α} = c ⋆ π(c₀)`. It works for every coherence c₀ of that type.

7. **Where the lax-square argument stops.** RC(3) holds on the lax square (x⁻, x⁺, y⁻, y⁺; x↑, y↑, f⁻, f⁺; F : f⁻ ∗₀ y↑ ⇒ x↑ ∗₀ f⁺): for a 2-term with strict image F, exactly one 0-column of its arity carries F, the others are point-supported and factor literally through point contexts, and this factorisation is stable under substitution. It gives normalisers compatible with every 3-term. In `c ⋆ Q` the apex c is the unique 0-source and the last vertex x_k of Q is the unique 0-sink, but a term like `p_x ∗₀ α` has a discarded column carrying the nonidentity edge `p_x : c → x`, so the point-support step fails.

8. **A tool that handles one fixed edge.** On the cylinder of a composable pair (x → y → z), a column that carries a lax square but does not span the unique source and sink factors literally through a retained pasting context `C_n ∗₀ D¹(h)` or `D¹(h) ∗₀ C_n` (C_n a vertical n-chain, D¹(h) one edge), where every relevant substitution sends h to the **same edge variable**. With fixed-whiskering 3-cells, path comparisons after whiskering, a compatibility cell φ^h with the vertical fold, and evaluation fusion, this gives normalisers and RC(3) for that cylinder.

9. **Strict case.** In strict ω-categories `c ⋆ −` is functorial on cells, so the strict analogue of N^cone holds in every dimension.

10. **Loop-freeness is needed.** In (x, a : 1_x → 1_x) the weak free object groupoidifies to S² and the strict one to K(ℤ, 2), so RC(3) fails there.

11. **The support lemma.** For every CaTT context Γ and term t, the generators used by the strict image of t form exactly the dependency-closed set of variables of t:
    ```
    supp_{πΓ}(πt) = supp_Γ(t).
    ```
    Strict support is invariant under the strict axioms. The proof is by induction on derivations: a composition head goes to the total composite, and a coherence head goes to 1_{πs} with s full.

    *Consequence.* A term whose strict image is supported in a dependency-closed sub-context S is literally a term of S. So parallel terms with strict image 1_w are joined by one full coherence over supp(w). Also, a point-supported column of an arity factors literally through a point, by renaming that point.
12. **RC(3) for Cone(D²; c).** Write a = p_x, b = p_y, u = p_f : b ⇒ a∗₀f, v = p_g : b ⇒ a∗₀g, w = a∗₀α, d = u∗₁w, M = p_α : v ⇛ d, and K = Cone(D²; c) without M.
    - *Strict cells.* The nonidentity strict 2-cells are α, u, v, w and d. The only nonidentity strict 3-cell is M, which has no whiskers and does not compose with itself.
    - *RC(3, K).* Each 2-term with strict image u, v or d has exactly one active column, a vertical chain C_n, while its other columns are point-supported. Normalisers N_A : A ⇛ ê_{p,q} keep the literal 1-boundaries. They come from a generic comparison χ_r over C_n followed by an evaluation E(σ; B) that is the same for every full r : B.
    - *Fusion.* A fusion lemma, proved by induction on the formal term with only depth-1 naturality, gives N_T ∗₂ N_B ≃ N_A for every 3-term T.
    - *Adjoining M.* Every U with πU = M satisfies 𝒯(U) ≃ A_U ∗₂ M ∗₂ B_U, with A_U and B_U in K. Level-1 whiskers are removed by depth-1 naturality and level-0 whiskers by one coherence over the 3-disc. RC(3, K) then compares the prefixes and suffixes.
    - *What it uses.*
      - Every 2-cell involving u or v spans c → y.
      - The other non-point whisker a∗₀α has a pasting context as its support.
      - The nonidentity vertical word is one of u, v, w, or u followed by w.
      - M has no whiskers.

13. **RC(3) for every vertical chain of 2-disks (proved).** Let Q_m = (x, y, f_0, …, f_m : x → y, α_i : f_{i−1} ⇒ f_i). Write a = p_x, b = p_y, F_i = a ∗₀ f_i, A_i = a ∗₀ α_i, u_i = p_{f_i} : b ⇒ F_i and M_i = p_{α_i} : u_i ⇛ u_{i−1} ∗₁ A_i, and K_m for Cone(Q_m; c) without the M_i. Then RC(3) holds for Cone(Q_m; c) for every m, in particular for Cone(D² ∗₁ D²; c).
    - *Strict cells.* The strict 2-cells c → y are 1_b, the pure intervals R_ij = A_{i+1} ⋯ A_j and the words W_ij = u_i R_ij. The nonidentity strict 3-cells are the chains W_ij → W_{i−1,j} → ⋯ → W_ℓj, applying M_i, M_{i−1}, …, M_{ℓ+1} in that forced order; each M_i acts on the initial letter u_i, with right whisker R_ij.
    - *RC(3, K_m).* Terms with pure-interval image lie in the pasting context D¹(a) ∗₀ Q_[i,j] (support lemma), so one coherence compares them. Terms with image W_ij factor through one vertical column C_n spanning c → y; item 12's normalisers and formal-term fusion extend to words of any length, the final word comparisons being coherences over C_{j−i+1}.
    - *Sequentialisation.* Generic interchange (X ∗_k Y ⇛ (X ∗_k s₂Y) ∗₂ (t₂X ∗_k Y), a coherence over D³ ∗_k D³) and distribution turn any 3-term into a ∗₂-sequence of K_m-terms and framed generators (L ∗₁ M_i) ∗₁ R, with πL = 1_b and πR = R_ij.
    - *One generator at a time.* A 4-cell over D³ ∗₁ D³ moves a frame comparison δ : R ∗₁ R_t ⇛ r_ij across M_i, giving T_j(E) ∗₂ b_E ⇛ a_E ∗₂ S_i^j with canonical S_i^j = (M_i ∗₁ r_ij) ∗₂ 𝔞. The K_m-factors between consecutive generators are endomorphisms of fixed literal words and are removed by RC(3, K_m).
    - *What it uses.* Every relevant strict 2-word is one initial cone generator followed by a pure interval with pasting support, and the cone 3-generators rewrite that initial generator in a unique order.
14. **A one-generator extension lemma (proved).** Let Λ be a context of dimension ≤ 2 with an acyclic generating 1-graph in which RC(3) holds, and let Γ = (Λ, z : A → B) be such that the only nonidentity 3-cell of the free strict ω-category on Γ is z. Then RC(3) holds in Γ. Proof outline: a 3-head over a 3-dimensional arity P is in the composition case, and is compared by one coherence over P with a boundary-corrected binary composite K_P of P's 3-variables; the additive z-count shows that exactly one substituted 3-argument uses z, so recursion follows one branch. Whiskers of the z-factor are path-supported (∗₁) or point-supported (∗₀) and are removed by explicit 4-coherences over D³, D² ∨₁ D³ ∨₁ D² ∨₁ D² and D²(f) ∨₁ D³(z) ∨₁ D³(e). This gives H ⇛ (L ∗₂ z_rs) ∗₂ R with z-free L, R, which RC(3) in Λ compares with chosen normalisers. It reproves item 12 (Cone(D²; c) has the single 3-generator p_α).
15. **What RC(4) needs.** Varying a 3-variable inside a 4-term is still depth-1 naturality. The additional requirement for RC(4) on Cone(D³; c) is a 5-dimensional compatibility q ∗₃ N_C ⇛ N_B for the chosen 3-term normalisers (q : B ⇛ C); the 4-dimensional fusion used in items 12–13 does not supply it.
16. **Two independent letters with a cut vertex (proved).** In the context a, b, c, d; X : a → b, f⁻ : a → c, f⁺ : b → c, g⁻, g⁺ : c → d; F : f⁻ ⇒ X ∗₀ f⁺, G : g⁻ ⇒ g⁺, the letters F and G act on the two sides of the cut vertex c and commute up to exchange. RC(3) holds there. The normal form of the diagonal 2-cell uses the horizontal composite F ∗₀ G (with horizontal identity factors kept for one-letter transitions); vertical composition of such normal forms is a matrix-style multiplication I ; (m^F ∗₀ m^G) with I : (α ∗₀ β) · (α′ ∗₀ β′) ⇛ (α · α′) ∗₀ (β · β′) over C₂ ∗₀ C₂; a horizontal fold over C₃ ∗₀ C₃ and a horizontal–vertical 4-cell built over C₆ ∗₀ C₆ give fusion; a separate clause absorbs a column that becomes point-supported under substitution.

## Questions

1. **The horizontal case: RC(3) for Cone(D² ∨ D²; c).** For Q = (x, y, z, f, f′ : x → y, g, g′ : y → z, α : f ⇒ f′, β : g ⇒ g′), the cone has three apex edges p_x, p_y, p_z and two 3-generators p_α, p_β. In the hom c → z the letters p_x ∗₀ (α ∗₀ g) and p_x ∗₀ (f ∗₀ β) act on disjoint parts of a path and commute up to exchange, so the strict 2-cells are no longer single forced words.
   - Does the horizontal normal form of item 16 (cut vertex y of Q, whiskered by p_x) give RC(3) for the 2-skeleton?
   - Do p_α and p_β then fit item 13's "prefix ; generator ; suffix" normal form, one generator at a time?
   Prove RC(3), or exhibit two parallel 3-terms with equal strict image and no 4-term between them.
2. **A several-generator extension lemma.** Generalise item 14 to Γ = (Λ, z_1, …, z_k) where every nonidentity strict 3-cell is a composite of whiskered z_i in a uniquely determined order (as in item 13). Is RC(3) in Λ enough, with item 13's sequentialisation?
3. **RC(3) for every join context Cone(Q; c).** By item 4 this gives N^cone through dimension 3. Is there an induction on Q, for example on the number of 2-generators, combining items 13, 14 and 16?
4. **RC(4) for Cone(D³; c).** Construct the 5-dimensional compatibility of item 15, or show it fails.
5. **RC(n) for join contexts.** Possible routes:
   - induction on Q;
   - the support lemma (item 11) combined with a normal form for terms whose strict image uses each top generator once;
   - a comparison between CaTT's term model and Campion's result that the attachment pushouts of loop-free Steiner complexes stay pushouts in flagged weak (∞,n)-categories (arXiv 2311.00200, Thm 5.11, Rem. 5.13).

## What would count as a solution

Any of the following:
- a proof of RC(3) for Cone(D² ∨ D²; c), for the extension lemma of Question 2, or for all join contexts;
- a proof of RC(4) for Cone(D³; c), or of RC(n);
- a counterexample: a join context with two parallel terms of equal strict image and no term between them;
- a reference that constructs cones over coherence terms in CaTT or another algebraic presentation of weak ω-categories.
