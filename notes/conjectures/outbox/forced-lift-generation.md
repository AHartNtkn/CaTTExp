# Forced lifts in free higher categories with directed interchange

*A self-contained problem. The theories below are defined in full in the repository files `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r5-2026-11-20.md` (Responses 1–34), `notes/conjectures/answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r8-2026-11-20.md` (Response 1) and `-r10-` (Responses 1–3); §1 fixes the notation and lists what is proved.*

**What we are asking for.** In the free algebras F_{V+S}(Γ) below, a pair of parallel cells x, y can be joined by several cells c : x → y that have the same image in the free Gray-enriched quotient, and these need not be connected by higher cells. Call a cell *forced* if it is the only value of the loop-free paths of letters between its endpoints that cover the whole diagram (definitions in §2). We ask whether forced cells, closed under composition and substitution, already generate every cell. The main question asks for the general statement, for every pasting diagram, every dimension and every setting S, or a counterexample.

## 1. Setting

**Composition.** Reflexive globular sets with partial compositions ∗ₖ of n-cells along k-cells (k < n), written in diagrammatic order, strictly associative and unital, with whiskering by lower-dimensional cells strictly functorial (ω-sesquicategories). For n-cells in interchange position along k < l, `L = (a ∗ₗ b) ∗ₖ (c ∗ₗ d)` and `R = (a ∗ₖ c) ∗ₗ (b ∗ₖ d)`. A strict ω-category requires L = R.

**The signature.** The T₁₂ signature of the cited round-5 answers: the sesquicategory operations; for every k < l an elementary interchanger, which for (l+1)-cells F : f ⇒ f′ and G : g ⇒ g′ in interchange position along a k-cell is the (l+2)-cell
```
ι(F, G) : (f ◁ G) ∗ₗ (F ▷ g′)  ⇛  (F ▷ g) ∗ₗ (f′ ◁ G);
```
one naturality cell per varying argument, with Leibniz-signed orientations; higher cells between routes of lower ones; and the auxiliary operations of the completion (binary joins h(A, B), lifts W(A, B) of binary globe tensors, H_L, H_R, K, J, shifted joins).

**Requirements.** A strict theory on this signature is *admissible* if its free objects F(Γ) on finite globular pasting diagrams Γ satisfy:
1. an explicit combinatorial description by generators with explicit boundaries;
2. two cells of the same dimension using the same generators, in an expanded basis, are equal;
3. every elementary interchanger exists and its reverse does not;
4. making every interchanger along a k-cell with k ≥ 1 an identity gives the free Gray-enriched category 𝔉(Γ) (homs ν(λΘᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘⱼ) for Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ, Steiner's λ, ⊗, ν), and making every interchanger an identity gives the free strict ω-category ν(λΓ);
5. closure under substitution of cells for generators.

Write π₁ : F(Γ) → 𝔉(Γ) and π₀ : F(Γ) → ν(λΓ) for the two quotient maps of requirement 4, and π_G : 𝔉(Γ) → ν(λΓ) for the Gray-to-strict collapse.

**Letters.** Every d-cell of F(Γ) is a ∗_{d−1}-path of *contextual letters* (a d-dimensional operation applied to arguments, whiskered by lower cells). π₁ sends each letter to a whiskered generator of 𝔉(Γ) (an **outer** letter) or to an identity (an **inner** letter). Inner letters include the elementary interchangers along k ≥ 1 and their naturality and route cells. Outer letters include the whiskered variables, the cylinders of a moving globe against an edge (naturality cells r, t of the cut-0 crossing), the joins h(A, B) and the lifts W(A, B).


**Known results (all proved).**
- **(M1) The least theory V.** There is an admissible theory V (the forced base B plus the guarded round-5 equations) such that for every admissible E and every Γ the identity on letters gives a generator-preserving map F_V(Γ) → F_E(Γ) sending cells to cells and equalities to equalities. B contains the structural theory, the support-free presentation P of the cited answers, the face and unit declarations (an interchanger with an identity input is an identity), and the outer unary subdivisions in both variables at cut 0 (a cylinder of a composite moving globe, or against a composite edge, is the paste of the cylinders). (r8 Response 1.)
- **(M2) The two visible switches.** The left split of the inner interchanger ("inner composite-crossing law": ι(F₁ ∗ F₂, G) is the paste of ι(F₁, G) and ι(F₂, G)) and the right split R (the same for a composite right input) are each visible on pasting diagrams and independent. For S ⊆ {left split, R}, V + S is admissible. In V the binary splits at inner cuts are absent and the outer binary subdivisions are present only in guarded form. (r8 Response 1; r9 Responses 2–5.)
- **(M3) The strip.** On the strip C_{2,1} (inner cells A₁, A₂ composable along a cell, B in interchange position with both), the direct crossing u = ι(A₁ ∗ A₂, B) and the paste v of ι(A₁, B) and ι(A₂, B) are parallel, π₁(u) = π₁(v), and in F_{V+S}(C_{2,1}) with the left split not in S there is no cell u ⇛ v and no cell v ⇛ u. This persists after formally inverting every letter sent to an identity by π₁.
- **(M4) Loops.** On Σ[2] ∨ Σ[2] (two composable 2-cells a₁, a₂ in one hom and b₁, b₂ in the next), V has a 4-cell H : w ⇛ w, a whiskered elementary inner interchanger, where w is the composite interchanger of a₁a₂ past b₁b₂. Its powers are pairwise distinct, and H and 1_w are not connected by any cell, even after formally inverting every letter sent to an identity by π₁.
- **(M5) Positivity.** Every non-identity cell of F_{V+S}(Γ) has positive conserved weight, so F_{V+S}(Γ) has no non-identity invertible cell. (r5 (K50).)
- **(M6) Decidability.** For every S, Γ and degree, the vertices (cells of F_{V+S}(Γ) of a given dimension with fixed lower faces and weight) form a finite computable set, the generating letters between them are finite and listable, and equality of cells is decidable. (r10 Response 3.)

## 2. Definitions

Fix S and a finite pasting diagram Γ, and write F = F_{V+S}(Γ) and π = π₁ : F → 𝔉(Γ).

- **Support.** For a cell e of 𝔉(Γ), supp(e) is the downward-closed set of Steiner basis elements of 𝔉(Γ) used by e and its iterated faces. A cell e has *full support* if supp(e) contains every generator of Γ.
- **Witness.** For (d−1)-cells x, y of F, a *witness* x → y is a ∗_{d−1}-path of d-dimensional contextual letters ℓ₁ ⋯ ℓ_m from x to y (m ≥ 0) such that the union of supp(π x), supp(π ℓᵢ) and supp(π y) is full. Its *value* is the composite cell of F. It is *simple* if its vertices x, ∂⁺ℓ₁, …, ∂⁺ℓ_m are pairwise distinct. (Every witness becomes a simple witness by deleting cycles: a cycle projects to an identity of the loop-free 𝔉(Γ), so the support is unchanged.)
- **Forced.** The pair (x, y) is *forced*, with value c, if a witness x → y exists and every simple witness x → y has value c. If x = y, the empty path is the only simple witness, so (x, x) is forced with value 1_x whenever supp(π x) is full.
- **Generated cells.** Let G(Γ) be the smallest family of cells of the free algebras F_{V+S}(Δ), over all finite pasting diagrams Δ, such that:
  1. every generator of Δ is in G(Δ);
  2. G is closed under the compositions ∗ₖ and under whiskering;
  3. if (x, y) is forced in F_{V+S}(Δ) with value c, and x, y ∈ G(Δ), then c ∈ G(Δ);
  4. if c ∈ G(Δ) and σ : F_{V+S}(Δ) → F_{V+S}(Γ) is the substitution sending each generator of Δ to a cell of G(Γ) of the right boundary, then σ(c) ∈ G(Γ).

  Write G⁺(Γ) for the family defined in the same way with "forced" dropped from clause 3 (c the value of any witness x → y).

## 3. Questions

**Q1 (main: forced generation).** For every setting S ⊆ {left split, R}, every finite pasting diagram Γ and every dimension d: is every d-cell c of F_{V+S}(Γ) with ∂⁻c, ∂⁺c ∈ G(Γ) itself in G(Γ)? Equivalently: is every cell between generated cells a composite of substitution instances of forced values? A proof for all Γ, d and S, or a description of exactly which cells fail, is the main result wanted. If the answer is negative, please also say whether the failing cells lie in the unrestricted family G⁺(Γ) (defined like G with "forced" dropped from clause 3), i.e. whether the failure is caused by the forcedness requirement.

**Q2 (main, the expected route).** Is every generating letter of V + S, applied to generators on its own arity diagram Δ_ℓ (the pasting diagram of its arguments), the forced value of its own boundary pair (∂⁻ℓ, ∂⁺ℓ) in F_{V+S}(Δ_ℓ)? If yes for every letter, Q1 follows when every cell is a composite of whiskered letter instances with boundaries in G. If no, which letters fail, and is the failing letter's cell nevertheless in G by another route?

**Q3 (fallback).** The binary-globe lift W(A, B) on ΣD² ∨ ΣD² (A, B 3-cells in consecutive homs, W a 5-cell whose image in 𝔉 is the generator A ⊗ B): are all simple witnesses between its faces equal to W? The same for the binary join h(A, B) and for the elementary inner interchanger ι(F, G) on its arity diagram.

## 4. What counts as a solution

A complete answer to Q1, positive or negative, in every dimension and setting (a counterexample is a pasting diagram and a cell outside G(Γ) with endpoints in G(Γ)); or a complete answer to Q2; or an answer to Q3.

# Researcher Response 1

**Q1 is false in each of the four settings. Its first failure is in dimension six, on two consecutive 5-globes.** The missing cell is a proper *auxiliary* of the binary disk recursion. Its two faces are generated and already contain the two top inputs. Its boundary admits a second simple witness in which a positional exchange is followed by a smaller bridge. This replacement also works *inside other witnesses*. That last fact is needed to exclude generation by substitution from other arities.

Q2 is therefore false as well. All three cells in Q3 are forced. Below we give the counterexample, its suspensions, and pointwise criteria for the two generation questions. In particular every cell belongs to the unrestricted family \(G^+\).

## The lower faces and the three small heads

Use hom degrees at the branching cut. At cut zero add one to get global degrees. Write juxtaposition in chronological order. On the \((2,2)\) disk the three events are the two updates \(A,B\) and the crossing \(C\). In the interval with all three events the proper 4-moves have the following placements.

| Start | Possible moves and destinations |
|---|---|
| \(S=BCA\) | \(a\) to \(CBA\), \(h\) to \(ABC\), contextual \(r_1\) to \(BAC\) |
| \(CBA\) | \(b\) to \(CAB\) |
| \(CAB\) | \(c\) to \(T=ACB\) |
| \(ABC\) | \(f\) to \(T\), \(e\) to \(BAC\) |
| \(BAC,T\) | none |

Here \(a,f\) are right prisms, \(c,r_1\) are left prisms, and \(b,e\) are positional squares. At cut zero \(h e=r_1\). The list follows by moving \(BC\) to \(CB\), moving \(CA\) to \(AC\), exchanging the two adjacent updates in positional order, or applying the join to \(BCA\). There is one update per short globe, so no operand splits. In particular \(h:S\to ABC\) is the unique path to its endpoint. The paths from \(S\) to \(T\) are precisely \(abc\) and \(hf\). The only proper move between these paths is \(W:abc\to hf\). A temporal exchange would require updates on two disjoint subwords. Each 4-move here uses at least two of the three positions. The other proper degree-five declarations require a variation in a short globe or two independent such variations. Their unit specializations give the prescribed identities or existing steps. The two faces of \(W\) have different Gray tableaux. Both inputs occur in the lower rows. This proves the two outer assertions of Q3. For the native inner square use the suspended pair of edges at its own cut. Only that cut branches. There are two schedules of the two advances and a single move, from right-first to left-first. No proper earlier operation has two positive spans. A later independent pair would need another advance on one of the spans. The schedules remain distinct by directedness and their common strict image in the hom uses both advances. This proves the assertion for \(\iota\), including its shifts. More generally, when the elementary square is written at a nonadjacent cut, expand the Godement columns. On its two generating globes the rows of a structural column specify a position for each of the two pure inputs. At the top row there is one occurrence of each and the forward move exchanges those two positions. To check uniqueness, a normalized mixed operation above the bare square at its defining cut has an earlier mixed move in its source rows: for a unary this is the preceding cylinder, for a join the crossing, and for a disk or bridge it is in the displayed seed or the old hemisphere. A pure structural schedule cannot expose such a move. In the low presentation each positive mixed equation has mixed occurrences on both sides, and above it the positive top equations subdivide or transport mixed occurrences. Thus starting from a pure schedule the only possible proper change is the positional square. Its two windows must contain the two different pure inputs. At their attachment row the two positions are consecutive, with no intervening entry. At lower rows the fixed actions are the given column actions: restriction to each short globe gives its single named face, and the source row fixes their order. No nonunit common exterior pad can be taken out of these columns, since it would use one of these face positions in addition to the one required by its own input. This gives the same two-schedule check. Inactive slots in the rectangular notation are fixed whiskers of the elementary operation. We will also need the two variations \(x,y\) of \(A,B\) and the seed \(J\) on the \((3,3)\) disk. In a word travelling from \(S\) to \(T\), the subsequence of non-variation steps is \(abc\) or \(hf\). Each variation is inserted at its named event. The degree-five moves on these words have the following finite description.

* Move \(ya,yb,yc,yh,yf\) to \(ay,by,cy,hy,fy\), respectively. Move \(xa,bx,cx,hx,fx\) to \(ax,xb,xc,xh,xf\), respectively.

This expansion need not be an equation. Every proper exchange expands into at least one swap and none of its swaps is \(W,K,J\). The short intervals themselves are globes, so all proper outer operands are their named cells. ## A bridge with a parallel two-step path

Use the actual recursive declaration of round 5, Response 10. Take \(\Gamma=\Sigma D^4\vee\Sigma D^4\), with hom-degree-four inputs \(X:x^0\to x^1\) and \(Y:y^0\to y^1\). The lower-case inputs have hom degree three. In the \((3,3)\) seed the two hom-3 words and its **positive hom-4 face** \(D\) are
\[
 u=yabcx,\qquad v=xhfy,\qquad
 D:\quad yabcx\longrightarrow yabxc\longrightarrow yaxbc
                \xrightarrow{J}xhyf\longrightarrow xhfy .
\]
Use \(D^{ij}\) in layers \((x^i,y^j)\). Write \(X_u^j,X_v^j\) for the pure insertion at \(x\) in the indicated word with right layer \(j\), and \(Y_v^i\) for the pure insertion at \(y\) with left layer \(i\).

First raise the **right** variable from degree three to four. At row \(a=4\) the sign is \((-1)^{4-3}=-1\). The positive hemisphere is therefore the layer-zero old positive face followed by the terminal insertion. In left layer \(i\) it is \(D^{i0}Y_v^i\). Now raise the **left** variable. Its sign is positive. At \(p=3,a=4,\epsilon=1\) the declared auxiliary \(b_4^1\), denoted \(B\) here, is a hom-5 cell with boundary
\[
 B:\quad D^{00}Y_v^0 X_v^1\ \longrightarrow\ X_u^0 D^{10}Y_v^1 .
 \tag{1}
\]
The fixed right input has degree \(a=4\), so the face restriction in the declaration uses the entire right globe. This is an actual binary operation with **two global degree-five arguments and global output six**. On the smaller pair with fixed right input \(y^0\) of degree three, the same row of the left raise gives a different auxiliary

 b:\quad D^{00}X_v^0\longrightarrow X_u^0D^{10}.

The other path of (1) is

 D^{00}Y_v^0X_v^1
 \ \longrightarrow\ D^{00}X_v^0Y_v^1

The first step is forward because in \(v=xhfy\) the \(x\)-position precedes the \(y\)-position. It interchanges the two disjoint updates, with \(D^{00}\) fixed. ## Why no substitution of forced values produces the bridge

Fix the rank and defining cut of \(B\). For a path in that rank, count all proper occurrences of this **constructor**, whatever its operands and fixed contexts. Rigid factorization makes the count independent of the normalized spelling. Structural composition adds the top occurrences after Godement expansion, and a fixed lower whisker transports them.

On *every* pasting diagram and every assignment, (2) provides a parallel replacement for a proper occurrence of \(B\). Its top letters are the positional square and the bridge on the smaller fixed face. The replacement still has count zero after substitution and normalization. In particular, a unit argument of the smaller bridge cannot increase the degree of its fixed input. The two paths have equal Gray value by substitution. Replacement in a witness preserves fullness: the downward closure of the rows of a Gray composite is the union of those of its pieces. Indeed rows above the composing cut add positive chains; a basis term at the intermediate row either survives in an outside row or occurs in the boundary support of a higher summand, by the tableau boundary identity. This also applies to an identity step. Consequently replacing all counted occurrences and then cutting cycles gives a **simple** witness of count zero. If the boundary pair was forced, its value must therefore have count zero. This argument applies also if the original one-letter spelling of a contextual step has a composite unit specialization.

There is a second check for the substitution clause. A count-zero rank-six word on \(\Delta\) stays count-zero under any substitution whose rank-six generator images have count zero. To see this, substitute into a *normalized* word. A surviving proper native call keeps its tag, and all its fields have smaller rank. A different native call becoming degenerate cannot expose a proper \(B\). In the unit rules for a principal or recursive bridge, a moving unit gives an identity or the prescribed lower value, and a fixed unit restricts to the lower fixed face. Any surviving binary call uses the surviving input and a face of the other input. The seed exceptions specialize to a unary, a smaller seed, or the displayed paste for \(J\). Pure insertions and identities introduce no such call. To produce \(B\) both arguments would have to retain effective degree five, while every proper rank-six declaration has both arguments of degree at most five. A unit branch thus lacks one required degree. The explicit face restriction for larger formal fixed degrees names the very same declaration on its actual face; it creates no additional proper head. The unit rules and their effective-rank forms are given in [round 5, Response 10](../answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r5-2026-11-20.md#raising-a-globe-in-arbitrary-bidegree) and [round 10, Response 15](../answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r10-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-15).

For clarity, take the following family across *all* arities. Below degree six include everything. In degree six take the count-zero cells. Above six require both six-faces to have count zero. This family contains the globular generators. It is closed under composition and whiskering by the structural face formulas. It contains the value at every forced pair with faces in the family, by replacement in degree six and by globularity above it. It is closed under the substitutions of clause 4 by the normalized-word check, applied also to the six-faces. Such a face can be spelled using only generators through rank six: face formulas evaluate higher operations on their declared boundaries. Minimality gives containment of \(G\) in this family. But \(B\) has count one, its two faces lie in \(G\) by the lower-degree result, and its single letter puts it in \(G^+\). This proves non-generation even allowing detours through other arities.

The construction can be suspended \(k\) times. Use the same two globes at cut \(k\), and count the shifted bridge in degree \(k+6\). Here are the details relevant to shifting. No cut below the branching cut admits two proper inputs. On the short branches there are only the two globes and their faces. In relative degrees through the seed \(J\), the schedule and placement lists above still exhaust the proper moves: the arguments are single advances or variations, and temporal block exchanges still expand to the indicated adjacent swaps for reachability. The shift can guard the equation \(he=r_1\); both spellings end at the sink \(BAC\) and neither participates in a route to \(T\). The two split laws have no proper instance on the single advances of the square in these seed tests. The cuts with a single stationary edge again give the two unary schedules. Thus the particular shifted heads building the boundary of (1) are generated on their bare arities, by induction up these same seed faces. Both degree-\((k+5)\) boundaries contain the two inputs in their strict hom image, so the shifted paths still have full support. The two rigid-word readings and both unit checks use the same degrees relative to the cut. In the shifted case both paths project to an identity in the strict hom. This proves failure on some diagram **in each degree at least six** for every \(S\).

## Which cells and which generic letters

There is also an exact uniform description if the input is an individual cell instead of a dimension. We give separate tests for the two properties in Q2 because a test of forcedness on the arity alone cannot establish non-generation.

First, **\(G^+(\Gamma)=F(\Gamma)\)**. On the actual arity of every proper native letter, its single step is a witness. One way to verify the support assertion in all dimensions is to contract each short globe in turn to the seam, retaining the other globe. In the remaining single globe the generic disk becomes an identity elevation of the retained input. For a principal this is the vertex specialization of the disk. For a raised bridge a fixed vertex leaves the bottom pure insertion under identities; a contracted moving variation leaves the indicated row of the old disk on the fixed globe. The degree of that row is at least the actual degree of the fixed input by the hemisphere restriction. For the seed auxiliaries, the same computation reads the retained input in the lower schedules: \(A,B\) in \(BCA,ABC\), the varying input in its pure insertion for \(H_L,H_R,K\), and \(x,y\) in \(yabcx,xhfy\) for \(J,G,H\). This also works at a shifted cut by first making its hom strict. The two restrictions factor through the Gray quotient, since the single-globe targets are strict. A Gray support missing a top input could use only its proper faces and the other globe, and its restriction could not contain that retained top input. In fact for a native operation of output \(j\) both top inputs have degree at most \(j-1\), so its immediate faces already pass the two restriction tests. Downward closure supplies the rest of the arity. An input generator itself is included by clause 1. Now induct on output degree for the generic faces and on term formation for compositions, contextual instances and substitution. This proves the assertion, so every failure of Q1 is specifically a failure of the forcedness requirement. [Vertex specializations and actual bridge arities](../answered/lax-interchange-every-dimension-r5-2026-11-20.md#raising-a-globe-in-arbitrary-bidegree)

**Finite classification of generic forcedness.** Variables on their globes, outer unaries and the native letters through degree five pass, as above. For every remaining proper rigid tag \(L\), form the finite (M6) directed multigraph at its generic source and target. Keep the selected setting when identifying vertices. If the endpoints coincide, \(L\) fails. Otherwise omit the direct edges from source to target whose *values equal* \(L\). Then the failing tags are exactly those for which the target is reachable in the resulting graph. This is one finite reachability computation per tag, including for every index of the recursive bridges. Indeed the endpoints already give full support. A bypass shortens to a simple path; if it has at least two proper steps its value cannot equal the single rigid occurrence with two empty unary gaps. At length one the value comparison was performed explicitly. Conversely a simple witness of a different value cannot use an omitted edge, since it could use that edge only as its entire path. This test uses distinct parallel edges and works even if the vertex graph contains loops. Equations subdividing the outer unaries are the reason to handle them separately. For (1), (2) is the surviving path.

**An exact criterion for membership after substitution.** The following description of the failing cells uses finite tables indexed by an integer \(n\). It gives a positive membership search in general. For the bridges above, the occurrence count proves exclusion at every index at once. The tables need only communicate *lower-rank* results between arities; they never test substitution of an arbitrary previously generated cell from another table.

Fix the query \((S,\Gamma,c)\), of degree \(d\), and a finite-alphabet encoding of trees and typed terms, for example fully parenthesized terms with binary indices. At index \(n\) list the trees and terms of code length at most \(n\), retaining well-typed terms of degrees through \(d\) on those trees. Include the query tree, the query and its faces, and the generating cells through \(d\) on every listed tree. Identify equal entries by (M6). Define sets \(T_j^n(\Theta)\) of marked entries by the following computation in order \(j=0,\ldots,d\).

1. Mark the rank-\(j\) generators. At rank zero this finishes the computation.
2. Make a common list of operations for rank \(j\). For each listed tree \(A\) of height at most \(j\) and parallel pair in \(T_{j-1}^n(A)\), compute the simple full witnesses in the **unrestricted** finite vertex graph of (M6). If there is at least one and their values coincide, record one representative word and its arity \(A\). The cutoff applies to the list of pairs; clipping their witness graph to the cutoff would give a wrong test.
3. Independently on each \(\Theta\), close its finite rank-\(j\) table under composition, fixed whiskering, identities on marked bases, and evaluation of the recorded operations. For the last instruction assign the named globular generators of \(A\) to marked entries of the indicated ranks on \(\Theta\), respecting boundaries, and evaluate the recorded word on that tuple. Mark every listed result of each instruction, using actual free-cell equality. Iterate until stable. Rank-\(j\) inputs to an operation, when present, use the growing table on \(\Theta\) itself.

In particular, for a cell with generated endpoints the exact failure criterion is
\[
                         c\notin T_d^n(\Gamma)\quad\text{for every }n .
\]
For a failing generic letter in the preceding graph test, membership by another route is equivalently membership in one of these tables with its own arity as \(\Gamma\). This is a countable-intersection description of the exceptional cells; no finite cutoff for a general negative query is asserted.

Here is the completeness argument for these tests. Regard every certified head as a term constructor on a tuple indexed by its arity. Its two boundary certificates have strictly smaller output degree. A full-support head of degree \(j\) cannot use an arity of height greater than \(j\), since all rows of its Gray witness lie in degrees at most \(j\) and cannot use a globular input of greater degree. A term built from variables, structural operations and certified heads can have a substitution pushed to its variables: at a head just substitute recursively into its tuple and leave its generic certificate fixed. At a forced-value introduction use the head on the tuple of named variables. Induction on a derivation with clauses 1–4 thus yields an ordinary finite term of this form together with finite certificates for the smaller-degree boundary terms at all the heads used. This reasoning also treats a derivation that originally substituted from an ambient diagram containing unused higher generators. Conversely every such certified term denotes a generated cell by the four clauses. Take \(n\) large enough to include the arities, intermediate evaluations and generic boundary evaluations in this finite certificate tree. Step 2 then admits each needed head by rank induction, and step 3 realizes each term by ordinary term induction. In the other direction every marking has those same certificates, by induction on rank and saturation stage. This proves the criterion without an assumption that the individual letters *within* a forced witness have generated endpoints.
