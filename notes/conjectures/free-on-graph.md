# Is the free weak ω-category on a graph equivalent to the free category?

## Background

- A **graph** (1-dimensional computad) G has vertices and edges.
- The free **weak** ω-category `F_ω(G)` on G, in the sense of Dean–Finster–Markakis–Reutter–Vicary (*Computads for weak ω-categories as an inductive type*, Adv. Math. 2024), has many 1-cells for each path: different bracketings, units, and coherence composites. It also has higher coherence cells between them.
- The free **1-category** `F_1(G)` has exactly one arrow per path.
- Benjamin–Markakis ([2402.01611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01611)) show that the hom ω-categories of free weak ω-categories are free, generated by indecomposable cells.

## Statement

The canonical comparison `F_ω(G) → F_1(G)` (viewing `F_1(G)` as a weak ω-category with only identity higher cells) is an ω-equivalence:
- it is essentially surjective on objects (trivially, a bijection);
- for each pair of vertices a, b, the induced map `Hom_{F_ω(G)}(a, b) → Hom_{F_1(G)}(a, b)` is an equivalence onto a discrete ω-category.

**Concretely:** every 2-cell of `F_ω(G)` is invertible, two 1-cells are connected by a 2-cell exactly when they represent the same path, and all higher cells are invertible and contractible in the same sense.

**Test case:** G is one vertex x with one loop s. The conjecture says `Hom_{F_ω(G)}(x, x)` is equivalent to the discrete ω-category on ℕ.

## What is known

- Every 2-cell of `F_ω(G)` is a coherence, since G has no 2-generators. Hence every 2-cell is invertible (Benjamin–Markakis, [2406.12127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12127): coherences are coinductively invertible).
- Two 1-cells with the same path should be connected by a coherence: if both are composites of the same sequence of edges, the CaTT coherence rule produces a cell between them. They are not connected if the paths differ, since every cell's boundary determines a path invariant.
- **Proof sketch for contractibility.** For parallel cells u, v of dimension ≥ 2 over a path of length k:
  1. Factor both through the linear chain pasting scheme `Θ_k` (k composable arrows) via the substitution `σ : Θ_k → G` that picks the path's edges. Since G has no higher generators, every cell is built from coherences and edges, and one can induct on term structure.
  2. Then `u = u'[σ]` and `v = v'[σ]` with `u'`, `v'` parallel and of full support in `Θ_k`.
  3. So `coh_{Θ_k, u'→v'}[σ] : u → v` exists.

  **The gap is step 2 when σ is not injective**, e.g. a loop traversed twice. The lifts `u'`, `v'` must be chosen *canonically*, by position along the path, so that their boundaries agree in `Θ_k` and not just after σ. This seems routine but has not been written out.

## Why it matters

It would give an object that behaves like the natural numbers, built only from a finite presentation and a hom-object construction, with no inductive types or computation rules.

## What would count as a solution

A proof (possibly short, via the coherence rule and Benjamin–Markakis), or a counterexample, or a reference.
