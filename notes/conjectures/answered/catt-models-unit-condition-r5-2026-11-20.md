# CaTT and its directed variant L as presentations of (∞,ω)-categories: relating the two comparisons, model structures and strict presentations

*A self-contained problem. No knowledge of the project that posed it is assumed. Everything under the "Known" headings is proved and is not re-asked.*

**What we are asking for.** Both comparisons are now proved (Known (X)): CaTT models localized at W are coinductively complete (∞,ω)-categories, and models of the directed variant L localized at W_L are coinductively complete oriented categories 𝒪. We ask for the whole remaining line: how the two comparisons relate to each other and to the published theories of oriented (∞,ω)-categories, whether they come from full model structures, and how far the passage from syntax to semantics can be made strict and explicit. Special computads or low dimensions are not what we want.

## Background

**CaTT.** CaTT (Finster–Mimram, LICS 2017) is a dependent type theory with one sort former, `⋆` and `arr A t u` (cells of type A from t to u), and one term former, `coh`. For a *pasting context* Θ (a context presenting a globular pasting diagram) and two parallel terms u, v over Θ satisfying a support (free-variable) condition, `coh Θ (u → v) σ` is a cell, for every substitution σ into Θ. Substitution is strict and syntactic. The models of CaTT are exactly Grothendieck–Maltsiniotis weak ω-categories: presheaves on the CaTT coherator sending globular sums to globular products (Benjamin–Finster–Mimram, arXiv 2106.04475, §3).

**A directed variant.** A variant L of CaTT keeps the same syntax but changes the side condition of `coh` so that, across objects, interchange is a directed cell (lax, not invertible). Its intended strict models are strict ω-categories with lax transformations (Ara–Maltsiniotis's ∞-Cat_lax, arXiv 1607.00668, Ex. C.18), and its intended homotopical models are oriented (∞,ω)-categories in the sense of Gepner–Heine (arXiv 2510.10504), whose homs carry Campion's Gray tensor (arXiv 2311.00205). The strict soundness of L is proved, using that cells of free strict ω-categories on Steiner complexes are determined by their supports.

**What is known about the homotopy theory.**
- Strict ω-categories have the Lafont–Métayer–Worytkiewicz model structure, cofibrantly generated by the boundary inclusions ∂Oₙ → Oₙ (arXiv 0712.0617, §4).
- Leinster's operad is the universal cofibrant replacement of the strict ω-category operad for that weak factorisation system (Garner, arXiv 0804.2663). Markakis extends this to generalised signatures: free algebras on computads are the cofibrant objects (arXiv 2303.11978).
- For Batanin–Leinster weak ω-categories, Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara prove 2-out-of-3 for ω-weak equivalences and call a (left semi-)model structure future work (arXiv 2406.13240, introduction). We know of no model structure on weak ω-categories.
- Cat_ω (Loubaton, arXiv 2406.05425, the inductive notion lim Cat_n) is presentable and cartesian closed (Prop. 2.2.1.54). Campion's Gray tensor is built on flagged (∞,∞)-categories and transferred to Cat_n for n ≤ ω and to the version with coinductive equivalences.
- CaTT's invertible cells are the coinductively invertible ones (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2406.12127).

**Known (I): the relative category and a strict coherent choice.**
- **A relative category.** Models of CaTT are equivalent to Batanin–Leinster weak ω-categories (Benjamin–Markakis–Sarti, arXiv 2405.00398, §7). With W the Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara ω-weak equivalences (2-out-of-6, arXiv 2406.13240 Thm 3.3.3, Cor. 3.3.4), 𝓗_CaTT := Mod(CaTT)[W⁻¹] exists. FHM (arXiv 2511.09849) characterise trivial fibrations as W ∩ ω-equifibrations (Prop. 4.1.5) and equifibrations as J-injectives (Thm 4.3.1). No (semi-)model structure is known.
- **Strict coherent choice, one direction.** Let 𝓜 be the injective complete Segal Θ_ω-space model category and 𝒜 its algebraically fibrant objects (Nikolaus). For each globular pasting shape P, yP is discrete and fibrant, so F Sp(P) → yP is a trivial fibration in 𝒜. Lifting the CaTT coherator's cell attachments against these gives R : 𝕋 → 𝒜 preserving globular sums, and S(Y) := 𝒜(R(−), Y) is a CaTT model with strict substitution and S(Y)_n = (UY)(D_n)₀. S sends weak equivalences to W, so it induces Cat_ω → 𝓗_CaTT. It has a formal left adjoint 𝓛_R(A) = ∫^P A(P) · R(P).
- **(2,2)-categories are presented** (Campbell, arXiv 1907.01999: 2-truncated (∞,2)-categories are bicategories; strictify).
- **Obstruction to the raw inductive nerve.** The coherent walking ω-equivalence E (Hadzihasanovic–Loubaton–Ozornova–Rovelli, arXiv 2404.14509, Thm 1.33) is a gaunt strict ω-category with E → 1 a folk weak equivalence, so no functor 𝓗_CaTT → Cat^ind_ω agrees with the raw inductive nerve on strict ω-categories. The Street nerve marking coinductively invertible cells does send E → 1 to an equivalence (Henry–Loubaton, arXiv 2301.11424, Prop. 4.58).
- **Free shapes agree.** Finite globular pasting shapes and their Gray products are the same free objects in the inductive and coinductive theories (Campion, arXiv 2311.00205, Cor. 3.5, Thm 3.14, Cor. 3.15).

**Known (II): coinductive completion and reconstruction tests.**
- **S factors through coinductive completion.** Write Q : Cat^ind_ω ⇄ Cat^coind_ω : j for coinductive completion. By Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde (arXiv 2603.11005: Lemma 5.9, Prop. 5.29 and 5.30, Cor. 5.31), completion is the localisation at ∞-surjective maps, and its units are ∞-surjective. S₀ sends every ∞-surjective map to W, because for an 𝒜-fibration, π₀-surjectivity of the Kan fibration Map(yDₙ, UY) → Map(Bₙ, UY) ×_{Map(Bₙ,UZ)} Map(yDₙ, UZ) gives strict globular boundary lifts, which are FHM trivial fibrations. Hence S ≃ S̄ ∘ Q with S̄ = S j.
- **The underived left adjoint is not relative.** The coend 𝓛_R ⊣ S₀ is an ordinary adjunction. For gaunt strict C with discrete nerve NC (canonically algebraically fibrant), π₀ Map(𝓛_R A, NC) ≅ Mod(CaTT)(A, C). Applied to the coherent walking equivalence w : E → 1, this would make Mod(1, E) → Mod(E, E) a bijection if 𝓛_R(w) were an equivalence, which is false. So 𝓛_R does not send W to inductive equivalences.
- **S₀ preserves categorical level.** If Y presents an (∞,n)-category, every cell of S₀(Y) above dimension n is coinductively invertible. The proof extends the cell strictly over N(Σ^{k−1} I), then uses that FHM weak equivalences reflect invertibility.
- **A necessary test for reconstruction.** If the ordinary counit ε_Y : 𝓛_R S₀(Y) → Y is an equivalence, then 𝒜(Y, NC) → Mod(CaTT)(S₀Y, C) is bijective for every gaunt strict C. In particular, for contractible Y every strict CaTT map S₀(Y) → E must be constant.
- **Marked targets.** Marking the coinductive equivalences of C does not change C. Localising at them gives QC (ORW Prop. 5.29).
- **Directed version, conditional.** Suppose the coherator of L has a Gray-enriched cellular presentation, and suppose the hom maps K(Q_a, Q_b) → K(Q_a, Y_b) are trivial fibrations. Then lifting into the enriched category of commuting squares gives an enriched R_L that strictly preserves composition. Still to be supplied are arity colimits in the semantic category, ordered tensor domains D^r ⊗ D^s (for example via a coloured operad), and invertibility declarations.

**Known (III): the counit and a conditional derived adjunction.**
- **The underived counit fails.** For every gaunt strict C with C → 1 a folk weak equivalence there is a contractible algebraically fibrant Y with S₀(Y) ≅ C (take Y = cosk₀ of the right Kan extension of C's reflexive globular set from the globes to Θ_ω, with fillers chosen to make NC → Y algebraic). For C = E this gives a non-constant S₀(Y) → E, so ε_Y : 𝓛_R S₀ Y → Y is not an equivalence. This is compatible with an equivalence 𝓗_CaTT ≃ Cat^coind_ω, since E ≃ 1 in 𝓗_CaTT and Q(NE) ≃ 1.
- **A conditional derived adjunction.** Let K be the computadic cofibrant-replacement comonad on Mod(CaTT) (Markakis, arXiv 2303.11978, Prop. 19, Cor. 21–22; its counit q and comultiplication are in W and K preserves and reflects W, Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara arXiv 2406.13240 Prop. 4.2.2). Assume

  ```
  (H)  f : B → B′ in W between computad-cofibrant models  ⇒  QU𝓛_R(f) is an equivalence.
  ```

  Then the co-Kleisli category of K (weak maps, Bourke–Garner) gives an adjunction Λ : 𝓗_CaTT ⇄ Cat^coind_ω : S̄ with Λ[A] ≃ QU𝓛_R(KA).
- **The reconstruction condition.** For Y presenting jC, the derived counit is QU(d_Y), with d_Y = ε_Y ∘ 𝓛_R(q_{S₀Y}). Since a map into a coinductively complete target is inverted by Q iff it is ∞-surjective, the counit condition is

  ```
  (R)  U𝓛_R K S₀ Y → UY is ∞-surjective.
  ```

- **The equivalence conjecture.** (H), (R) for every coinductively complete C, and the unit condition "A ← KA → S₀𝓛_R KA is an equivalence in 𝓗_CaTT" (for instance η_{KA} ∈ W) together imply 𝓗_CaTT ≃ Cat^coind_ω.
- **A correction.** Full faithfulness of S̄ restricted to a full subcategory 𝓑 implies that the counits at 𝓑 are equivalences only if Λ S̄(𝓑) ⊆ 𝓑.
- **Directed tensor arities.** For L, binary global operations through global dimension 3 have hom tensor domains D⁰ ⊗ D⁰, D¹ ⊗ D⁰, D⁰ ⊗ D¹, D² ⊗ D⁰, D⁰ ⊗ D² and D¹ ⊗ D¹. The last gives the directed interchanger. For α : f₀ ⇒ f₁ : x → y and β : g₀ ⇒ g₁ : y → z, in execution order, it goes (f₀◁β) ; (α▷g₁) ⇛ (α▷g₀) ; (f₁◁β): β first, then α, to α first, then β. In global dimension 4, the interchanger's naturality and the cube of three interchangers are 4-cells in an untruncated theory but equalities in a strict 3-truncation.

**Known (IV): units on graphs, detection, and the reduction to finite computads.**
- **Units on representables and graphs.** α_G : F_w G → F_s G is a globular trivial fibration for every globular set G, since B → T is cartesian and contractible. Hence η_{h_P} ∈ W for every coherator representable h_P = F_w G_P, and η_{F_w G} ∈ W for every graph G. The graph case uses 𝓛_R F_w G ≅ F|G| ≃ N Path(G).
- **Detection.** For f : X → Y in 𝒜, S₀(f) ∈ W ⇒ QU(f) is an equivalence. Algebraic identities and composites agree with geometric ones relative to boundaries, so algebraic invertibility gives geometric coinductive invertibility (ORW Prop. 5.42), and Q inverts the resulting maps (ORW Prop. 5.30).
- **One assertion suffices.** Let (U) be the assertion η_B ∈ W for every computad-cofibrant B. Then (U) implies (H), (R) and 𝓗_CaTT ≃ Cat^coind_ω. For (R), S₀(d_Y)η_{KA} = q_A. Moreover (U) reduces to
  ```
  (11)  η_{A_𝔠} ∈ W for every finite computad 𝔠,
  ```
  by filtered colimits of finite subcomputads (the algebraic colimit is W-equivalent to S₀ of the 𝒜-colimit; FHM Prop. 3.4.4). (11) is an infinite family.
- **The (∞,1) fragment.** Free models on computads with bounded generator dimensions and invertible cells above dimension 1 are free on graphs, so (H) holds between them. (R) for general Kan complexes or quasi-categories is not obtained: finite subcomputads of K S₀ Y can omit the inverse witnesses.
- **L, ordinary presentation.** The coherator of L is the colimit of free globular extensions, adjoining each admissible head once. Through global dimension 3 the generators are classified by active hom-dimension d:
  - r = 1: operation heads with d = 0;
  - r = 2: operation heads with d = 1, and coherence heads with d = 0;
  - r = 3: operation heads with d = 2, either one factor of dimension 2 or two of dimension 1 (the interchangers), and coherence heads with d ≤ 1.

  Whole-grid interchangers are separate generators. Composites of elementary ones are derived, and the two are linked by dimension-4 coherence heads when formally parallel. In dimension 4, the operation profiles are (3), (2,1), (1,2) and (1,1,1), the last being the three-interchanger cube. No Gray-enriched presentation was given.

**Known (V): the triangle, spaces, saturation and enrichment.**
- **The triangle reduces to relative coherence.** Let 𝔠_△ be the computad x, y, z; f : x → y, g : y → z, h : x → z; α : m(f, g) ⇒ h, with A_△ its free CaTT model and T_△ its free strict 2-category. The Θ-nerve of T_△ is yP ∪ yD₂ glued along yD₁ (P = [2]), a homotopy pushout, and so is the corresponding pushout of R-values; hence 𝓛_R A_△ is weakly equivalent to N T_△ and

  ```
  (4)  η_{A_△} ∈ W  ⟺  π : A_△ → T_△ ∈ W.
  ```

  π has strict boundary lifting in dimensions 0–3: all cells of A_△ above dimension 2 are invertible, and lifting in dimension 3 is the statement that parallel 2-terms with equal strict image are joined by a 3-term, which holds in every CaTT context. What remains is

  ```
  (5)  any two parallel r-terms of the CaTT context (x, y, z, f, g, h, α : f ∗₀ g ⇒ h), r ≥ 3, are joined by an (r+1)-term.
  ```

- **(5) holds for r = 3.** The context has a unique source x and sink z, and the two boundary paths share only their endpoints. A normal-form argument (for a 2-term with strict image α, exactly one 0-column of its arity carries α and spans x → z, all other columns factor literally through a point; normalisers built from this factorisation are compatible with every 3-term by induction on syntax) joins any two parallel 3-terms. So the first open case of (5) is r = 4.
- **Spaces.** For Y presenting a space, Z = QU𝓛_R K S₀ Y is a space, and the reconstruction map Z → Y is bijective on π₀, an isomorphism on π₁ and surjective on every π_n. Injectivity on π_n, n ≥ 2, follows from the **spherical representation condition**: every class of π_n(QU𝓛_R B, x), n ≥ 2, is represented by the interpretation of an algebraic n-cell with iterated identity boundary (only B = K S₀ Y is needed). Cellular approximation does not give this.
- **Saturation.** Units in the saturation W^sat give 𝓗 ≃ 𝒜[V⁻¹] for the class V = {f : S₀ f ∈ W^sat}, which is a further localisation of Cat^coind_ω. Equality with Cat^coind_ω needs

  ```
  (10)  S₀ f ∈ W^sat  ⇒  QU f is an equivalence.
  ```

  Two-out-of-six, retracts and filtered colimits do not formally imply saturation (an eight-element poset with a non-saturated class having all three closure properties). The condition

  ```
  (12)  pushouts of maps in Cof ∩ W remain in W
  ```

  (Cof the computad-cofibrations) would give W = W^sat, via the factorisation w = p i with p a trivial fibration.
- **Enrichment.** The semantic tensor arities for L exist in oriented (∞,ω)-categories (Gepner–Heine enriched suspension and wedge). A *strict* Gray enrichment of the raw coherator of L that represents cells is impossible: it would require the raw equation c(f, i(y)) = f for a composition head c and an identity head i, and CaTT-style syntax has no definitional equations. A weak enrichment is not ruled out.

**Known (VI): degree-2 representation, a saturation criterion and the two-square arity.**
- **(5) for r ≥ 4 is a relative-coherence statement.** It asks that parallel 4-terms of the 2-dimensional triangle context with equal strict image be joined. Extending the r = 3 normalisers needs, for each 4-term θ : a → b and chosen comparisons H_a : a → ν, a 5-cell θ ; H_b ⇒ H_a. This is not asked here.
- **Spherical representation in degree 2.** Let B be free on a computad with every positive-dimensional cell coinductively invertible, and X = g U 𝓛_R B (g = groupoidification). Every class of π₂(X, x) is the interpretation of a cell b : 1_x ⇒ 1_x of B.
  - *Filtration.* 𝓛_R(F_w D_n) = F(yD_n) and 𝓛_R(F_w ∂D_n) = F(∂_Θ D_n), so generator attachments become homotopy pushouts of S^{n−1} → D^n. This gives a relative CW model X₁ ⊆ X₂ ⊆ X with X₁ ≃ |G|, X₂ from the 2-generators and higher cells of dimension ≥ 3, so π₂X₁ = 0 and π₂X₂ → π₂X is onto.
  - *Crossed modules.* The homotopy bigroupoid H₂B (2-cells modulo invertible 3-cells; FHM Prop. 2.4.6, Thm 2.4.8, Cor. 2.4.9) maps to Π₂X by a pseudofunctor s whose coherence is checked in generic shapes. Over the free groupoid F = π₁(X₁, G₀), an algebraic crossed module M_B with ker ∂_x = Aut(1_x) in H₂B maps to π₂(X, X₁, G₀). Composed with j from the free crossed module C ≅ π₂(X₂, X₁, G₀) (Whitehead; Brown–Razak Salleh Thm 5.1), it is the canonical map, checked on generators.
  - *Consequence.* For Y presenting a space, d : QU𝓛_R K S₀ Y → Y is an isomorphism on π₀, π₁ and π₂ and surjective on every π_n. Injectivity uses the literal-boundary lift of a nullhomotopy through the Kan fibration Map(yD₃, UY) → Map(∂_Θ D₃, UY) and the trivial fibration q_A.
- **A saturation criterion.** Let 𝒞ⁿ = F_w D_n and j_n : 𝒞ⁿ → ℰⁿ⁺¹ the source inclusion into the FHM weak walking (n+1)-equivalence, so J = {j_n} has J^⋔ = equifibrations and W ∩ J^⋔ = TF (FHM arXiv 2511.09849, Prop. 5.0.4, Prop. 4.1.5). Consider
  ```
  (FEA)  A_𝔠 → A_𝔠 ∐_{𝒞ⁿ} ℰⁿ⁺¹ lies in W, for every finite computad 𝔠, every n and every map 𝒞ⁿ → A_𝔠.
  ```
  (FEA) implies W = W^sat, hence the detection statement (10). Proof: (FEA) extends to cofibrant sources by filtered colimits. Every relative J-cell complex with cofibrant source is in W. A trivial cofibration with cofibrant source is a retract of such a complex (factor through J, then use W ∩ J^⋔ = TF). This gives the precofibration-category axiom CF3, and Rădulescu-Banu (arXiv math/0610009) Thm 7.2.7 gives saturation from 2-out-of-6. Unconditionally, every J-cofibration i : A → B has a strict retraction (A → 1 is an equifibration). (FEA) contains j_n ∈ W itself, which FHM prove only after strict reflection.
- **The two-square arity of L.** For Γ = ΣΔ₂ ∨ ΣΔ₁ (α₁ : f₀ ⇒ f₁, α₂ : f₁ ⇒ f₂ : x → y, β : g₀ ⇒ g₁ : y → z), U_Γ = Δ₂ ⊗ Δ₁ has a canonical 2-cell ρ : P ⇒ Q with P = (f₀◁β) ; (α₁▷g₁) ; (α₂▷g₁) and Q = (α₁▷g₀) ; (α₂▷g₀) ; (f₂◁β), the paste of two elementary squares through M = (α₁▷g₀) ; (f₁◁β) ; (α₂▷g₁).
  - The raw coherator has an independent whole-grid head G_Γ : P ⇒ Q distinct from the elementary paste E_Γ, and evaluating both in the unchanged strict arity identifies them.
  - Adjoining a second parallel 2-generator g ↦ ρ (U_Γ⁺ → U_Γ) is not a folk weak equivalence: g and ρ have the same image and no 3-cell joins them.
  - Attaching the strict walking 3-equivalence along ρ, Û_Γ = U_Γ ∐_{D²} E_s³, gives a folk trivial cofibration U_Γ → Û_Γ and a folk weak equivalence Û_Γ → U_Γ. Its 2-skeleton is U_Γ⁺, and its new 3-generators supply the global 4-dimensional equivalence between G_Γ and E_Γ.
  - This is a repair of one arity. Substitution maps between resolved arities, weak Gray composition, the augmentation to semantic arities, and the model comparison are not constructed. The coloured-operad Boardman–Vogt route (Berger–Moerdijk) does not apply directly: the directed Gray tensor has no braiding.

**The coh rule of L (two-tier).** Contexts are CaTT pasting contexts. Each decomposes uniquely as Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ, with objects a₀, …, aₙ and Θᵢ the pasting scheme in the hom from aᵢ₋₁ to aᵢ.
- **Gray unfolding.** U_{ij}(Γ) := Θᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ Θⱼ (factors in execution order) is the Steiner tensor of the Steiner complexes λ(Θ). Its generators are tensors of generators, dimensions add, and the boundary is the Leibniz formula. It is strongly loop-free, so its cells are determined by their supports.
- **Supports.** For a term t : A with endpoints aᵢ, aⱼ, supp(t : A) is the downward-closed set of generators of U_{ij}(Γ) used by the strict image of t and of the cells in its type, computed in the free strict Gray-enriched ω-category on Γ.
- **The rule.** coh_{Γ, t →_A u}[σ] is well formed when either
  - (operation) supp(t : A) = ∂⁻U_{ij}(Γ), supp(u : A) = ∂⁺U_{ij}(Γ), and dim A = dim U_{ij}(Γ) (so the cell has dimension dim U_{ij}(Γ) + 1); or
  - (coherence) supp(t : A) = U_{ij}(Γ) = supp(u : A).

  **Here (i, j) = (0, n) is required** (proper intervals would let a 1-head over ΣD⁰ ∨ ΣD^{m−1} depend formally on an m-dimensional argument, destroying the filtration by output dimension).
- **Consequences.**
  - On a single hom (n = 1) this is CaTT.
  - For Γ = ΣD¹ ∨ ΣD¹ there is no horizontal composite of α and β. There is an operation-case 3-cell (f◁β) ; (α▷g′) ⇛ (α▷g) ; (f′◁β), and none in the reverse direction.
  - The intended strict model is ∞-Cat_lax (strict ω-categories with lax transformations), where the rule is sound.
  - "Two-tier" means interchange is invertible inside a hom and directed across objects.

**Known (VII): torsion-free computads, and the homotopy hypothesis.**
- **Torsion-free computads reduce to relative coherence.** Let 𝔠 be a finite computad coming from a torsion-free complex in Campion's sense (arXiv 2311.00200; this includes strongly loop-free Steiner complexes), with A = A_𝔠, T = F(𝔠) and π : A → T. Then
  ```
  η_A ∈ W  ⟺  π ∈ W  ⟺  π is a globular trivial fibration  ⟺  RC,
  ```
  where RC says that any two parallel n-cells of A with equal image in T are joined by an (n+1)-cell, for every n. Proof:
  - S₀(e_𝔠) ∘ η = π, where e_𝔠 : 𝓛_R A → NT is the adjunct of π. It is a semantic equivalence by cellular induction, since Campion's Thm 5.11 says the strict attachments of a torsion-free complex are homotopy pushouts.
  - Endo-cells of T are identities (Campion Def. 1.8, Prop. 1.10), so RC fillers map to identities.
  - A boundary-correction lemma whiskers by such fillers one dimension at a time. Together with initial lifts by induction on strict expressions, this gives boundary lifting.
  - Conversely, FHM essential surjectivity at 1_{πa} gives RC.

  So (11) for torsion-free computads is exactly RC in all dimensions. For the triangle this is Known (V), (4)–(5).
- **(11) implies the homotopy hypothesis for a specified coherator.** Let 𝕋^bi be Lanari's theory 𝕋^W (arXiv 1809.07923, Def. 4.3): the CaTT coherator with freely adjoined, independent left and right inverses and cancellation cells for every positive-dimensional cell, J ⊣ V : Mod(𝕋^bi) → Mod(CaTT). Write (U_gpd) for "η_B ∈ W for every groupoidal computad-cofibrant B, infinite computads included". Then
  ```
  (11) for all finite computads  ⟹  (U_gpd)  ⟹  𝕋^bi is a Grothendieck groupoid coherator satisfying Henry's pushout condition,
  ```
  and Mod(𝕋^bi)[W_bi⁻¹] ≃ Mod(CaTT)_gpd[W_gpd⁻¹] ≃ 𝒮 (spaces). Proof outline:
  - A reflection principle: for groupoidal computad-cofibrant B, B′, a map f is in W iff its realization E(f) = gQU𝓛_R(f) is an equivalence. This uses a completion map that preserves algebraic fillers.
  - The inverse-data attachments β_k : 𝔻_k → I_k are inverted by E: the fibre is a product of two contractible homotopy fibres of composition with a path.
  - Bourke's algebraic-injectivity factorizations (arXiv 1811.09532, Thms 4.3–4.4).
  - Henry's Thm 5.3.5 and Cor. 5.3.13 (arXiv 1609.04622).
  So (11) in general is at least as hard as the homotopy hypothesis for 𝕋^bi.
- **The groupoidal restriction is vacuous on finite computads.** A finite ordinary computad has a groupoidal free model only if it has no positive-dimensional generators: a top generator has nonempty support, so it is not invertible (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv 2406.12127, Prop. 37). So the infinite quantifier in (U_gpd) is essential.
- **Not known:** a converse for (11) itself (the converse for (U_gpd) is Known (VIII)(A)); the comparison of 𝕋^bi with an arbitrary Grothendieck groupoid coherator; (11) for any finite computad with torsion beyond those in Known (IV)–(VI).


**Known (VIII): the groupoidal equivalence, the semi-model structure, and the two missing comparisons.**
- **(A) The groupoidal condition is exactly the homotopy hypothesis for 𝕋^bi.**
  ```
  (U_gpd)  ⟺  𝕋^bi is a Grothendieck groupoid coherator satisfying Henry's pushout condition.
  ```
  The converse uses: an algebraic completion map r_Z (factor UZ → Z^c and extend Z's algebraic fillers along the monomorphism), giving S₀(f) ∈ W ⟺ QU(f) equivalence; Φ(B) is a space for groupoidal cofibrant B (every characteristic globe factors through the core); v_B ∈ W by Lanari's Prop. 4.5 with c_Z = ε^{bi}_Z ∘ J q_{VZ}; and identification of the realization with Henry's comparison (a colimit-preserving endofunctor of spaces fixing the point is the identity). # Researcher Response 1

Relative to Known (X), both algebra categories have full cofibrantly generated model structures, and every object is fibrant. There is a change of coherator from L to CaTT. Restriction along it presents the fully faithful inclusion of ordinary higher categories into oriented ones. ## The tensor and the map of theories

Write $V$ for coinductively complete higher categories, $i:V\to\mathcal O$ for change of enrichment along the lax monoidal identity with structure map $c_{P,Q}:P\otimes Q\to P\times Q$, and $a$ for its left adjoint. Here and below a string of tensor factors is in execution order. For the two edges $u,v$ of the generating square the Steiner differential is
$$
 d(u\otimes v)=u_1\otimes v+u\otimes v_0-u_0\otimes v-u\otimes v_1 .
$$
Thus its source traverses the second factor first. This is the square of Known (III). Cube orientations in higher ranks follow by the tensor differential with the Koszul sign. Gepner–Heine define their ordered strict cube by exactly this Steiner tensor and the differential target minus source. [Their strict cube construction, §2.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29373v1#S2.SS6) Campion extends this tensor on strict cubes by the biclosed universal property. In Gepner–Heine's notation the ordered tensor is the unbarred $\boxtimes$, with $P\boxtimes(-)\dashv\mathrm{Fun}^{\rm lax}(P,-)$. Their oriented category is **right** enriched in this product, equivalently left enriched in the reversed product. Their Remark 3.2.3 displays composition in execution order. Loubaton's lax transformation with domain $P$ uses the cylinder $P\otimes[1]$. Loubaton also defines an enhanced product $X\mathbin{\ominus}Y$ by a pushout using $m_b([a,n])=[a\times b,n]$ (Construction 2.3.1.17). It agrees with his Gray product for a 1-categorical second parameter. The tensor used for enrichment here is Campion’s monoidal extension from the cubes. [Campion, Theorem A and §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00205) · [Gepner–Heine, §§1.6, 3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10504v2#S3.SS2) · [Loubaton, §§1.2.3, 2.3.1 and Chapter 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05425)

More precisely, start with Gepner–Heine's oriented categories, pass on the homs from their flagged base to its univalent completion and then to its hypercompletion, and complete the outer space of objects. The essential image of the right adjoint consists of objects with hypercomplete homs and complete outer object space. Indeed a monoidal localization of the base changes the homs of an enriched algebra by applying the localization to its composition operations. Maps to a local algebra before and after that change agree by adjunction on each tensor profile, compatibly with substitution and units. Outer completion has the same universal property for univalent targets and preserves the homs between the original objects. This identifies the reflection with $Q_o$ of Known (X). Loubaton's inductive limit theory still needs the coinductive localization $Q$. For example the inductively gaunt walking coherent equivalence of Known (I) becomes a point after $Q$. In the terminology of Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde the relevant base before $Q$ is the right higher categories. Their dictionary identifies hypercompleteness with coinductive completeness. [Gepner–Heine, §§2.5, 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10504v2#S2.SS5) · [Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde, Remark 2.2 and §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11005v1#S2.SS1)

Gepner–Heine's Theorem 4.2.7(2) gives the image test. For every three objects and every pair of globe labels in their consecutive homs, composition must factor through the comparison from the Gray product of the two globes to their cartesian product. Factorization is in the higher category. The epimorphism theorem for the comparison makes the space of such factorizations contractible whenever inhabited. The test survives completion: maps from the localized products to a local hom are the original mapping spaces by monoidal localization. Conversely, in an object passing the test the cartesian-enriched algebra recovered by the theorem has the given local homs and the same complete object space, so it is in $V$. Indeed the suspended walking-equivalence tests are exactly the tests on homs. For the unsuspended test the two cancellation cells for a global coinductive inverse are equivalences in the completed homs. They give inverse laws in the underlying $(\infty,1)$-category, and outer completeness identifies the resulting equivalence with an object path. Explicitly, $a(Z)=Q\,\mathrm{Uv}(\lVert j_o Z\rVert_{\boxtimes})$, where $j_o$ includes the completed oriented objects, the norm is Gepner–Heine’s oriented realization and $\mathrm{Uv}$ is ordinary univalent completion. This follows by mapping into each local object and applying the three adjunctions. [Gepner–Heine, Theorems 4.1.10, 4.2.7 and Definition 4.2.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10504v2#S4.SS2) · [Ozornova–Rovelli–Walde, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11005v1#S5)

There is no right adjoint to $i$. Glue two suspensions of $[1]$ end to end. The extreme hom of the pushout is $[1]\times[1]$ in the cartesian theory and $[1]\otimes[1]$ in the oriented theory, by free enriched composition. In the second hom the two paths across the square are joined by a noninvertible 2-cell. In the first they are equal. Between opposite vertices the Gray square has a walking arrow as its hom, and the cartesian square has a point. Both homs are already coinductively complete. Hence $i$ fails to preserve this pushout. [Gepner–Heine, Corollary 2.2.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10504v2#S2.SS2)

Write $h^w_\Gamma$ for the free ordinary weak algebra of a pasting diagram and $P_\Gamma$ for its free strict ordinary category. The free strict oriented wedge $G_\Gamma$ has a canonical map
$$
                  c_\Gamma:G_\Gamma\longrightarrow iP_\Gamma .
$$
It fixes the diagram of generators and on a forward block it is the many-factor tensor comparison. It exhibits $aG_\Gamma=P_\Gamma$: mapping the wedge into $iZ$ chooses the same consecutive labels as mapping the ordinary pasting diagram into $Z$.

Construct $t:\mathbb T_L\to\mathbb T$ in the free cellular order of the directed heads. Fix the globes and their globular sums. Suppose a head over $\Gamma$ has strictly interpreted cell $z$ in $G_\Gamma$. At its already translated source and target lift $c_\Gamma(z)$ along $h^w_\Gamma\to P_\Gamma$. This is a globular trivial fibration by Known (IV), since a pasting arity is free on its globular set of generators. The lifting square commutes by induction on the faces. In particular the construction works even when a direct CaTT head at the proposed type would fail its support test. For every substitution the strict evaluations commute with the maps $c_\Gamma$, by induction on its term expressions. We identify the induced adjunction after constructing the model structures.

## Arbitrary sources in the model axioms

Use $I$ for the free globe boundary maps in the theory under consideration. Both categories of models are locally presentable categories of models of small finite-limit sketches. Thus the small object argument applies to the sets below, including countably generated codomains. We use the following recognition argument. If $J\subset I$-cof, $J$-cof $\subset W$, and $J^\pitchfork\cap W=I^\pitchfork$, then $I$-cof $\cap W=J$-cof. Indeed, factor a map in the intersection as a $J$-cell map followed by a $J$-injective. Two-out-of-three puts the second map in $I^\pitchfork$, and lifting makes the original map a retract of the first. The two small object factorizations and the retract and two-out-of-three properties give the full model axioms. Here $W$ denotes the appropriate intrinsic class.

**Acyclicity lemma for the two raw theories.** Write $N B(P)=\mathrm{Hom}(h_P,B)$, $G_P$ for the strict semantic arity, and $E B=\operatorname{colim}_{h_P\to B}G_P$ for the colimit in the completed semantic category. We use two facts from [the previous round, Responses 15–16](../answered/catt-models-unit-condition-r4-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-15). On *all* algebras the chart functor $E$ preserves and detects intrinsic weak equivalences. On computad-cofibrants it agrees naturally with completed realization. The first fact matters below for the target of a lifting problem of unrestricted size and cofibrancy.

Suppose each member $j:C\to D$ of a set $J$ is an $I$-cofibration between computad-cofibrants, $E j$ is an equivalence, and $j$ has a strict retraction. Then **every** $J$-cofibration is an intrinsic weak equivalence. We give the proof, including how to make a semantic chart cocone usable in a strict lifting square.

Fix any algebra $B$. On raw arities let $H(P)$ be a simplicial model of $\mathrm{Map}(G_P,E B)$ which sends globular sums to strict limits and has Kan fibrations as globe matching maps. Concretely take a fibrant algebraic representative of the local object $E B$, a Reedy fibrant right frame $Y_\bullet$ on it, and $H(P)=\mathrm{Hom}(R P,Y_\bullet)$, using $R_L$ on the directed side. The arities are realized by cofibrant resolutions of $G_P$ preserving globular sums. Boundary-to-globe realizations are cofibrations, so the frame has the claimed mapping and matching properties. For any computad-cofibrant $D$, the simplicial set of strict maps $N D\to H$ is $\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal L_R D,Y_\bullet)$ by the coend adjunction. It computes $\mathrm{Map}(E D,E B)$ since $\mathcal L_R D$ is cofibrant and $Y_\bullet$ represents a local target.

The identity of $E B$ gives a coherent transformation $N B\to H$ by the chart colimit. Resolve the discrete presheaf $N B$ by its simplicial replacement $M$. In degree $k$ it is the coproduct of $yP_0$ over strings $P_0\to\cdots\to P_k$ with a label in $N B(P_k)$. It is projectively cofibrant: each nondegenerate string attaches a representable times a simplex along its whole boundary. At a fixed arity, the augmentation to $N B$ has contractible fibres by inserting the identity factorization of the given element. Therefore the coherent transformation is represented by a strict map $M\to H$. In each degree $M$ preserves the nonempty connected globular sums as limits: compatible restrictions in the coproduct must have the same summand tag, and that summand is representable.

Form the objectwise double mapping cylinder $K=N B\amalg_{M\times\{0\}}(M\times\Delta^1)\amalg_{M\times\{1\}}H$, with endpoint identifications along the two maps just described. Then $H\to K$ is an objectwise weak equivalence by homotopy pushout. On the inverse category of globe faces, take an objectwise trivial monomorphism $K|_{\rm globes}\hookrightarrow T|_{\rm globes}$ into a Reedy fibrant diagram (factor the terminal map). Define $T(P)$ by the strict limit of these globe values over the generating cells and faces of $P$.

The resulting map $K(P)\to T(P)$ is a **trivial monomorphism**. Only nonempty pasting arities occur as domains of head operations. For injectivity work in one simplicial degree. A simplex in the cylinder is at one endpoint or has a nonconstant time coordinate. Its restriction to any generating globe remembers which case, and in the last case remembers that same time coordinate. All restrictions together recover the simplex since $N B,H,M$ preserve the globular sum. For the weak equivalence, $H(P)\to T(P)$ is a weak equivalence because both sides compute homotopy face limits of objectwise equivalent globe diagrams. Explicitly, assemble the diagram one generating globe at a time along its boundary. The restriction maps of both diagrams to the full boundary are matching fibrations, so each gluing gives a homotopy pullback. Now use $H(P)\to K(P)$ and two-out-of-three.

Extend $T$ as a strict simplicial algebra over the free raw heads. At a head $D_n\to P$, the map already specified on $K(P)$ and the translated faces on $T(P)$ give a lifting square for the trivial monomorphism just proved against $T(D_n)\to T(\partial D_n)$. A lift defines the operation. The map out of $K$ commutes with this head, and freeness extends the construction to substitutions. Thus we have strict maps of simplicial presheaves $N B\xrightarrow{\rho}T\leftarrow H$, the latter an objectwise equivalence, and $T$ is a strict simplicial algebra. In the presheaf infinity-category $\rho$ represents the original chart cocone, by the cylinder homotopy. In particular its adjunct under the chart colimit is an equivalence $E B\to E B$.

Write $T(D)$ also for the simplicial set of strict maps from an algebra $D$ into $T$ degreewise. Matching fibrancy implies $T(D)\to T(C)$ is a Kan fibration for every $I$-cofibration $C\to D$, by lifting one free globe at a time and taking retracts. For computad-cofibrant $D$, the map $H(D)\to T(D)$ is a weak equivalence. The same cellular induction uses homotopy pullbacks at successor stages and limits of towers of fibrations at limit stages, starting at a point. In particular $T(j)$ is a trivial Kan fibration for every $j$ in the lemma. Consequently the endpoint map of *ordinary algebras*
$$
                (T^{\Delta^1})_0\longrightarrow T_0\times T_0
$$
is $J$-injective. Its lifting problem at $j$ is the boundary-of-an-interval lifting problem for $T(j)$.

Take $b:A\to B$ in $J$-cof. Every terminal map is $J$-injective by the specified retractions, so $b$ has a strict retraction $r$. Use $\rho br,\rho$ as the two endpoints at $B$ and the constant path at $A$. Lifting against the endpoint map gives a homotopy of presheaf maps $\rho N(br)\simeq\rho$. By the chart adjunction and invertibility of the adjunct of $\rho$ this gives $E(br)\simeq 1$. Also $rb=1$, so $E b$ is an equivalence. Detection by $E$ proves the lemma. This argument makes no cofibrancy assumption on $A$ or $B$.

**The ordinary generators.** Apply the lemma to the FHM generators $j_n$. Their computadic inclusions, their strict identity retractions, and $j_n\in W$ are Known (VI) and (X). The equality $W\cap J^\pitchfork=I^\pitchfork$ is the FHM trivial fibration theorem. Recognition gives the full model structure with the stated fibrations and all objects fibrant. [Fujii–Hoshino–Maehara, §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09849)

**The directed generators.** In positive hom ranks use the free bipointed directed suspensions of the ordinary $j_n$ for all $n\geq0$, forgetting the bipointing after forming the maps. A map out of such a suspension chooses two poles and an ordinary map to their fixed hom. On cofibrant ordinary computads $D$ its realization is naturally $\Sigma E D$ under its two poles. Indeed the universal labelling $D\to (sD)(0,1)$, followed by ordinary realization and the semantic bipointed suspension adjunction, supplies the comparison. On globes it is the suspended generating globe map. Both constructions preserve the defining cellular pushouts and their transfinite colimits in bipointed objects, by Known (X) on cofibrants and the free suspension adjunction. Induction over the cells proves the assertion, including the two-pole initial object. Thus the suspended $j_n$ satisfy the acyclicity lemma.

One additional generator lifts equivalences of *objects*. Begin with two points $x,y$ and arrows $f:x\to y$, $g,h:y\to x$. In the hom at $x$ attach a copy of the ordinary FHM walking equivalence $\mathcal E^1$ with endpoints $gf,1_x$. In the hom at $y$ attach a second copy with endpoints $fh,1_y$. Here juxtaposition is usual function order, using fixed global binary and identity operations. These are pushouts of the suspended inclusion of both endpoints into $\mathcal E^1$. Call the resulting directed computad $D$ and use $j_*:\{x\}\to D$. This is a relative globe complex. It retracts to $x$ using the weak identity and its unitor equivalences, with their inverse data. More generally a map out of $D$ specifies a global arrow together with independent left and right inverse data. Any coinductively invertible global arrow admits such a labelling: take its two cancellations in the fixed ordinary homs and classify their invertibility by maps from $\mathcal E^1$. Conversely the two inverse data give an invertible global arrow. If a common inverse convention is used, the two inverses are equivalent by composing the cancellations and unit and associativity comparisons, so the two conventions give the same property.

To check $E j_*$, map $E D$ into any completed oriented $Z$. All attachments are cellular between cofibrants, so they calculate homotopy pushouts. The suspension calculation and $E\mathcal E^1\simeq *$ identify each added labelling with a path between the indicated points of the core of a hom. This keeps both endpoints: restriction is equivalent to the diagonal of that core. The two chosen composites evaluate as ordinary composition in the underlying category of $Z$, since the binary arrow arity and the identity arity have point forward and unit homs, respectively. Thus the mapping space is the space of $x,y,f,g,h$ with paths $gf\simeq 1_x$, $fh\simeq 1_y$ there. For fixed $f$ this space of inverse data is empty unless $f$ is an equivalence. For an equivalence, each factor is the homotopy fibre over an identity of composition with $f$ on mapping spaces, hence contractible. Finally choice of $y$ and an equivalence from a specified $x$ to $y$ is contractible by completeness of the object space. This proves $E j_*$ is an equivalence by Yoneda.

Let $J_L$ be this set of directed generators. An $I$-injective belongs to $W_L$ by literal globular lifting, and lifts against $J_L\subset I$-cof. Conversely let $p$ be $J_L$-injective in $W_L$. On objects, choose a representative up to an invertible arrow by essential surjectivity, classify that arrow by $D$, and use $j_*$ to obtain an object with precisely the requested image. At given poles the hom map is an ordinary weak equivalence and lifts against every ordinary $j_n$ by the suspension adjunction. The FHM characterization gives literal boundary lifts there, which account for every positive global dimension. Thus $W_L\cap J_L^\pitchfork=I^\pitchfork$. Known (X) gives two-out-of-six and the acyclicity lemma gives $J_L$-cof $\subset W_L$. Recognition gives the full directed model structure, with fibrations $J_L^\pitchfork$. Its fibrant objects are all directed algebras.

## Identification of the derived change of coherator

The ordinary extension and restriction adjunction is $t_!\dashv t^*$, with $t_!$ going from directed to ordinary algebras. It sends free globes and boundary inclusions to their ordinary counterparts, by the universal property of a free labelling, and preserves their pushouts. On charts the maps $c_\Gamma$ give a natural comparison
$$
                     a E_L B\longrightarrow E_w(t_! B).
$$
Indeed, applying $t_!$ to a chart produces an ordinary chart. The comparison on each arity is the isomorphism $aG_\Gamma\simeq P_\Gamma$ above, natural on raw substitutions by the strict evaluation equation for $t$. Both sides send cellular attachments on cofibrants to homotopy pushouts and cellular unions to colimits, using Known (X) and preservation of cellular attachments by $t_!$. The comparison is therefore an equivalence on every computad-cofibrant by induction and retracts, including on the boundaries used in the induction (build these out of lower globes). In particular $t_!$ sends $J_L$ to ordinary trivial cofibrations. It is left Quillen. Its left derived functor corresponds to $a$ by the comparison just constructed. Uniqueness of right adjoints now identifies $t^*$ with $i$ under the two equivalences of Known (X). All algebras are fibrant, so ordinary restriction already computes this right derived functor. This proves commutation on maps as well as objects, identifies the left adjoint, and transports the pushout obstruction to a further right adjoint.

## Which objects have strict presentations?

Here strict means that the algebra is obtained by evaluating the soundness interpretation in a strict $\omega$-category, or in a category strictly enriched in strict $\omega$-categories with the directed Gray tensor. Ordinary set-valued models of either coherator already have strict substitution. The extra requirement concerns the target of that substitution.

The following is one all-dimensional classification by relations and boundary tests. It uses a single fixed computad for a given input. In particular the size of a possible strict presentation and the domains of all tests are specified before choosing any relations.

1. Choose any raw representative $X$ of the semantic object (for example its singular algebra), and set $\kappa=\max(\aleph_0,|\coprod_n X_n|)$. Form $P_X\to X$ in increasing dimension. At *each* already constructed sphere and for each cell of $X$ filling its image, put $\kappa$ generators with exactly those faces and that label. Include the empty sphere in degree zero. Each skeleton here includes the cells freely generated in it. Taking the union gives a globular trivial fibration, with at most $\kappa$ cells.
2. Let $S_X$ be the free **strict** interpretation of this computad. At each generator attach the strict globe along the strict evaluations of the attaching terms. Denote by $u:P_X\to S_X$ the evaluation of raw terms, viewing a strict object as a raw model. Range over subsets $R\subseteq\coprod_n (S_X)_n^2$. Compute $S_X/R$ in the strict theory, by imposing the indicated equations between the two maps from the strict $n$-globe for each pair in $R$. This means a strict coequalizer, including any compositions enabled by the identifications.
3. The input admits a strict presentation **if and only if** there exists a single $R$ satisfying the following test in every rank. For every sphere $s$ of $P_X$ and every cell $d$ of $S_X/R$ whose boundary is the image of $s$, some $p$ with boundary *exactly* $s$ evaluates to $d$. In degree zero require surjectivity on objects.

These constructions use sets. For the directed strict theory one can construct the free objects and quotients by the term presentation of strict enrichment. Use identities and compositions in each strict hom, the global units, and the values of enriched composition on the cells of $D^p\otimes D^q$ labelling a pair of hom cells, subject to the face, functor, unit and associativity equations. The finite strict polygraphs of the globes and their tensor products supply these operations and equations: every tensor is generated by pairs of cells of its factors, with the compositional identifications in each variable, by separate cocontinuity of the strict Gray tensor and the strict globe-pasting presentation. Equivalently one can use compositions on tuples of finite labelled strict arities, imposing restriction along maps of arities. Either description has countably many finitary operations and gives the same strict enriched functors. In particular a subset of at most $\kappa$ cells generates at most $\kappa$ strict cells. For ordinary algebras the same bound follows directly from the finitary globular operations.

**Proof of the test.** A successful test gives a span of trivial fibrations $X\leftarrow P_X\to S_X/R$. For necessity suppose $X$ is equivalent to a strict presentation $C$. First produce a span $X\xleftarrow{p}U\xrightarrow{q} C$ of globular trivial fibrations. All objects are fibrant in the model structures just proved. A cofibrant replacement of $X$ admits an actual weak equivalence to $C$ representing the chosen isomorphism in the homotopy category. Factor its map to $X\times C$ as a trivial cofibration followed by a fibration. Both projections from the middle object are fibrations, and both are equivalences by two-out-of-three. The two trivial-fibration characterizations identify these with globular trivial fibrations.

Shrink the span simultaneously on its middle and strict ends. Start with empty generating subsets. Close the middle subset under raw operations and faces, and include its image under $q$ in the strict subset, closed under strict operations and faces. For every sphere in the middle subalgebra include one lift in $U$ for *each* compatible target cell on the $X$ side, and one for each compatible cell on the current strict side. Repeat countably many times, always including the empty sphere. Every stage has size at most $\kappa$, and every sphere and individual target cell occur at a finite stage of this process. Finitarity makes the unions a raw subalgebra $U_0$ and a strict subobject $C_0$. Thus $X\leftarrow U_0\to C_0$ still consists of globular trivial fibrations. In particular every strictly presentable input has a strict presentation of size at most $\kappa$.

Build $P_X\to U_0$ over $X$ one rank at a time. For each sphere and fixed label in $X$, the set of lifts in $U_0$ at the chosen image sphere is nonempty and has cardinal at most $\kappa$. Map the $\kappa$ generators onto that set. This makes $P_X\to U_0$, hence $P_X\to C_0$, a globular trivial fibration. By strict freeness the latter factors through a strict $v:S_X\to C_0$. Use as $R$ all equal-image cell pairs for $v$. We claim $S_X/R\cong C_0$, including its compositions. Every cell of $C_0$ lifts by induction on dimension. Any strict map out of $S_X$ equalizing $R$ therefore defines a unique function on the cells of $C_0$. This function commutes with faces. To see it preserves operations, lift a compatible diagram of inputs in $C_0$ first through $P_X\to C_0$, choosing generators' faces before their interiors, and evaluate those labels in $S_X$. For ordinary strict composition these input shapes are the globular pasting diagrams. For directed composition on $D^p\otimes D^q$ one lifts the two input globes at their shared poles. Preserving pure hom compositions and these pairs implies preservation of the entire strict enriched composition by strict functoriality and generation of the tensor. The descended function thus preserves every operation. This is the coequalizer universal property, proving the claim and necessity. The criterion only quantifies over relations on the prescribed $S_X$ and individual globular cells. It is a recognition theorem for arbitrary objects, which need not have effective presentations.

For example the strict condition excludes the space $P_3 S^2$. A strict presentation of this groupoidal object would be a strict category with weakly invertible cells, since the comparison preserves and reflects coinductive invertibility. Its 3-truncation identifies parallel 3-cells joined by a 4-cell and retains the lower cells. Strict compositions descend by interchange. The projection presents the same 3-type: up through degree three it induces the defining globular homotopy groups, and above degree three the groups vanish. For groupoidal objects the comparison with spaces computes these groups by cells with iterated identity boundary modulo the next cells, as in [the previous round, Response 1](../answered/catt-models-unit-condition-r4-2026-11-20.md#researcher-response-1). This contradicts Simpson's obstruction for a strict 3-category with weak inverses. Its directed suspension is also excluded from strict Gray presentations: the strict hom between representatives of its two poles would strictly present $P_3 S^2$, by the fixed-hom description of Known (X). [Simpson, Proposition 5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9810059) · [Ara, §6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.2945)

## The unit and counit without term search

Fix the two realization diagrams and their singular functors as in Known (X). Use $R,S,L$ for either triple, $q:K\to1$ for the computadic replacement counit (here $1$ is the identity functor), and $Q$ for the relevant semantic completion. For a finite computad $B$, start with the initial algebraically fibrant object and, for each generator of rank $n$, push out $L(\partial h_{D_n})\to R D_n$ at the evaluations of its prescribed faces. The boundary for rank zero is initial. This finite list of pushouts gives $L B$ itself, since $L$ is a left adjoint. In coend coordinates the unit at any term $t\in B(P)$ is the actual map
$$
                  R P\longrightarrow L B,\qquad r\longmapsto[t,r].
$$
Here bracket notation denotes the coprojection of the $t$-summand; it also applies in a nonconcrete realization category. The formula respects a substitution $k:P'\to P$ by $[B(k)t,r']=[t,R(k)r']$. On a generator it is the characteristic map of the corresponding pushout. Since $B$ is cofibrant this is the raw representative of the derived unit, followed on the semantic side by the identification induced by completion.

For any local object presented by a fibrant $Y$, the derived counit is represented by $Q U$ applied to
$$
 L K S Y\longrightarrow Y,\qquad [b,r]\longmapsto (q b)(r).
$$
This is a prescription on every summand: $q b$ is a label in $S Y(P)$ and hence a map $R P\to Y$. If $Y$ represents the completed realization of a finite computad, the formula applies with the same finite characteristic maps on its realization side. The source $K S Y$ can be taken explicitly by adjoining one generator for each pair consisting of an already constructed sphere and an $S Y$-cell at its image, in increasing rank. Its projection is a globular trivial fibration; this is a functorial cofibrant replacement suitable for computing the counit.

There is also a formula in the targets using only strict arities. In simplicial degree $k$ take the coproduct of $G_{P_0}$ over $P_0\to\cdots\to P_k$ and $t\in B(P_k)$. The first face applies the first strict arity map, the internal faces compose substitutions, the last restricts the label, and degeneracies insert identities. Geometric realization computes $E B$ by the chart theorem. The length-zero inclusions are the named charts of the unit; the strings with a fixed last label give all substitution homotopies and their compatibilities. For the counit evaluate the last label after projection by $q$, preceded by the strict arity maps in the string, using the natural resolution $R P\simeq G_P$. This is the chart cocone of the evaluation formula. The triangle identity on a chart sends $[t,r]$ back to that same coprojection, because the adjunct of the unit label is the characteristic map itself. Conversely evaluation after the unit at $S Y$ sends each label $R P\to Y$ to itself. These descriptions specify the maps without searching for raw representatives of coherences.

A bound on witnesses depending *only* on the finite computad is impossible for the literal lifting tests. Already on the one-loop graph, fix rank one and take the unit images of the composites $f^m$ for $m\geq1$. If one raw lift served two different powers up to invertible comparison, full faithfulness in the homotopy category of the fixed hom would identify the two powers. Strict evaluation in the free monoid distinguishes them. Thus infinitely many raw lifts are required in this fixed rank, whereas a finite-alphabet syntax has only finitely many expressions of bounded size. Iterated identities give tests in arbitrarily large dimension even on a single vertex. A bound parameterized by an individual lifting problem would be a different quantitative assertion.
