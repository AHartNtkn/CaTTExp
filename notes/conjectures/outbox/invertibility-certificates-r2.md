# Finite invertibility certificates with recorded inverses in free Gray-enriched ω-categories

## Setting

**Gray ω-categories.** Let ω-Cat be the category of strict ω-categories, with the lax Gray tensor product ⊗ of Ara–Maltsiniotis (*Joint et tranches pour les ∞-catégories strictes*, arXiv:1607.00668). A *Gray ω-category* is a category enriched in (ω-Cat, ⊗). Inside each hom K(a, b), cells compose as in a strict ω-category. Composition across objects sends the top cell of x ⊗ y (for x, y of positive dimension) to a *directed interchanger*, which in general is not invertible. The weak analogue is an oriented (∞,ω)-category in the sense of Gepner–Heine (arXiv:2510.10504). Throughout, f ; g is composition along the (n−1)-boundary in diagrammatic order (first f, then g).

**Invertibility (coinductive).** A cell f : x → y of dimension n ≥ 1 is *invertible* if there are cells g, g′ : y → x and invertible (n+1)-cells l : g ; f → 1_y and r : f ; g′ → 1_x.

**Free models with chosen inverses.** Fix a finite computad C and a set M of its positive-dimensional generators, the *marked* ones. Work with Gray ω-categories *equipped with inverse operations*: a partial operation that assigns to each cell f in a designated class Inv a package

  P(f) = (f⁻ˡ, f⁻ʳ, λ_f : f⁻ˡ ; f → 1, ρ_f : f ; f⁻ʳ → 1),

with λ_f, ρ_f ∈ Inv. The class must contain the marked generators, identities and the coherence cells (in the weak case), and be closed under the rules below. A cell certified in several ways may carry several packages. Rule 5 may use any package assigned to the cell, but no other inverse. Morphisms preserve Inv and the packages. Let F = F(C, M) be the free such structure on (C, M).

**Recorded certificates.** Cert ⊆ cells of F is the class Inv of F, generated by the following rules. Each rule also fixes the package of the cell it certifies.
1. **Generators.** Every marked generator m is in Cert, with its free package.
2. **Identities and coherences.** Identities are in Cert, with package (1, 1, unitor, unitor). In the weak case, coherence cells are in Cert, with packages made of coherence cells.
3. **Composites.** A pasting composite inside a hom is in Cert if each of its top-dimensional constituents is in Cert. Its package is built from the constituents' packages by the standard formulas (Benjamin–Markakis, arXiv:2406.12127, Thm 33).
4. **Gray cells.** The image of the top cell of x₁ ⊗ … ⊗ x_k under composition across objects is in Cert if some xᵢ of positive dimension in its hom is in Cert, or if all xᵢ are 1-cells of K and in Cert. Its package is built from that factor's package.
5. **Recorded inverses.** If f ∈ Cert, then f⁻ˡ, f⁻ʳ, λ_f, ρ_f ∈ Cert, with the packages the structure assigns. Only the package recorded for f is used. No other choice of inverse of f is admitted.
6. **inv-intro.** If f : x → y, g, g′ : y → x, l : g ; f → 1_y and r : f ; g′ → 1_x with l, r ∈ Cert, then f ∈ Cert, with package (g, g′, l, r).

**Why rule 5 is restricted.** If rule 5 admitted *any* inverse of a certified cell together with any witnesses, then applying it to an identity would certify every invertible contraction c → 1_z. Every invertible cell would then be certified trivially, and membership would not be decidable even without rule 6. Rule 5 here admits only the recorded package. Given a derivation, membership is therefore checked syntactically.

**What is known.**
- *Ordinary (non-Gray) case.* Every cell of F of dimension greater than dim C is in Cert. By induction on the term: no generator has that dimension; composites have top constituents of the same dimension; identities and coherences are certified; inverses are certified by rule 5. Hence if f is invertible in F, one can choose its coinductive tower of witnesses inside F. After at most dim C − dim f + 1 levels the witnesses are certified, and rule 6 certifies f by finitely many steps.
  - This uses the fact that a cell invertible in F has witnesses that are cells of F, which holds because F is a model.
  - This also needs the following, which is clear in the ordinary case: any cell of dimension > dim C that a witness tower might use is in Cert.
- *Gray case.* That argument fails. Directed interchangers exist in every dimension above dim C and are not invertible. For example, in the free Gray ω-category on two non-invertible 2-cells composable across an object, the 3-cell interchanger has no inverse.
- *Characterization (any model).* f is invertible iff its class is an isomorphism in the quotient H_n(E). Here E is the relation "connected by an invertible (n+1)-cell" on parallel n-cells, and H_n(E) is the category of (n−1)-boundary-fixed n-cells modulo E. This criterion is not algorithmic.
  - No test on supports or multiplicities of normal forms can decide invertibility. In the one-object presentation with 1-generators a, b and one marked 2-generator abab ⇒ 1, the normal forms ab and ba each use every generator once. ab is invertible and ba is not: interpret a as successor and b as truncated predecessor on ℕ.
- *Undecidability.* Membership in Cert, uniformly in (C, M), is undecidable once rule 6 is present. It decides the unit problem of finitely presented monoids: take one object, the generators as 1-cells, and each relation u = v as a marked 2-cell u ⇒ v. A finite monoid presentation presents a group iff every generator is a unit, and that is undecidable (Narendran–Ó'Dúnlaing–Otto, Discrete Math. 98 (1991)). Without rule 6, membership is decided by a syntax-directed check.

## Questions

1. **(Main question.)** For every finite marked computad (C, M), is every cell of F(C, M) that is invertible (coinductively, as a cell of the underlying Gray ω-category) in Cert? Equivalently: does every invertible cell have a finite derivation by rules 1–6, with rule 5 restricted to recorded packages? Answer for strict Gray ω-categories and for the weak (oriented (∞,ω)) version, and say where they differ.

2. **(If not.)** Give a finite marked computad and an invertible cell of F(C, M) that is not in Cert. Then give the smallest extension of rules 1–6 that is complete. One candidate is a recursor: a rule that takes a term over the walking invertible cell (the free structure on one marked generator) as a finite description of an infinite tower.

3. **(High dimensions.)** Whatever the answer to 1, is there a bound N(C), computable from C, such that every invertible cell of F(C, M) of dimension greater than N(C) is in the closure of rules 1–5? A positive answer would make the coinductive tower of any invertible cell finite, and would answer 1 positively. If no such bound exists, describe the invertible cells of high dimension, for example as composites of interchangers with an invertible factor together with cells from rules 1–5.
