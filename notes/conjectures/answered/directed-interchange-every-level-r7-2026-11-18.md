# Free higher categories with directed interchange in every dimension (seventh round)

*A self-contained problem. No background beyond standard higher category theory is assumed. Rounds 1–6, with their answers, are in `answered/`. Questions are in §4, smallest first.*

## 1. Setting

**Globular sets and composition.** Work with reflexive globular sets with source and target maps s, t, identities, and for k < n a partial composition ∗ₖ of n-cells along k-cells. Composition is written in **diagrammatic order** (u ∗ₖ v means "first u, then v"), and it is strictly associative and unital. The boundary formulas hold: for k < m and (m+1)-cells u, v,

```
s(u ∗ₖ v) = s(u) ∗ₖ s(v),     t(u ∗ₖ v) = t(u) ∗ₖ t(v).        (B)
```

For k < l < n and n-cells in interchange position put `L(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₗ b) ∗ₖ (c ∗ₗ d)` and `R(a,b,c,d) := (a ∗ₖ c) ∗ₗ (b ∗ₖ d)`. A strict ω-category requires L = R.

**The Gray case (directed interchange along 0-cells only).** In a category enriched in strict ω-categories with the lax Gray tensor product (Ara–Maltsiniotis, arXiv 1607.00668), for 2-cells α : f ⇒ f′ : x → y and β : g ⇒ g′ : y → z there is a 3-cell

```
χ_{α,β} : (f ◁ β) ∗₁ (α ▷ g′)  ⇛  (α ▷ g) ∗₁ (f′ ◁ β)
```

and nothing in the reverse direction. Composition along k-cells with k ≥ 1 is strict. For a finite globular pasting diagram Γ = ΣΘ₁ ∨ … ∨ ΣΘₙ, the free such structure has homs ν(λΘᵢ₊₁ ⊗ … ⊗ λΘⱼ). Here λ is Steiner's directed complex, ⊗ is Steiner's tensor with `∂(x ⊗ y) = ∂x ⊗ y + (−1)^{|x|} x ⊗ ∂y`, and ν is Steiner's functor to strict ω-categories (Steiner, HHA 2004). Each hom is generated by tuples of cells, and a cell is determined by the set of generators it uses.

**The aim** is the same for directed interchange along k-cells for **every** k, including inside homs.

## 2. What is already known (all proved)

**(K1) Raw higher interchangers are impossible.** If every pair of n-cells in interchange position had an (n+1)-cell L → R, then applying this to identities and using (B) forces L = R one dimension down. That contradicts directedness.

**(K2) A single comparison for each simultaneous variation is impossible.** The obvious repair of (K1) adds, for 3-cells A : a ⇛ a′, …, D : d ⇛ d′ in interchange position, a 4-cell between the parallel 3-cells

```
L(A,B,C,D) ∗₂ χ_{a′}     and     χ_a ∗₂ R(A,B,C,D).        (N)
```

This cannot be required for every instance. Take Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², with two 3-cells A : α ⇛ α′ and B : β ⇛ β′ side by side along a 0-cell, and the instance (A, 1, 1, B). Its two sides map to the hom 2-cells

```
U = (v₀ ∘₀ u₁) ∘₁ c₁₁,     V = c₀₀ ∘₁ (u₀ ∘₀ v₁)
```

of ν(λD² ⊗ λD²), where `u_j = A ⊗ g_j`, `v_i = f_i ⊗ B` and `c_ij = α_i ⊗ β_j`. There is no 4-cell between U and V in either direction: the degree-3 chain would need a positive coefficient at u₀ (or at v₀), but every basis element of degree 3 has a non-positive coefficient there.

**(K3) The two variables have opposite naturality directions.** The degree-3 basis elements are `r_j = A ⊗ β_j` and `t_i = α_i ⊗ B`. Their differentials are

```
∂r_j = c_1j + u₁ − c_0j − u₀,     ∂t_i = c_i0 + v₁ − c_i1 − v₀.
```

So when only A varies, the Gray structure has a naturality 4-cell from the "χ first" side to the "variation first" side (V ⇛ U). When only B varies, it goes the other way (U ⇛ V). The simultaneous variation is the 5-cell A ⊗ B. It goes between two routes of 4-cells, `S → V → T` ⇛ `S → U → T`, where S and T are the composites through c₀₁ and c₁₀. In short, the higher structure is **tensor-shaped**: one Leibniz-signed naturality cell per variable, and higher cells between routes.

**(K4) Supports must use an expanded basis.** If every composite's support were the union of the supports of its parts, L and R would always have equal support. Then "support determines the cell" would force strict interchange.

**(K5) One finite basis forces finitely many cells.** If one finite set B admits injective support maps in every dimension, the structure has finitely many non-identity cells in total.

**(K6) No single directed complex presents the answer, and neither does a hom-wise family of them.** In ν(K), the two composites defining χ have the same chain table, so they are equal. A description must therefore be an iterated tower: objects, then homs presented again by objects and homs, and so on.

**(K7) The first uniform case through hom-dimension 3.** For Γ = ΣD² ∨ ΣD², directing interchange inside the hom 𝒦(x, z) adds exactly two generators of hom-dimension 3 beyond the 25 tensor generators. They are the interchangers χ₁ of (A⊗g, f′⊗B) and χ₂ of (f⊗B, A⊗g′), the only pairs of hom-2-cells composable along a hom-0-cell. Their two sides have different supports. Neither occurs in the boundary of A ⊗ B.

- Its arrows are BC → CB, CA → AC and DX → XD. The arrow between A and B is positional: AB → BA when C follows the pair, and BA → AB when C precedes it.
- Compare with the graph that uses BA → AB everywhere, which has one source, DBCA, and one sink, ACBD. The positional graph has sources {DBCA, DABC} and sinks {ACBD, BACD}.
- Of the three reversed arrows, BADC → ABDC creates neither a source nor a sink.

So a flipped face does not create one extra source/sink pair in the full comparison graph. It does so only for an isolated polygon.

## 3. Requirements

A **tensor-shaped lax-interchange ω-category** has, for every k < l, elementary interchangers χ : L ⇛ R for (l+1)-cells with no reverse cell in free objects; one naturality cell per varying argument, oriented by the Leibniz sign; higher cells between routes of lower naturality cells; and coherence equations to be determined. The free objects F(Γ) must satisfy:
1. an explicit combinatorial description by generators with explicit boundaries;
2. two cells of the same dimension using the same generators, in the expanded basis, are equal;
3. every elementary interchanger exists and its reverse does not;
4. making all interchangers with k ≥ 1 identities gives the free Gray-enriched category, and making all interchangers identities gives the free strict ω-category;
5. closure under substitution of cells for generators.

## 1. Q3: a finite source-only completion with unique normal forms

Let \(Q\) be a finite quiver whose generating arrows are partitioned into:

- \(E\): inner-interchanger arrows;
- \(P\): the remaining generating arrows.

Assume:

1. the subquiver consisting of \(E\) is acyclic;
2. there is a function
   \[
   \phi:V(Q)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z
   \]
   such that
   \[
   \phi(s(e))=\phi(t(e))\quad(e\in E),
   \qquad
   \phi(s(\alpha))<\phi(t(\alpha))\quad(\alpha\in P).
   \]

The second assumption is stronger than acyclicity of \(Q\). It holds for the K14 graph, as verified below.

### Construction

For every generating arrow \(\alpha:s\to t\) in \(P\), and every **literal directed \(E\)-path**
\[
R:u\longrightarrow t,
\]
adjoin a generator
\[
j_{\alpha,R}:s\longrightarrow u.
\]

Identify the empty-path case with the original generator:
\[
j_{\alpha,1_t}=\alpha.
\]

For an inner arrow \(e:u\to v\) and an inner path \(R:v\to t\), impose
\[
\boxed{
j_{\alpha,e;R};e=j_{\alpha,R}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Thus a previously added joining arrow can itself be factored. Distinct paths \(R\) remain distinct indices, even when they have the same endpoints.

### Termination and order independence of adjoining generators

There are finitely many directed paths in the finite acyclic quiver \(E\). Hence the number of positive generators is exactly
\[
|J|
=
\sum_{\alpha\in P}
\#\{\text{directed \(E\)-paths ending at }t(\alpha)\}.
\tag{2}
\]

Starting with empty-path indices and repeatedly prepending an inner arrow generates every pair \((\alpha,R)\), by induction on the length of \(R\). A fair saturation procedure that identifies records by the exact pair \((\alpha,R)\) therefore terminates with the same generators and equations, independently of processing order.

This is a rule on the entire comparison graph; it does not count sources or sinks face by face.

### Confluence of the equations

Orient (1) as contraction:
\[
j_{\alpha,e;R};e\longrightarrow j_{\alpha,R}.
\tag{3}
\]

Every contraction decreases path length by one.

Every left-hand side has arrow types
\[
(\text{positive},\text{inner}).
\]
Two such left-hand sides cannot overlap in one arrow: that arrow would have to be both positive and inner. Disjoint contractions commute. Moreover, a left-hand side determines its right-hand side uniquely because its recorded path has a specified first edge.

Induction on path length now proves that every path has a unique normal form. In particular, equations applied within longer paths preserve that normal form.

Every original path is irreducible: its positive generators have empty path indices. Consequently, the original free path category embeds faithfully in the completed category.

### Injective supports

For \(R:u\to t(\alpha)\),
\[
\phi(u)=\phi(t(\alpha))>\phi(s(\alpha)).
\]
Thus every new positive generator strictly increases \(\phi\). A directed cycle using a positive generator is impossible; an all-inner cycle is excluded by assumption. The completed quiver is acyclic.

Define support from the **unique normal path**, using its vertex IDs and actual generating-arrow IDs:
\[
\operatorname{supp}(f)
=
\operatorname{Vert}(\operatorname{NF}(f))
\;\sqcup\;
\operatorname{Edges}(\operatorname{NF}(f)).
\tag{4}
\]

A directed path in an acyclic quiver is determined by its edge set: a topological ordering recovers the order of those edges. Vertex IDs distinguish identity paths. Therefore (4) is injective.

This uses actual generators \(j_{\alpha,R}\), rather than arbitrary labels assigned to completed cells. As in K16, support is taken **after normalization**. Composition is concatenation followed by normalization; support is not union of the letters in arbitrary expressions.

### Exact preservation of the inner quotient

Making \(E\) identities also identifies their source and target vertices. In that quotient, repeated use of (1) gives
\[
j_{\alpha,R}=\alpha.
\]

The inclusion of the original presentation and the assignment
\[
e\longmapsto 1,\qquad j_{\alpha,R}\longmapsto\alpha
\]
therefore induce inverse functors:
\[
\boxed{
C(Q)/(E=1)
\cong
\operatorname{Path}(Q)/(E=1).
}
\tag{5}
\]

Thus the completion preserves an already established inner quotient. It does not establish that the original quotient was the desired Gray category.

## 2. Why this completion does not give substitution closure

There is a concrete failure under a generator-to-composite map using the existing \(h_z\)-factorization.

Use these five word vertices:
\[

w_2&=\texttt{bapcqr},&

\end{aligned}

The inner graph has arrows
\[

\qquad
q:u\to w_2,\qquad f:w_2\to v.
\]

The original tensor arrow
\[

\]
has the K14 factorization
\[
\tau=A;f,
\qquad
A=h_z:x\to w_2.
\tag{6}
\]

Consider the comparison-category functor fixing the four inner arrows and sending \(\tau\) to \(A;f\).

The source completion has
\[

\qquad
j_{\tau,e};e=\tau.
\]
An extension of the functor would require an arrow \(H:x\to w_1\) in the target completion satisfying
\[
H;e=A;f.
\tag{7}
\]

No such \(H\) exists.

### Proof

Every contraction (3) preserves the ordered list of positive seed IDs. Therefore equality with \(A;f\) forces \(H\) to use exactly one positive generator, whose seed is \(A\).

Its initial inner path would run from \(x\) to \(x\), so it is empty. Write
\[
H=j_{A,R};P
\]
for inner paths \(R,P\).

The path \(A;f\) is irreducible. For \(j_{A,R};P;e\) to normalize to \(A;f\), contractions must consume exactly \(R\) and leave exactly \(f\). Hence the literal inner-edge words would have to satisfy
\[
P;e=R;f.
\]
Their final edges differ, since \(e\ne f\). This is impossible.

The argument also applies to the full K14 target completion: introducing another positive seed into \(H\) cannot help, because its ID survives normalization. The checker independently verifies the actual arrows and enumerates the sole candidate in the relevant subgraph.

This proves that the completion is **not natural under arbitrary comparison-category functors sending generators to composites**. Restricted naturality does hold when positive seeds map to positive seeds and inner arrows map to inner paths.

I have not realized the displayed functor as a substitution of the original globular generators of \(\Gamma\). Therefore this is a counterexample to the proposed completion’s stronger functoriality, not an impossibility proof for requirement 5.
